## Part I. Executive Structural Audit: Testing the Six Core Shifts (As of Nov 19, 2026)

By Q4 2026, data-center AI compute has split into two diverging hardware regimes: **general-purpose rack-scale GPUs** (led by NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 and AMD Instinct MI450 Helios) and **workload-specialized custom ASICs** (Google TPU v7/v8, AWS Trainium3, Microsoft Maia 200, Meta MTIA Iris, OpenAI Jalapeño), which TrendForce projects will reach **27.8% of AI server shipments in 2026** (vs. 69.7% GPU-based). Inference now accounts for roughly **two-thirds of total AI compute in 2026** (up from half in 2025, per Deloitte), driving the top four US hyperscalers (Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta) toward **~$725B+ in combined 2026 CapEx**—pushing both Alphabet and Amazon into negative free cash flow.

### 1. Real-World Perf/Watt & Perf/$ (Independent Data vs. Vendor Claims)
The gap between peak datasheet FLOPS and realized production throughput widened dramatically in 2026 as low-precision sparse marketing (NVFP4/MXFP4) collided with memory-bandwidth-bound decode and multi-step agentic workloads:
* **Independent Inference Telemetry (SemiAnalysis InferenceX, Sept–Oct 2026):** In SemiAnalysis's own InferenceX runs, Google's **Ironwood (TPU v7)** delivered **~$0.181 per million tokens** versus **$0.222 for NVIDIA B200** and **$0.276 for NVIDIA B300**. On SemiAnalysis's agentic inference benchmark, Google's TPU v7 submission was revised downward from an initial **1.67×** claim to **1.42× performance-per-dollar** over NVIDIA GB300 NVL72.
* **MLPerf Training v6.1 (Released Nov 16, 2026):** NVIDIA swept every benchmark and debuted **Vera Rubin NVL72**, claiming up to **2× performance per GPU over GB300 NVL72** on training and **>10× tokens/watt** on MLPerf Endpoints. Independent analysts (Dr. Ian Cutress) flagged that the 10× Endpoints figure mixes architecture, rack design, and software maturity and lacks independent third-party reproduction. In the same round, Google submitted **8,192 Ironwood TPUs** completing DeepSeek-V3 (671B) pre-training in **1.70 minutes** and **640 Ironwood TPUs** completing Qwen3.5-397B agentic RL (GRPO) in **18.02 minutes** (<3% idle wait time), while 256 GB300 GPUs on GCP took 31.96 minutes on DeepSeek-V3 (non-iso-scale comparisons).
* **MLPerf Inference v6.1 (Released Sept 16, 2026):** AMD submitted its broadest entry yet across 7 models on **MI355X, MI350X, and MI350P**, hitting **1M tokens/sec** on 72-GPU GPT-OSS-120B (95% scaling) and submitting a 512-GPU MI355X run with Crusoe. Notably absent from audited MLPerf v6.1 datacenter inference/training submissions: AWS Trainium3, Microsoft Maia 200, Meta MTIA, Cerebras CS-3/CS-4, Etched Sohu, SambaNova SN50, and Qualcomm AI200.
* **Hot Chips 2026 Vendor Claims Requiring Caveats:** OpenAI benchmarked its Broadcom-built **Jalapeño** inference ASIC (TSMC 3nm, 6-stack HBM4, 216 GiB, 15.4 TB/s, 700W) on SemiAnalysis's public InferenceX suite, claiming **1.5–1.9× throughput/kW** and **1.7–3.6× lower end-to-end latency** vs. GB200/GB300 racks on GPT-OSS-120B, DeepSeek-R1 670B, and Kimi K2.5—*however*, it was not tested against Vera Rubin, does not train, and compared single-token prediction where NVIDIA's production multi-token prediction narrows Jalapeño's efficiency lead to ~1.5×. Similarly, AMD's Nov 12, 2026 **MI450X** claim of 21,333 tok/s on DeepSeek-R1 was internal AMD Performance Labs testing rather than an independent reproduction.

### 3. Physical Supply Bottlenecks: HBM4, CoWoS, Substrates & Scale-Up Fabrics
* **Memory Wall & Oligopoly Pricing Power:** HBM is the primary physical governor of global AI compute through 2028. Counterpoint Research (Sept 3, 2026) puts Q2 2026 HBM revenue share at **SK Hynix 50%**, **Samsung 33%** (rebounding sharply from 15% in Q2 2025), and **Micron 18%**. SK Hynix posted record Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 60.5T (~$42B) on mass HBM4 shipments; Micron confirmed the "vast majority" of calendar 2027 HBM supply is already booked at significantly higher prices. DRAM+NAND is projected to reach **47% of hyperscaler CapEx in 2026 and 68% in 2027**, driving NVIDIA's **$111B single-quarter jump in supply commitments** (to $390B) specifically for multi-year memory procurement.
* **CoWoS Advanced Packaging Chokepoint:** Global CoWoS demand approaches **~1 million wafers in 2026** (up from 370k in 2024 and 670k in 2025). TSMC is expanding monthly capacity to **130,000–140,000 wafers/month** by end-2026 yet still faces a **20–30%+ supply gap** into 2027, prompting **10–20% CoWoS price hikes** announced in Oct 2026 and spillovers to ASE/Amkor. Crucially, **zero CoWoS modules are assembled in the US today**—TSMC Arizona Fab 21 wafers still fly back to Taiwan for packaging until Amkor Peoria starts in early 2028. CoWoS-S bottlenecks forced SemiAnalysis to slash Google TPU v7 2026 estimates by 500k units in Aug 2026, prompting Google to explore **Intel EMIB-T** for TPU v9.
* **Scale-Up Interconnect Wars (NVLink 6 vs. SUE vs. UALink):**
  * **NVIDIA NVLink 6** (shipping now in Vera Rubin NVL72) delivers **3.6 TB/s per GPU** (260 TB/s coherent rack domain). NVIDIA opened **NVLink Fusion** to third-party custom silicon (signing Fujitsu and buying **$3.5B of MediaTek convertibles** on Aug 31, 2026 to pull custom XPUs into NVLink racks).
  * **Broadcom Scale-Up Ethernet (SUE / Tomahawk Ultra)** is shipping today as a sub-250ns lossless Ethernet scale-up alternative (51.2T, 77 Bpps) scaling up to 1,024 endpoints without memory coherency. However, ServeTheHome noted in Oct 2026 that current Tomahawk Ultra uses 112G PAM4 SerDes while AMD Helios requires 224G PAM4 (forcing Helios onto higher-capacity Ethernet switches like Tomahawk 6).
  * **UALink (Open NVLink Equivalent):** Despite publishing the 1.0 spec (April 2025) backed by AMD, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and Intel, **no commercial UALink switch chip had shipped as of mid-to-late 2026**; first lab silicon is only arriving late 2026, leaving Broadcom's SUE and proprietary fabrics (Google ICI, AWS NeuronLink-v4, Huawei Unified Bus) filling the non-NVIDIA void.

### 4. CUDA vs. Compiler Portability & The Model-Churn Trap
* **Tile-Level Abstraction Is Eroding Single-Device CUDA Lock-In—Slowly:** PyTorch 2's TorchInductor emits **OpenAI Triton** by default; **TileLang** (now merged natively into the Ascend 950 backend as of Oct 2026), **JAX/Pallas**, **DITRON** (ByteDance/ICML 2026, matching/beating cuBLAS by 6–30% across NVIDIA and AMD), and **Modular's Mojo/MAX** (acquired by Qualcomm for $3.1–3.9B in July 2026, Apache 2.0 Mojo compiler) have lowered single-kernel porting barriers.
* **Where the Moat Still Bites (Distributed Collectives & Day-0 Architecture Churn):** As the Nov 2026 *Obaleth* paper (arXiv) demonstrates, while single-chip compute compilers have commoditized, **distributed GPU-initiated networking remains trapped inside hand-tuned vendor libraries** (NCCL, NVSHMEM). Moreover, NVIDIA countered compiler abstraction by releasing **CuTe DSL / cuTile** inside CUTLASS 4 and moving to buy **Hugging Face for $12.93B** (Sept 2026) to anchor the 18M-developer model distribution layer. Meanwhile, rapid shifts from dense Transformers to sparse MoE (DeepSeek-V3/V4, Qwen3.5), multi-token prediction (MTP), MLA attention, and long-context agentic RL punish rigid silicon: hardwired/fixed-function approaches (AMD's newly acquired **Taalas** mask-ROM HC1, or **Etched's** transformer-only bet) face severe obsolescence risk whenever attention mechanisms or quantization formats mutate faster than a 2–24 month tape-out cycle.

### 5. AI-Assisted Chip Design Speed: Reality vs. Marketing
* **Verified Production Gains (PPA & Floorplanning, Not Zero-Human Tape-Outs):** Reinforcement-learning EDA tools—**Synopsys DSO.ai**, **Cadence Cerebrus** (>1,000 production designs reported), and **Google AlphaChip** (used in TPU v5e/v5p/Trillium, Axion, and extended by MediaTek)—genuinely compress macro floorplanning and PPA tuning from months to hours/days. OpenAI credited its own models in verification/design flows for helping design **Jalapeño** with Broadcom in roughly **9 months**, and Taalas claims a **~2-month** metal-layer-only respin for fixed models.
* **The Reality Gap:** An exhaustive Feb 2026 academic survey (*The Dawn of Agentic EDA*, arXiv) confirms that **no LLM-based autonomous agent has achieved a zero-human-in-the-loop tape-out of an industrial-grade chip**. Academic researchers continue to dispute AlphaChip's original reproducibility and baseline comparisons, and DAC 2026 panels (EE Times, July 2026) highlighted the core bottleneck: **no internet-scale corpus of proprietary RTL/layout data exists**, meaning fine-tuned general LLMs plateau quickly while 3D chiplet/CoWoS thermal-mechanical sign-off adds months back onto schedules.

### 6. China Domestic Silicon & The Nov 19, 2026 BIS Remote-Compute Crackdown
* **Domestic Share Surge Built on Sanctions, Not Parity:** Per IDC, NVIDIA's China data-center AI accelerator unit share fell from absolute dominance to **34% in H1 2026** (678k units, -44% YoY), while **Huawei hit 28% (572k units, ~2× YoY)**, Alibaba T-Head 7%, Cambricon 5%, Baidu Kunlunxin 4%, and Hygon 3%. Nine of eleven tracked Chinese vendors grew shipments YoY.
* **The Memory & Lithography Ceiling:** Without EUV or legal Western HBM imports (cut Dec 2024), Epoch AI models that Huawei's ~1.5M 2026 Ascend units rely heavily on non-HBM variants (~750k 950PR) and finite stockpiled/smuggled HBM (~510k units), with domestic CXMT/XMC HBM supporting only ~240k 950DT units. Epoch estimates Huawei's total 2026 compute output is **under 4% of NVIDIA's** and trails by 3–4 years through 2030.
* **BIS Interim Final Rule RIN 0694-AK50 (Effective Today, Nov 19, 2026):** Filed Nov 16 by BIS at the direction of Treasury and the NSC, this rule closes the overseas cloud loophole—requiring a US license for any overseas data center supplying **>12 million aggregate TPP (~750 H100/H200 GPUs)** remotely to China/Macau/D:5 entities (with a 90-day compliance window), imposing direct chip export licenses on **Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil**, and formally rescinding the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. IFP estimates **~1.05M H100-equivalents** of overseas compute accessed by Chinese entities (75% in Southeast Asia) are now exposed, forcibly redirecting demand back onto domestic silicon.

#### Science & Engineering Audit
* **Solid Ground:** Vera Rubin NVL72 directly solves the twin bottlenecks of 2026 agentic/MoE inference: HBM4 doubles the bus width per stack to achieve **22 TB/s per GPU** (nearly 3× Blackwell's 8 TB/s), while NVLink 6 delivers **260 TB/s** coherent rack bandwidth and cableless 3rd-gen MGX trays slash rack assembly from ~2 hours to ~5 minutes. #### Science & Engineering Audit
* **Contrarian Physics Bet (Anti-HBM / Anti-CoWoS / Networked AI):** Tenstorrent deliberately rejects HBM and 2.5D CoWoS interposers—using commodity **TSMC 6nm + GDDR6 + on-die 400GbE Ethernet switching + integrated RISC-V host orchestration**. That slashes BOM cost by an order of magnitude ($999–$1,399/card; $110k/32-chip rack) and avoids TSMC's 50-week CoWoS/HBM4 queue entirely.
* **Binding Limits:**
  1. *Memory Bandwidth Wall:* A full 32-chip Galaxy Blackhole rack delivers **16 TB/s aggregate GDDR6 bandwidth**—**less than a single NVIDIA Rubin GPU (22 TB/s HBM4)** and only twice a single B300/MI355X (8 TB/s). That forces Tenstorrent to rely heavily on on-chip SRAM tiling and multi-chip Ethernet sharding, creating severe compiler complexity for large-context/dense LLMs.
  2. *Production Silicon Shortfall:* Shipping Blackhole silicon dropped from roadmap targets of **140 Tensix cores / 774 TFLOPS down to 120 cores / 664 TFLOPS**, with firmware revisions lowering earlier performance claims.
  3. *Open-Source Stack Maturity:* **TT-Metalium / TT-Forge** are genuinely open source (allowing buyers like Virtu Financial and sovereign labs to inspect and modify the entire stack), but still lack turnkey vLLM/SGLang parity and zero audited MLPerf v6.1 datacenter entries exist.
* **Verdict on Engineering Ground:** **Credible, differentiated architecture for sovereign IP licensing, HFT/edge-datacenter customizers, and GDDR-budget inference**, but **physics-limited against HBM4 hyperscale clusters** on frontier models until next-gen 2nm/photonic chiplets arrive.

#### Hostile Cross-Examination
* **Claim: "$6.2B valuation and $1.5B Series E prove broad enterprise datacenter adoption."** *Counter-evidence:* Despite shipping Wormhole and Blackhole hardware for years and employing ~1,220 staff, Tenstorrent has **never disclosed revenue, unit volumes, or independent MLPerf/InferenceX perf/watt benchmarks**—and its headline production deployments remain small specialized users (**Virtu Financial** HFT, **Cirrascale** cloud hosting, **Japan ai&**, sovereign NEDO/Rapidus R&D). Meanwhile, its flagship Rapidus 2nm foundry partner faces steep unproven-fab execution risk in Hokkaido.
* **Claim: "No-HBM GDDR6 + Ethernet beats GPUs on TCO."** *Counter-evidence:* GDDR6 saves upfront hardware capex, *but* when one single Vera Rubin GPU delivers more memory bandwidth (22 TB/s) than an entire 32-chip Blackhole rack (16 TB/s), memory-bandwidth-bound LLM decode shifts total cluster footprint, networking overhead, and compiler engineering hours back onto the buyer—leaving Tenstorrent's TCO claim unverified at production scale.

# Camtek Ltd. (CAMT): export-control rules on HBM and advanced-packaging equipment, and China exposure (Module A addition)

**Scope:** Module A addition on export rules. The notes cover: (a) the US rules (BIS October 2022, October 2023 and December 2024 rules, HBM controls, FDP rules and de minimis, Entity List status of Chinese memory, HBM and packaging firms, 2025–2026 BIS rules) and whether Camtek-type inspection and metrology tools fall under ECCNs that a non-US tool can be captured by; (b) Israeli rules; (c) everything Camtek has said on export controls, China restrictions, Entity List customers and licensing (20-F FY2023–FY2025, releases, calls); (d) independent reporting on Camtek sales to Chinese HBM and packaging makers, and US congressional or competitor scrutiny; and a cross-check of China share of revenue. Fiscal year = calendar year. Latest reported quarter: **Q3 2026** (release and call 4 Nov 2026) [CO]. Notes compiled 21 Nov 2026. Data only: no recommendations, no investment opinion.

## 0. Summary of findings (data only)

1. **No US rule names Camtek-type tools specifically.** The only US controls written for wafer inspection and metrology target front-end, advanced-node tools: 3B993.q.1 (patterned-wafer defect inspection on ≥300 mm wafers detecting defects ≤21 nm with a <400 nm light source or an e-beam source), 3B993.q.2 (overlay and wafer-shape metrology), 3B994.q.3 (OCD and thin-film metrology for non-planar transistors), and 3B002.c (EUV mask inspection) [2], [3]. The HBM-specific controls of December 2024 cover the HBM chips themselves (3A090.c) and TSV etch tools (3B001.c.3 and c.4), **not** inspection or metrology [3].
2. **ANALYSIS:** Camtek's 2D automated optical inspection (Eagle, Hawk) and 3D metrology fit the wording of the AT-only ECCN **3B992.b.1** ("stored program controlled" inspection for defects of 0.6 µm or less on processed wafers using optical pattern comparison) and possibly **3B992.b.2.b/c** (line-width measurement at 1 µm resolution or finer; flatness measurement) [3]. Camtek has not disclosed its classifications. "Not disclosed by Camtek."
3. **Made in Israel and Germany, Camtek's tools are not subject to the EAR by default.** They become subject only through (i) de minimis US content, (ii) a foreign direct product (FDP) rule, or (iii) US-person rules [6], [3]. Camtek itself states this same mechanism in its 20-F risk factors [24]–[26].
4. **The December 2024 rule (as extended in January 2025) is the one that can reach Camtek.** For a Category 3B item (including 3B992) that contains **any** US-origin Category 3/4/5 integrated circuit, there is **no de minimis threshold** when the tool goes to a Footnote 5 entity on the Entity List, or to a China/Macau facility known to produce logic or DRAM "advanced-node ICs" (§734.4(a)(9)) [3], [5], [6]. Under §744.11(a)(2)(v)(A)(3)(i), a licence is then required for 3B991, 3B992, 3B993 and 3B994 items exported by an entity headquartered in a country that is **not in Country Group A:5 and not in Supplement No. 4 to Part 742** [6]. **Israel is in Country Group A:6 only, not A:5, and is not in Supplement No. 4 to Part 742** (Germany, Japan and the Netherlands are) [3], [6]. ANALYSIS: Camtek (Israeli-headquartered) is therefore in the group for which the FN5/advanced-node-facility licence requirement applies to 3B992-class tools, if those tools contain a US-origin IC. Whether they do is "Not disclosed by Camtek."
5. **The broad China-wide SME controls (3B001/3B090 lists, the SME FDP in §734.9(k), and the 0% de minimis of §734.4(a)(8)) cover front-end tool types, not 3B992-class inspection** [2], [3]. ANALYSIS: sales of Camtek-type tools to Chinese OSATs that are not on the Entity List and are not advanced-node logic or DRAM fabs do not appear to need a US licence, on the text reviewed.
6. **Entity List status of Chinese HBM and packaging firms** (§2.6): YMTC listed Dec 2022 [8]; Wuhan Xinxin (XMC) listed Dec 2024 with Footnote 5, but eligible for the new License Exception RFF for 3B991/3B992 items [4]; SMIC group entries including SJ Semiconductor tightened Dec 2024 [4]; Huawei-linked SwaySure, Pengxinxu and SiEn listed with FN5 [4]; **CXMT is not on the Entity List** as of 13 Nov 2026 (Sen. Cotton asked for its addition on 12 Nov 2026) [12]; Tongfu is not on any US list (Apr 2026) [21]; JCET: not found on the Entity List.
7. **No new BIS SME rule was published between 1 Jan 2025 and 19 Nov 2026** in the Federal Register's list of BIS rules [7]. 2025–2026 actions were Entity List additions, VEU revocations for Samsung, SK hynix and Intel China fabs (Sep 2025), the 50% "affiliates" rule (Sep 2025, suspended since Nov 2025, now through 10 Jan 2027), a chip licensing-policy change (Jan 2026) and a remote-access rule (19 Nov 2026) [7]. A third-party report of a "May 15, 2026" BIS amendment adding AOI+XRF tools to 3B001 does **not** appear in the Federal Register list (see Contradictions).
8. **Israel:** dual-use exports are controlled under the 2006 Import and Export Order (Ministry of Economy, Export Control Agency), based on Wassenaar and other regime lists, and military end-users fall under the Defense Export Control Law 2007 (Ministry of Defense) [14]–[17]. Israel is not a Wassenaar member [14]. **Not found: any Israeli semiconductor-equipment control aligned with the US rules.** A draft dual-use law (26 Mar 2026) would add catch-all and national-list powers. It was open for comment until 10 May 2026, and it does not mention semiconductors or China [17].
9. **Camtek's own statements:** (Q3 2022) the October 2022 rules' "direct revenue impact will be marginal, if any" (QUALITATIVE) [33]. (FY2023 20-F) Entity-Listed Chinese manufacturers "have acquired and may continue to acquire several of our metrology solutions" (QUALITATIVE) [24]. This sentence was **dropped** from the FY2024 and FY2025 20-Fs [25], [26]. Camtek has **never quantified** revenue from Entity-Listed customers, licences applied for or denied, or any revenue lost to export controls. "Not disclosed by Camtek."
10. **Independent scrutiny:** Onto Innovation, Camtek's US competitor, told BIS on 17 Jan 2024 that "Israeli-based companies such as Nova and Camtek ... do not abide by U.S. export control rules", that "Israeli companies such as Camtek ... are supplying to entity-listed companies", and it asked that "the same U.S. export control rules ... apply to Israel as well, in particular Nova and Camtek" [13]. The House Select Committee (Oct 2025) noted that the December 2024 FDPR now reaches tools made in Israel [9]. The pending MATCH Act (not enacted as of 17 Nov 2026) would apply US controls to allied-made tools with **>0% US content** if allies do not match within 150 days. It presumptively covers 3B001/3B002/3B993 (not 3B992) and names CXMT [10], [11].
11. **China share of revenue:** Camtek does not disclose it by quarter. Annual figures from the 20-F: 2021 54.8% (DERIVED), 2022 44.2% (DERIVED), 2023 47%, 2024 31%, 2025 49%; FY2026 guided "45-ish" (Q2 2026 call) and "around 45%" (Analyst Day, 14 Oct 2026) [24]–[26], [46], [48]. These cross-check with [49].

ANALYSIS on the "US nexus" question:
- Camtek USA Inc. is a US person. Under the October 2022 rule, "specific activities of 'U.S. persons' that 'support' the 'development' or 'production' of certain ICs in the PRC require a license" [1]. This can limit what Camtek USA staff (and any US citizens employed elsewhere in the group) can do for advanced-node fabs in China. Camtek does not say how this affects it.
- FRT GmbH ships from Germany, which is in Supplement No. 4 to Part 742 [3]. But FRT's ultimate parent is headquartered in Israel. The current §744.11(a)(2)(v)(A)(4)(i) (in-country transfers) and (A)(3)(i) look at the headquarters of the ultimate parent [6]. How these apply to an FRT export from Germany is not resolved by the text I read, because (A)(3) is triggered by the country of export ("countries not listed in Country Group A:5") and Germany is in A:5. **Not verified.**

## 7. Contradictions

1. **Entity-List customers, FY2023 vs FY2024/FY2025 20-F.** FY2023 20-F: Entity-Listed Chinese manufacturers "have acquired and may continue to acquire several of our metrology solutions" [24]. The FY2024 and FY2025 20-Fs drop this sentence without explanation [25], [26]. The filings do not say whether the sales stopped or only the disclosure changed.
2. **Revenue-loss language added, then removed.** The FY2024 20-F warns that additional US controls "on our products or systems ... could lead to further revenue losses" [25]. The word "further" implies earlier losses, which Camtek never quantified. The FY2025 20-F drops the sentence, although the December 2024 rules had by then extended US reach to Israeli-made tools [3], [26].
3. **Camtek's "marginal, if any" vs Onto's claims.** Camtek (Nov 2022): direct impact of US rules "marginal, if any"; customers "not ... under this restriction" [33]. Onto (Jan 2024): Camtek "do[es] not abide by U.S. export control rules" and is "supplying to entity-listed companies" [13]. In Oct 2022 and Jan 2024 Israeli-made tools were generally outside the EAR, so "do not abide" describes non-coverage rather than a violation. No source reviewed alleges a violation by Camtek, and no BIS enforcement action against Camtek was found.
4. **Onto's footnote does not support its Camtek claim.** The sentence "Israeli companies such as Camtek ... are supplying to entity-listed companies" cites footnote 4, a Tom's Hardware piece on South Korean toolmakers [13].
5. **SIA on Israeli authority (same document).** "Israel does not have national authority to exercise controls ... beyond those identified in the regime control lists" vs "The MOE has the authority to impose a unilateral control on semiconductors ... without the approval of the Knesset" [14].
6. **Unverified "May 15, 2026" BIS rule.** A third-party site (jsjtoop.com, "US Expands Export Controls on Semiconductor Equipment", undated) claims BIS added "online monitoring equipment for advanced photoresist coating and defect inspection" (AOI+XRF hybrids) to ECCN 3B001 on 15 May 2026. No such rule appears in the Federal Register list of BIS rules for 2025–2026 [7]. The article gives no FR citation. **Treat as unsupported.** URL: https://www.jsjtoop.com/news/active-components/MCU_Chipsets/US_Expands_Export_Controls_on_Semiconductor_Manufacturing_Equipment_726737.html
7. **Tender record manufacturer country.** The 2024 Zhejiang tender lists the manufacturer as "Camtek H.K. Limited", country "中国" (China) [19]. The 2025 Shaanxi tender lists "Camtek Ltd.", "以色列" (Israel) [20]. Camtek's 20-F says manufacturing is only in Israel and Germany [26]. The 2024 entry probably reflects the Hong Kong selling entity, not a production site. **Not verified.**
8. **Q1 2026 transcript wording.** The analyst said 2026 growth "seems to be more driven by the non-China market". Ramy Langer replied "As you mentioned, the overall, the major contribution will come out of China" [45]. "Out of China" probably means "outside China", given the analyst's premise, but the transcript wording reads the other way. Also flagged in [49].
9. **China "largest territory" vs 2024 data.** The FY2024 20-F calls China "our largest territory" [25], yet the same 20-F shows "Asia Pacific" (ex-China, ex-Korea) at $133.8M vs China $132.6M [25]. As a single country, China was still the largest, because Asia Pacific is a multi-country group. Recorded as a wording ambiguity.
10. **FY2025 China share: forecast vs actual.** Q4 2024 call: "anywhere between 30% and 35%" for 2025 [40]. Q2 2025 call: "a little bit higher" than ~30% [42]. Actual per the FY2025 20-F: 49% [26]. Also recorded in [49].

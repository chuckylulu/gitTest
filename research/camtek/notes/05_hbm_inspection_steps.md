## 1. Summary of findings

- **Camtek has never given a step count per HBM generation.** Searches covered 15 calls (Q1 2023 to Q3 2026), the October 2026 investor day, three 20-Fs and the order releases. No number was found for inspection or metrology steps per HBM3E, HBM4, HBM4E or hybrid-bonded stack. No figure was found for dollars per wafer or tools per 10k wafers of HBM capacity either. The only quantified "steps" statement is "tens of new inspection and metrology steps" developed "over the last year" at one major customer [4]. Camtek later said those steps "are in the advanced packaging world", without tying them to HBM.
- **Camtek's story on what drives HBM content changed over time:**
  - **May 2024 [5].** Stack height "doesn't change our business… we scan wafers". There is "no difference" between 8-high and 12-high, and "no" ASP uplift.
  - **November 2024 to August 2025 [6][7][9].** More layers mean "we will scan more wafers". Intensity "will probably grow as you're putting more and more dies in the same module".
  - **November 2025 to February 2026 [10][11].** HBM4 is "inspection and metrology intensive because the bumps are getting tighter. There are going to be more bumps".
  - **October 2026 [15][16].** Generic "more steps × beyond-installed-base demands = higher inspection and metrology intensity". The HBM-specific quantified items are bump height scaling (6 → 5 → 3 µm) and a die-warp specification (1 mm → 0.1 mm).
- **Quantified HBM figures Camtek has given are about revenue and orders, not steps:**
  - HBM revenue share: single digits before 2023, then ~10% in Q1 2023, ~20% in H2 2023 and 40% in Q1 2024 [1][5].
  - Orders and forecasts from two HBM makers: more than $260M for 2026–27 [12][17].
  - HBM players' share of the more than $600M H1-2026 order intake: over 20% [13].
  - A Hawk-only order from "a leading HBM player": more than $50M, for 2027 delivery [28].
- **What Camtek sells into HBM, by its own account:**
  - 2D inspection and 3D bump-height/coplanarity metrology on bumped DRAM wafers (Eagle G5, Hawk, CTS 8th/9th-generation sensors). Camtek claims to be the "tool of reference for all the 3D metrology for HBM4 at all the HBM manufacturers" and "several 2D inspection steps" [10].
  - "Die stack planarity" and "known-good-package" inspection, both named in the 20-F [21].
  - **Roadmap items for 2027–28:** die-warp metrology, TSV depth, post-CMP topology and Cu dishing, sub-150 nm pre-bond particle detection, post-bond void detection, and a 10th-generation sensor for bumps below 5 µm [15][16].
  - **Not found:** any Camtek statement about the HBM4 **logic base die** made at a foundry, about post-dicing/known-good-die inspection specific to HBM, or about die-to-wafer stack inspection.
- **Independent views give partial agreement.**
  - **KLA [35][36]:** HBM has raised DRAM front-end process-control intensity by "another point or so" on top of EUV. This is a front-end fab measure, not packaging. KLA also expects packaging inspection frequency to fall as yields are learned.
  - **Onto [34]:** packaging process-control intensity is rising "fairly dramatically". For HBM4 → HBM4E, however, demand is "driven a lot by volume… It's all about the wafers for us". That matches Camtek's 2024 position more than its 2025–26 "intensity" framing.
  - **Trade press [37][38][43][44]:** describes thinner dies (20–50 µm), finer pitch (about 40 µm → about 10 µm) and more defect types. No source gave a per-generation step count.
- **Hybrid bonding:**
  - Camtek says hybrid bonding adds new steps: pre-bond particles below 150 nm, Cu dishing, roughness, die/wafer warp and post-bond voids [15][16]. It says it has tools at pilot lines [7][10].
  - Camtek has repeatedly pushed back the timing of volume hybrid-bonding revenue (2027–28 → 2028+ → "end of this decade") [7][10][14][15].
  - In October 2026 Camtek said HBM5 "is going to continue to be based on bumps" [15][16]. Independent reports in 2026 still place hybrid bonding at HBM4E 16-high or HBM5 [38][44][45][48]. See Contradictions.

## 6. DERIVED arithmetic

1. **HBM share of H1-2026 orders (DERIVED, lower bound).** Over 20% × over $600M = **over $120M** of orders from HBM players between 1 January and about 10 August 2026 [13]. This overlaps with the "over $260M" two-customer orders-plus-forecast figure for 2026–27 [12], so the two cannot be added.
2. **DRAM core dies per stack (DERIVED).** 8 → 12-high = +50% dies per stack (12/8 = 1.5). 12 → 16-high = +33% (16/12 = 1.333).
3. **DRAM core dies per GB shipped (DERIVED, illustrative).** This assumes equal die size and yield and ignores the base die and TSV area.
   - HBM3E 12-high with 24 Gb dies = 36 GB, so 12/36 = 0.333 dies per GB.
   - HBM4 12-high with 24 Gb dies = 36 GB, so 0.333 dies per GB.
   - HBM4 16-high with 32 Gb dies = 64 GB [42], so 16/64 = 0.25 dies per GB (−25% vs 12-high 24 Gb).
   - If Camtek's revenue follows "the number of DRAMs that are going to be used" [5] and wafers scanned [9], then a taller stack raises wafers per **stack** but not per **GB**. Wafers per GB fall when die density rises. Wafers per GB rise if TSV area per die grows: Samsung says TSV count keeps rising and takes silicon area [40]. Neither effect has been quantified by Camtek.
4. **Hybrid-bonding share (from the Camtek chart, approximate).** About 3% in 2026 and about 10% in 2030, a ratio of about 3.3 (10/3). The slide states "~3×" [16 slide 18].

## Contradictions

1. **Stack height vs Camtek content (Camtek earlier vs later).**
   - **May 2024 [5]:** "moving from eight to 12 doesn't change our business… our machines don't… care because we scan wafers… there is no difference… any upside for ASP improvement? No there isn't". In the same answer Ramy said what matters is "the number of DRAMs", which taller stacks raise per HBM but which fewer HBMs per GPU can offset.
   - **Nov 2024 to Aug 2025 [6][7][9]:** "as they continue into 12 and 16 stacks, this is good news for us", and "moving to more layers… we will scan more wafers".
   - **From Nov 2025 [10][11]:** the stated driver shifts to HBM4 being "inspection and metrology intensive" (tighter and more bumps).
   - The positions can be partly reconciled (wafer count versus per-wafer intensity), but the emphasis changed.
2. **"Up to 16 layers" (CO-relayed) vs "we scan wafers" (CO).** Asia Economy (2025-02-20; caption "Provided by CAMT") says Hawk is "capable of testing up to 16 layers of HBM3E" [30]. Camtek management said in May 2024 that its machines inspect wafers before dicing and that stack count needs no machine change [5].
3. **HBM5 interconnect: Camtek vs independent sources.**
   - **Camtek, Oct 2026 [15][16 slide 17]:** HBM5 "is not going to be [hybrid bonding]. It's going to continue to be based on bumps".
   - **SemiEngineering, Jan 2026 [38]:** "HBM5 and its successors are expected to take advantage of hybrid bonding."
   - **Tom's Hardware, Sep 2026 [45]:** hybrid bonding is "now set to debut in HBM4E and HBM5 at the end of the decade".
   - **TrendForce, Jul 2026 [44]:** 16-high HBM4E is the "earliest likely adoption point". JEDEC is discussing raising HBM5 (20-high) height to 900–1,000 µm, which would ease the pressure.
   - **ServeTheHome, Hot Chips 2026 (snippet) [48]:** SK hynix positions direct bonding for HBM5 / >20-high.
   - The sources agree hybrid bonding is delayed but disagree on whether HBM5 uses it.
4. **"Tool of reference/record for all 3D metrology for HBM4 at all HBM makers" vs Onto's HBM4 wins.**
   - **Camtek, Nov 2025 and Feb 2026 [10][11]:** this claim, and "we have not lost any market share". Repeated in Oct 2026 as "Reference for bump-height metrology… HBM4 and HBM4e" [16].
   - **Onto, 2026-03-16 [31]:** a leading HBM maker ordered double-digit Dragonfly G5 units for "the majority of their 2D inspection needs" and "a similar number of orders for 3Di technology" (Onto's 3D bump metrology), "displacing the previously established tool of record".
   - **Onto, Feb 2026 [32], unverified:** a VPA of over $240M for 2D inspection and 3D bump metrology with an HBM maker, through 2027.
   - Onto does not name the displaced vendor, so this is not proof that Camtek lost the account. Neither statement can be verified independently (see file on Question 5).
5. **What the "$260M" HBM figure is (Camtek vs Camtek).**
   - **Call, 2026-05-12 [12]:** "already received order and forecast… representing expected revenue exceeding… $260 million for 2026 and 2027".
   - **Same-day slide [17]:** "Orders in the pipeline from two HBM manufacturers with revenues exceeding the amount of $260 million".

   - **May 2025 [8] and Nov 2025 [10]:** a "major change in the density of the memory" in 2027 (288 GB → "closer to a terabyte" per GPU), presented as an opportunity for added capacity.
   - **Oct 2026 [15]:** "there is a tendency not to go with higher memory density in each of the module, actually to reduce it".

9. **Sampling vs 100% inspection.**
   - **Camtek:** "in inspection, most of them do 100%" and intensity will "stay or improve" in HBM4 [7]. The 20-F says "100% inspection and metrology of all the components in the stack" [21]. In the same call, Camtek said "in metrology they do maybe more sampling" [7].

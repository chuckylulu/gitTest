## 1. Summary of findings

- **No hybrid-bonding revenue, system count or customer name has been disclosed by Camtek.** The descriptions are: "working with customers that are doing hybrid bonding" (May 2023) [16]; "installed machines at several sites… for several steps in the hybrid bonding" (Feb 2025) [13]; "supplying machines to pilot lines" (May 2025) [12]; "a few machines at customer sites, major customer sites… for the pilot lines or for the initial preproduction" (Nov 2025) [10]; "some business, but really on a low magnitude" (Nov 2026) [3]. All QUALITATIVE.
- **What Camtek sells for HB today** (per Camtek): Hawk (and some Eagle) for 2D pre-bond inspection at defects down to 150 nm [2][11][13]; FRT MicroProf AP and NanoProf for metrology, with "pre-bonding metrology" on NanoProf [1][19]; 360°Scan for edge/bevel of stacked wafers including "bonding alignment" metrology [19]. **Post-bond void detection: none.** Camtek says it is "evaluating several technologies" and lists it as a 2027–28 roadmap item [1][2]. Cu dishing metrology and improved post-CMP roughness metrology are also listed as 2027–28 roadmap items [1][2].
- **Timing for HB volume, per Camtek, has moved later:** "starting 2027, 2028" (Feb 2025) [13] → "higher in '27" (Nov 2025) [10] → "2028 and onwards", nothing substantial in 2027 (Nov 2026) [3] → "wouldn't be full steam before the end of this decade" (Oct 2026) [2].
- **Net effect, per Camtek:** HB is an "additional opportunity" [10][13]; HB will reach ~10% of 2.5D+3D interconnects by 2030 (Yole 2024 + Camtek analysis) [1][2]; micro-bump interconnect "still growing" and HBM5 "is going to continue to be based on bumps" [1][2]. Independent sources in 2024–2026 say HB for HBM is expected at 16-high HBM4E at the earliest, or at 20-high HBM5 around 2029–2030 [34][35][36][37][38]. See Contradictions.
- **ESTIMATE (§6):** In the LTM to Q3 2026 (revenue $544.3M, DERIVED), revenue tied to bump inspection/metrology of all kinds is about **48% base (35%–57% range)**. The narrower part tied to the die-to-die micro-bumps that HB would remove (HBM core-die stacking and logic 3D stacking) is about **10% base (5%–26% range)**. Most of Camtek's bump exposure sits on bumps HB does not replace in the forecast window (CoWoS-type interposer micro-bumps, HBM base-die-to-interposer bumps, flip-chip/wafer bumping at OSATs).

### 4.3 Which bumps hybrid bonding removes, and on what timeline (independent sources)

**Structure (what HB replaces and what it does not):**
- **HBM:** HB would replace the micro-bumps (TC-NCF at Samsung and Micron, MR-MUF at SK hynix) **between stacked DRAM dies** [29]. The base die still "talks to the XPU through the microbumps and interposer" [30]. Industry sources describe intermediate schemes that keep some micro-bumps: "DRAM dies are face-to-face hybrid bonded, and these bonded pairs will be stacked back-to-back using microbumps… those TCB and MR tools will still be used" (Brewer Science) [28]; "so-called hybrid-hybrid bonding… two wafers use hybrid bonding… while the next level uses microbumping" (Onto) [29]; "Not every interconnect will become hybrid bonding in HBM's case" [28].
- **Logic (SoIC-type):** HB is already in volume for die-on-die logic stacking: AMD 3D V-Cache and MI300; Intel Foveros Direct in Clearwater Forest (H1 2026, 9 µm pitch); TSMC SoIC pitch 9 µm (2023) → 6 µm (2025) → 4.5 µm (by 2029) [38]. In MI300X, SoIC HB stacks sit on a CoWoS-S interposer with "microbump 3D memory stacks, 2.5D silicon interposers, and 3D hybrid bonding" [43] (abstract only). So logic HB adds a bonded layer **inside** the chiplet and leaves the chiplet-to-interposer and HBM-to-interposer micro-bumps in place. This is inferred from [38] and [43]; no source states it as a general rule.
- **CIS:** HB has been in volume since the mid-2010s (wafer-to-wafer) [44]. It does not affect Camtek's bump business.

## 6. ESTIMATE: share of LTM revenue that depends on micro-bump inspection and metrology

Two measures, because "depends on micro-bumps" and "exposed to hybrid bonding" are different sets:

- **M1, bump-linked revenue (all bumps):** systems revenue from tools that measure or inspect bumped wafers, whether solder, Cu pillar or micro-bump, on HBM, CoWoS-type, flip-chip or WLP wafers.
- **M2, HB-displaceable revenue:** the subset tied to **die-to-die micro-bumps that HB would remove**. These are HBM core-die stacking bumps and logic 3D-stacking bumps. Interposer, base-die-to-interposer, C4 and flip-chip bumps are excluded because no source found expects HB to remove them in the forecast window (§4.3).

Service revenue is excluded from both (conservative), and the results are expressed as % of **total** LTM revenue.

### 6.1 M1: bump-linked revenue

Method: (revenue share of tools carrying a 3D bump sensor × systems revenue) + (fraction of 2D-only tool revenue used on bumped layers × 2D-only revenue).

| Case | Revenue share of 3D-bump-capable tools (units = 40%) | 2D-only tool revenue used on bumped wafers | M1 $ | M1 % of LTM revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 30% (dedicated bump-metrology tools priced below Hawk/inspection tools) | 10% of 2D-only revenue | 0.30×507 + 0.10×0.70×507 = 152.1 + 35.5 = **$187.7M** | **34.5%** |
| Base | 40% (revenue share = unit share) | 20% | 0.40×507 + 0.20×0.60×507 = 202.9 + 60.9 = **$263.8M** | **48.5%** |
| High | 45% (Hawk/Eagle-AP with 3D sensor priced above 2D-only tools) | 30% | 0.45×507 + 0.30×0.55×507 = 228.2 + 83.7 = **$312.0M** | **57.3%** |

Sanity check: total advanced packaging LTM ≈ 74% of revenue (DERIVED: 55.7% + 18.5%). Bumps occur only in AP, so M1 must sit below ~74%. The base case (48.5%) is ~65% of AP revenue (DERIVED: 48.5 / 74.2). The rest of AP is fan-out/RDL, interposer RDL and non-bump 2D steps.

Weak points: the 60/25/15 split is a **2026 unit** count, not LTM revenue, and Camtek does not give ASPs by tool type. The "2D on bumped wafers" fraction is pure assumption, because Camtek gives no 2D split by layer.

### 6.2 M2: HB-displaceable revenue (die-to-die micro-bumps)

Method: HBM share of revenue × share of HBM revenue that is bump-specific (3D bump metrology plus bump-defect inspection) × share of HBM bumped wafers that are core-die (die-to-die) wafers rather than base-die wafers, **plus** a logic 3D-stacking allowance.

| Case | HBM share of LTM revenue | Bump-specific share of HBM revenue | Core-die share of HBM bumped wafers | Logic die-to-die allowance | M2 % | M2 $ (×544.3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 15% | 40% | 90% | 0% | 0.15×0.40×0.90 = **5.4%** | ~$29M |
| Base | 20% | 50% | 90% | 1% | 0.20×0.50×0.90 + 0.01 = **10.0%** | ~$54M |
| High | 30% | 80% (counts all 2D inspection of core-die bump layers) | 95% | 3% | 0.30×0.80×0.95 + 0.03 = **25.8%** | ~$140M |

Input reasoning:
- **HBM share 15–30%.** Over 20% of 2026 YTD orders were from HBM players [6]. The $260M HBM orders-plus-forecast over 2026–27 is ≈18.6% of two-year revenue (DERIVED) [5][8]. HBM peaked at 40% in Q1 2024 [27]. Korea revenue fell to 7.4% in 2025, which suggests a lower 2025 HBM share; Micron's HBM sits in "Asia Pacific" or the U.S., so Korea is only a partial proxy.
- **Bump-specific share of HBM revenue 40–80%.** Camtek is "tool of record for all the 3D metrology for HBM4" and for "several 2D inspection steps" [10]. The split is not disclosed.
- **Core-die share 90–95%.** In a 12- to 16-high stack, each base-die wafer is matched by many core-die wafers. This is structural reasoning, not a sourced count. Not every core-die bump layer disappears under "hybrid-hybrid" schemes [28][29], so this input biases M2 upward.
- **Logic 0–3%.** Logic HB is already in volume and additive (§4.3), so the remaining die-to-die micro-bump logic revenue at risk is small. No source quantifies it.

Timing overlay (not in the %): independent sources place HB in HBM no earlier than 16-high HBM4E and more likely at 20-high HBM5, ~2029–2030 [34][36][37][38]. Even then, 12-high HBM4E is expected to stay mainstream [34], and mixed schemes keep some micro-bumps [28][29]. The M2 revenue is therefore exposed over a multi-year transition, not a single switch. Camtek's own chart has HB at ~10% of 2.5D+3D interconnects in 2030 [1].

   - Q3 2025 (Nov 2025): "We'll start to see them higher in '27" [10].
   - Q3 2026 (Nov 2026): "2028 and onwards… in 2027, I do not expect to see any substantial business" [3].
   - Analyst Day (Oct 2026): "wouldn't be full steam before the end of this decade"; "slower than we expected" [2].
2. **HBM5 on bumps (Camtek) vs HBM5 on hybrid bonding (independent).**
   - Camtek, Oct 2026: HBM5 "is not going to be like that. It's going to continue to be based on bumps" [2]; roadmap "Micro bumps for future HBM5" [1].
   - TrendForce, Oct 2024: "Manufacturers have confirmed that hybrid bonding will be used in the HBM5 20hi stack generation" [35].
   - SemiEngineering, Jan 2026: "HBM5 and its successors are expected to take advantage of hybrid bonding" [28].
   - Counterpoint, Apr 2026: HB mass adoption with SK hynix HBM5, 2029–30 [36].
   - SK hynix, Aug 2026: developing HB for 20+ layer stacks [37]; Herald, Sep 2026: HB expected at HBM4E 16-layer or HBM5 20-layer [37].
   - Partial support for Camtek's view: TrendForce, Jul 2026, says JEDEC may raise the HBM5 20-high height limit to up to 1,000 µm, which would ease the need for HB, and that adoption "could slip further" [34]. Camtek's claim is narrower than "HB is delayed": it says HBM5 will not use HB at all. No independent source found states that.
3. **"Bumpless" definition.** Ramy Langer, May 2023: "even when people talk about bumpless, it means very low-profile bumps. It's not exactly without bumps" [16]. Eyal Segev, Oct 2026: HB "eliminates the need for micro-bumps and metal or reduce the amount of metal there" [2]; the deck says "Hybrid bonding – Bumpless interconnect" [1].
4. **HB share metric.** The spoken figure is "10% of the advanced packaging applications interconnect by 2030" [2]. The slide's metric is "share of total 2.5D and 3D interconnects", "~3× by 2030" [1]. The two denominators are not the same. The chart reads ~3% → ~10%, which is 3.3× (DERIVED).
5. **150 nm capability vs the HB pre-bond requirement.**
   - Ramy Langer: "We provide solutions today to… hybrid bonding applications with our current technology, enabling detection down to 150 nm" [2].
   - Eyal Segev, same event: pre-bond HB "requires the capability of detecting **sub** 150 nm particles" [2]; the roadmap lists "Sub 150nm defects" for 2027–28 [1].
   - The Hawk webpage claims "Down to 0.1um defect sensitivity" [19]. The Q1 2026 call cited "inspection capability down to 100 nanometer" as an industry requirement [8].
   - Current sensitivity is therefore stated as 150 nm (deck, 20-F) or 100 nm (website), while the stated HB need is sub-150 nm, which is on the 2027–28 roadmap (see also `01_products.md` Contradiction 1).
6. **20-F wording vs HB scale.** The FY2023–FY2025 20-Fs each say "The bumps and hybrid bonding are becoming the main interface instead of the conventional wire bonding" [17][18]. Camtek's own chart puts HB at ~3% of 2.5D+3D interconnects in 2026 [1], and it reports HB business as "low magnitude" [3]. These are not strictly inconsistent, since the sentence groups bumps and HB together, but the 20-F wording gives no sense of HB's small current share.
7. **Search-tool summary vs primary text (verification note).** A web-search summary stated that the 2026-03-30 $31M OSAT order release mentions "hybrid bonding interfaces". The release text read directly [23] contains no mention of hybrid bonding (it says "mainly for CoWoS-like packaging supporting AI applications"). The primary text is used.
8. **Q3 2026 de-spec vs bump-metrology orders.** Needham said customers were hesitating on "dedicated 3D bump metrology" in HBM amid de-spec and the HB-or-not decision. Camtek replied "We do not see this hesitancy" [2]. The Needham remark is an analyst's market check with no data shown, so it is recorded as an unresolved difference rather than a verified contradiction.

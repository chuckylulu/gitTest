# SMP: sampling intensity of inspection, metrology and test (HBM4/HBM4E and hybrid-bonded logic)

Module run 2026-11-21. Period: 1 Jan 2025 to 21 Nov 2026. Evidence rows: `research/out/customer_side/parts/SMP.csv` (SMP-001 to SMP-047; quote_check: 43 FOUND, 4 [I] rows without quote).

## 1. Findings

### 1a. HBM and memory (SK hynix, Samsung, Micron; OSAT context)

**Test steps for HBM stacks.** Nothing in period shows a chipmaker cutting HBM test steps. The evidence points toward more steps, but none of it counts them:
- SK hynix has reportedly developed its own HBM4 package-level (SiP / system-level) tester. It lets the memory maker run the post-integration defect test that foundries used to run, and it passed internal qualification in January 2026 (SMP-009, TheElec; SMP-010, TrendForce rewrite). The article gives its purpose as advance compatibility checks and re-testing of failed units, not as a new per-unit production step. Both are trade-press reports [S] based on unnamed industry sources. SK hynix has not confirmed the tool in any document I could retrieve.
- At Hot Chips 2026, SK hynix showed a flow with base/core wafer test plus a KGSD (known-good stacked die) wafer test, so each cube is tested before system-level packaging (SMP-011, ServeTheHome [S]). This shows which insertions exist, not whether their number changed from HBM3E.
- Samsung (DRAM Yield Enhancement) proposed a new mass-production pre-screen of buffer-die micro-bump interface resistance. It says this "can be an essential quality monitoring item for buffer die", which would add one test item before interposer assembly; adoption in production is not stated (SMP-014, IRPS 2025, abstract only).
- Samsung's EDS test group describes "the rising test cost of high-density HBM and DRAM products" and offers a repair/ECC integration to recover productivity (SMP-015, ITC 2026, abstract only). This says test cost is rising, not test time per insertion.
- Amkor's test page offers "in-situ test between key assembly steps and final test (SLT and ATE) for 2.5D & 3D" (SMP-038 [C]). HBM appears only as market context, and hybrid bonding only in a separate handler list. The page was last modified June 2026; the date of this text is not stated. An Amkor test VP told Semiconductor Engineering that intermediate testing during assembly "is becoming increasingly critical, especially as the cost of HBM continues to rise" (SMP-039 [S]). The same article puts insertions for a 12-high stack at 3 to 12 depending on the assembly house. That figure is unattributed and is context only (SMP-040 [S]).
- Micron's only in-period test statement I could read is a capacity one: FY25 capex includes a "Taiwan DRAM test facility" next to the Singapore HBM packaging facility (SMP-028, FQ2 FY25 prepared remarks p6). Micron's later remarks that I could read (FQ3 FY25, FQ3 FY26, FQ4 FY26) do not mention HBM test time or test steps (see gaps).
- One SK hynix newsroom employee interview names test-time reduction as a core goal of the test function. It is about DRAM/SoC test in general, not HBM (SMP-012). It is the only "fewer" statement on electrical test from a memory maker, and it is an aspiration.
- Inference SMP-041 [I] (Medium): the direction for HBM is more test items and insertions and higher test cost (the memory-side SiP item rests on press reports only; the KGSD wafer test is part of the existing flow). No chipmaker gives an insertion count or a test-time figure.

**HBM package and stack inspection.** Samsung's 2026 "master" interview describes a new non-destructive monitoring solution, using a red light source split from white light, that detects scratches too small to see before. It is set against HBM stacking damage and a goal of zero package defects (SMP-013). This adds a method; the article gives no sampling rate or numbers.

**Front-end memory metrology: virtual metrology and AI.**
- SK hynix says its AI-based virtual measurement system gives "quality inspections for every wafer" (SMP-001 [C]). It says the VM detects anomalies "across all wafers without increasing the total production time" (SMP-002 [C]). These are untestable company claims in an HBM smart-factory article; their scope (fab, sites or steps) is not stated. The article gives no reduction in physical measurement count.
- SK hynix says Panoptes VM has been in its production lines since 2022 (SMP-045 [C]). Its SPIE AL 2026 work uses VM because physical metrology has "low sampling rates" and is "sparse" (SMP-005, SMP-006). SK hynix states the GRACE overlay-VM framework has been in its production since 2025 (SMP-007 [C]); the paper presents VM as complementing "throughput limitations and sampling blind spots of physical metrology" (SMP-008).
- SK hynix's company claims of +31% productivity and +21% yield belong to the HBM smart factory overall, not to VM (SMP-003 [C]). VP Doh says its AI defect-image analysis "drastically reduced" analysis time, with no number given (SMP-004 [C]).
- Micron says that with AI-ADC technicians and engineers "no longer need to classify wafer defects manually" (SMP-022 [C]). Each week, 100 million wafer images in 436 million control points run through AI models (SMP-023). Company claim: products launch twice as fast and 1 million work hours are saved a year (SMP-024 [C]). A Micron cloud VM in an HVM memory fab (dry etch) is meant to "improve sampling efficiency" and met production release criteria on one year of data (SMP-025). A new inline CD-SEM metric for DRAM container slant is offered as a "practical alternative" to BSE or destructive checks (SMP-026). ML/GenAI anomaly detection now runs on in-line CD-SEM images (SMP-027).
- Samsung (device type not stated): an AI super-resolution and denoising method cuts SEM metrology/inspection image throughput time by up to 75% (3.9x) for "massive metrology" (SMP-017). M. Lee (Samsung) co-edited, with D. Schmidt (IBM), a JMM special section on "High-Density Sampling and Massive Metrology" (SMP-018). The GTC 2026 talk stresses HBM4E sensitivity to process variation and AI defect detection in process control, but it makes no claim that physical measurements are replaced (SMP-019; the defect-detection and Pyeongtaek digital-twin statements are capability claims, SMP-046 and SMP-047 [C]). The digital-twin "AI Megafactory" is a plan (SMP-020 [C]). Next-gen DRAM development uses a "massive" on-chip test that reads millions of sense amplifiers per chip (SMP-021).
- Context only: a SPIE 2026 paper on ML-guided e-beam inspection run on ASML's VCP reports up to 4.3x defective-die capture from hybrid sampling on Micron memory devices. The saved record has no affiliations, so neither ASML nor Micron authorship is shown (SMP-029).
- Inference SMP-042 [I] (Medium): SK hynix and an Intel paper present VM and AI as raising coverage to every wafer and offsetting sparse physical sampling; Micron cites VM to "improve sampling efficiency" without a number. None discloses a % reduction in physical measurements, saved cycle time or reduced sampling rate for HBM in period.

**Hybrid bonding in memory.** Samsung (with Nearfield) says wafer-level control for hybrid bonding in VNAND "essentially call[s] for massive metrology", using a high-throughput in-line AFM on production wafers (SMP-016). This is W2W VNAND, not HBM. No in-period chipmaker statement on sampling for hybrid-bonded HBM was found.

### 1b. Hybrid-bonded logic (Intel, TSMC; ASE context)

- Intel (with EV Group) at ECTC 2026: current chip-to-wafer bond-overlay metrology uses "reduced sampling plans" (SMP-030). Intel says 100% overlay measurement is needed and describes on-the-move image capture to reach "high overlay metrology throughput with 100% chiplet inspection" across hybrid-bonding generations (SMP-031). This is the clearest "more" statement in the module (sampled to 100%). The abstract gives no throughput numbers and does not name Foveros Direct.
- Intel test: on a next-generation multi-die client CPU, scan-network DFT "significantly reduced" overall test application time (SMP-032, ITC 2026). This is the only "fewer" statement on logic test, and the abstract does not say the part is hybrid-bonded.
- Intel front end, as context: a proposed litho-scanner topography virtual defect metrology covers "every wafer and die at each photolithography step", against the abstract's general description of traditional sampling of "a small percentage of wafers" (SMP-033; deployment in Intel HVM not stated). Full-wafer e-beam voltage-contrast inspection runs at 1 billion hot spots per hour (SMP-034).
- TSMC: the only in-period items I could save are papers. One is an ML "Die of Interest" ranking from TSMC's Advanced Packaging Technology and Service unit: a "9-fold improvement in identifying outlier dies" vs baseline (metric not defined), with an envisioned second stage of adaptive, targeted stress testing (SMP-035). The other is an IEEE 1838 3D test access architecture for system-on-wafer, with no numbers (SMP-036). Neither names SoIC. TSMC's own sites refuse scripted access (gaps).
- ASE (with PVA TePla and MCL): AI enhancement of low-resolution SAM scans "decreases significantly SAM scanning time" in a flip-chip failure-analysis workflow (SMP-037). This is not hybrid bonding.
- Inference SMP-043 [I] (Low): for hybrid-bonded logic, Intel's ECTC 2026 abstract (development and roadmap; HVM use not stated) is the main chipmaker statement on sampling intensity found, plus an Intel-organised VLSI 2025 workshop needs list (TALK-022). Inference SMP-044 [I] (Medium): the AI time savings stated in period apply to image acquisition or FA (Samsung -75%; ASE and SK hynix [C] unquantified) or to enterprise manufacturing (Micron [C]), not to HBM production inspection counts; Intel's test-time cut (SMP-032) comes from DFT streaming, not AI.

- The prompt names Intel Foveros Direct and TSMC SoIC. Intel's 100% bond-overlay abstract (SMP-031) is about C2W hybrid-bonding metrology "at Intel" and does not name Foveros Direct. No in-period TSMC statement names SoIC sampling.

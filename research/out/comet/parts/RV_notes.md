# RV - Rivals to the Comet Yxlon CA20 (3D X-ray for semiconductor advanced packaging)

Question Q3. Rival systems competing for the same sockets as the CA20: 3D X-ray / CT / laminography inspection and metrology of HBM stacks, CoWoS-type 2.5D, hybrid bonding, micro-bumps and TSV/TGV voids.
Evidence: `research/out/comet/parts/RV.csv` (82 rows: 12 [F], 51 [C], 19 [S], 0 [I]). All rival documents are saved under `research/sources/comet/` (issuer = rival or publisher). No arithmetic was needed, so there is no calc script.
Tag rule used here: maker spec and adoption claims from the maker's own page or release = [C]; dated launches, results and filings = [F]; press, analysts, trade sites and distributor listings that do not name the maker = [S]. Company words [C] are kept apart from third-party evidence [S]/[F] in every customer column.
Fiscal years: Comet, Rigaku and Unicomp use calendar FY; Nordson's FY ends 31 Oct (FQ4 FY2025 = Aug-Oct 2025); Zeiss and others are not needed here.

## Premise corrections

1. **"Rigaku XHENA" does not exist.** No Rigaku product of that name turned up on rigaku.com or in web search. The nearest name is XHEMIS (TX-3000), a TXRF wafer-contamination tool, found only in search leads and not relevant to package inspection. Rigaku has **no released 3D X-ray imaging/CT tool for advanced-packaging interconnects**. Its packaging page says in-line X-ray imaging for micro-bumps/TSVs is "developing" [C] (RV-024). Its packaging products are metrology: ONYX 3200, an optical confocal scanner plus XRF launched 17 Dec 2025 [F] (RV-026, RV-027), and WaferX 310 WDXRF. XTRAIA XT is in-line X-ray topography for crystal defects, not interconnects [C] (RV-031).
2. **Sigray now belongs to Applied Materials.** sigray.com 301-redirects to appliedmaterials.com/us/en/sigray.html (observed 2026-11-21). Applied's site refused the fetch, and no deal date or terms were found [S] (RV-059). Sigray rows therefore describe an Applied Materials product line.
3. **Bruker:** the X200 in-line X-ray inspection product page no longer exists. The URL now resolves to an X-ray *diffraction-imaging* (substrate defect) overview [C] (RV-044). The old X200 specs survive only in search snippets [S] (RV-045). Bruker's current packaging X-ray offer is µXRF bump-composition metrology [C] (RV-043). Its June 2025 "AI advanced packaging" order was 27 *optical* WLI systems [F] (RV-046). No Bruker-Nova X-ray deal was found ("Bruker/Nova XPS?" is not supported).
4. **Onto Innovation** sells no X-ray imaging tool. Its X-ray link is a 27% stake in Rigaku (agreed 20 Apr 2026 [F], RV-033) and a joint CD-SAXS offering [C] (RV-034). That is dimensional metrology, not interconnect void inspection.
5. **ZEISS's wafer-level in-line offering is ZEISS NLX** (NLX-100 / NLX-100M), sold by ZEISS SMT (Semiconductor Fab Solutions). It is not a Xradia Versa/Context/Ultra product. Those remain lab / failure-analysis XRMs (RV-010..RV-023).
6. **SEC Co., Ltd.** is "쎄크" in Korean; the ticker 081180 KOSDAQ is consistent with search leads. Its HBM product names are the **NF120** (manual, X-EYE line) and the **Semi-Scan** series (Semi-Scan-SW / "Semi-scan H" / "Semi-scan Wafer"). The 2025 KOSDAQ listing comes from press [S] (RV-066..RV-075). The KRX/DART filings themselves were refused.
7. **Vieworks / Rayence** appear in search leads as X-ray *detector* suppliers to inspection-tool makers, not as system makers. **Nano-X**, **Saki**, **Seamark/Zhengye (正业科技)**, **Hitachi High-Tech**, **Nikon**, **Waygate (Phoenix)**, **Lumafield**, **Xwinsys**, **KLA**, **Nova**, **Camtek** and **Toray**: no wafer-level or HBM 3D X-ray product was found in the searches run (see Gaps). They are not shown as CA20 rivals here.
8. **Omron VT-X950 launch date is inconsistent across sources.** Coverage of the Nov 2023 announcement gives sequential release "from February 2024" [S] (RV-063). MONOist says sales started December 2024 [S] (RV-064). The Omron press release itself was refused (403).

## Findings in prose

**Who actually competes for the same in-line wafer-level 3D socket.** Four rival offerings are documented, at least in company words, as fab-integrable 3D X-ray at 300 mm wafer level for micro-bumps/TSVs:
- ZEISS NLX-100: in-line, EFEM [C] (RV-010, RV-011).
- Sigray/Applied Materials Apex XCT-300: EFEM, SECS/GEM, up to 10 WPH site inspection [C] (RV-055), still "Coming Soon" in Aug 2024 with no shipment shown.
- Nordson XM8000/XM8000Pro: "production-speed throughput" for wafer- and panel-level packaging [C] (RV-037).
- Korea's XAVIS Xscan-9860A: in-line WLP, 0.25 µm, EFEM [C] (RV-076).

Omron's VT-X950 is a cleanroom in-line CT-type AXI for mid-process / W2W bonding [C] (RV-060). Its conveyor handling and the missing wafer-EFEM claim (RV-061) make it closer to package/substrate inspection than to 300 mm wafer handling.

None of these makers states a resolution together with a time per 300 mm wafer for the same configuration. Sigray comes closest: 12 min at 0.2 µm pixel on a 300 mm wafer [C] (RV-051), and 2-3 min per spot [C] (RV-054). ZEISS discloses no resolution, FOV or throughput for NLX at all (RV-016 note).

**Who has production evidence in HBM.** The only third-party evidence of an X-ray tool on an HBM mass-production line is for **SEC (Korea)**:
- Its *manual* NF120 has been used on Micron's HBM line since 2023 [S] (RV-066).
- Eight units were sold to Micron in 2025 [S] (RV-071).
- Micron placed follow-on orders of about KRW 1.8bn (Feb 2026) and KRW 5.8bn (Nov 2026, Micron Singapore) [S] (RV-067, RV-068).
- SEC's in-line Semi-Scan won a first OSAT WLP line contract in Oct 2026 [S] (RV-069). For HBM it remains in validation at a domestic memory maker, with an order expected no earlier than Q1 2027 [S] (RV-071, RV-073).

All of this comes from Korean/Taiwanese press. The KRX disclosures were refused (Gaps).

No rival document read names an HBM or CoWoS customer for an in-line 3D X-ray tool. ZEISS shows NLX-100 only as a demo tool in its Korean innovation center [F] (RV-019). Rigaku's only named packaging shipment is an XRF/optical metrology tool, not an imager [C] (RV-028). Sigray claims "multiple POs" without names [C] (RV-053).

**Lab / FA rivals.** These compete with the CA20's lab (MXI) version, not with in-line sockets: ZEISS Xradia Versa / VersaXRM 5 Insight (0.8 µm/voxel, ~1.5 h per scan) [C] (RV-020, RV-023), Sigray Apex Hybrid (350 nm) [C] (RV-057), Nordson Micro CT / Quadra 7 Pro [C] (RV-039), and SEC NF120 [S] (RV-074). Nordson itself frames full 3D CT as impractical per part in production [S] (RV-041) and pushes limited-angle X-Plane Pro for high throughput [C] (RV-039).

**Dose to memory.** Of the rivals, only SEC (patent on reducing X-ray damage to HBM [S], RV-072) and Nordson (IC-Safe; product page refused, snippet only [S], RV-042) make memory-damage statements comparable to the CA20 Dose Manager's explicit flash/DRAM cut-off [C] (RV-009). ZEISS claims X-Guard shielding "to avoid any damage" without naming memory or giving a dose [C] (RV-013). XAVIS states "dose reduction technology" [C] (RV-077). No rival publishes a dose number. Trade press calls HBM overexposure "a significant concern" [S] (RV-049).

**Listed rivals' financial statements on X-ray for packaging.**
- Nordson: FQ4 FY2025 (Aug-Oct 2025) ATS sales fell 4%, with "fewer deliveries of test and inspection systems, particularly x-ray systems" [F] (RV-040). No HBM/advanced-packaging X-ray order was found in Nordson materials read.
- Rigaku: the H1 2026 (Jan-Jun 2026) summary credits semiconductor process control revenue (+18.2% YoY, JPY 12,791m) to memory/logic demand and new products, with no X-ray imaging statement [F] (RV-032).
- Bruker: the AI-packaging order it publicised was optical [F] (RV-046).
- Unicomp: its FY2025 annual report says advanced-packaging X-ray CT equipment is in customer validation [F] (RV-078).

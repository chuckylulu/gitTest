    SEC Develops Inline X-Ray Inspection Tool for HBM Production

    Company to Unveil System at SSPA 2026 and Begin Alpha Testing with Customers

    • JEONG IL JOO
    • Published 2026.04.01 14:34
    • Reply 0

    SEC Co. said it has completed development of an automated inline inspection system for high bandwidth memory (HBM) production lines based on X-ray technology.

    The system, named the Semi-Scan-SW model, can detect defects ranging from 3 micrometers (μm) to 5 μm that occur during HBM stacking processes. Unlike optical inspection equipment, X-ray technology enables non-destructive internal inspection of semiconductor chips. The tool can also be applied to through-glass via (TGV) process inspection for glass substrates.

    It also supports inspection of bonding processes between semiconductor chips and interposers in wafer-level packaging (WLP). The company expects the system to broaden its customer base to include foundries and outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) providers.

    SEC will showcase the Semi-Scan-SW model for the first time at the Smart SMT&PCB Assembly (SSPA) 2026 exhibition, which opens at the Suwon Convention Center on April 1. The company aims to secure additional global customers at the event.

    Starting this month, SEC plans to obtain evaluation samples from major HBM manufacturers and advanced packaging companies and begin internal alpha testing. The next stages will include beta testing, involving the supply of demo equipment to customers, followed by qualification testing.

    SEC has participated in the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy's core technology development program for PIM AI semiconductors since 2022. Through the program, it secured X-ray-based inline inspection technology for HBM last year, which underpins the Semi-Scan-SW model.

    Founded in 2000, SEC has developed X-ray inspection equipment since 2002 and commercialized industrial X-ray generators in 2006. The company initially focused on surface-mount technology (SMT) and has since expanded into automotive electronics, semiconductors, batteries and defense. It is also developing electron-beam-based glass substrate drilling equipment, cancer treatment devices and sterilization systems.

To compare radiation sensitivity across different DRAM devices, we introduce the dose sensitivity (DS), defined as the logarithmic ratio of post- to pre-irradiation retention failed-bit counts normalized by the absorbed dose. Retention time measurements were carried out on six DDR4 components from three manufacturers irradiated with a 130-kV X-ray inspection machine at both <inline-formula> <tex-math notation=\"LaTeX\">$45~^{\\circ }$ </tex-math></inline-formula>C and <inline-formula> <tex-math notation=\"LaTeX\">$85~^{\\circ }$ </tex-math></inline-formula>C. The analysis is organized around three aspects of DS evaluation: dependence on total dose, manufacturer-dependent radiation sensitivity, and the influence of the dose quantity used in the denominator. Together, these three aspects validate DS as a normalized metric for comparing TID-induced retention degradation. Within a single sample, DS varies by a factor of <inline-formula> <tex-math notation=\"LaTeX\">$5.46\\times $ </tex-math></inline-formula> depending only on which of four dose quantities is chosen: the dose measured by a dosimeter or the equilibrium dose, with or without the integrated circuit (IC) package. This variation is comparable in magnitude to the variation between the most and least sensitive components tested (<inline-formula> <tex-math notation=\"LaTeX\">$5.25\\times $ </tex-math></inline-formula>) at the same retention condition. When the DS values reported under different dose quantities are compared across different components, the two variations can combine multiplicatively, yielding a total range of up to approximately <inline-formula> <tex-math notation=\"LaTeX\">$28.7\\times $ </tex-math></inline-formula>, substantially larger than either variation alone. When DS is extrapolated to the JEDEC retention requirement of 64 ms at <inline-formula> <tex-math notation=\"LaTeX\">$85~^{\\circ }$ </tex-math></inline-formula>C, the ranking of the tested components shifts compared with that obtained in the directly measured 1\u201320-s range at <inline-formula> <tex-math notation=\"LaTeX\">$45~^{\\circ }$ </tex-math></inline-formula>C, indicating that DS depends on the temperature and retention time conditions under which it is evaluated. The equilibrium dose is the most consistent choice for cross-study comparison because it is independent of the detector, but it requires knowledge of the X-ray spectrum, which is often not available in practice. For this reason, the dose quantity and the evaluation condition must always be clearly stated when reporting DS values."}

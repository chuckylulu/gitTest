              Warpage Control Strategies in 3D Advanced Packaging: A Focus on
                     Post-Processing and Material-Level Optimization

              Eunah Ko, Ohguk Kwon, Yuna Kim, Huiyeong Jang, Jongyong Kim, Chaemin Im, Dougkuk Lee, Sun Jae Kim,
                                          Yonghoe Cho, Moonsoo Lee, Taehwi Kim, Chajea Jo

           Abstract
              As semiconductor architectures vertically scale through high-density 3D advanced packaging, wafer-level
           warpage introduces severe failure modes that threaten process yield and structural reliability. Driven by the
           coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) mismatch between heterogeneous materials, uncontrolled warpage
           induces critical defects—such as warpage induced cracks and difficulties in post processing—while
           complicating the package integration. To address these challenges, this study presents a comprehensive,
           empirical investigation focusing on warpage control; material-level optimization, process impacts, and
           structural variations, using real-case wafer integration. We systematically evaluate warpage behavior from
           three engineering perspectives: material CTE/component optimization of epoxy molding compounds (EMCs)
           under thermal cycling; comparative analysis between capillary underfill (CUF) and molded underfill (MUF)
           structures; and mechanical configuration tuning using wafer support systems (WSS). Adjusting these critical
           parameters establishes data-driven process guidelines that enhance manufacturing yield, improve process
           predictability, and ensure robust compatibility with next-generation 3D integration schemes.

           Key words
             3D advanced packaging, Wafer-level warpage, Epoxy molding compound (EMC), Underfill
           encapsulation, Wafer support system (WSS)

Samsung Electronics Adopts Hybrid Bonding Inspection Equipment

Joint development with Onto Innovation advances non-destructive testing for next-generation HBM

Onto Innovation's picosecond laser ultrasound inspection system. (Source: Onto Innovation)

Samsung Electronics has begun introducing new inspection equipment for hybrid bonding, with Onto Innovation’s systems reportedly undergoing validation on mass production lines.

According to industry sources on Tuesday, Samsung is in discussions or joint development with multiple vendors specializing in non-destructive inspection technologies based on ultrasound and X-ray. The primary objective is to detect microscopic defects, such as voids at hybrid bonding interfaces, which are difficult to identify using conventional optical inspection systems. The approach also minimizes potential losses of high-value wafers and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) during inspection.

U.S.-based Onto Innovation is currently leading among suppliers. The company has entered into a joint development program (JDP) with Samsung and has already supplied inspection equipment for use in production lines. The system utilizes laser ultrasound technology based on photoacoustic measurement.

When an ultrafast pump laser pulse on the order of picoseconds (one trillionth of a second) is applied to the inspection target, the material undergoes rapid thermal expansion, generating ultrasonic waves that propagate into the structure. These waves pass through optically opaque layers, such as metal, and are reflected upon encountering subsurface features or voids. A probe laser then detects minute surface deformations caused by the reflected waves, enabling analysis of internal structures. This method overcomes the limitations of optical inspection and allows measurement of overlay alignment errors between upper and lower structures.

Domestic equipment suppliers are expected to begin securing equipment orders or entering into JDP agreements as early as the second quarter. Some companies have already received bonded wafer samples from Samsung and completed performance testing, with results reportedly positive. Other vendors are also continuing discussions with Samsung while preparing non-destructive inspection systems for production lines.

An industry official said Samsung divisions are holding discussions with multiple equipment vendors behind the scenes, considering solutions for both through-silicon via (TSV) structures in HBM and hybrid bonding interfaces.

Earlier this month, Samsung unveiled a video showcasing hybrid bonding technology for HBM at Nvidia’s annual developer conference, GTC 2026. Industry observers expect Samsung to adopt hybrid bonding for next-generation HBM4E with 16 or more layers. The company also presented a paper at the International Solid-State Circuits Conference (ISSCC) last month on manufacturing 4F² DRAM using wafer-to-wafer (W2W) hybrid bonding. The approach incorporates a vertical channel transistor (VCT) structure and a cell-over-periphery (COP) design, stacking the peripheral circuit wafer and the cell array wafer with high precision. Inspection equipment capable of analyzing hybrid bonding interfaces is essential for validating these next-generation memory technologies.

Typically, qualification testing for semiconductor back-end equipment on production lines takes about six months. Based on this timeline, Samsung is expected to evaluate and compare the performance of multiple inspection systems through the end of this year.

Hybrid bonding is an advanced packaging technology that directly connects copper (Cu) surfaces between stacked semiconductor dies. Unlike thermocompression (TC) bonding, commonly used in HBM manufacturing, it does not rely on micro-bumps for interconnection. Compared with conventional soldering, hybrid bonding offers a larger bonding area and lower interfacial resistance, resulting in superior thermal and electrical performance.

We demonstrate laser-plasma X-ray emission in the soft-to-tender X-ray range. Soft X-rays emitted from ionized bismuth show unresolved transition arrays between 1 nm and 5 nm. The energy conversion efficiency into X-rays between 2.3 nm and 4.4 nm is approx. 10-3. Ionized gallium emits a broad spectrum in the soft-to-tender X-ray range. The flux scales linearly with laser power. The measured flux for the emission peak at 1.2 keV is 1012 photons/(s×sr) at 80 W laser power. These emission lines are suitable for applications such as X-ray fluorescence (XRF), especially µ-XRF, because of the high brilliance of the plasma source.

DOI 10.1117/12.3090702 | Beyond top-down CD: e-tilt based pattern profile metrology for multipatterning process control in early stage
Spacer double patterning is essential for advanced DRAM manufacturing, but early-stage variations in three-dimensional (3D) pattern profiles - especially sidewall slope - can lead to severe downstream defects such as collapse, bridging, and wiggling. Conventional critical dimension scanning electron microscope (CD-SEM) tools often lack the sensitivity to detect these subtle slope changes before irreversible damage occurs. To address this, we investigated tilted e-beam metrology as an inline solution for early detection of slope-related defects. Through a structured design of experiment (DOE), we evaluated the sensitivity and stability of tilted beam conditions to sidewall variations, while Monte Carlo simulations were used to provide physical insight into the observed contrast behavior. Experimental validation against transmission electron microscope (TEM) confirmed that tilted e-beam measurements accurately captured slope differences missed by standard non-tilt methods. Implemented in high-volume manufacturing, this approach enabled proactive process control, reducing yield risk and improving stability. Tilted e-beam metrology offers a scalable solution for advanced nodes, bridging the gap between invisible process drift and actionable insight.

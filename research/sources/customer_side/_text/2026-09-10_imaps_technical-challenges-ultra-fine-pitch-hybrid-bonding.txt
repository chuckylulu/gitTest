           Abstract
           Hybrid bonding is rapidly emerging as a foundational technology for next-generation advanced packaging,
           enabling ultra-high-density interconnects required for heterogeneous integration of chiplets, logic, memory,
           and specialized accelerators. As the industry pushes toward ultra-fine pitch interconnects approaching single-
           digit microns and below, significant technical challenges must be addressed across the process flow. These
           challenges include achieving extremely tight wafer and die alignment tolerances, maintaining surface
           planarity and cleanliness at the nanometer scale, controlling copper dishing and dielectric erosion during
           CMP, and ensuring defect-free oxide and copper-copper bonding interfaces. In addition, scaling hybrid
           bonding to ultra-fine pitch requires improvements in metrology, defect inspection, thermal management, and
           yield control to support high-volume manufacturing. Integration with complementary technologies such as
           silicon interposers, wafer-to-wafer and die-to-wafer bonding architectures, and heterogeneous material stacks
           further increases process complexity. This presentation reviews the key technical barriers to ultra-fine pitch
           hybrid bonding and discusses emerging process solutions and equipment innovations required to enable
           reliable, high-density interconnects for future high-performance computing, AI, and advanced system-in-
           package applications.

A central advantage of the AI approach is multimodal data
fusion, since no single modality fully characterizes a hybrid-
bonded interface. X-ray CT resolves buried copper
geometry, SAM reveals interfacial voids and delamination,
and THz and SEM add dielectric and surface sensitivity. By
learning across these complementary channels, machine-
learning models can classify defects more reliably than any
individual technique and at speeds compatible with
production cadence, which reduces the manual-inspection
bottleneck that has long limited advanced-packaging
throughput. Surveys of die-attachment and bonding-defect
inspection confirm that combining optical, radiological, and
acoustic sensing with deep learning is now the dominant
direction for manufacturing-grade defect detection [12].

Keynote Session

40 years of semiconductor metrology: connecting the past to the future

A. Charley
, 

J. Bogdanowicz
, 

D. Cerbu
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        Metrology has been a cornerstone of semiconductor manufacturing for over four decades, evolving from simple, manual measurement tools in the 1980s to today’s complex landscape covering a large variety of sensors and data-driven ecosystems. This presentation reviews key milestones across three eras—early development, transition, and recent advancements—highlighting the drivers and challenges that shaped progress: increasing complexity with new and innovative integration schemes as well as scaling and tighter tolerances under cost and throughput constraints. Looking ahead, emerging technologies such as High-NA EUV and 3D architecture demand a paradigm shift toward hardware extension and data driven metrology. This paper outlines how these shifts will redefine the metrology roadmap and unlock the next generation of semiconductor innovation. 

Emerging AI/ML opportunities in metrology, inspection, and process control

Steve McCandless

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        The semiconductor industry faces increasing complexity in device architectures and tighter process windows, driving the need for advanced metrology and inspection solutions. Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) are emerging as transformative enablers in this domain, offering unprecedented capabilities for data-driven decision-making and predictive analytics. Leveraging high-dimensional datasets from optical, e-beam, and X-ray tools, AI/ML enhances defect detection, real-time process control, and fleet productivity. Key opportunities include hybrid metrology, continuous learning for overlay prediction, and anomaly detection to accelerate development. AI-driven correlation of inline and offline measurements improves wafer health monitoring and yield learning. This presentation examines practical implementations, challenges in model robustness and data governance, and future directions for scalable AI/ML in high-volume manufacturing.

Hybrid and Virtual Metrology

The art of holistic metrology (in memory of Alok Vaid)

Igor Turovets

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        As device dimensions continued to shrink and semiconductor architectures evolved into increasingly complex 3D structures, single‑tool metrology approaches began to encounter fundamental limitations in accuracy and precision. This challenge led to the development of holistic metrology, which enhanced scatterometry performance by incorporating multiple information channels, azimuth angles, and measurement targets. The concept of holistic metrology later expanded into hybrid metrology, enabling complementary use of OCD, CD‑SEM, AFM, XPS, and other metrology techniques to further strengthen measurement robustness. More recently, AI‑enabled hybrid approaches have extended these capabilities. Alok Vaid played a central role in pioneering all these innovations, and this article serves both as a technical review of these developments and as a tribute to his lasting contributions to the metrology community.

Leveraging AI and ML in multimodal combined and hybrid metrology

Mark-Alexander Henn
, 

Aaron Chew
, 

Peter Bajcsy
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        We address the problem of integrating different measurement tools, each relying on different mathematical and statistical models to measure distinct parameters into a single, consistent measurement methodology. This problem is motivated by advances in data-driven modeling leveraged in advanced semiconductor device manufacturing. Our approach combines several ensemble learning methods to fuse two indirect measurement techniques, Optical Critical Dimension (OCD) metrology and Normal Incidence Spectroscopic Reflectometry (NISR). We report results for standard ensemble learning as well as an approach based on a physics-informed neural network (PINN), resulting in a methodology that can be used to combine multimodal sources. We find with experiments on simulated OCD and NISR data that ensemble learning methods consistently outperform traditional methods with only one model.

Overcoming conventional limit of process variation by using virtual metrology with mass-based monitoring in V-NAND

Ju-Young Cho
, 

Joonsung Lim
, 

Dong-Hyeon Ko
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        The scaling of 3D V-NAND has introduced deeply etched high-aspect-ratio contacts and buried layers, which increase the difficulty of consistent process control. To address these challenges, this study evaluated 9th-generation VNAND product using the Mass Measurement Tool (MMT), an in-line wafer-level measurement of mass change (Δmass). Coupled with process-specific modeling, MMT translates Δmass into virtual estimates of structural parameters correlated with yield-related failures. MMT was also applied to advanced process control of cell ONO formation, improving acrosswafer variation of cell reliability. While limitations remain—near-zero site coverage and limited directional information—these can be mitigated through modeling and hybrid metrology. Taken together, MMT is a key virtual metrology (VM) enabler for mass production, providing wafer-level sensing and excursion-gated inspection for fab-wide feedback. 

A virtual metrology-driven tool-to-tool matching: toward early anomaly detection and diagnosis

Danah Kim
, 

Sol Kwon
, 

Seonwoo Lim
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        A Tool-to-Tool Matching (TTTM), also known as chamber matching, is essential for stable fab operations. Still, physical metrology is sparse and requires a considerable amount of time to reach statistical significance, thereby delaying anomaly detection and diagnosis, and limiting the application of physical metrology-based TTTM. We present a virtual metrology (VM)-driven TTTM framework that produces predictions for each wafer in near real-time, enabling rapid chamber-level monitoring and early detection of mismatches. Dense outputs support statistically robust analysis, and model explainability identifies contributing inputs to guide root cause analysis. We propose a novel chamber mismatch score that quantifies discrepancies across chambers and enables reliable identification of anomalous chambers. We show that this mismatchbased monitoring allows earlier detection of chamber-level abnormalities and provides interpretable insights into potential causes. Furthermore, we demonstrate that our VM model with adaptive online learning reproduces chamber-wise distributional patterns observed in physical metrology more accurately. Taken together, our framework enables earlier, statistically grounded monitoring at the chamber level and provides practical diagnostics for production fabs.

Metrology and Inspection for Advanced Logic Devices

AI-driven optical metrology for advanced nodes

Chien-Hung Coco Tseng

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        The development of the cutting-edge 2nm CMOS platform technology, designed for energy-efficient computing in AI, mobile, and HPC applications, was highlighted at the 2024 IEDM. Traditional in-line metrology methods such as optical, X-ray, and CDSEM face challenges related to time-to-recipe, throughput, and see-through capability in the advanced nodes. In this work, we present an innovative FEOL individual gate metrology approach based on multi-stage-information ellipsometry, advanced process control (APC), and advanced machine learning techniques.

Enabling metrology and inspection for CFET structures using hybrid qualification approaches

H. Yang
, 

J. Bogdanowicz
, 

M. Hasan
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        Complementary field-effect transistor (CFET) integration increases metrology and inspection complexity by combining vertically stacked device tiers, multilayer high-aspect-ratio (HAR) pattern transfer, coupled 3D recess geometries, waferto-wafer (W2W) bonding interfaces, and backside processing. This work presents a hybrid qualification methodology that integrates high-throughput inspection, targeted high-resolution review, and model-based parameter extraction to support process-window qualification (PWQ) and in-line monitoring. For HAR gate patterning, broadband optical inspection with deep-learning (DL) classification and electron-beam (e-beam) review enables defect-type-aware PWQ within highly sampled care areas, while high-precision SEM metrology is applied to isolate stochastic line-edge roughness (LER) components. For vertical control, SEM backscattered-electron (BSE) indices provide a scalable proxy for trench depth under stated monotonic-response assumptions. For 3D recess modules, multi-channel optical geometry extraction tracks global fin/cavity parameters and is validated against cross-sectional references, while micro-spot X-ray fluorescence (µXRF) provides model-free, material-sensitive SiGe recess depth monitoring on dense device patterns. For bonding, high-frequency scanning acoustic microscopy (SAM) detects buried-interface voids at sub-µm pattern resolution. Finally, e-beam inspection evaluates source/drain uniformity, while voltage-contrast e-beam inspection screens backside metal-to-source/drain connectivity, correlating with electrical test signatures. 

In-line XPS for advanced semiconductor manufacturing and metrology on fully integrated targets

Christopher J. Lee
, 

Manasa Medikonda
, 

Trevor McDonough
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        The continual demands to shrink device sizes and length scales in the semiconductor industry have accelerated the adoption of X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) as a critical step for in-line metrology. As the ultrathin film layers and related structures of devices increase in complexity through scaling, a more rigorous assignment of spectral components to their material intensity source is necessary to use the full potential of XPS. In this paper, the application areas for in-line XPS are reviewed and modeling practices are demonstrated that significantly enhance XPS measurement characterization related to gate-all-around (GAA) transistor architectures as well as trace residue detection on fully integrated targets and device areas. As dedicated measurement targets become a less reliable proxy for active device areas, the need for advanced XPS modeling capabilities beyond 1D film stack characterization and towards direct 2D/3D measurement on fully integrated structures will be highlighted.

AI-based detection algorithm for optical inspection of critical defects on 2nm and 1.4nm technology nodes

Jaebong Kim
, 

Hyunjae Chung
, 

Seunghwan Moon
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        Defect inspection remains a pivotal process in semiconductor manufacturing; however, the continuous miniaturization of devices poses significant challenges to accurate detection. Addressing this challenge requires techniques that preserve high detection sensitivity while ensuring reliable discrimination of false positives. This study, performed on a DUV wafer inspection system and evaluated with Samsung’s 2nm and 1.4nm nodes, proposes an AI-based detection algorithm incorporating image enhancement and classification to tackle this challenge. The deep learning-based image enhancer improves optically scanned images to increase defect visibility, while the classifier utilizes the extracted features to predict the probability of a defect being yield-killing. Together, these components facilitate the separation of yield-killing defects from nuisance signals. Results demonstrate up to a 20% improvement in defect capture rate or an 89% reduction in SEM review budget, highlighting improved sensitivity for enhanced defect control.

Modeling and Data Analysis

Systematic defect risk assessment based on silicon digital twins

Sunghoon Choi
, 

Byeongseong Ahn
, 

Woobok Lee
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        As semiconductor design scales down and grows in complexity, manufacturing processes face tighter tolerances and increased risk of systematic defects. Identifying an optimal process window that is robust against such defects is traditionally achieved through design-of-experiments (DOE) on actual wafers, which is both time- and cost-intensive. Yield-based analysis provides additional insights but requires fab-out and is limited by the scarcity of defective cases and the sparsity of metrology data, making accurate defect characterization difficult. This paper introduces a virtual silicon process modeling framework based on a 3D TCAD emulator that constructs a silicon digital twin (Si-DT) by simulating fabrication steps directly on a GDS layout. Virtual DOE with Monte Carlo simulations enables the rapid generation of thousands of 3D structures, where process-induced variations and systematic defect margins can be measured at nanometer precision. Statistical modeling is then applied to derive defect indices from virtual silicon metrology data, providing a predictive and interpretable approach to process monitoring and optimization. 

Nanostructure characterization via RSoXS: inverse scattering with FEM-based Maxwell solvers

Kas Andrle
, 

Qi Zhang
, 

Harlan Heilman
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        We present a non-destructive metrology approach for nanostructure characterization using Resonant Soft X-ray Scattering (RSoXR) combined with FEM-based Maxwell solvers. Energy scans near absorption edges provide chemically sensitive, depth-resolved insights into materials like nickel and photoresist. This synergy enables both shape reconstruction and spatial mapping of composition, offering valuable capabilities for advancing EUV lithography and complex device architectures.

Model-based Raman simulations for optimized metrology in nanosheet transistor devices

Stefan Schoeche
, 

Aron Cepler
, 

Marjorie Cheng
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        In-line Raman spectroscopy is an increasingly important metrology technique for nanosheet gate-all-around devices because it provides direct access to intrinsic material properties such as strain and SiGe composition through phonon mode analysis. While Raman peak positions primarily reflect these intrinsic properties, peak amplitudes are jointly governed by material properties and the three-dimensional (3D) device geometry. This amplitude-geometry relationship becomes critical in fully integrated 3D device structures, where multiple materials, deep canyons, and strong near-field localization can drive measurement sensitivity and signal crosstalk. Here, we demonstrate the value of model-based Raman (MBR) simulations to (i) attribute measured Raman contributions to specific regions of complex 3D structures, (ii) optimize wavelength and polarization configurations for targeted sensitivity (e.g., nanosheet channel versus substrate/fin), and (iii) mitigate interference between signals from different materials in the structure. Proof-of-concept results on Si1-xGex line/space gratings and TiN-containing stacks, as well as case studies on integrated targets after high‑k deposition and after pFET source/drain epitaxy, illustrate how MBR-guided configuration selection enhances sensitivity and supports robust in-line monitoring of strain and composition in nanosheet transistor technology.  

Poisson-CDF modeling of in‑die optimization for electron beam inspection under random defect distribution

Po-Jen Mo
, 

Tsan-Yu Ho
, 

Xing Ji
, 

et al.

                                                    Show abstract

                                                        Electron-beam inspection (EBI) is essential for detecting buried defects in advanced DRAM manufacturing; however, achieving strong inline–probe correlation in high-volume production remains challenging due to limited in-die coverage and random defect distribution. A predictive model based on the Poisson cumulative distribution function (CDF) was developed to quantitatively link defect density, coverage ratio, and defective die thresholds, enabling calculation of the minimum coverage required for a specified confidence level. Validation on a 1β DRAM storage node contact short case demonstrated that applying the model’s recommendation of 1.99% coverage improved inline-to-probe correlation from R² = 0.36 to 0.81, while reducing a previously prolonged learning cycle to a significantly shorter timeframe.

Need for Speed: Boosting X-ray Inspection Speed in Advanced Electronics

October 28, 2026

                    As electronics become more powerful and advanced packaging technologies more complex, manufacturers face the challenge of detecting ever-smaller defects without reducing throughput. Optical inspection methods cannot reveal the buried structures found in high-power electronics, chiplets, HBM, through glass vias (TGVs), and advanced 3D packages. X-ray inspection and automated X-ray inspection (AXI) provide the required visibility, but higher resolution and penetration have traditionally come at the expense of speed. Recent advances in micro and nano focus X-ray source technology are overcoming this trade-off by delivering higher photon flux, improved contrast-to-noise ratio, and faster imaging. In this post we review these developments in high-power electronics, where higher voltage and power are required to penetrate through dense structures while maintaining micro-scale imaging, and advanced packaging where increasing complexity from vertical integration and sub-micron resolution combine. We highlight how increased throughput can be achieved while maintaining reliable defect detection.

Focus topic. Battery manufacturing process control: Why gigafactories fail at scale

July 9, 2026

                    Scale is visible, control is not. Battery manufacturing today is often discussed in terms of scale. Gigafactories are announced, capacity targets are published, and investment volumes are tracked closely. These indicators are easy to measure. Control is not. Yet control ultimately determines whether scale creates economic value. Without stable processes, expanded output does not translate into predictable yield, protected margins, or reduced risk. Instead, hidden variability accumulates in the background. Ramp-up is commonly described as a temporary phase in which processes stabilize and performance improves. Root causes remain only partially understood.

BLOX. Here’s how BLOX helps protect your X-ray tube

September 2, 2025

                    X-ray tubes are precision components designed for years of service, but like any high-performance part, they have operating limits. One issue we sometimes see in the field is accelerated filament wear, especially in systems running at low kV and high tube current. Left unchecked, these operating conditions put heavy stress on the filament.

While high current increases the number of X-rays produced, leading to a higher flux density and potentially faster acquisition times, it also puts more stress on the tungsten filament, shortening its lifespan.

Conversely, a lower kV reduces the energy of the X-ray photons, potentially requiring a higher mAs value to achieve the same image quality, which can also impact tube life.

SUMMARY

KLA (KLAC -10.33%) delivered quarterly revenue of $3.415 billion, exceeding internal forecasts and supported by robust demand in leading-edge foundry logic and advanced packaging. Gross margin outperformed guidance, propelled by favorable business mix and manufacturing scale, while free cash flow generation remained strong at $622 million. Management raised expectations for 2026 wafer equipment market growth and provided confidence in accelerated 2027 growth visibility, pointing to substantial customer urgency and expanded backlogs. The company increased its advanced packaging revenue outlook to approximately $1 billion, up from $635 million, following a surge in orders and higher customer adoption. An incremental $7 billion share repurchase authorization and sustained dividend growth highlighted KLA's focus on returning capital.

  • Management indicated that WFE is literally just as fast as we can go as an industry. clarifying that industry-wide capacity constraints, not end-customer CapEx, are the binding factor for near-term growth.
  • KLA’s services business is projected to remain at the high end of its 13%-15% CAGR model as customers prioritize tool performance and uptime.
  • Customer demand for new installations and support services prompted active hiring and significant expansion of support infrastructure, with emphasis on readiness for 2027 ramp.
  • The CFO stated that operating expenses will increase incrementally by about $15 million per quarter through year-end, attributed to investments in next-generation product development and infrastructure.
  • Management expects quarter-to-quarter revenue growth throughout 2026 and strong business momentum leading into 2027.

INDUSTRY GLOSSARY

  • WFE (Wafer Fab Equipment): Capital equipment used for semiconductor manufacturing and wafer processing, a key indicator for sector demand.
  • Process Control: Systems and technologies for defect detection, metrology, and yield management during semiconductor fabrication.
  • Advanced Packaging: Technologies enabling integration of multiple semiconductor components through architectures such as 2.5D, 3D, flip-chip, hybrid bonding, and system-in-package, critical for next-generation performance.
  • SOIC (System-on-Integrated-Chip): An advanced packaging technology combining heterogeneous elements in a unified package, often cited in high-performance compute applications.
  • DRAM (Dynamic Random Access Memory): A semiconductor memory technology widely used in computing; represents a significant portion of memory segment demand for KLA.
  • NAND: A type of non-volatile flash memory, used in solid-state storage and mobile devices.
  • Free Cash Flow Margin: Free cash flow divided by total revenue, providing a measure of cash efficiency after operating and capital expenditures.

ABSTRACT:
As EUV lithography is broadly deployed across advanced logic, memory, and High-NA technologies, mask inspection requirements increasingly extend beyond sensitivity alone to include application-specific defect characterization and disposition. In this study, we report Samsung's application of a multi-column electron beam inspection (EBMI) system for EUV mask inspection across diverse device technologies and pattern types, demonstrating practical deployment with a balance of resolution and throughput suitable for both development and manufacturing environments. We further demonstrate the capability to detect extremely small defects, on the order of 1× nm, in 2× nm pitch line-and-space patterns, taking advantage of the direct edge-localization characteristics of the electron beam. On Low-NA production masks the system delivered above-specification sensitivity, and on High-NA-relevant P2x line-and-space and P2y contact-and-hole patterns it met the sensitivity target, with defect capture rates of 98% and 96% against specifications of 84% and 79%, respectively. Beyond defect detection, the EBMI system supports high-resolution defect review and printability evaluation through integrated lithography simulation workflows. These include machine-learning-based advanced nuisance control (ANC) and auto defect classification (ADC) for early filtering and labeling of defects using inspection images; lithography printability review for coarse printability assessment at the lithography plane; post-inspection image capture (PIIC) for higher-resolution defect imaging; and aerial image simulation workflows that enable fine printability evaluation. PIIC-based SEM-to-aerial (S2A) analysis correlated with conventional review-SEM-based S2A with R² ≈ 0.97, allowing inspection results to feed directly into disposition without routine SEM review. Finally, an anamorphic pixel matched to the 4×/8× magnification of High-NA masks is introduced as a path to balance sensitivity and throughput. A PDM-to-production matching methodology, in which the recipe qualified on a programmed defect mask (PDM) is applied unchanged to production masks and validated for false and nuisance (F/N) performance and throughput, is used to establish production readiness. This work demonstrates the value of multi-column EBMI for EUV masks across diverse pattern types and device applications and highlights its contribution to mask readiness for advanced logic, memory, and High-NA lithography.

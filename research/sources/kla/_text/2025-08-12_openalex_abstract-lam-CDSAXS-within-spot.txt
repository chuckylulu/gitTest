TITLE: Study of critical dimension small-angle X-ray scattering within-spot uniformity with mass critical dimension scanning electron microscope and 3D tomography

ABSTRACT:
Critical dimension small-angle X-ray scattering (CD-SAXS) technology was introduced a few years ago as a next-generation nondestructive metrology method for semiconductor devices, especially for high aspect ratio (HAR) structures, such as VNAND memory holes and DRAM storage nodes. Small angle scattered beamlets emerging from arrays of HAR holes from along their long axis within the X-ray spot are analyzed from 0th to nth orders to provide an average profile within the illuminated area. This average profile has limited value for process improvement and control as it does not reveal key process-dependent deviations such as hole-to-hole variations of CD, depth, ellipticity, etc. To provide this detail, other techniques can see individual hole profiles, but by themselves take far too long to analyze a usefully large area. Combining information from both large spot and small field of view approaches can help deliver a more complete information set to guide process refinements, especially at the wafer edge, which is so important to delivering the highest productivity processes. In this work, we have evaluated the high aspect ratio contact mask hole structure of a 3D NAND device with CD-SAXS for hole-to-hole variations. The top-down CD scanning electron microscope (CDSEM) and 3D tomography (3DT) technologies, which are a combination of focused ion beam and SEM, are used as references to verify within-spot uniformity. It is simulated how hole-to-hole variations of individual hole CD, tilt, depth, and pitch in xy plane affect CD-SAXS raw images/signals. The correlation between measured CD-SAXS Qr and Qz signals and hole-to-hole variations in profile parameters extracted from CDSEM and 3DT within 300 μm spot of CD-SAXS has been found as a result. Correlation of variation in some profile parameters between CD-SAXS signal and image-based technologies shows good linearity. On the other hand, depth variation has a low correlation. This is because scattering raw signal movement is induced by not only one parameter but also multiple profile shapes, and in principle CD-SAXS has low sensitivity in depth. In conclusion, raw signal analysis approaches of CD-SAXS images can show the profile variation trends but it is still impacted by multiple profile parameters, which cause low correlations. Hence, it is needed to take comprehensive signal analysis and total information into account for higher accuracy.

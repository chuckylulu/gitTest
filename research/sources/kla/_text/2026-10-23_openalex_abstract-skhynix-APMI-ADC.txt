TITLE: Automatic classification of EUV mask defects inspected using actinic patterned mask inspection tool (APMI)
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1117/12.3118221
PUBLICATION DATE: 2026-10-23
VENUE: SPIE Photomask Technology Conference
AUTHORS: Kiwoo Jun (SK hynix Inc. (Korea, Republic of)) | Kangjoon Seo (SK hynix Inc. (Korea, Republic of)) | Seongjin Park (SK hynix Inc. (Korea, Republic of)) | Jongmin Kim (SK hynix Inc. ABSTRACT:
This paper presents the development of an economical and high-performance Rule-Based Auto Defect Classification (ADC) system for EUV mask yield management. Conventional 193nm-wavelength DUV inspection tools are physically limited in detecting fine patterns and phase defects due to diffraction constraints. In contrast, the 13.5nm-wavelength Actinic Patterned Mask Inspection (APMI) tool provides high-resolution images under actual exposure conditions, thereby overcoming these physical limitations. Leveraging the high-resolution, high-quality images from APMI, we developed a topology-based Rule-Based ADC algorithm that can be implemented without additional GPU infrastructure. The proposed algorithm precisely extracts contours from both defect and reference images and classifies them according to pattern structures (Cell, Core, Peri, Sub-pattern, etc.). Notably, it achieves perfect separation even when clustered defects are detected, effectively distinguishing non-critical sub-pattern groups from genuine defects. Furthermore, the system incorporates Advanced ADC capabilities to accurately identify local CD errors and automatically correct die swap errors, a critical issue in two-die mask configurations where reference coordinates are frequently inverted. By analyzing combinations of key physical parameters—such as %CDE (Critical Dimension Error), EPE (Edge Placement Error), Residue, SNR, and Pattern-width—the system resolves the overkill (false detection) issues inherent in conventional difference-image-based approaches. Experimental results demonstrate a dramatic improvement in defect classification accuracy—from 30% to 95%—and consistent reliability (>95%) across major technologies (N-2 to N+2) and their layers. This study suggests that the combination of high-resolution optical data and refined rule-based analysis offers the most practical defect management solution for EUV mask processes.

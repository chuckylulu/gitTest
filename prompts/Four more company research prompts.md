# Four more company research prompts (concise)

Four self-contained prompts: leading-edge chip fabrication and lithography, AI processors, long-term energy for computing, and quantum computing.

Each asks for the same things: a global examination, a ranking by quality alone, a check on whether each company has a coherent path forward, a separate entry-point assessment, and a separate list of two short candidates. The researcher sets its own quality criteria and is told it may disagree with the context given.

The company names are starting points to verify, added from general knowledge as well as from the gathered material. Ownership and listing status of some may have changed.

## 1. Leading-edge chip fabrication and lithography

```text
Assess the companies in leading-edge chip fabrication and lithography, worldwide.

The field covers the manufacture of the most advanced chips, and the lithography and other equipment and materials it depends on. I am exploring it and want your independent assessment of the companies in it. Form your own view: the names and the context below are starting points, not conclusions, and you are free to disagree with them.

Scope. The examination is global. Cover companies in every region, listed or private. Include at least the following, and add any others you judge important: TSMC, Samsung Electronics, Intel, Rapidus, SMIC; ASML, Nikon, Canon, Carl Zeiss, Trumpf, SMEE; Applied Materials, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron, ASM International, Screen Holdings, Kokusai Electric, Naura, AMEC; JSR, Tokyo Ohka Kogyo, Shin-Etsu Chemical, Hoya, Tekscend Photomask, Photronics.

What I need:
1. Quality. Decide what makes a company high quality in this field, state your criteria, and assess each company against them. Judge quality independently of the share price.
2. Path forward. Say whether each company has a coherent path forward: what it has to get through in the coming years, what it has actually done about it, and whether the plan is funded and plausible.
3. Ranking. Rank the companies by quality alone, in one global list.
4. Entry point, kept separate. Only after ranking, say for each company whether now is a sensible entry point for an investment, and why. A high-quality company can be a poor entry and the reverse, so do not blend the two judgments.
5. Short candidates, kept separate. Name the two companies in this field that you judge the strongest candidates for a short position, with your reasoning, what the price already reflects, the main risks to the position, and what would prove it wrong. If you cannot justify two, say so instead of forcing it.

Context from earlier work, to test and challenge: it treated leading-edge fabrication and lithography as constraints that AI demand cannot remove. It found that over 90% of the most advanced chips are made in Taiwan, that TSMC's A16 process slipped from late 2026 to 2027, that ASML ships about 65 EUV tools a year and plans about 30% more for 2027, and that Intel has said it may pause its 14A process without outside customers. It also saw roughly a one-in-four chance of a pause in AI spending around 2028.

Use public sources, preferring filings and other primary material. Date every figure, mark company claims that no outside party has confirmed, and say what you could not verify. This is analysis for my own exploration, not personal financial advice.

Return a short verdict first, then the quality ranking, the path-forward notes, the entry-point assessment, the two short candidates, and your sources.
```

# Chokepoint owners top AI's system-hardware field

*Research date: 12 November 2026. Built only from the research notes for this question, which rest mostly on company filings, exchange data and company releases. This is analysis for your own exploration, not personal financial advice; nothing here is an instruction to buy or sell.*

**Short verdict.** The highest-quality company in system-level AI hardware is **TSMC**, which owns the binding packaging constraint and both its bridge and successor technologies, funded from a 58.7% operating margin and net cash of about NT$3.2tn (Q3 2026). **Disco** (thinning and dicing tools plus consumables) and **Ajinomoto's ABF build-up-film franchise** tie for second: both own chokepoints that every bonding or substrate successor still needs, with consumable revenue and the best resilience in the field, although outside evidence of their market share is thinner than for the memory makers. SK hynix and Micron tie next, then Ibiden. On the open question, the successor already in volume is **hybrid bonding, but in logic chiplets inside TSMC** (SoIC at 9 µm), not in memory, where SK hynix now says it will not arrive before HBM5 (a generation Micron's roadmap dates to 2028, with Counterpoint expecting the real turning point around 2029-30). Among the system-level transitions, **co-packaged optics in scale-out switches shipped first** (about 10-15k switches in 2026, a SemiAnalysis estimate) and is first in line for volume in 2027-28, followed by ±400 VDC sidecar power racks from 2027. **The integrated giants are capturing the successor value** (TSMC, NVIDIA, Broadcom, Samsung, Marvell with Lumentum); specialists keep value only where they own a physical chokepoint that the giants pre-fund or buy rather than replace (indium-phosphide lasers, ABF film and substrates, thinning and dicing, test), and specialists without one are being acquired. On price, most of the ranked companies screen fair on 12 November 2026; Ibiden and Monolithic Power look stretched; Micron looks attractive only if memory stays supply-limited; Innolight looks attractive on consensus with low confidence; Lumentum's price now tracks Marvell's; and Ajinomoto's cannot be read apart from its food business. **NVIDIA reports on 17 November 2026, and press reports on 11-12 November said its board was weighing the removal of its chief executive**; either event could move several entry labels within days.

How to read the figures: every figure carries the period it covers and its publication date. "Company claim, unconfirmed" marks statements no outside party has confirmed; "press report" marks items from news coverage rather than filings; "aggregator" marks third-party computations (mainly stockanalysis.com) rather than company filings. Where the notes disagree, the primary-filing figure is used and the discrepancy is noted.

**Five power centres hold the field.** Taiwan holds advanced packaging (TSMC, ASE), rack power (Delta, Lite-On) and cold plates (AVC, Jentech). Japan holds the upstream chokepoints: ABF film (Ajinomoto), T-glass cloth (Nittobo), the leading AI substrate maker (Ibiden), thinning and dicing (Disco) and AI fibre (Fujikura). Korea holds two of the three HBM makers and the main thermo-compression bonder supplier (Hanmi). The United States holds the lasers (Lumentum, Coherent), signal-conditioning silicon (Credo, Astera, Marvell), the largest connector and cooling houses (Amphenol, Vertiv) and chip-level power (MPS). Mainland China holds plug-in optical-module assembly (Innolight and Eoptolink ranked first and second in 2025) and a parallel, domestically directed packaging and memory chain (JCET, Tongfu, SJ Semiconductor, CXMT). Europe contributes Besi, AT&S, Infineon and Schneider, plus private specialists (EV Group, Rittal, Schott). Israel contributes Tower's silicon-photonics foundry and Camtek's inspection tools; no Israeli packaging or substrate maker of note surfaced.

**Coverage was uneven, and three regions are thin.** North America is covered from primary SEC filings. Taiwan is covered well through the exchange's open-data feeds and company filings. Japan is covered for the key names from their TDnet results filings, but EDINET and Japanese-language press were not searched, so Kyocera, Toppan, DNP, Shinko, Nidec, Daikin and the twinax-cable makers are thin. **Korea is thin**: the regulator's DART system was not searched, Korean news was readable only at headline level, and customer concentration for Hanmi, Daeduck and Samsung could not be found. **Mainland China is thin** beyond Innolight and Eoptolink, whose annual and Q3 reports were read on cninfo: Huawei's HBM and CPO switches, CXMT's HBM progress, Chinese bonder makers and SJ Semiconductor's financials were not verified. **India is thin, and on the evidence it has no AI system-hardware company of scale**: the relevant projects are Tata Electronics' pilot assembly plant in Assam (pilot runs from about early October 2026, not 2.5D), Micron's assembly-and-test plant in Sanand, 3D Glass Solutions' Odisha glass-substrate unit (production targeted for 2030) and Navin Fluorine's contract production of Chemours' two-phase fluid (start unconfirmed); NSE and BSE filings were not searched ([Business Standard, 19 Apr 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/india-s-1st-advanced-3d-glass-chip-packaging-unit-to-come-up-in-bhubaneswar-126041900360_1.html); [EFY](https://www.electronicsforyou.biz/industry-buzz/tata-electronics-starts-pilot-runs-at-assam-semiconductor-plant/)).

**How the fifteen were chosen, and who was left out.** The ranking covers the fifteen companies that matter most to the binding constraints worldwide; importance decided who is in, and quality alone decided the order. NVIDIA and Broadcom are left out of the ranking because they are processor companies: NVIDIA's networking business, larger than Cisco by quarterly revenue at above US$17.5bn in the quarter to July 2026, is still only about 18% of the group ([The Register, 31 Oct 2026](https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/10/31/backbone-of-a-beast-charting-nvidias-rise-to-networking-titan/5306499)), and it cannot be owned separately. Both appear throughout as the integrated giants that decide the open question. Marvell is left out as a partly coherent processor and DSP company whose scale-up optics revenue was "virtually zero" in October 2026. ASE is left out narrowly: it is the largest outsourced packager and NVIDIA's optical-engine partner, but its path is only partly coherent because its record capex is debt-funded with negative free cash flow in 2026-27, and it would rank around fourteenth. Credo (coherent, but three end customers took about 84% of FY2026 revenue), Fujikura (coherent on its financials but with thin evidence on its path) and Corning (a fibre chokepoint with only ~12% return on capital) would sit in the lower middle. The substrate peers (Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Unimicron, AT&S, Nittobo, SKC/Absolics), the bonder and inspection makers other than Besi and Disco (Tokyo Electron, Hanmi, ASMPT, Camtek, Onto), the other cooling names (AVC, Jentech, nVent, Boyd, CoolIT), the other optical names (Coherent, Fabrinet, Eoptolink), the other copper and power names (TE, Astera, Lite-On, Eaton, Schneider, Infineon, Vicor) and the other packagers (Amkor, Intel, JCET, KYEC) rank below their segment leaders on the same tests. Private companies (EV Group, Shinko, Ayar Labs, Lightmatter, ZutaCore, Heron Power, Rapidus, Silicon Box) were left out because too little about them could be verified.

Ties are stated rather than forced. **The top group is ranks 1-3. Companies whose path forward is only partly coherent are kept out of the top two groups**; none of the fifteen has a path judged not coherent.

**TSMC is first by a margin no one else approaches.** It is the only company that owns the binding constraint today and both sides of three transitions: its own wafer interposer is the bridge, extended to 14 reticles in 2028, while its in-house panel line, hybrid bonding and photonic engine are the successors. Its chief executive said in July 2026 that "our packaging capacity is so tight that now it limits my customers' growth" ([TSMC 2Q26 call, 16 Jul 2026](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-08/3e494f0c14dd0890f897aa044415e21d93486cc4/TSMC%202Q26%20Transcript.pdf)), and its Q3 2026 accounts show a 66.4% gross margin, NT$476.56bn of free cash flow after record capex, and net cash of about NT$3.2tn ([TSMC 6-K, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000726/a3q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm)). Its weaknesses are concentration rather than position: the ten largest customers were 78% of 2025 revenue and North America 75% ([TSMC 20-F, filed 16 Apr 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000162828026025362/tsm-20251231.htm)), and its packaging sits almost entirely in Taiwan.

**Disco and Ajinomoto tie for second, ahead of the memory makers, because their chokepoints survive every transition in this report.** Disco's grinders, dicers and consumables are needed in both the thermo-compression and hybrid-bonding eras, its consumables follow customers' utilisation, and it earned a 45.4% operating margin in April-September 2026 with no debt ([Disco tanshin, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.disco.co.jp/jp/ir/library/doc/fr/fr20261022.pdf)). Ajinomoto's build-up film is still laminated onto glass cores, so the glass transition that threatens the substrate makers leaves its film in place; its Functional Materials segment earned a business profit of ¥42.9bn on ¥73.3bn of sales in H1 FY2026, about 58.5% ([Ajinomoto Q2 deck, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/presentation/main/011111116/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/FY26Q2_Presentation_E_with%20Script.pdf)). The two cannot be separated: Disco's outside evidence is thinner (no third-party share was found) and Ajinomoto's franchise is diluted inside a food group, but both score best in the field on revenue model and resilience. **SK hynix and Micron tie at fourth.** SK hynix wins on HBM position and margin; Micron wins on contract quality (take-or-pay agreements with deposits and price floors), customer spread and low China exposure; both are funded through any slip, but both are the most cyclical businesses in the ranking and their lead is re-contested at every memory generation, which is why they sit below the transition-robust chokepoints. **Ibiden ranks sixth**: its lead is the best customer-confirmed in the field, but its chokepoint is a bridge, its new capacity ramps from Q1 2028 inside the pause window, and capex will equal or exceed EBITDA in the years to March 2027 and March 2028.

**Besi and Lumentum tie at seventh**, each owning the leading position on a successor (hybrid-bonding placement; lasers for co-packaged optics) with strong outside validation (Applied's stake; NVIDIA's equity and Marvell's bid), and each carrying a large deduction (Besi's cyclicality and ~40% China exposure; Lumentum's 37% customer and merger risk). **Delta and MPS tie at ninth** with ~33-35% returns on capital and net cash, but positions built on scale and know-how rather than an owned constraint. Amphenol is eleventh on breadth and resilience; Vertiv and Tower tie at twelfth, Vertiv with services and returns, Tower with contract-backed revenue but the risk that TSMC captures the optical engines. **Samsung Electronics and Innolight close the list because their paths are only partly coherent**: Samsung's memory business would tie with SK hynix and Micron, but the listed group's non-memory businesses lost money in Q3 2026 and its packaging lead is unproven; Innolight is the world's largest transceiver maker, but its core product is a bridge with no verified successor qualification.

**Cross-border exposure is concentrated in a few companies rather than spread across the field.** US tariffs barely touch AI build-outs today: the Section 232 proclamation of 14 January 2026 put 25% on a narrow set of advanced chips but exempted US data-centre use, and a broader second phase covering chips and equipment had not been imposed by November 2026 ([Covington, Feb 2026](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/02/a-month-in-semiconductor-policy-section-232-measures-bis-rule-and-taiwan-deal-signal-strategic-push/)). The live exposures are company-specific. SK hynix's China fabs have run on an annual US licence since their validated-end-user status ended on 31 December 2025, and US controls on advanced HBM sales to China have applied since December 2024 ([BIS, 2 Dec 2024](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-strengthens-export-controls-restrict-chinas-capability-produce-advanced-semiconductors-military)). Micron is still barred from Chinese critical-infrastructure buyers under China's May 2023 ruling. Besi (about 40% of revenue) and Tokyo Electron (36% in its September quarter; [TEL presentation, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.tel.com/ir/library/report/i61j9u00000000gf-att/fy27q2presentations-e2.pdf)) still sell heavily into China, which shows back-end tools are not yet blocked. Lumentum's sale to Marvell needs Chinese approval (per TrendForce) while China and Hong Kong take 27.6% of its shipments, and Innolight serves US demand from Thailand and Mexico under 25% Section 301 tariffs ([Photoncap, undated, about late 2025](https://photoncap.net/p/chinese-optical-modules-own-7-of)). The policy calendar concentrates risk on 10 January 2027, when the US suspension of its "affiliates" export rule ends unless extended ([Baker McKenzie, Nov 2026](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/bis-extends-suspension-of-the-affiliates-rule-through-january-10-2027/)) and China's suspension of its October 2025 rare-earth controls is reported to lapse (a single secondary source) ([Discovery Alert, 12 Nov 2026](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/rare-earth-export-controls-china-pause-prices-november-2026/)).

## 3. Path forward: the transition that tests each company, and what must go right

The six transitions are (1) copper to optical links between processors, (2) plug-in optical modules to co-packaged optics, (3) solder-bump stacking to direct hybrid bonding, (4) silicon interposers to panel and glass packaging, (5) cold plates to two-phase cooling, and (6) today's rack power to 600 kW and 1 MW racks on 800 VDC. For each company, "said" is the plan, "done" is what has shipped, been qualified, funded or bought, and the verdict asks whether the plan survives a one-to-two-year slip.

**TSMC — coherent.** It must pass transitions (4), (3) and (2). Its 2026 roadmap extends the wafer interposer to 14 reticles with 20 memory stacks in 2028 and beyond 14 reticles with 24 stacks in 2029, takes hybrid-bond pitch from 9 µm today to 6 µm in 2028 and 4.5 µm in 2029, and puts its first 200G photonic modulator in production in 2026 (company claim, unconfirmed) ([SemiEngineering, 27 Apr 2026](https://semiengineering.com/tsmc-tech-symposium-2026-by-the-numbers/)). Done: 5.5-reticle CoWoS in production, SoIC hybrid bonding in mass production with AMD as a key customer, a panel pilot line built with pilot production reported for mid-2027 and full-scale output in 2028-29 with NVIDIA's Feynman as first adopter ([TrendForce, 15 Jun 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/06/15/news-tsmc-cowos-supply-demand-gap-reportedly-seen-narrowing-from-20-to-10-by-end-2026-as-capacity-expands/)), a five-year interposer second source at GlobalFoundries from 1H 2028 ([GF 6-K, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001709048/000170904826000259/globalfoundries3q2026earni.htm)) and 2026 capex raised to US$60-64bn. A panel slip is covered because the bridge it would extend is TSMC's own. What must go right: panel warpage and yield, tool deliveries (stretched from 3-6 to 6-8 months), Arizona packaging (sources conflict on 2028 or 2029), and the A16 node, which slipped from late 2026 to 2027.

**Disco — coherent.** Transition (3) helps rather than threatens it: hybrid bonding needs thinner dies and cleaner singulation, so its grinders and dicers serve both eras. Done: record shipments, customer advances up from ¥50.0bn to ¥79.6bn between 31 March and 30 September 2026, and April-September operating cash flow of ¥111.7bn against ¥12.9bn of plant capex, with ¥342.4bn of cash and no debt ([Disco tanshin, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.disco.co.jp/jp/ir/library/doc/fr/fr20261022.pdf)). The plan needs no transition to arrive on time. What must go right: utilisation-linked consumables demand must hold through a pause; its tool roles specific to hybrid bonding and panel processing, its product split, its share against Tokyo Seimitsu and its export-control exposure were all not verified.

**Ajinomoto — coherent.** Transition (4) is the test, and the film survives it: glass replaces the substrate core but keeps build-up layers, so the threat is only CoWoP, which would remove the substrate entirely and has found no 2026 adopter because it needs line widths below 10 µm against 20-30 µm in today's board processes ([GlobalSMT](https://www.globalsmt.net/advanced-packaging/nvidias-cowop-pcb-architecture-targets-abf-substrates-but-faces-warpage-risk/)). Done: a ~¥10bn plant started in 2025, a ~¥21.5bn varnish plant at Gunma for mass production in FY2030, and land bought for demand "from 2030" ([Ajinomoto deck, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.ajinomoto.co.jp/company/en/ir/event/presentation/main/011111116/teaserItems1/01/linkList/03/link/FY26Q2_Presentation_E_with%20Script.pdf)). It says its supply chain can meet current demand (company claim, unconfirmed). What must go right: the FY2030 capacity step must not lag an acceleration scenario, and Chinese substitutes must stay slow despite a reported price rise of up to 30% and a reported ~30% cut in China allocations (both secondary and unconfirmed).

**SK hynix — coherent.** Transition (3) has been deferred on its own terms: it builds 16-high HBM4 with its MR-MUF process, keeps that process for 16-high HBM4E, and told Hot Chips in August 2026 that hybrid bonding "will not meet HBM4E requirements", putting it at HBM5 at the earliest ([Bits&Chips, 26 Aug 2026](https://bits-chips.com/article/besi-faces-longer-wait-as-sk-hynix-pushes-hybrid-bonding-to-hbm5/)). Done: 12-high hybrid-bonding validation, HBM4E samples in June 2026, HBM4 in full-scale production from Q3 2026 after a roughly one-quarter slip, new cleanrooms at M15X and Yongin, an Indiana packaging plant for 2H 2028, and US$26.2bn of net proceeds from its July 2026 US share listing ([SK hynix 424B4, Jul 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002120882/000119312526299963/d32785d424b4.htm)). Net cash of KRW98.7tn funds any slip ([SK hynix 6-K, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2120882/000119312526434994/d162547d6k.htm)). What must go right: HBM4E qualification for NVIDIA's 2027 platform, keeping most of NVIDIA's allocation as Samsung gains, the TSMC base-die partnership for custom HBM, and renewal of the annual US licence for its China fabs.

**Micron — coherent.** It faces transition (3) and the move to custom logic base dies. Its plan is a new HBM generation every year (HBM4E in 2027, HBM5 in 2028, HBM5E in 2029, HBM6 in 2030, per secondary coverage of its 21 October 2026 webinar; [TechTimes, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329350/20261022/micron-maps-annual-hbm-advances-2030-rejects-chinese-density-workaround.htm)) and a custom HBM4E with NVIDIA ("NVHBM") ramping in 2027. Done: HBM4 in volume since March 2026, 16-high samples, the Tongluo fab bought from PSMC for US$1.8bn, a Hiroshima HBM cleanroom under way for late 2028, and Singapore HBM packaging due early 2027 ([Micron 10-K, FY to 3 Sep 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm)). Net cash of US$68.3bn plus US$12.75bn of customer deposits fund the plan ([Micron prepared remarks, 30 Sep 2026](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). What must go right: NVHBM qualification, the Singapore and Tongluo ramps in 2027, and a hybrid-bonding path, which it has not disclosed.

**Ibiden — coherent.** Transition (4) is the core test, plus CoWoP and Intel's EMIB-T (Ibiden's fiscal years run April to March, so FY2027 is April 2027 to March 2028). Its roadmap stays organic to substrates above 130 mm by 2030 and beyond, and it says "there is currently no technology that can replace leading-edge IC package substrates" (company claim, unconfirmed) ([Ibiden Q&A, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.ibiden.com/ir/items/en_QA_FY2026Q2.pdf)). Done: the Ono plant in mass production, Ono Cell8 (¥280bn, ramping from Q1 2028) and Gama Cell6 (¥370bn in total, mass production from FY2029) committed, EMIB-T substrates due in 2H FY2027 "with no significant change", and an NVIDIA-funded tripling of Ono ([NVIDIA, 21 Oct 2026](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-and-Supply-Chain-Partners-Expand-Capacity-to-Accelerate-AI-Infrastructure-Buildout/default.aspx)). Funded: yes, from ~¥258bn net cash and ¥199.7bn of advances, though FY2027 free cash flow turns negative unless customers prepay more. The lack of a dated glass plan matters only if glass arrives before 2030, against the weight of independent evidence. What must go right: demand through FY2029, yields on 110 mm-plus substrates, and no repeat of the underused plants of 2007 and 2021.

**Besi — coherent.** Transition (3) is its business. Done: six integrated Applied-Besi hybrid-bonding lines with 30 bonders at a leading logic customer in 2025, 23 hybrid-bonding customers and more than 215 systems of cumulative demand (company claim), hybrid-bonding orders more than doubling in 2026 as logic chiplets, CPO and edge-AI devices entered production, and orders from a leading memory customer for development ([Besi Q3 2026 release, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.besi.com/fileadmin/user_upload/PR_Q3-2026.pdf); [Besi Annual Report 2025](https://www.besi.com/fileadmin/data/Investor_Relations/_Semi__Annual_Reports/Annual_Report_2025.pdf)). Applied Materials is raising its stake to about 20% without seeking a board seat ([Applied 8-K, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/6951/000162828026067303/ex991prforprojectbasil.htm)). The successor already ships in logic, so the memory slip to HBM5 (which Micron's roadmap dates to 2028, while Counterpoint puts hybrid bonding's real turning point there around 2029-30, later than Besi itself assumed in early 2026) is funded by net cash and ~€223.5m of nine-month free cash flow. What must go right: memory makers buying open-market tools rather than captive ones (Samsung uses its subsidiary Semes), keeping the accuracy and throughput lead, and China demand holding or moving to Southeast Asia.

**Lumentum — coherent, pending its merger with Marvell.** It faces transitions (2) and (1) as the laser source for co-packaged optics, plus optical circuit switching. Said: an ultra-high-power laser run-rate of ~US$50m in the December 2026 quarter, high-volume shipments for scale-up CPO from 2H 2027 ahead of 2028 deployments, and near-packaged optics in late 2027-28. Done: a first purchase order for external laser-source modules for 2H 2027 delivery, US$2.0bn of NVIDIA preferred, net plant up from US$1,159.1m to US$1,410.4m in one quarter ([Lumentum Q1 FY27 release, 2 Nov 2026](https://investor.lumentum.com/financial-news-releases/news-details/2026/Lumentum-Announces-First-Quarter-of-Fiscal-Year-2027-Financial-Results/default.aspx)), and the all-stock agreement with Marvell (4.5 Marvell shares per share, closing expected 1H 2028, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals) ([Marvell, 2 Nov 2026](https://investor.marvell.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1040/marvell-and-lumentum-to-combine-creating-a-category-leader-in-ai-connectivity)), which TrendForce says needs Chinese as well as US clearance ([TrendForce, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261103-13294.html)). Bridge and successor share the same fabs, so a CPO slip shifts volume back to pluggable lasers rather than stranding capacity. What must go right: high-power laser reliability at scale, deal approval, a lower 37% customer concentration, and an answer to Cignal's forecast that silicon photonics with continuous-wave lasers takes 60% of the optical front end by 2030 at the expense of the EMLs it leads in ([Cignal AI, 15 Oct 2026](https://cignal.ai/2026/10/update-to-cignal-ais-datacom-optical-front-end-forecast/)).

**Delta Electronics — coherent.** Transition (6) is the test, with (5) through its coolant distribution units. Done: a 660 kW 800 VDC in-row power rack with 480 kW of battery backup and a solid-state transformer shown at GTC in March 2026 ([Power Electronics News, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.powerelectronicsnews.com/nvidia-gtc-2026-800-vdc-power-partnerships-from-grid-to-processor/)), a 2.5 MW CDU at OCP 2026 with Google's "Deschutes" CDU due in mass production in 1Q 2027 ([Dell'Oro, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.delloro.com/inside-ocp-global-summit-2026-cold-plates-800-vdc-sidecars-and-a-buildout-at-full-steam/)), and a Penang site approved. Shipments of 800 VDC racks were not verified. It funds the successor from the bridge (trailing free cash flow NT$66.5bn, aggregator). Timing favours it: the hyperscalers' ±400 VDC sidecar specification became final on 8 October 2026, while NVIDIA's native 800 VDC is reported to have slipped beyond 2028. What must go right: first-source status as power moves from in-rack shelves into sidecars, where Eaton, Schneider and Vertiv also compete, and no damage from the new US ITC case on vertical power delivery, which names Delta among 20 respondents (Delta says its products are not affected) ([Taipei Times, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2026/10/12/2003865784)).

**Monolithic Power Systems — coherent.** Transition (6) at the chip: lateral regulators give way to vertical delivery, and silicon to GaN and SiC at 800 V. Its chief executive says "you run out of peripheral, you have to do vertical"; it is sampling 800 V GaN parts, expects 800 V revenue "probably end of the '27 and 2028", and says capacity supports more than US$6bn of revenue, rising to US$8bn by 2H 2027 ([MPS 8-K, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001280452/000162828026068202/mpwr-20260930xexx991.htm)). Its growth does not depend on that timing, and net cash plus a 38.6% operating margin fund a slip. What must go right: winning vertical-power sockets against ADI, which closed its ~US$1.5bn purchase of Empower on 7 July 2026 ([ADI 10-Q, filed 19 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/6281/000000628126000073/adi-20260801.htm)), Vicor's patent-backed modules, Infineon and module makers such as Delta.

**Amphenol — coherent.** It faces transitions (1), (2) and (6) and already ships on both sides. Done: the CommScope connectivity business, closed in January 2026, is now expected to sell US$5.1bn in 2026 with half going to IT datacom and its glass supply secured; further fibre and board bolt-ons worth about US$1.6bn; and US$3.66bn of nine-month free cash flow ([Amphenol 8-K, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000820313/000110465926119421/aph-ex99d1.htm)). Timing suits it: NVIDIA's 2027 Kyber NVL144 rack is copper-only and its 2028 Feynman racks keep copper alongside optics ([Klockwood, 23 Mar 2026](https://blog.glennklockwood.com/2026/03/gtc-2026-recap.html)). What must go right: replacing the cable-cartridge revenue a board midplane removes, managing US$17.9bn of gross debt, and its NVLink copper share, which only conflicting Chinese secondary sources describe.

**Vertiv — coherent in cooling, partly coherent in power.** It faces transitions (5) and (6). In cooling its value sits in layers every successor still needs: facility heat rejection, coolant distribution, fluid services (PurgeRite, US$1,138.3m) and cold-plate validation (Strategic Thermal Labs), with chiller capacity doubling ([Vertiv Q3 call, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/28/vertiv-vrt-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). The evidence that single-phase cooling stretches to ~600 kW racks helps it. In power, its 800 VDC portfolio slipped from a planned 2H 2026 release ([Vertiv, 13 Oct 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/from-vision-to-readiness-vertiv-collaborates-with-nvidia-to-advance-800-vdc-platform-designs-to-power-the-next-generation-of-ai-factories-302582190.html)) to validation now, revenue in 2027 and deployment in 2028, and its UPS line is the product most directly displaced by native DC. Funded: net cash and a US$2.6-2.8bn 2026 free-cash-flow guide. What must go right: a two-phase-ready offer when needed (none verified) and price discipline as about 40 CDU vendors compete.

**Tower Semiconductor — coherent.** It faces transitions (1), (2) and (3), answering TSMC's integrated photonic engine with die-to-wafer bonding of InP lasers onto silicon photonics. Said: tripled capacity by Q3 2027 on a 300 mm line in Japan, "appreciable" near-packaged-optics shipments in 2H 2027, contracts extended through 2029. Done: all tools for that line ordered, a US$920m programme about 65% paid and self-funded, US$341m of customer advances, and laser-on-silicon-photonics parts in initial production for NewPhotonics ([Tower 6-K, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000928876/000117891326004949/exhibit_99-1.htm)). A CPO slip extends pluggable demand it already serves. What must go right: near-packaged-optics wins converting in 2H 2027, resolution of GlobalFoundries' patent claims and an Intel capacity dispute, and NVIDIA and Broadcom optical engines not concentrating entirely at TSMC.

**Samsung Electronics — partly coherent as a group, coherent in memory.** It faces transition (3) and the custom base-die shift, where it alone has the logic foundry in-house. Done: the industry's first HBM4 mass production (12 February 2026), first HBM4E samples (May 2026), NVIDIA and AMD qualifications, HBM4 yield reported near 80%, and HBM4 at 60% of HBM sales in Q3 2026 ([TrendForce, 29 May 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/05/29/news-samsung-starts-shipping-industry-first-hbm4e-samples-three-months-after-hbm4-mass-production-performance-up-over-20/); [Samsung Q3 2026 call transcript, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-samsung-electronics-posts-record-q3-2026-profit-93CH-4981365); [Samsung 3Q26 deck, 29 Oct 2026](https://images.samsung.com/is/content/samsung/assets/global/ir/docs/2026_3Q_conference_eng.pdf)). It plans HBM4E mass production in 1H 2027 with partial hybrid copper bonding, an earlier bet than SK hynix's that carries yield risk. The group verdict is partly coherent because the device division lost KRW0.8tn in Q3 2026, the foundry recovery leans on HBM base dies, and no named AI-processor packaging customer was found. What must go right: HBM4E yield and qualification in 1H 2027, price parity with SK hynix (press reports said it sold HBM3E about 30% cheaper; unconfirmed), and converting memory leadership into combined memory-logic-packaging wins.

**Zhongji Innolight — partly coherent.** Transition (2) is existential. The bridge is extremely well funded: the July 2026 Hong Kong listing raised about HK$60.9bn, nine-month 2026 capex was RMB9.16bn against RMB1.61bn a year earlier, and net cash is about RMB55bn ([Innolight Q3 report, cninfo, 30 Oct 2026](http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-30/1225643953.PDF)). Pluggables are outlasting roadmaps: TrendForce expects pluggable transceiver sales of US$84.1bn in 2027 (+76%) ([TrendForce, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261102-13291.html)), and LightCounting expects about 48% of ports to still be retimed pluggables in 2031. But no CPO or near-packaged-optics qualification was verified, and in co-packaged optics the optical engine moves into the switch package built by the switch-chip owner and its foundry, leaving module makers assembly or laser sub-assembly; LightCounting expects transceivers' share of capex to fall from about 5% to 3.5-4% by 2029-30 ([SimpleTechTrend, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.simpletechtrend.com/en/post/ocp-2026-lightcounting-cpo-npo-forecast-scenarios-en)). What must go right: a named role in near-packaged or co-packaged optics, holding share with customers its filings anonymise, and no US restriction on Chinese modules (none found).

## 7. What could not be verified, and why

**The method had hard limits, and they shape what the report can claim.** The shared web-search budget for the research session ran out early (in some workstreams after about 14-25 searches), and the Exa and Firecrawl tools ran out of credits soon after; they were retried at about 12:55 UTC on 12 November 2026 and were still unavailable. From then on, evidence came only from direct fetches of known pages: company filings (SEC EDGAR, Japan's TDnet, China's cninfo, Korean exchange disclosures relayed by Naver), exchange data (TWSE and TPEx open data, Eastmoney), company investor pages and pages that earlier searches had surfaced. That biases the evidence towards companies with English-language primary filings, and it is why customer lists, third-party market shares and several 2026 product dates are missing below.

**Local-language coverage of mainland China, Japan and Korea is thin.** No Chinese-, Japanese- or Korean-language searching was possible; Korean news could be read only at headline level; Korea's DART, Japan's EDINET, Taiwan's MOPS (in part), Israel's MAYA and India's NSE and BSE filing systems were not searched. As a result, Huawei's HBM and CPO switches, CXMT's HBM progress, Chinese bonder, thinning and inspection makers, SJ Semiconductor's financials, Korean bonder makers' customer splits (Hanmi, Semes, Hanwha), and Japanese private and mid-sized makers (Shinko, Kyocera, DNP, Toppan, Nidec, the twinax-cable makers) are covered weakly or not at all.

**Some financial figures come from a data aggregator rather than the companies' own filings.** Many return-on-capital, net-cash and trailing free-cash-flow figures are stockanalysis.com computations from S&P Global data. Their definitions vary and sometimes mislead: Unimicron shows a 23.9% ROIC against a 7.3% ROCE, Arista and AVC show ROICs of ~240% and ~160% that reflect tiny invested capital, and Korean trailing figures may lag a quarter. Valuation history is mostly five fiscal-year-end points rather than continuous ten-year ranges, and the aggregator's forward P/E does not state its basis.

**Specific items could not be confirmed.** Customer concentration is missing for Ibiden, Delta, Disco, Tower, Samsung Electronics, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Unimicron and Hanmi, and Lumentum's 37% customer and Innolight's five customers are unnamed in their filings. Unconfirmed or unverified: the 52-78-week packaging lead times; Ajinomoto's reported ABF price rise and China allocation cut; the ~¥280bn size of NVIDIA's advance to Ibiden (a Bunshun report); Intel's first named EMIB-T customer; Absolics' reported AMD and AWS qualification; Feynman's process node, Rubin Ultra's die count and rack form factor, and the 12 November 2026 claim that base Rubin volume has slipped to 2027; NVIDIA's own Kyber date beyond "roadmap intact"; JEDEC's final HBM4E height; Samsung's HBM4 price talks with NVIDIA; the EU F-gas milestones; and the actual rule texts for export controls on bonding, dicing and inspection tools. The substance of the US "AI regulation" headlines of 5-6 November 2026 and the NVIDIA governance reports of 11-12 November are known only from press coverage, with no company filing. China's extension of its rare-earth export-control suspension to 10 January 2027 rests on a single secondary source, and the status of its gallium and germanium ban suspension (due to lapse on 27 November 2026 according to background knowledge) was not checked.

**The notes disagree in a few places, and the report says which figure it used.** One researcher could not find NVIDIA's 15x optical-engine claim; two others found it in trade press citing NVIDIA (used). One could not verify Micron's annual HBM cadence beyond 2027; another found secondary coverage of its October webinar (used, flagged as secondary). Amphenol's CommScope purchase closed on 9 January 2026 per its 10-Q, although its press release is dated 12 January. The notes give different updated probabilities for the 600 kW rack slipping to 2028 (~70-75% from the power researcher, ~75-85% from the cooling researcher) and for 1 MW racks by 2030 (35-45% against ~50%), so the report gives ranges. JCET's aggregator growth (+3.8%) conflicts with its reported Q3 2026 growth (+13.5% year on year); the basis was not resolved.

## Conclusion: own the input the giants have to buy

The evidence moves the question from sub-segments to inputs. The companies that earn the highest margins and the most customer money in 2026 are not those in the fastest-growing segment but those that own an input every architecture still needs: TSMC's CoWoS back end, DRAM cleanrooms, ABF film and T-glass cloth, indium-phosphide lasers and substrates, thinning and dicing, and test. The gross-margin ladder in optics shows it plainly, from 56% at the InP substrate maker AXT and 53% at Lumentum down to 12% at the contract assembler Fabrinet (quarters to September 2026), as does test, where KYEC earns a ~27% operating margin against 5-8% at Chinese assembly houses (aggregator, to Sep 2026). The integrated giants are not routing around these owners; they are paying them in advance (NVIDIA's US$2bn stakes in Lumentum and Coherent, and capacity reservations at Ibiden, Unimicron, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, KYEC and Amkor) or buying them (Marvell-Lumentum, Applied's 20% of Besi, Eaton-Boyd, Ecolab-CoolIT, and Credo's purchase of DustPhotonics, completed on 28 May 2026 for about US$1.25bn including a US$310m earn-out). The pure successor bets without a chokepoint (Absolics in glass, Accelsius in two-phase cooling) carry the weakest balance sheets in the field, which is the practical meaning of "successor dates slip".

The prices tell a different story from the operating data. Most of these shares fell 40-65% from their mid-2026 highs while Q3 orders still accelerated, and almost every price now assumes the bridge lasts while earnings normalise, the reverse of a bet on successors arriving on schedule. That makes the next eight weeks unusually informative: NVIDIA's results on 17 November and the outcome of its governance crisis, Broadcom on 9 December, Micron around 16 December (the direct test of whether memory stays supply-limited), the 10 January 2027 expiry of both the US Affiliates Rule suspension and China's reported rare-earth pause, and the hyperscalers' 2027 capex guidance in late January. A company whose quality rank depends on owning a chokepoint should be judged by whether customers keep pre-paying for it through those events, not by its share price.

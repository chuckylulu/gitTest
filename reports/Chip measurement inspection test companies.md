# Wafer judges keep their power as chips stack

**Research date: 12 November 2026.** Prices are closes of 11 November (US, Europe) or 12 November (Asia). As of that date, the three highest-quality companies in chip measurement, inspection and test are **KLA, Advantest and Nova**, in that order, with **Onto Innovation a close fourth**. All four own a measurement or test that chipmakers use to release product, and each has a funded path through the coming transitions that is already partly shipped. KLA is in a class of its own: an outside tally gives it **73.8% of 2025 metrology and inspection spending against 9.8% for the next supplier** ([Information Network via 24/7 Wall St][X1]), and it earns a 61% gross margin with a contracted service annuity. Advantest owns the electrical test that decides ship-or-scrap for most AI processors; Nova sells measurement by inference (optical signal plus physical models plus machine learning) as its core product. On the open question the evidence points one way: **the chipmakers own the reference data and are keeping it.** They generate the TEM cross-sections, build their models in-house (TSMC on NVIDIA tools, SK hynix with Gauss Labs, a company it funds and supplies with data) ([NVIDIA/TSMC summary][NV1]; [SK hynix][G1]), and no tool maker was found holding a pooled reference dataset or a data-licensing deal; in the industry's own words, "it's tough to get device and process data out" ([Semiconductor Engineering, 13 Jan 2026][SE-DS]). Tool makers keep value where their physics is the anchor that models are calibrated against (X-ray, e-beam, AFM, TEM) or where models must run inside their tools; data revenue reaches vendors only as tolls (PDF Solutions) or inside tool prices. Entry is a separate judgment. After a 40-60% fall from late-June highs, with no order or estimate cuts, most names are **fair**; ASML (as a lithography holding), Onto, Rigaku and FormFactor look **attractive**; Applied Materials, Thermo Fisher and Technoprobe look **stretched**; and the top-quality pair, KLA and Advantest, offers no discount for its quality. The two dates that matter most are **27 November 2026**, when China's suspension of its gallium and germanium export ban lapses, and **10 January 2027**, when the US suspension of the Entity List "affiliates" rule ends unless extended again.

## 1. Field map: the judges sit in five countries, the challengers everywhere else

The field divides into nine sub-segments, and concentration is extreme at the top of most of them. KLA spans nearly all of front-end inspection, metrology and overlay and says it is now the largest supplier of packaging process control (co. claim), at about **$1.1 billion in 2026, up more than 70%** ([KLA shareholder letter, 28 Oct 2026][K1]). Specialists own the niches KLA does not dominate: Hitachi High-Tech in CD-SEM, Lasertec in actinic EUV mask inspection, Rigaku in X-ray metrology (No. 1 with nearly 40% of X-ray metrology in 2025, a Yole figure reproduced by Rigaku) ([Rigaku 2Q presentation][R2]), Nova and Onto in optical critical-dimension (OCD) and materials metrology. Electrical test is a duopoly: Advantest estimates its own share of the tester market at **65% in 2025** ([Advantest FY2025 slides][V1]). Probe cards are a three-way race in a **$3.19 billion market (2025, TechInsights)**, where matching unlabelled chart slices to filed revenue puts Technoprobe near 22%, FormFactor near 20% and Micronics Japan at 14% overall and **41% of memory cards** ([Micronics Japan presentation, 13 Aug 2026][J1]). In China, the domestic suppliers together held roughly 13% of the local metrology and inspection market in 2025 against **58% for KLA alone**, on a table whose original source is not named ([pedaily, 23 Jul 2026][CN4]).

Coverage was deepest where primary filings could be read in full and thinnest where sources are not in English or companies are private. **North America** rests on SEC filings and is deep. **Japan** is deep for Advantest, Lasertec, Hitachi, Rigaku, JEOL and Micronics Japan (Japanese-language results summaries and decks were read), but thin for NuFlare, Toray's TASMIT unit, Nikon, Horiba and Hamamatsu, and Canon, Ushio, Takano, Topcon, Otsuka, Shimadzu's metrology side and the niche testers TESEC and Shibasoku were not researched. **Israel** is deep for Nova and Camtek through their SEC filings, but Hebrew-language press and TASE filings were not read. **Europe** is deep for ASML and Technoprobe and thin for ZEISS (whose semiconductor metrology revenue is not disclosed), Nearfield, Comet, PVA TePla and Excillum. **South Korea** is deep only for Park Systems (its half-year report); Nextin, Auros, Koh Young, Intekplus, SEC and the memory-tester makers rest on Korean press summaries, not DART filings, and SEMES, ATI, NanoScope and Oros were not researched. **Taiwan** rests on exchange revenue and valuation feeds plus Chinese-language press summaries; V5 Technologies' reported TSMC qualification is media-only and Cheng Mei Instrument was not found in listed data. **China** rests on the companies' own CNINFO filings read in Chinese, which are detailed, but every node and tool-of-record claim is the company's own, the private firms (SiCarrier, Dongfang Jingyuan, RSIC, Yuwei, Angstrom) are thinly documented, and US investment-restriction status (NS-CMIC and 1260H lists) was not checked. **India** and **Southeast Asia** are thin: India has no indigenous supplier of global significance (the only metrology effort found, Makr Microsystems, raised a Rs 10.2 crore seed round in September 2026) ([Electronics For You][IN1]), and the Malaysian names (ViTrox, Pentamaster, MI Technovation) serve board-level and back-end inspection rather than wafer metrology.

The map carries a finding of its own. Every company that owns a global wafer-release judge is headquartered in the US, Japan, the Netherlands, Israel or Italy. No Korean, Taiwanese, Chinese or Indian company was found in that position. The nearest are Park Systems, the AFM leader in a nanometre-topography niche that matters for hybrid-bond copper pads; the Taiwanese analysis labs, which hold the outsourced share of TEM reference work and grew revenue 17-27% in January-October 2026 (MA-tek, MSScorps; iST fell 6%) ([TWSE][TW1] and [TPEx][TW2] monthly revenue); and China's Skyverse and Jingce, whose growth is policy-led substitution behind US export controls that have put most of China's front-end metrology makers on the Entity List since December 2024 ([Federal Register 2024-28267][CN2]).

## 3. Paths forward: who has shipped for the 3D transition and who has only said so

The six transitions are numbered as in the brief: T1 imaging to inference with a stated uncertainty; T2 hand-built to AI-built recipes; T3 front-end wafers to bonded stacks and packaging; T4 hardware to software and data; T5 AI on the chipmaker's premises; T6 a possible fall in tools per wafer after 2030. No company in the field publishes a measurement with a stated uncertainty, so T1 is judged on movement toward inference.

**1. KLA — coherent.** KLA faces all six transitions and has acted on four. On bonded stacks (T3) its record is the strongest: packaging process-control revenue rose from about $635 million in 2025 to a guided ~$1.1 billion in 2026 ([KLA letter, 28 Oct 2026][K1]), and management says four next-generation hybrid-bonding systems are "already developed" (co. claim) ([Investor Day, 12 Mar 2026][K3]). On-premises AI (T5) is already its architecture: one bright-field tool captures "78 trillion pixels of images in an hour", data that must be processed on the tool. Services (T4) are 23% of revenue, with over 80% on multi-year contracts and median tool lives above 24 years ([Investor Day, services][K5]). Two weaknesses stand out. KLA is openly sceptical of inference replacing sensors: "Hybrid metrology 10 years ago was going to just take over everything but nothing. It doesn't work" ([Investor Day Q&A][K4]). And its 1,500 applications engineers, bundled into tool prices, are exactly the work AI-built recipes (T2) would remove; no evidence shows AI shrinking that work yet. Its 10-K names lower sampling rates and customer insourcing as risks (T6) ([10-K][K2]). The plan is funded (free cash flow $3.77 billion in FY2026). What must go right: KLA's argument that only its "pure signal" makes AI work must keep holding as chipmakers build their own models. If inference plus reference data proves good enough, its scepticism becomes a liability.

**2. Advantest — coherent.** Its transitions are T1, T2, T3 and T4, and it has shipped on each of the first three. NVIDIA "selected ACS RTDI for high-volume production" for Blackwell and later parts, moving test "from validation to prediction" (Advantest's announcement) ([Advantest, 6 Oct 2025][V5]). The AllianceATE purchase produced Velocity, which streams design files into "first-time-right" test patterns instead of "weeks of developing custom code" ([Advantest, 18 May 2026][V6]). For stacked dies it is co-developing a die-level prober with Tokyo Seimitsu ([Advantest, 16 Dec 2025][V8]) and holds minority stakes in Technoprobe and FormFactor, with a partnership and planned stake in Micronics Japan. The plan is funded many times over: first-half FY2026 operating profit was JPY 456.8 billion ([H1 results, 28 Oct 2026][V4]). Two things weaken it. Services stay below 10% of sales, so T4 ("a comprehensive solutions provider") is said more than done. And it sold its entire PDF Solutions stake in May 2026, stepping back from owning analytics. What must go right: the capacity it is building, above 5,000 testers a year now, toward 10,000 "well ahead" of plan, with 20,000 under discussion ([Q&A, 28 Oct 2026][V3]), must not meet a 2028 pause; and test content per device must keep rising faster than inference removes insertions.

**3. Nova — coherent.** Nova's core product already is T1: optical, X-ray and Raman signals inverted through physical models (MARS) and machine learning (FIT). Research co-authored with IBM shows it working at the leading edge. A 2026 SPIE paper used "modeless" machine learning with physics-based spectral filtering to measure copper recess on bonded-wafer structures against AFM references ([IBM/Nova, SPIE 13981-16][S9]). A 2025 paper correlated spectra directly to TEM-validated reference metrology in a nanosheet flow without an optical model ([IBM Research, 2025][S10]). On T3, the $60.2 million Sentronics purchase became the WMC packaging line, now at more than ten customers and chosen as tool of record at a leading foundry (co. claim) ([Nova, 2 Jul 2026][N3]; [Nova call][N4]). T4 is weaker: software is sold with tools and licence revenue is recognised at a point in time, so software is not separately priced ([20-F][N2]). The plan is funded (net cash about $1.05 billion). What must go right: Nova must keep access to customers' reference data for training, which its filings do not explain, and hold its gate-all-around share as new XPS competitors arrive in the second half of 2027 (company expectation).

**4. Onto Innovation — coherent, with execution risk.** Onto has committed more capital to T1 and T3 than any peer of its size. It paid $719.5 million for 27% of Rigaku, funded by $1.5 billion of zero-coupon convertibles, to pair optical-CD speed with X-ray accuracy in a joint "hybrid engine" aimed at memory lines in early 2027 ([Onto 8-K][R4]; [Rigaku 3Q presentation][R1]). For bonded stacks it sells Dragonfly G5 for hybrid-bond and sub-surface defects, 3Di bump metrology and picosecond-ultrasonic inspection. SK hynix gave it a technology award for detecting hidden HBM defects ([BriefGlance summary of the Onto release][O4]), and Samsung is reported to be validating its laser-ultrasound tool on hybrid-bond lines, which Samsung has not confirmed ([Cloud News, from TheElec][O5]). It claims a G5 win that displaced an HBM maker's established tool of record ([Onto, 16 Mar 2026][O6]). T2, T4 and T5 are thin: machine learning "reduces recipe setup time" (co. claim), and software is not reported separately. What must go right: the 2025 setback, when systems revenue fell on "fewer inspection units shipped for advanced packaging for AI chips" ([10-K][O2]), must not recur; the Rigaku stake, marked down $40.8 million in Q3, must earn its cost; and the guided packaging growth of more than 35% in 2027 must arrive ([Onto Q3 call][O7]).

**5. ASML (metrology and inspection) — partly coherent.** The unit faces T1, T3 and T6. Its strength is structural: YieldStar overlay and CD metrology sit inside the lithography control loop, the YieldStar 550B shipped in Q3 2026 for 14-angstrom logic and DRAM (company statement in press reports) ([eeNews Europe][M5]), and multibeam e-beam inspection is on its R&D roadmap ([ASML annual report][M2]). ASML says it will "extend holistic lithography into supporting 3D front end integration", but its packaging business is lithography (the XT:260), not measurement, and it let its soft X-ray scatterometry effort go: Invisix was spun out of ASML R&D, and ASML kept only scanner-feedback metrology ([Bits&Chips, 23 Apr 2026][M3]). Its AI move is an 11% stake in Mistral AI for EUR 1.3 billion, not a measurement product. The unit is funded without strain by a parent with a EUR 46.5 billion backlog. What must go right: overlay control must stay tied to the scanner as bonded wafers distort, and multibeam inspection must win customers (adoption could not be verified). The verdict is partly coherent because the unit has no visible answer to buried interfaces.

**6. Lasertec — partly coherent.** Of the six transitions, only T2, T4 and T6 apply to mask inspection, and on those Lasertec has shown nothing beyond rising service revenue (25% of FY2026 sales, +36.5%) ([Lasertec business report][L1]). Its path is instead a hardware ladder inside a physical constraint: ACTIS A150, then the A200HiT at three times the speed, then the A300 for high-NA masks, with A300 orders expected from logic mask shops in the second half of 2026 and from DRAM mask shops in the first half of 2027 (analyst account of company guidance) ([Rakuten Securities, 17 Aug 2026][L2]). On 30 October it reportedly raised its FY2027 order guidance to JPY 400-500 billion on repeat A200HiT orders "for mass production" ([Investing.com][L3]). That cuts against the 2024 short-seller claim of defective tools. A special committee found no accounting fraud, and an outside research firm judged fraud "not proven" while sharing concerns about inventory and falling advances ([GMT Research][L5]). The plan is funded (no debt, ROIC about 68%). What must go right: EUV layer counts and high-NA adoption must rise; no rival (KLA, or NuFlare's 199 nm DUV tool) may qualify a non-actinic alternative; and revenue must catch up with the shipments awaiting acceptance.

**7. Teradyne — partly coherent.** Teradyne has the clearest plan in the field for T3. Its engineers set out the new insertions for chiplets, from wafer through chip-on-wafer modules to final assembly, and note that electrical access at chip-on-wafer is "typically limited to C4 bump interfaces" ([Teradyne blog, 27 Jul 2026][T7]). It has shipped for that world: Magnum 7H for HBM from base-die wafer test to burn-in, a known-good-die cell with Tokyo Electron, UCIe scan test (behind its 2025 TSMC 3DFabric award) ([Teradyne, Sep 2025][T6]) and Titan HP burn-in. It holds 10% of Technoprobe and on 6 October 2026 agreed a $300 million stake in FormFactor ([FormFactor release][F4]). On T2 it bought TestInsight, which automates test-pattern conversion for every tester brand ([Teradyne, 16 Apr 2026][T5]). It has no analytics or inference product (T1), nothing on customers' premises (T5), and services are 11% of revenue. What must go right: the dual-vendor compute wins it describes, with capacity buys due in mid and late 2027, must be confirmed by customers ([prepared remarks, 28 Oct 2026][T3]); and its HBM share must hold as Korean suppliers take wafer-test and burn-in insertions.

**8. Rigaku — coherent, but thinly financed.** X-ray scattering and diffraction are model-based inverse measurements, so Rigaku is natively on T1, and it sells into the role the thesis values: the accurate anchor for fast optical tools. Its high-resolution diffraction tool is process of record at "a majority of customers" for nanosheet superlattices, and transmission small-angle scattering (CD-SAXS) has begun adoption on a memory mass-production line (both co. claims) ([Rigaku 3Q presentation][R1]). For T3, X-ray inspection of through-silicon-via fill, voids and bond integrity ships as evaluation units in 4Q 2026, aimed at a $300 million 2030 market in which Rigaku claims to be the only tool able to inspect voids in fine HBM vias (co. claim) ([Integrated Report][R3]). Onto's 27% stake supplies a channel into fabs. The weak points: logic use needs X-ray sources at least 100 times brighter than today's ([IRDS 2024][S13]); recurring revenue is partly labour, through service contracts with dedicated engineers; and free cash flow was about JPY 2.8 billion in FY2025 against net debt of about twice EBITDA (September 2026). What must go right: memory CD-SAXS and the hybrid engine must convert into repeat orders, and the academic instrument business must stop diluting margins.

**9=. Applied Materials (process diagnostics and control) — partly coherent.** The unit faces T1 and T3 most directly and has acted on both through e-beam. Cold-field-emission e-beam revenue was guided to "more than $1.0 billion in calendar 2026" ([Futurum, Feb 2026][A5]), the unit to grow more than 50% in 2026 ([Newsquawk, 13 Aug 2026][A4]), and VeritySEM 7AP and SEMVision G7AP target thick, warped HBM and chiplet substrates ([Futurum, Aug 2026][A2]). The parent is raising its stake in Besi, the hybrid-bonder maker, to about 20% ([AMAT 8-K, 26 Oct 2026][A3]), and the X-ray start-up Sigray's website now redirects to Applied's (deal terms not verified). Nothing specific to the unit was found on AI-built recipes, software revenue, on-premises AI or tools per wafer, and its outside share fell from 11.1% to 9.8% in 2025 ([Information Network via 24/7 Wall St][X1]). What must go right: e-beam must keep taking inspection steps from optical tools, and Applied must narrow the optical gap with KLA.

**9=. Hitachi High-Tech — partly coherent.** Its main transitions are T1, T3 and T4, and "said" far exceeds "done". Management says the business has "completely shifted from simple 'product delivery' to 'advanced solutions and services' centered on data" and plans acquisitions in measurement ([Investor Day Q&A, 10 Jun 2026][H2]), but no software or data revenue is disclosed, and its Recipe AI (claimed to halve development time) targets process-tool recipes, not metrology recipes ([Hitachi CI strategy][H1]). Done: an in-equipment AI chip that replaced multi-image overlay metrology with one processed image in a test (co. claim), and a multibeam inspection prototype with ZEISS that found small defects optical tools miss and now seeks process-of-record status on production wafers ([Hitachi, 30 Sep 2026][H4]). No hybrid-bond, HBM or packaging metrology product was found. Hitachi funds the plan easily; measurement-segment orders rose 77% in the September quarter ([Hitachi Q2 FY2026][H3]). What must go right: CD-SEM must stay the in-line standard as AFM, X-ray and optical inference compete for CD measurement, and the ZEISS programme must reach production.

**11. Thermo Fisher (semiconductor electron microscopy) — coherent for its role.** Thermo sells the destructive reference step, so the brief's open question is its business question, and its answer is automation and placement in the fab rather than data ownership. It sells Metrios TEMs, Helios FIB-SEMs and the Vulcan automated lab, which combines robotic handling, AI imaging and "real-time lab-to-fab data exchange" and is pitched at lab labour shortages ([R&D World, 19 Mar 2025][E4]), plus the Helios MX1 plasma FIB, placed in the fab for "logic, memory, and advanced packaging" ([BusinessWire, 28 Oct 2025][E5]). It calls TEM "the primary source for reference data" and says manufacturers generate "tens of thousands of TEM datasets monthly" (co. claims) ([Thermo Fisher blog][E3]). This fits T5, since tools and their AI sit on the customer's premises, and turns T6 into an opportunity: if inference replaces in-line measurement, demand for reference cross-sections rises. It has no inference product and no data revenue. What must go right: semiconductor demand for electron microscopy must keep rising, and because the unit's size is undisclosed, investors cannot see whether it does.

**12=. Technoprobe — partly coherent.** Its transition is T3. It has entered HBM with vertical MEMS cards "up to full-wafer coverage" and added electro-optical probing for co-packaged optics ([9M 2026 presentation][P2]); its own HBM test flow uses sacrificial pads to test partial stacks on reconstituted wafers and marks that step with a question mark ([company presentation, May 2026][P3]). Capacity is to reach a EUR 2.0 billion run-rate by end-2027, self-funded. It has no software, data or inference activity. The execution flags are recent: Q4 guidance fell to EUR 270 million (-16% quarter on quarter) after an ERP and manufacturing-systems go-live, the top of the FY2026 range was cut ([9M release, 11 Nov 2026][P1]), and the CTO resigned on 4 November ([Technoprobe][P4]). What must go right: probe-card value must survive the move to bonded faces that cannot be probed, which favours sacrificial-pad and full-wafer cards, and the operating problems must clear by early 2027.

**12=. FormFactor — partly coherent.** Also T3-only. Done: HBM cards for 8-, 12- and 16-high stacks, a 40 µm-pitch MEMS card with Intel (20 µm in development), optical probing for silicon photonics through the Keystone purchase, and a record systems quarter on co-packaged optics ([Q3 2026 release][F1]; [10-K][F3]). Said: collaboration with Teradyne on HBM, packaging and photonics ([FormFactor release][F4]). Its 10-K does not mention hybrid bonding, and the physics is against probing: imec reports the "absence of any realistic roadmap for probing dense hybrid-bond arrays below ~25 µm pitch" ([EE Times, 26 Jan 2026][S3]), and the IEEE roadmap says 40 µm probing has been shown "only single-site" ([IEEE HIR, Oct 2026][S4]). What must go right: known-good-die and stacked-die wafer sort must add insertions faster than on-chip test circuits remove them, and dependence on SK hynix (21% of Q3 revenue) must fall.

**14. Camtek — partly coherent.** Camtek's tools image bumps and redistribution layers directly; they do not infer buried features (T1), and its hybrid-bonding activity is "low", meaningful only from 2028 (company, Nov 2026) ([Camtek call][C3]). On T2 and T4 it bought Visual Layer, a visual-AI start-up, in April 2026 and plans software sales from Q1 2027, with about $40 million of software in its long-term model ([20-F][C2]). Its claim to be "tool of record for all the 3D metrology for HBM4 at all the HBM manufacturers" ([Camtek Q3 2025 call][C4]) conflicts with Onto's claim to have displaced an incumbent at an HBM maker; neither is confirmed. What must go right: packaging inspection must keep growing (orders above $800 million so far in 2026, about 80% packaging), China (49% of 2025 revenue) must stay open, and Camtek must reach buried-interface inspection before 2028.

**15. PDF Solutions — partly coherent.** PDF is the only company built for T4, and it earns from data flow rather than reference data: secureWISE charges per connected tool and per data transferred, Cimetrix per tool shipped, and Gainshare royalties on yield ([10-K][D3]). On T2 it launched Exensio Aurora, whose LLM agents assemble analytics workflows, in beta for "a small number of early adopters"; the CEO calls feedback "mostly demo-related" ([Aurora release][D5]; [Q3 call][D4]). It offers on-premises deployment (T5) and runs about 12 eProbe e-beam tools that find buried electrical defects (T1); per-tool fees expose it to T6. The plan strains the balance sheet: eProbe hardware-as-a-service pushed capex to about 21% of revenue and trailing free cash flow to roughly zero ([Q3 management report][D8]). What must go right: agents must convert from demos to priced products, the eight- and nine-figure renewals expected around the turn of the year must land, and the two customers that made 46% of Q3 revenue must stay.

## 4. Entry point: a de-rating without estimate cuts leaves most names fair

This section is kept apart from the ranking. The equipment cycle is late but still rising and is limited by supply, not demand. Wafer-fab equipment spending set a record of **$116.9 billion in 2025** and is forecast at $143.9 billion for 2026 by SEMI (July) and in the "mid-$150 billion" range by KLA and Lam (October); KLA and Lam see more than $200 billion in 2027, against about $175 billion on SEMI's path, and SEMI's growth slows to 14% in 2028 ([SEMI via I-Connect007, 15 Jul 2026][Y1]; [KLA letter][K1]). Test equipment is forecast at **$15.3 billion in 2026 (+31%) after +55% in 2025**. History is the warning: in 2019 test equipment fell 11% and assembly and packaging equipment 27%, and in 2023 they fell 17% and 30% while wafer-processing equipment grew 1% ([SEMI 2019][Y7]; [SEMI 2023 via The Star][Y8]). Test and packaging names are therefore the most exposed to a 2028 financing-led pause, and the signs of that risk sit in how AI capex is funded rather than in equipment orders: Alphabet sold $12.4 billion of stock and had negative third-quarter free cash flow, Meta launched an equity raise, and Amazon's trailing free cash flow was -$42.4 billion ([Universal Asset Owners, 1 Nov 2026][Y5]).

Share prices have already moved. From late-June highs to the low of 5 November, KLA fell 57%, Applied Materials 56%, Onto 59%, Teradyne 56% and FormFactor 55%, while ASML fell about a third over the same period and Advantest about a third from its 6 October peak to 6 November. The causes were a de-rating from peak multiples (Applied's forward P/E went from 49x on 30 June to 21x on 2 November), a Federal Reserve that raised rates in September and held them on 28 October, when the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.03% ([stockanalysis, 28 Oct 2026][Q-FED]), stress in AI-infrastructure credit in mid-October, Lam's supply warning on 21-22 October, and a political shock after the 3 November midterms that took the Nasdaq down 5.5% on 5 November ([TIKR, 5 Nov 2026][Q-TIKR]; [GPB/NPR, 5 Nov 2026][Q-GPB]). **No company reported order cuts**, and analysts cut price targets rather than earnings estimates. Forward P/Es now cluster at 22-27x: the peer medians are 24.6x for front-end process control, 21.6x for packaging inspection, 23.1x for testers, 23.7x for probe cards and 38.1x for the Chinese domestic suppliers, against about 16x for the semiconductor ETF SOXX ([stockanalysis SOXX][Q-SOXX]) and 17.3x for the S&P 500 technology sector, below its 25-year average of 20.0 ([FactSet, 6 Nov 2026][Q-FS]). At the 2022 trough the large equipment names traded at 12-13x. Prices therefore discount a higher discount rate and some slowdown after 2027, but not a 2018-scale downturn; that is an interpretation, since no sell-side source states what prices assume.

## Conclusion

The thesis that measurement is the judge survives, but the judge is moving. In bonded stacks, the decisive judgments are becoming electrical (daisy chains, electron-beam absorbed current, known-good-die test before stacking) and pre-bond (surface and copper-recess metrology), not images of the buried interface. That favours companies spanning test and pre-bond metrology (KLA, Advantest, Nova, Onto with Rigaku) over pure packaging imagers and contact-hardware makers, whose probes physically cannot reach a 6 µm bond face. Because chipmakers keep the reference data, a tool maker's best defence is to be the anchor that models are calibrated against (X-ray, e-beam, AFM, TEM) and to run the models inside its own tools on the customer's premises; the weakest positions are those built on labour, from bundled applications engineers to analysis sold by the case. The evidence also inverts one prior's timing: AI raises demand for reference measurements before it lowers demand for in-line ones, and nothing yet supports fewer tools per wafer after 2030.

On entry, the market has already halved most of these share prices without a single estimate cut, so the question is no longer valuation in a boom but resilience in a pause. The best businesses, KLA and Advantest, are priced for continuity, while the combinations of good quality with an attractive entry are Onto, which carries the highest packaging beta, and ASML, as a lithography holding rather than a measurement one. The next decisive evidence arrives in a short window: 27 November and 10 January for export rules, then TSMC's first 2027 capital-spending guide in mid-January and the hyperscalers' 2027 guides in late January and February, which will show whether the funding strain visible in 2026 starts to reach equipment orders.

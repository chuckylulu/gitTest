# Certified judges, not faster solvers, rule simulation

**Synopsys and Cadence share first place on quality and cannot be separated on the evidence; Siemens is third.** The three are the only vendors on TSMC's certification tables for its A14 and A16 processes, which makes their solvers the accepted judges of a chip design before it is manufactured, and all three have funded, partly shipped plans for agents, GPUs and new pricing ([TSMC EDA Alliance, tables as of 1 Oct 2026](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/oip/eda_alliance); [Futurum, 24 Sep 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/tsmcs-oip-forum-turns-agentic-chip-design-demos-into-production-flows/)). Synopsys owns more sign-off judges: its PrimeTime tool holds **90%+ of timing sign-off**, and Ansys adds power-integrity, electromagnetic and regulator-named engines. Cadence owns the analog-design and hardware-emulation judges and has the stronger finances, with a return on invested capital of about 29% against 9% at Synopsys on adjusted profit, or 16% against 2% on reported profit, after the Ansys purchase (researchers' calculations) ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)). Siemens's place rests on Calibre, which holds **85%+ of physical verification** and is required for tape-out at TSMC, Samsung and Intel; its general simulation tools are respected but carry little documented sign-off authority. On the open question, the boundary of trust sits in the same place in every industry: **physics-based simulation validated against physical tests counts as primary evidence wherever a regulator or customer has written a credibility route**. That route is furthest forward in chip sign-off and nuclear accident analysis and furthest back in vehicle type approval. Learned models have crossed the boundary only in medicine, as cleared devices and as human-verified trial tools, and in operational weather forecasting, where they stay anchored to physics-based analyses. No aviation or nuclear regulator accepts them as evidence, and a study for the US nuclear regulator found them "practically not allowed" ([NRC/SwRI, Oct 2024](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2429/ML24290A059.pdf)). The bodies placed to move the boundary are the FDA and EMA, which already qualify models for a defined context of use; EASA, whose June 2026 draft sets objectives for surrogates that fully replace high-fidelity models, with policy due by 2028 ([EASA Concept Paper Issue 03, 3 Jun 2026](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/143701/en)); and, in chips, the foundries, which have no validation regime yet for the output of AI agents. Price is judged separately (section 4): as of 11-12 November 2026 the strongest franchises look fair (Synopsys, Siemens) to stretched (Cadence), while the cheapest design-software stocks are the seat-heavy ones the background expects to lose pricing power.

## 1. Field map: three certified vendors sit atop a fragmented field

The field divides into six sub-segments, and they differ most in how firmly a third party has named the tools it trusts. Chip-design sign-off is the most concentrated and the most trusted: the three largest vendors hold **more than 85% of electronic design automation (EDA)**, and no other vendor holds more than 5% of any core category ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)). General engineering simulation is about as large, at **US$11.67bn in 2025** (CIMdata, +8.5%) or US$11.6bn (Cambashi), but less certified and more contestable; the five largest vendors hold 50-55% ([CIMdata](https://www.cimdata.com/en/news/item/30365-cimdata-publishes-simulation-and-analysis-market-report); [Digital Engineering 24/7, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/gpu-acceleration-expands); [MarketsandMarkets summary](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/ResearchInsight/computer-aided-engineering-market.asp)). Materials and weather each have a handful of de facto judges surrounded by fast-improving open models, while the AI-native entrants and simulation clouds mostly sell learned models, delivery work or compute brokerage on top of other people's solvers.

### Regions covered, and where coverage is thin

North America and Europe are covered from filings, regulator documents and company releases, and that coverage is deep. **Japan** is well covered for listed firms and national computing, but thin for private solver owners (JMAG, Prometech and Matlantis disclose no revenue) and for market shares, which sit in paid Yano reports. **China** is covered from annual reports, exchange data and US government lists, but private firms (IBE, PERA, Tongyuan, Dongfang Jingyuan, DP Technology) and defence customers are thin. **South Korea** is thin: the national filing system (DART) could not be reached, so MIDAS IT's figures come from an aggregator. **Taiwan** is thin: its one solver owner with international reach, CoreTech, is private and undisclosed, and no start-up scan was possible. **India** is well covered for listed engineering-services firms but thin for product start-ups (one site was down) and government codes. **Israel** is thin because no Hebrew-language sources were searched. ### How the order was set, and where it cannot be forced

**Synopsys and Cadence cannot be separated.** Synopsys wins on position: it owns more judges across chips and regulated systems, from timing sign-off to an FDA-qualified tool and TÜV-certified code generation. Cadence wins on financial strength and momentum: its revenue grew 14% in FY2025 (reported, including small acquisitions) against roughly 3% organic growth at Synopsys excluding Ansys, and its return on capital and leverage are far better ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)). Their revenue models and resilience are similar, so the two offsets cancel. **Siemens is third** because Calibre is as strong a judge as either rival's best, but it covers one category; its simulation half lacks documented sign-off authority, and software is only about 8.6% of a conglomerate's revenue (researchers' calculation).

**ASML and NVIDIA own constraints, not software judges**, which is why they sit below the three despite stronger finances on several measures. Both earn mostly one-off hardware revenue and are the most exposed to a 2028 financing pause. ASML ranks above NVIDIA because lithography is a constraint the background names as irremovable, whereas NVIDIA's position in simulation rests on a platform whose retreat from native double precision weakens it for sign-off solvers, and whose revenue is concentrated (two customers made up 36% of FY2026 revenue). The unresolved press reports of 12 November 2026 about NVIDIA's board and chief executive bear on governance and entry risk (section 4), and nothing in the evidence links them to its position in simulation, so they do not move the ranking.

**Ranks 6-8 are close, and their order is low-confidence.** Dassault has the broadest solver portfolio and the best finances of the three, but its path is only partly coherent; MathWorks holds the best-documented certification role but discloses little; Bentley has the most advanced usage pricing, but its meter tracks engineer time. **Ranks 9 and 10 are also close**: Schrödinger has the stronger outside evidence and a coherent path but weak finances, while Verisk is the reverse. **Empyrean and PhysicsX tie at 14**. No company ranked in the top group has a path verdict below "coherent".

### Left out of the ranking, and why

Several important names now sit inside ranked companies: Ansys (Synopsys, since 17 Jul 2025), Altair (Siemens, since 26 Mar 2025), and MSC Software and Software Cradle (Cadence, since 23 Feb 2026). Others are exiting or changing hands. Hexagon sold its CAE business and is buying back in only at the scale of Rocscience, a geotechnical tool firm with about US$50m of revenue ([Hexagon via Inderes, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.inderes.se/en/releases/hexagon-to-acquire-rocscience-closing-the-loop-between-real-world-monitoring-and-advanced-simulation)). PTC is being bought by Schneider Electric, and Octave sells asset-lifecycle software with only a niche code-check analysis line. A second group are gap-fillers in this framework. Autodesk's simulation is an undisclosed slice of a US$1.38bn manufacturing segment. **COMSOL** is the borderline case: it is unusually profitable, with EUR159.5m of revenue and EUR91.9m of pre-tax profit in 2024 on aggregator data, but no sign-off role was found, and its moat, coupling physics through a graphical interface, is the one agents attack most directly ([largestcompanies.com](https://www.largestcompanies.com/company/Comsol-AB-6187/closing-figures-and-key-ratios)). The gap-fillers also include Nemetschek, the simulation clouds (Rescale, SimScale), Neural Concept (the most widely deployed surrogate layer, but no solver) and Japan's Matlantis, a paid learned potential whose edge open models have matched.

Some real judges are too small or too opaque to rank: VASP and Thermo-Calc (reference codes and databases in materials), Studsvik (reactor codes under NRC review for small modular reactors), DNV (it both certifies and sells analysis software), and the grid tools PSCAD and DIgSILENT, whose owners' financials were not found. Silvaco, the listed TCAD specialist, had 2025 revenue of US$63.1m and a net loss of US$41.2m ([Silvaco 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1943289/000162828026017378/svco-20251231.htm)). In chip manufacturing, Lam, Applied Materials and KLA use simulation to sell equipment rather than as a business, and Camtek's inspection relies less on physical models than Nova's metrology. In weather, Google DeepMind leads on independently verified forecast skill, but simulation is immaterial to Alphabet; Weathernews is a sound business, 96% recurring with net cash, but owns no judge; Vaisala's weather software is small (Xweather ARR €60.9m at a 9.4% EBITA margin); and Spire burns cash.

The regional omissions follow from the evidence rather than from a US default. **Japan's** strongest solver owners, JMAG (inside JSOL, owned by NTT DATA and SMFG) and Matlantis, are private and show no regulator-accepted judge or outside share data; listed Zuken is mainly a board-design vendor. **Korea's** MIDAS IT owns real civil and mechanical solvers, with 2025 revenue of KRW68.9bn at a ~33% operating margin on aggregator data, but no GPU, agent or pricing move was found ([JobKorea/NICE](https://m.jobkorea.co.kr/company/1776019?tabType=I)). **Taiwan's** CoreTech (Moldex3D) owns a moulding and package-warpage solver but is private and absent from foundry certification lists. **India's** scale is engineering labour: KPIT, Tata Elxsi, LTTS, Tata Technologies and Cyient sell the hours the background expects agents to compress, and none discloses simulation revenue. **Israel** beyond Nova has private autonomous-vehicle verification and HPC-hardware firms without evidence of regulator acceptance. In **China**, Primarius and Semitronix are smaller than Empyrean. Suochen's simulation software revenue fell 13% to RMB198m in 2025; its customers are "mainly military units and research institutes", and it was added to the US Entity List in September 2025 ([Suochen 2025 annual report](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202604271821616055_1.pdf); [eCFR Part 744](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-VII/subchapter-C/part-744/appendix-Supplement%20No.%204%20to%20Part%20744)).

## 3. Paths forward: every leader has shipped agents, none has proven the new price

The same pattern repeats across the field. Vendors have shipped GPU ports and agent interfaces quickly, they keep conventional solvers as the final check on learned models, and none has yet shown that agent-era pricing earns more than the seats it replaces. The notes below take each ranked company in order.

**Synopsys: coherent.** Its position is documented by outsiders: SemiAnalysis puts PrimeTime above 90% of timing sign-off; China's competition regulator put Ansys at 55-60% of gate-level power integrity and 45-50% of electromagnetic simulation (2023 data, via a translated summary); and the FDA has qualified a medical-device tool built on Ansys HFSS and Mechanical ([IThome summary of the SAMR decision, Jul 2025](https://www.ithome.com/0/867/981.htm); [FDA MDDT list, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-device-development-tools-mddt)). The transitions that matter most are pricing (transition 1) and agent interfaces (3); GPUs (2) are largely done outside its core sign-off judges. Management says it will earn through "a combination of subscription and consumption-based models across tools, agents and platform" ([Synopsys Investor Day, 30 Sep 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-30-Synopsys-Details-Growth-Strategy-and-Long-term-Financial-Model-at-2026-Investor-Day)). What it has done goes further than any rival. GPT-Synopsys, built with OpenAI, adds consumption fees and an outcome-based revenue share paid when a chip's power, performance and area beat the customer's own result, and an Amazon IP agreement worth more than US$1bn adds royalties from about 14 months out ([Futurum, 2 Oct 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/synopsys-investor-day-2026-turns-eda-into-a-royalty-and-ai-model-revenue-share-business/)). It counts **more than 20 GPU-enabled products** ([Synopsys, 21 Oct 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-10-21-Synopsys-at-NVIDIA-GTC-Berlin-Delivering-Engineering-Precision-with-Speed)) and meters GPU use per streaming multiprocessor, so the move to GPUs does not shrink licence revenue ([Ansys licensing help, 2026 R1](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/licensing/HPC_detail.html)); PyAnsys exposes six solvers through agent (MCP) servers ([PyAnsys](https://docs.pyansys.com/)). The plan is funded: R&D was US$2.48bn, 35% of revenue, in FY2025, and NVIDIA bought US$2bn of its stock in December 2025. Its weak links are proof and timing. Futurum notes that outcome pricing "has a thin track record in software" and that the FY2027 guide rests on classic EDA, and organic EDA growth was 8.5% in Q3 FY2026 ([Q3 FY2026 call](https://www.webull.com/news/15497781661541376)). What must go right: agent and consumption revenue per customer must outgrow any lost seats from 2027-28; gross debt must fall below 2x adjusted operating profit by mid-2027 while buybacks compete for cash; and its timing, extraction and physical-verification judges, not yet shown on GPUs, must not let a rival port first.

**Cadence: coherent.** SemiAnalysis credits its Palladium systems with 55-60% of hardware emulation, says its Virtuoso analog platform has "no credible competitor" and finds its Innovus tool gained 10-15 points of share in 2015-20 ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)). Its finances are the strongest among the software judge owners: an adjusted operating margin of 44.6% and free cash flow of 30% of revenue in 2025, net debt of about 0.4x adjusted operating profit and no customer at 10% or more. The risks are China, 13% of 2025 revenue and 16% in Q3 2026, and a 2025 guilty plea over exports that left ongoing compliance duties ([10-K FY2025](https://s206.q4cdn.com/597110084/files/doc_downloads/2026/Mar/CDNS-FY-2025-Form-10-K.pdf); [US Department of Justice, 28 Jul 2025](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/cadence-design-systems-agrees-plead-guilty-and-pay-over-140-million-unlawfully-exporting)). Cadence faces the same pricing and agent transitions as Synopsys plus an open-source one, and has answered each by aiming to be the engine that any agent calls. Management says agents will be priced "like a virtual engineer", that subscriptions "will remain the anchor" and that usage pricing will be added for incremental capacity; 2026 guidance excludes consumption revenue ([TIKR, IR remarks of 9 Jun 2026](https://www.tikr.com/blog/cadence-design-systems-just-unveiled-its-agentic-ai-revenue-model-heres-why-it-could-reset-the-stocks-growth-ceiling); [Q4 2025 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/17/cadence-cdns-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)). Done so far: Coin-based agent licences allocated per user, a seat-like anchor with a meter on top ([Cadence agentic AI terms, Sep 2026](https://www.cadence.com/content/dam/cadence-www/global/en_US/documents/terms-and-conditions/Cadence-agenticaicard-2026Sep.pdf)); ChipStack, bought in November 2025 and now in production at MediaTek; Maestro models in early access from Q4 2026, in which "deterministic solvers" score agent output against ground truth, although an analyst notes its performance claims lack benchmark detail ([CIO.com, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.cio.com/article/4241825/cadence-maestro-puts-domain-trained-ai-agents-to-work-for-chip-design.html)); the M2000 GPU system and 14 GPU tools slated for 2026 ([HPCwire, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/cadence-and-nvidia-unveil-accelerated-engineering-solutions-for-agentic-ai-chip-and-system-design/)); and US$3.12bn for Hexagon's design and engineering unit (MSC Nastran, Adams), which contributes little operating profit this year ([10-Q, Q3 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/813672/000081367226000121/cdns-20260930.htm)). On open source, Moonshot's Kimi K3 blog (17 Jul 2026) described a 48-hour autonomous design run on open-source tools at 45 nm that stayed in simulation, with no tools named, no layout checks and no manufacture ([Kimi blog](https://www.kimi.com/blog/kimi-k3)). It cut Cadence's shares 9.47% that day, but it threatens mature-node and low-end pricing rather than certified judges, and Cadence's reply has been rhetorical: designers "still need EDA tools" ([36Kr, 29 Sep 2026](https://eu.36kr.com/de/p/4003768849258377)). The plan is self-funded, with R&D near 33% of revenue and free cash flow near 30%. What must go right: agents must multiply engine calls faster than they shrink the number of human users; customers must accept value-based "virtual engineer" pricing rather than token-cost pricing; its digital sign-off judges Tempus and Pegasus, absent from the GPU list, must not fall behind; and cross-selling must make the D&E purchase pay.

**Siemens: coherent.** Calibre's position is confirmed outside the company: SemiAnalysis puts it above 85% of physical verification and says TSMC, Samsung and Intel require "Calibre-clean" layouts, and TSMC certified it for A14 in September 2026 ([Siemens, 23 Sep 2026](https://news.siemens.com/en-us/siemens-tsmc-oip-ecosystem-forum-2026/)). The simulation build-out has cost returns: Digital Industries' pre-tax return on capital fell from about 33% in FY2024 to about 16% in FY2026 after the Altair and Dotmatics purchases (researchers' calculation from the [FY2025](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:3948cdd4-35e0-4c1d-8412-9aed4097b3d0/HQCOPR202511117277EN.pdf) and [FY2026](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:11b1a70b-f540-45bb-824f-0b3fbb927e1b/dHQCOPR202611097494EN.pdf) releases). Siemens must carry Calibre through the GPU transition without losing its foundry qualifications, and move a named-user simulation business to agent pricing. It says it will "complete GPU acceleration across its entire simulation portfolio", without dates or money ([NVIDIA and Siemens, 6 Jan 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/siemens-and-nvidia-expand-partnership-industrial-ai-operating-system)), and its CFO keeps "the existing user-based, named user subscription foundation" while planning to "introduce an agentic surcharge" ([Q4 FY2026 call, 12 Nov 2026](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-siemens-posts-record-orders-in-q4-2026-as-growth-quickens-93CH-5698664)). Done: a Calibre GPU rasterizer that Siemens says is "available now" with under 1% error against its CPU reference ([Calibre blog, 13 Apr 2026](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/calibre/2026/04/13/why-a-gpu-rasterizer-matters-for-computational-lithography-in-both-performance-and-precision-at-scale/)); a cuLitho claim of "up to 12x" for full-chip optical proximity correction and 10-17x on key workloads that names no baseline, customer or release date and remains a company claim ([Siemens, 14 Oct 2026](https://news.siemens.com/en-us/siemens-calibre-nvidia-culitho/)); GPU-native multiphase solvers in STAR-CCM+ 2602 ([Siemens blog, Feb 2026](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/simcenter/simcenter-star-ccm-2602-released/)); an automation-engineering agent (Eigen) generally available since April 2026; and PhysicsAI surrogates sold with Siemens's own advice that "critical designs should still be validated with a traditional CAE, CFD solver" ([Siemens PhysicsAI](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/simcenter/engineering-data-science-ai/physicsai/)). On open source it has moved backwards: the OpenRadioss website "is being retired" in favour of a "managed 'Shared Source' environment" ([Siemens Radioss page](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/simcenter/mechanical-simulation/radioss/rd/)). Funding is no constraint, with group free cash flow of €12.1bn in FY2026 and a EUR1bn AI programme over three years. What must go right: GPU Calibre results must be requalified at TSMC, Samsung and Intel; customers must accept surcharges on seats; the Altair purchase must produce its synergies; and no new US export curbs may hit Siemens EDA.

**ASML: coherent, as a constraint owner.** ASML's simulation transitions are narrow: putting computational lithography on GPUs and adding learned models without displacing the wafer as the judge. In 2023 it said it planned "to integrate support for GPUs into all of our computational lithography software products" ([NVIDIA, 21 Mar 2023](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-asml-tsmc-and-synopsys-set-foundation-for-next-generation-chip-manufacturing)). No public evidence of shipment was found, while TSMC runs Synopsys's Proteus on GPUs in production and Samsung has built cuLitho into its own correction platform. Its AI work with Mistral, in which it holds about 11%, covers internal agents, log analysis and documentation, not lithography products ([ASML Annual Report 2025](https://ourbrand.asml.com/m/419103cb23dfeaa4/original/asml-2025-annual-report-financial-performance-section.pdf)). The path is coherent anyway, because the moat is the scanner, and the business behind it earned €5.0bn of net income on €11.7bn of sales in Q3 2026 ([ASML Q3 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026065813/pressreleasefinancialresul.htm)). Its models are "calibrated with machine data and test wafers" ([ASML](https://www.asml.com/en/products/computational-lithography)), R&D was €4.7bn in 2025, and high-NA exposures add physics to model at every node. What must go right: high-NA adoption, with TSMC planning volume production in 2030, must keep computational lithography tied to ASML's machines ([SemiEngineering, 11 Sep 2026](https://semiengineering.com/chip-industry-week-in-review-155/)), and a 2028 financing pause must not repeat 2009, when revenue fell 49%.

**NVIDIA: coherent, as an enabler.** NVIDIA holds more than 90% of the GPU market relevant to engineering simulation ([Digital Engineering 24/7, 10 Feb 2026](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/gpu-acceleration-expands)), but two customers made up 22% and 14% of its FY2026 revenue, and it describes itself as "effectively foreclosed" from China's data-centre market ([10-K FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000021/nvda-20260125.htm)). Its plan is to give the physics software away and sell the hardware it runs on. PhysicsNeMo is open source, Omniverse has been free for production use since May 2026, and cuDSS, the sparse solver inside the GPU paths of COMSOL, HFSS and Abaqus, is licensed "only for use in systems with NVIDIA GPUs" ([cuDSS licence](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cudss/latest/license.html); [Omniverse](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/omniverse/enterprise/)). It has shipped what it promised, with GPU solvers from every major vendor and cuLitho in production at TSMC, except its Apollo open physics models: announced in November 2025, they had not been released by 12 November 2026 ([NVIDIA](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/apollo-open-models)). The weak spot is precision. **Native double precision per rack fell from 2,880 TFLOPS (GB200 NVL72) to 100 TFLOPS (GB300 NVL72)**; Rubin's native rate is about 33 TFLOPS, and emulation helps only dense matrix work, which AMD says leaves 60-70% of HPC workloads with "little to no benefit" ([NVIDIA GB300](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gb300-nvl72/); [The Register, 18 Jan 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/18/nvidia_fp64_emulation/)). Its own cuDSS licence says the library is not certified for avionics, medical or autonomous-vehicle applications. What must go right: vendors must keep porting; foundries and regulators must accept mixed or emulated precision in sign-off solvers; and surrogates must gain acceptance so that GPU hours keep growing.

**Dassault Systèmes: partly coherent.** CIMdata profiles SIMULIA among the leaders in simulation, but no share data were found, and the evidence of regulator acceptance is old or vendor-sourced, such as a 2020 company blog on simulation for European emissions testing ([CIMdata](https://www.cimdata.com/en/online-store/market-analysis-reports/product/351-2025-simulation-analysis-market-analysis-report); [3DS blog, 21 Aug 2020](https://blog.3ds.com/brands/simulia/working-with-wltp-simulation-solutions-for-lower-vehicle-emissions/)). Its finances are excellent: an adjusted operating margin of 32.0% in 2025 and net cash of about €1.05bn after buying ArisGlobal ([FY2025 release](https://www.3ds.com/assets/invest/2026-02/dassault-systemes-25q4-earnings_pr_va.pdf); [Q3 2026 release](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/dassault-syst-mes-strong-q3-055900413.html)). Dassault must move a user-defined business to agents and show its solvers on GPUs and learned models. Management says the agentic platform "continues to be user-based ... It can be humans, it is the agent", with units of work and tokens metered, and that 2027 will be "the first year" of revenue from its 3D UNIV+RSES offer ([Q3 2026 call, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-dassault-systemes-beats-q3-2026-estimates-shares-fall-93CH-4978361)). Done: SimUnit token licences; AI "Virtual Companions" for all cloud customers and 12-14% of the on-premise base (company claims); and CST GPU support that needs "GPUs with good double precision performance (e.g. B200 or H100)" ([CST GPU guide](https://updates.cst.com/downloads/GPU_Computing_Guide.pdf)). No shipped learned-model-in-solver product with verification metrics was found, and no open-source move. The decisive issue is strategy. Its chief executive rejected "frontier model + MCP" approaches and called a general model "useless" for advanced design exploration ([Schnitger, 26 Aug 2026](https://schnitgercorp.com/2026/08/26/plmish-earnings-check-what-have-we-learned/)), a bet that outside agents will not drive its tools that runs against what Synopsys, MathWorks, Keysight and Neural Concept are building. It is well funded, with net cash and R&D at 21% of revenue. What must go right: a recovery at Medidata, a rebound in European autos, conversion of 3D UNIV+RSES in 2027 and credible GPU and learned-model solver releases; the Capital Markets Day on 17 November 2026 is the next test.

**MathWorks: partly coherent.** MathWorks has done the agent and certification work and nothing on pricing. It ships an official MATLAB MCP server (1,813 GitHub stars) and agentic toolkits for MATLAB and Simulink ([GitHub, 9-11 Nov 2026](https://github.com/matlab)). Its IEC Certification Kit carries TÜV SÜD certificates and TÜV SÜD-certified guidance for classifying AI-assisted model editing, review and test generation, which addresses regulator acceptance of AI work directly ([MathWorks IEC kit](https://www.mathworks.com/products/iec-61508.html); [DO-178 kit](https://www.mathworks.com/products/do-178.html)). On pricing, its Simulink Copilot is an add-on "on the same license as MATLAB and Simulink" ([release notes](https://www.mathworks.com/help/simulink-copilot/release-notes-content.html)). Revenue was stated as US$1.5bn in both its 2025 and 2026 factsheets, and growth is undisclosed ([2026 factsheet](https://ch.mathworks.com/content/dam/mathworks/fact-sheet/2026-company-factsheet-8-5x11-8282v27.pdf)). What must go right: agent-written Simulink models must stay inside qualified workflows, which keeps MathWorks the cheapest route to certified code, and it needs a price for machine use before agents reduce the number of licensed engineers.

**Bentley Systems: partly coherent.** Bentley's question is whether its consumption meter survives the loss of the engineer-hours it measures. Its E365 plans, 46% of ARR, charge mainly on daily use, and chief executive Cumins calls application days "a proxy for how much time ... engineers are spending with our software"; "If they start to use our application at machine speed, then on top of that, there should be another price." Monetisation of AI in design starts in 2027 but is not expected to be "a material growth driver in 2027", and European consultancies are shifting work to delivery centres in India ([Q3 2026 call, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.earningswhispers.com/transcript/BSY/Q32026); [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1031308/000103130826000007/bsy-20251231.htm)). Done: an MCP server lets "Claude and other AI agents" automate STAAD loads, model revisions and analysis ([Bentley STAAD](https://www.bentley.com/software/staad/)). No GPU or learned-model work was found. What must go right: customers must accept a machine-speed price as engineer hours fall, and the design codes built into its checkers must remain the accepted route to a stamped design.

**Schrödinger: coherent.** Schrödinger has already made the pricing move the background expects others to struggle with. Its licences grant a set number of calculations, tiered by volume, which management calls "throughput-based", and hosted delivery reached **45% of software revenue** in Q3 2026 against a target of 75% by end-2028 ([10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1490978/000149097826000010/sdgr-20251231.htm); [Q3 2026 results](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001490978/000149097826000075/sdgr-20260930x8kxexx991.htm)). Its agent, Bunsen, a "co-scientist" that runs Schrödinger's physics engines, entered early access in Q2 2026, and Bristol Myers Squibb is deploying it across research. The company is not pricing it separately, betting that agents raise the calculation volume its licences already capture ([BioSpace, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/schrodinger-announces-strategic-collaboration-and-software-agreement-with-bristol-myers-squibb-to-deploy-ai-co-scientist-bunsen-for-agentic-drug-discovery); [Q2 2026 call](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9009674/schrodinger-inc-sdgr-q2-2026-earnings-call-highlights-acv-surges-27-on-bunsen-launch-and-strong-drug-discovery-milestones)). Its learned force fields have been checked against experiment, in a Columbia-Schrödinger paper that paired them with solid-state NMR (secondary coverage, 14 Oct 2026) ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/research-reveals-unexpected-lithium-ion-clusters)). The threat is open source: OpenFE, an MIT-licensed free-energy tool tested blind by 15 pharma partners, was "competitive" at ranking compounds but had more outliers, while FEP+ showed "superior low error performance" ([OMSF, 6 May 2025](https://omsf.substack.com/p/the-free-energy-of-everything-benchmarking-openfe)). It holds US$373m of cash and securities and no debt, the 10-K puts its runway at 24 months or more, and it targets positive adjusted EBITDA by end-2028. What must go right: hosted software margins must return to the high 70s (75% in Q3 2026), Bunsen must lift throughput, and dependence on the two customers behind 35% of Q3 revenue must fall.

**Verisk: partly coherent.** Verisk's judge is regulatory: its hurricane model (version 3.0.0) is one of six accepted by the Florida Commission under the 2023 standards, and all six acceptances expire on 1 November 2027 ([Florida Commission, list revised 2 Oct 2026](https://fchlpm.sbafla.com/media/fdwlbsvn/20261002_2023hurricane_2021floodacceptedmodels.pdf)). The 2025 standards add G-3, "Artificial Intelligence Use", which requires modellers to document AI "training procedures, inference procedures, and measures of effectiveness", backed by audits and on-site review ([Florida Commission 2025 report](https://fchlpm.sbafla.com/media/vjophvvg/2025-hurricane-roa.pdf)). The route for learned components is therefore open but audited. Industry practice so far puts AI into exposure data and component updates rather than into learned hazard models; the rival modeller KCC describes "physical models enhanced by AI" ([Reinsurance News, Aug 2026](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/ai-reshaping-cat-modelling-as-re-insurers-seek-more-current-risk-insight-reports-kcc/)). For Verisk itself, the evidence gathered is an announcement of integrations with AI platforms, including Claude (28 Oct 2026, via [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/vrsk/)), and nothing on GPUs, agents or pricing. What must go right: re-acceptance under the 2025 standards before November 2027, with any AI component surviving the audit.

**Nova: partly coherent.** Nova's moat is the pairing of measurement hardware with physical models that fabs qualify for in-line process control: AI can improve the models but cannot take the measurement. The company describes its metrology as "combined with proprietary modeling algorithms and machine learning software" ([Nova](https://www.novami.com/)), but its Q3 2026 results credit record memory and advanced-packaging demand, not modelling or AI, and disclose nothing on GPUs or agent interfaces ([Nova Q3 2026, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/il/news-releases/nova-reports-record-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-302930609.html)). What must go right: its models must stay ahead in accuracy at gate-all-around transistors and high-bandwidth memory, and growth must hold through a pause in memory or packaging capex.

**Keysight: partly coherent.** Keysight's simulation business is small and undisclosed inside a hardware boom: software and services fell from 37% of revenue in FY2025 to about 33% in Q3 FY2026, and recurring revenue from 29% to 24% ([Q3 FY2026 remarks](https://s22.q4cdn.com/444849635/files/doc_earnings/2026/q3/transcript/Q3-FY2026-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). China was 17.5% of FY2025 revenue, and the company says it has "been prevented from selling large orders to certain key customers due to trade restrictions" ([2025 Annual Report](https://s22.q4cdn.com/444849635/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/2025-Annual-Report.pdf)). It shipped Keysight-built MCP servers in ADS 2027, so customers' own models can drive design while "Keysight simulation" validates the output, with no licensing change ([Keysight, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.keysight.com/us/en/about/newsroom/catalog/news-release.2026.1006_pr26-095-keysight-advances-its-design-software-with-agentic-ai.html)). Its OpenCFD unit released the first OpenFOAM version with GPU offloading, whose results are non-deterministic ([openfoam.com](https://www.openfoam.com/node/684)), and its photonic design tool was certified for Tower Semiconductor's PH18 process on 9 November 2026 ([Business Wire via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/419300/keysight-brings-radio-frequency-and-photonic-design-together-for-tower-semiconductor)). What must go right: radio-frequency and photonic co-design for AI optical links must pull its tools into sign-off-adjacent flows, and it needs a pricing answer for agent use.

**Flexcompute: partly coherent.** Flexcompute has completed more of the six transitions than any other entrant, all except acceptance. Flow360 (CFD) and Tidy3D (electromagnetics) are GPU-native solvers sold on "usage-based pricing", with an agent (FlexAgent) and a Python interface ([Flexcompute](https://www.flexcompute.com/)). Its customer cases are vendor-published: Vertical Aerospace cut turnaround from two weeks to one day with results that matched the wind tunnel "well within the required error margins", but kept the wind tunnel ([Flexcompute, 15 Jun 2026](https://flexcompute.com/blog/vertical-aerospace-10x-more-designs)). No FAA or EASA acceptance of Flow360 results was found. Funding is thinly disclosed: a US$22m Series B in 2021, a US$53m Series C seen only on one data aggregator, and an undisclosed strategic investment from Samsung Ventures and Applied Ventures on 12 October 2026 ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/flexcompute-raises-strategic-investment-from-samsung-ventures-and-applied-ventures/)). What must go right: Flow360 must enter carmakers' and aircraft makers' own sign-off processes, through the existing route by which regulators accept validated CFD evidence, which learned-model vendors cannot yet use.

**Empyrean: partly coherent.** Empyrean's path is import substitution under state control. It claims certifications for its ALPS circuit simulator at 8, 5 and 4 nm and calls its Argus checker the "standard DRC" tool in a leading domestic fab's tape-out flow, but it names no foundry ([2025 annual report](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202604271821642344_1.pdf)). Its digital sign-off tools launched only in 2025, and it is buying the rest: 6.90% of a place-and-route firm for RMB149m and 50.38% of a logic-optimisation firm for RMB219m in October and November 2026. It says it has ported simulators to domestic Hygon accelerators and launched a coding agent for its Python interface (company claims) ([H1 2026 report](https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H2_AN202608280006757902_1.pdf)). A broker warns that digital tool iteration "may lengthen" because domestic processes iterate slowly ([Shenwan Hongyuan via Gelonghui, 30 Oct 2026](https://m.gelonghui.com/news/5340715)). The money comes from the state: its controller, the state-owned CEC, passes capital-budget funds through an entrusted loan, and government support of about RMB150m in 2025 was 2.5 times net profit (researchers' sum). What must go right: certification at advanced nodes by a named foundry, and profit without subsidies.

**PhysicsX: partly coherent.** PhysicsX sells learned models and embedded delivery teams rather than a solver. Its flagship aerodynamics model was trained on more than 25 million geometries using data from Siemens's STAR-CCM+ and Nastran solvers ([Siemens, 4 Dec 2024](https://news.siemens.com/en-us/siemens-physicsx/)), and it sells through jointly scoped one-to-three-month pilots with data-science and simulation staff embedded at the customer ([PhysicsX](https://physicsx.ai/newsroom/why-we-do-pilots-and-how-to-choose-a-good-one)). It has production use: an unnamed aerospace manufacturer runs its models on live casting lines, and Fortescue expanded a two-year collaboration in October 2026. Its benchmark claims are self-reported, and an AI agent reverse-engineered the hidden simulator behind one leaderboard it had topped. It is well funded, with about US$500m raised and around 350 staff ([Tech.eu, 8 Jun 2026](https://tech.eu/2026/06/08/physicsx-raises-300m-at-24bn-valuation/)). What must go right: delivery work must become repeatable software revenue at software margins, its pre-trained models must be shown to generalise, and Siemens, which owns the rival PhysicsAI product, must remain a partner rather than a competitor.

## 6. Where the evidence contradicts the background

| Background claim | What the evidence shows (dated) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Speed comes first, "mostly from AI rewriting existing solver codes for graphics processors" | Deployed GPU speed came overwhelmingly from hand-written vendor and laboratory work: OpenFOAM v2606, MOOSE, MPAS, ICON, LAMMPS, and Synopsys's 20+ GPU products. AI-rewritten ports are 2026 preprints with modest gains: NICAM 1.71x over an existing GPU version, FESOM2 1.6-3.7x, CReSS 5.1x ([NICAM, 30 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41313); [FESOM2, 9 Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11356)) | Contradicted |
| Prior 1: half of widely used CPU-only open codes have an AI-built GPU version in use by 2028 (60%) | At least 12 of 28 listed codes already had hand-written GPU paths; no AI-built port of a CPU-only code is in production; the closest, CPPWRF (a C++/CUDA port of the WRF weather model, Oct 2026), is unaffiliated with WRF's developers and has one GitHub star ([CPPWRF](https://github.com/Xassemblianist/CPPWRF)). Researchers' estimate today: ~25-40% | Too high |
| arXiv 2608.13122: 162 routines validated, 5.1x faster | The 162 are GPU kernels exercised by one typhoon case (of 387 OpenMP regions), checked element-wise at single-precision tolerance against one dumped state; 5.1x is per step on one GH200 node; the authors call it "a scenario-specific validated GPU port, not a complete GPU port" ([arXiv, 13 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13122)) | Overstated |
| arXiv 2608.04400: learned starting guess cut remaining error by seven orders out of distribution; 15.5x faster | Seven orders is the median residual ratio; the mean fell about three orders and stayed above the 1e-8 acceptance level; 15.5x is per optimisation generation, with a GPU added and up to six Newton updates ([arXiv, 5 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04400)) | Overstated, direction right |
| arXiv 2605.08988: learned potentials about ten times worse on unseen molecules | "Often an order of magnitude": force errors 1.8-18.9x for MACE, energy errors up to ~900x, on small H/C/N/O molecules in vacuum ([arXiv v2, 22 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08988v2)) | Roughly right, not uniform |

Three of these matter most for the ranking. First, **the speed gain did not come from AI rewriting code**, so the advantage went to the vendors that hand-ported their own solvers and to NVIDIA, not to open-source projects. Third, **the regulatory prior fails in medicine**: there, learned models are judged as products on clinical evidence, a route that aviation and nuclear regulators have not opened.

## Conclusion: the rulebook, not the solver, is the asset

The evidence sharpens the background's thesis into something more specific. **The durable asset is not a better solver but a named place in someone else's rulebook.** Every judge that held its value through 2026 owes its position to a third party that writes down which tools it trusts: Calibre and PrimeTime at the foundries, the six Florida-accepted hurricane models, the PSS/E and PSCAD formats that grid operators require, and MathWorks's TÜV SÜD-certified kits. Two things follow. First, the next changes in quality will come from the rule-writers rather than the vendors: a foundry rule for validating agent output, EASA's surrogate policy (due by 2028) and Florida's first acceptances under its AI standard (before November 2027) are the events that would reorder this ranking. Second, the newcomer most likely to reach the top group is not the best-funded learned-model company but the first GPU-native solver whose results enter a certification route. That route already exists for validated CFD; for learned models it does not yet exist.

The market has already priced its own version of the judge thesis: the certified EDA franchises are valued for roughly 12-18% a year cash-flow growth, the seat-heavy design-software names for about 2-3.5%. The risk that neither the background nor current prices fully reflect sits in the hardware. AI machines are retreating from the native double precision that many sign-off solvers rely on, so the judges may not get faster on AI hardware while their learned rivals do. If that happens, the pressure on sign-off will come from the hardware rather than from regulators. Validated mixed-precision paths, such as the weather-model core that MeteoSwiss checked against a double-precision version before using it in operational forecasts, are therefore the quiet test of which judges keep their place ([GMD, 23 Jan 2026](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/19/755/2026/)).

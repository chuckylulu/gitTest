# Firm order books outrank announced gigawatts

*Research date: 12 November 2026. Share prices and multiples are closes of 11 November (US) or 12 November (Europe and Asia) unless stated. This is analysis for the reader's own exploration, not personal financial advice, and it gives no buy or sell instruction.*

**Verdict.** On quality alone, the top three are **Siemens Energy and GE Vernova (ranked 1-2, not separable)** and **Mitsubishi Heavy Industries (3)**, with Hitachi (through Hitachi Energy) a close fourth. The first two sell both the grid connection and the gas turbine, so they earn whichever way a data centre solves its power problem. Mitsubishi Heavy has the firmest order book in the field. On the open question, delivery is running far behind announcement: operating on-site generation at US data centres is still **about 2 GW against a proposed pipeline of 90-116 GW**, roughly 2%, and the growth in the proposed figure during 2026 was almost entirely on paper ([Cleanview](https://www.cleanview.co/reports/behind-the-meter-data-centers); [Cleanview tracker](https://cleanview.co/products/behind-the-meter-tracker)). Order books are firm in three places: turbine orders from utilities, transmission-level transformer and HVDC orders with prepaid capacity reservations, and existing power plants under 15-20 year contracts. They are soft in three others: turbine slot reservations (58 GW of GE Vernova's 122 GW), short-cycle data-centre switchgear with under a year of backlog, and headline "backlogs" such as Bloom Energy's roughly $20bn against $0.44bn of audited product and installation obligations. SoftBank's November cancellation showed that a deposit-backed reservation puts the loss on the buyer: SoftBank wrote off about $3.7bn and the supplier kept the cash. Quality and entry point now pull apart for most of the field, because shares fell 15-70% from their 2026 highs while reported results were at records. Coverage is deep for the US, good for Europe, Japan, Korea and Taiwan, and thin for China, India, Israel and the Gulf.

## 1. The field map: nine sub-segments, and scarcity sits at the grid edge

The scarce items are large gas turbines, large power transformers, high-voltage switchgear and HVDC, and sites that already have power. Everything else is either a bridge, a commodity or an option on the 2030s. Data centres are a minority of the order books that matter most. They are **about 20% of GE Vernova's gas commitments, under 10% of Siemens Energy's backlog and about 6% of Mitsubishi Heavy's turbine orders** ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-in-9b-deal-to-buy-high-voltage-manufacturer-as-gas-turbine-orde/873223/); [Siemens Energy call, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-siemens-energy-shares-jump-on-record-q4-2026-results-93CH-5018439); [MHI Q&A, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.mhi.com/finance/library/result/pdf/fy20262q/qa_20262q.pdf)).

**The fifteen selected.** GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, Hitachi, Constellation Energy, Vertiv, Delta Electronics, Schneider Electric, Eaton, ABB, HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung Heavy Industries, Caterpillar, Wärtsilä and Bloom Energy. Between them they cover every sub-segment with real revenue today, and eight home countries (the US, Germany, Japan, France, Taiwan, Switzerland, South Korea and Finland). No company from China, India or Israel made the fifteen; the reasons are below, and for several of them the reason is thin evidence, not weak business.

**Left out, and why.** These were researched and judged secondary. *Cummins* and *Rolls-Royce mtu* sell mainly standby gensets, which are needed in every scenario but earn less service revenue and are not the fast-power constraint. *INNIO* is the purest on-site prime-power exposure, but it carries 5.2x debt to EBITDA, a controlling sponsor, and a customer (VoltaGrid) under a Georgia stop-work order ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/inio/statistics/); [Inside Climate News](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/31072026/georgia-data-center-private-power-plant/)). *Doosan Enerbility* has a 5.9% operating margin and a net loss to owners. *LS Electric* has 1.2 years of backlog, negative free cash flow and the highest US anti-dumping rate of the Korean three. *Fortune Electric* is a small single-product company. *Baker Hughes* is dominated by LNG and oilfield work. *Quanta, EMCOR, Comfort Systems and IES* sell labour hours; they have real pricing power now, but it rests on a gap that prefabrication and any pause would close. *CATL, Sungrow and BYD* have the best battery economics (CATL holds CNY 243bn of net cash), but no named hyperscaler order was found and US rules are closing that market to them ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/she/300750/statistics/)). *Tesla* has the clearest data-centre battery orders, but energy is about 10% of group revenue. *Fluence* lost its chief executive and finance chief and earns a 5% gross margin. *Vistra and Talen* have the same position as Constellation, with more Texas policy risk and more leverage respectively. *Oklo, NuScale, X-energy, Kairos, TerraPower and Fervo* deliver almost nothing before 2030.

Not researched in enough depth to rank (this is a coverage gap, not a judgement): Chinese grid and battery makers from their own filings; the Indian subsidiaries beyond aggregator data; Japanese mid-caps (Fuji Electric, Meidensha, Toshiba); non-US site owners (CDC, DayOne, YTL Power, AdaniConneX), where figures come from search summaries; European utilities; Huawei Digital Power; and Israeli names.

Three points explain the order. First, the top four own something a data-centre pause cannot remove: grid operators and utilities place most of their orders, and those orders carry prepayments. Second, Hitachi ranks below the turbine makers despite owning the scarcest product, because its lead rests on the company's own description and its revenue is almost entirely one-off equipment ([Toyo Keizai](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/937543)). Third, the Korean transformer makers rank below the in-building suppliers despite a more durable product, because their returns are far above their own history, their business is concentrated in one product and one country, and their last US-driven cycle ended badly (HD Hyundai Electric's return on equity was -5% in 2021) ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/krx/267260/financials/ratios/)).

## 3. Path-forward notes: six transitions, and nobody sells a solid-state transformer yet

The six transitions are: (1) grid-only to on-site generation with storage; (2) on-site gas as a bridge; (3) AC to 800 VDC and solid-state transformers; (4) adding factory capacity without adding it into a pause; (5) scarce labour pushing work into prefabrication; (6) local opposition and permitting.

Two findings apply to every company. No incumbent has a solid-state transformer on sale: ABB's is not orderable until the second half of 2027, Eaton has ten pilots, Siemens partnered with Reinhausen in August 2026, and Hitachi Energy and Schneider are at the research stage ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/abb-q3-fy-2026-earnings-show-record-orders-outpacing-revenue-on-data-centers/); [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-eaton-q3-earnings-call-highlights-2026-11-03/); [DG Matrix, an interested party](https://www.dgmatrix.com/news-and-media/news/solid-state-transformers-could-reshape-ai-infrastructure/1223)). Dell'Oro's summary of the October 2026 OCP summit is that "the sidecar is here" while the upstream 800 VDC architecture is still "engineering ambition and speculation" ([Dell'Oro](https://www.delloro.com/inside-ocp-global-summit-2026-cold-plates-800-vdc-sidecars-and-a-buildout-at-full-steam/)). And the capacity wave lands together in 2027-29 across turbines, transformers and engines, which is when a financing-led pause would bite.

**Siemens Energy — coherent.** It faces transitions 2, 4 and 6. The plan is to raise gas-turbine, transformer and switchgear capacity by more than 50% between FY2026 and FY2030 on capex of about €2bn a year, and to grow service revenue on a larger fleet. What it has done: FY2026 orders of €68.1bn, backlog of €164bn, Gas Services margin up to 16.4% from 13.0%, and a $421m transformer plant in Charlotte ([Siemens Energy release, 11 Nov 2026](https://assets.siemens-energy.com/dam/51f29849-97c1-4490-8c14-b4fe01556b26/Press-Release-English-pdf_Original%20file.pdf); [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/)). The plan is funded from about €8bn of net cash. What must go right: the 39 GW of slot reservations must convert in FY2027 as management expects. An analyst put firm gas orders in the latest quarter at only about 2 GW, and grid orders fell 19%; management called both timing effects, and that is a company claim.

**GE Vernova — coherent.** It faces transitions 2, 4 and 6. The plan is 20 GW a year of turbine output now, 24 GW in 2028 and 30 GW in 2030, plus a larger grid business through Prolec GE (now fully owned) and the roughly $9bn Trench purchase ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-gas-turbine-backlog-climbs-to-116-gw/826039/); [GE Vernova](https://www.gevernova.com/news/articles/ge-vernova-releases-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-announces-agreement-acquire)). The evidence is 122 GW of backlog and reservations at 30 September 2026. The mix is improving: firm backlog rose from 53 GW to 64 GW in the quarter while reservations fell from 63 GW to 58 GW. But net additions slowed to 6 GW from double digits earlier in the year ([SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000162/gevpressrelease3q26.htm)). What must go right: reservation conversion, wind losses ending, and a 30 GW plan that one analyst described as "the jury's out". Solid-state transformers are listed as a product in commercialisation, with none sold.

**Mitsubishi Heavy Industries — coherent.** It faces transitions 2 and 4, and handles 4 best. About ¥500bn of investment goes mainly to hot-part capacity for a service stream it can already see, and new-unit output doubles "without major capital investment". It books only projects with concrete plans and reports no cancellations in one to two years (company claim) ([MHI Q&A](https://www.mhi.com/finance/library/result/pdf/fy20262q/qa_20262q.pdf)). Its chief executive says openly that on-site small turbines "can be considerably more expensive" and that grid power wins over time. What must go right: the installed base of its largest turbines roughly doubling by FY2030, as the company expects.

**Hitachi — coherent.** It faces transition 4 above all. The programme is about $9bn over FY2020-27 across more than 40 projects, including a large-transformer plant in Virginia due in 2028. Management says the added capacity "is already contracted and, in many cases, prepaid" (company claim) ([Quartr summary of the 2 Nov 2026 investor day](https://quartr.com/events/hitachi-ltd-6501-hitachi-energy-investor-day-2026_3P84Osoi)). The service plan (from under 1% of the installed base under contract to about 25% of revenue) is a stated aim, not a result. What must go right: delivery from factories that the chief executive admits cannot yet fulfil the backlog, and hiring more than 5,000 people a year.

**Constellation — coherent.** It faces transitions 1 and 6. The plan is restarts, uprates and long contracts on an existing fleet, plus Calpine's gas. The evidence: the Crane restart is still "on track" for the second half of 2027, a $3bn Calvert Cliffs upgrade tied to an Amazon contract, and Google contracts for about 3,600 MW ([RTTNews, date unclear](https://www.rttnews.com/3696896/constellation-energy-shares-up-on-3-60-gw-power-deals-with-google-on-pjm-grid.aspx); [Constellation call, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-constellation-energy-tops-q3-2026-forecasts-shares-rise-93CH-5012724); [The Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/10/01/constellation-plans-upgrade-maryland-calvert-cliffs-amazon/)). What must go right: Crane on time, and PJM and FERC rules on co-located load staying workable.

**Vertiv — partly coherent.** It faces transitions 3, 4 and 5. It announced an 800 VDC portfolio for the second half of 2026, but no shipment confirmation was found. Its own guide treats a transformer-plus-rectifier "DC UPS" as the first step and says the solid-state transformer supply chain "still has ground to cover" ([Vertiv](https://www.vertiv.com/zh-CN/insights/articles/educational-articles/the-800-vdc-decision-a-practical-guide-for-ai-power-architecture/)). Capex tripled to $425m in nine months and inventories more than doubled to $3.3bn ([Vertiv release](https://investors.vertiv.com/news/news-details/2026/Vertiv-Reports-Strong-Third-Quarter-2026-with-Net-Sales-Growth-of-61-Organic-Orders-Growth-over-100-and-Diluted-EPS-Growth-of-91-Adjusted-Diluted-EPS-Growth-of-78-Raises-Full-Year-2026-Guidance-Across-All-Key-Metrics/)). What must go right: products shipping on NVIDIA's 2027 timetable before AC UPS lines shrink.

**Delta Electronics — coherent.** It faces transition 3 and is furthest along. It ships ±400 V power racks in the second half of 2026, expects to ship 100 solid-state transformer units by end-2026 (company statement), and is building a Malaysian base ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2026/10/15/2003865972)). Analysts expect these products to be under 1% of 2026 revenue, with the ramp in 2027. The warning sign is on transition 4: gross margin fell to 34.1% and inventory provisions reached about 1.7% of revenue, "mainly due to customer order adjustments" and construction delays ([Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/biz/archives/2026/10/31/2003866890)).

**Schneider Electric — partly coherent.** It faces transitions 3 and 5. Prefabricated modules drove third-quarter growth, and a 1.2 MW sidecar developed with NVIDIA has been announced but not confirmed as shipped ([Schneider Q3 release](https://www.se.com/ww/en/assets/pdf/release-Q3-revenues-2026)). The $22.6bn PTC acquisition adds debt and integration work unrelated to power ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/159791-schneider-electric-to-buy-ptc-for-22-6b-in-industrial-software-push/)).

**Eaton — coherent.** It faces transitions 3, 4 and 5 and has bought the pieces: Resilient Power (a 15 kV, 2 MW solid-state transformer that passed UL testing), Fibrebond (prefabricated enclosures) and Boyd (liquid cooling) ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-eaton-q3-earnings-call-highlights-2026-11-03/)). Eighteen of 25 capacity projects are complete. What must go right: backlog that covers under a year must keep refilling, on a balance sheet with $20.1bn of net debt.

**ABB — partly coherent.** It faces transition 3 with a product that cannot be ordered until late 2027, and handles transition 4 by holding capex near $1bn a year ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/abb-q3-fy-2026-earnings-show-record-orders-outpacing-revenue-on-data-centers/)). That discipline protects returns in a pause and risks share loss if growth continues.

**HD Hyundai Electric and Hyosung Heavy — coherent.** Both face transitions 4 and 6, plus US trade policy. Projects are small ($150-200m each) relative to profit: HD's second Alabama plant is due in April 2027 and Hyosung's Memphis expansion in 2028 ([Asia Economy](https://core.asiae.co.kr/article/2026030809155331992); [CEO Score Daily](https://ceoscoredaily.com/page/view/2025111810543580316)). Neither showed a solid-state transformer, but the 345-765 kV units they make are still needed at the grid interface under any in-building design. What must go right: US permitting not slowing new orders in 2027, and prices holding when Western plants ramp in 2028-30. HD's big-tech contract is a framework with staged call-offs, which is softer than a firm order ([Digital Daily](https://m.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2026070217543516911)).

**Caterpillar and Wärtsilä — partly coherent.** Both face transition 2. Caterpillar plans roughly to triple large-engine capacity by 2029-30 (reports differ, and the primary filing was not checked), which is the largest capacity bet into a possible pause ([DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/caterpillar-seeking-to-expand-production-of-large-reciprocating-engines-and-turbines-to-meet-huge-data-center-demand)). Wärtsilä is adding 65% in two "disciplined, stepwise" phases landing in 2028 and 2029 for about €230m ([Wärtsilä](https://www.wartsila.com/media/news/26-05-2026-wartsila-to-further-expand-its-technical-production-capacity-by-an-additional-30-to-meet-the-growing-global-demand-in-the-energy-and-marine-industries-3754327)). Fleets of small on-site units carry the most permit risk. Their fallback markets (mining, gas compression, marine, grid balancing) are real, which is why the verdict is partly coherent rather than not coherent.

**Bloom Energy — partly coherent.** It faces transitions 2, 3 and 6. Its story has moved from hydrogen to carbon capture, heat recovery and 800 VDC, and every element is still at the announcement stage ([Q3 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/22/bloom-energy-be-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Adding capacity is cheap ($100-150m per GW), so transition 4 is not the risk. The risk is that current contract assets rose from $179m to $793m in nine months while third-quarter operating cash flow was negative, and that revenue depends on Brookfield-linked financing vehicles closing on schedule ([Bloom 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1664703/000162828026066890/ex991_q326financialresults.htm)).

## 5. Where quality and entry point pull apart

**High quality, entry no better than fair.** GE Vernova and Siemens Energy rank first on quality, but both prices require 30-36% yearly earnings growth for three years and no slippage in the reservation tier, which is the least firm part of the industry's book. Mitsubishi Heavy has the firmest book, yet at 21x forward earnings it is priced like Siemens Energy.

**Mid-ranked quality, most discounted entry.** HD Hyundai Electric ranks 11-12 on quality because of concentration and peak-cycle returns. It trades at 13.5x forward earnings after a fall of about 70% from its peak, back near its 2022-23 multiple. The caution is that analysts' targets still sit at more than double the price, so estimate cuts have not yet happened.

**Fourth on quality, cheapest large-cap route.** Hitachi owns the largest transformer and HVDC business at 11.7x EV/EBITDA with net cash. The cost is that about 60% of profit comes from outside the grid business.

**Lowest quality rank of the fifteen, most demanding price.** Bloom Energy's products are in real demand and its margins are real. But at 43x forward earnings the price assumes several more years of rapid growth, on a base where one counterparty supplied about 73% of a quarter's revenue.

**Outside the fifteen.** The labour-based contractors with the heaviest data-centre exposure (EMCOR at 17x forward earnings, Comfort Systems at 19x, IES at 14x) trade as if a pause were already priced, while the grid-led Quanta trades at 29x ([EMCOR](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eme/statistics/); [Quanta](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pwr/statistics/)). CATL at about 11x forward earnings is the cheapest high-return business in the wider field, with the weakest link to US data centres ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/she/300750/statistics/)).

## 6. Contradictions with the background

**The figures that have moved.** GE Vernova's gas backlog plus reservations is 122 GW, not "heading to 100 GW", and its growth slowed sharply in the third quarter. The proposed US on-site pipeline is 90-116 GW, not about 48 GW; operating capacity is unchanged at about 2 GW, and "about 3 GW by year-end" remains Cleanview's projection, not an outcome. Lead times for large transformers are lengthening: generator step-up units exceeded 160 weeks in early 2026 against the 128-144 weeks in the background, and only distribution transformers have eased, to about 30 weeks ([AJOT/Reuters, 9 Jul 2026](https://www.ajot.com/news/us-power-companies-scramble-to-secure-equipment-as-surging-data-center-demand-strains-supplies)). The Bloom-Brookfield framework was raised from $5bn to $25bn on 30 June 2026, and it is a funding ceiling, not revenue ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/319573/20260702/bloom-energy-lands-25-billion-brookfield-commitment-ai-data-center-fuel-cells.htm)). INNIO, Fervo and X-energy are no longer private.

**Claims that do not hold as stated.** No source in Siemens Energy's FY2026 materials says it is "sold out into the early 2030s"; the firmer statements are 4-6 years of visibility for large transformers and 85% of next year's revenue in backlog. The "40% of planned capacity in states considering pauses" now refers to campaign pledges by governors-elect in Ohio, Georgia, Michigan, New Mexico and Texas; nothing has been enacted, and most pledges need legislation in 2027. Sightline's "30-50% delayed or cancelled" is disputed: SemiAnalysis calls its denominator "off by multiples", and says slippage is concentrated in early-stage announcements ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/stop-saying-half-of-2026-us-datacenter)). Bernstein's finding that only about a third of a 586 GW North American pipeline is credible fits both views ([456X Morning Brief, 12 Nov 2026](https://404kresearch.substack.com/p/456x-morning-brief-november-12-2026)). The "row-level units up to 2 MW in 2027" rating appears in one secondary source and is not confirmed by Counterpoint's account of NVIDIA's roadmap. No primary source confirms that 800 VDC power racks shipped in volume in the second half of 2026.

**"Buyers pay for speed" needs a qualifier.** The IEA says that, given the turbine supply crunch, "it is not clear that onsite generation necessarily promises a faster route to market", and its global estimate of 15-27 GW by 2030 sits well below a US-only pipeline of 47 GW ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary)). On-site gas at gigawatt scale is largely a US phenomenon.

**The five prior views.**

- *Power, not chips, is the main limit through 2030 (65%).* This holds for the US, Europe and Japan. It does not hold in China, where national supply is ample and the limit is chips ([OIES](https://www.oxfordenergy.org/?p=49239)). Memory is a parallel limit in 2026-27, and financing and local politics have emerged as co-limits. The International AI Safety Report 2026 takes the opposite view on energy ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21012)).
- *New nuclear under 5 GW before 2032 (75%).* This holds with a wide margin. New build tied to data centres is realistically 0.1-0.6 GW; restarts add about 2.25 GW and uprates roughly 2.5-3.5 GW. The 75% looks too low.
- *1 MW racks in production by end-2030 (50%).* Evidence leans slightly against. No 1 MW rack has a ship date, current reference racks top out at 250 kW, and Dell'Oro says such racks "may arrive later and be rarer than roadmaps implied".
- *Powered sites hold value better than chips.* This needs conditions: an energised site, a creditworthy tenant and no pending pause. SoftBank lost $3.7bn on power equipment bought ahead of sites. NextDC lost about 40% in a day when a distribution upgrade slipped. Microsoft left Nscale's Loughton site when grid power moved to the 2030s. Meanwhile CoreWeave raised rental rates on older GPUs by 25-50% ([investingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/nextdc-shares-plunge-44-as-power-delays-expose-ai-data-centre-bottleneck/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/microsoft-pulls-out-nscale-loughton-data-centre-ipo)).
- *In a pause orders are cancelled, so firm books matter.* This is supported, with a correction. SoftBank cancelled, but GE Vernova reported "no negative or positive impact" because the deposits were not recoverable ([The Next Web, 12 Nov 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/softbank-loss-7bn-ai-data-centre-power-equipment-writedown)). The exposure for suppliers is price and conversion rate on new orders, not refunds.

**Scenario timing.** The background places a financing-led pause around 2028. Parts of it were rehearsed in October-November 2026: CoreWeave bonds yielded above 15%, Microsoft trimmed 2026 capex because third-party capacity arrived late, and SoftBank began "a thorough reassessment" of equipment purchases ([CNBC, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/coreweave-earnings-preview-debt-capex.html)). No large hyperscaler has guided 2027 spending down, so this is tightening among leveraged intermediaries and not yet a pause.

**Regions with thin coverage.** China (company filings and Chinese trade press not read), India (aggregator data only), Japan (no Japanese-language filings), Israel, the Gulf (whether Stargate UAE's first 200 MW is live is unconfirmed), Taiwan's grid rules, and European utilities as site owners. Companies in these regions that are absent from the ranking were mostly not researched, not judged weak.

## Conclusion

The useful distinction in this field is not gas against grid, or on-site against utility. It is who has been paid. Suppliers holding non-refundable deposits from utilities and grid operators are insulated for two to five years whatever happens to AI spending. Suppliers whose "backlog" is a framework, an option or a pipeline are exposed first, and so are buyers who secured equipment before they had sites and tenants. That reverses part of the background view: owning the constraint protects the seller of the scarce item more than it protects the buyer who hoards it.

The second lesson is about timing. Capacity for turbines, transformers and engines all arrives in 2027-29, and the companies' own demand forecasts sit below the capacity they are jointly building. The record margins of 2026 are the thing at risk in a pause, more than volumes. The single observation that would most change this report is the reservation conversion rate at GE Vernova and Siemens Energy over the next two quarters. If it holds, the November sell-off was about politics. If it slips, the order of the top group is safe, but several of the "fair" entry labels are not.

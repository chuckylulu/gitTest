# Certified judges keep value as proofs cheapen

**Verdict.** On business quality alone, **Cadence and Synopsys rank joint first and cannot be honestly separated, and Siemens EDA ranks third.** Synopsys owns more of the verdicts a chip must pass, above all timing sign-off, where PrimeTime holds an estimated **90%+ share** ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)). Cadence has the better 2026 execution (revenue guided up about **21%** ([Cadence CFO commentary, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000813672/000081367226000116/cfocommentary10262026ex9902.htm)) against industry growth of about 12% ([ESD Alliance, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/electronic-system-design-industry-posts-5-7-billion-in-revenue-in-q2-2026--esd-alliance-reports-302912151.html))), the stronger balance sheet and the only fully coherent path of the three. Siemens owns Calibre, the physical-verification judge whose "Calibre-clean" output TSMC requires by an analyst's account, but it is a challenger everywhere else and discloses nothing about EDA on its own. On the open question, the evidence says that **specifications with commercial or legal force are owned by whoever controls the judge (the architecture owner and its compliance suite, the foundry and its certified rule decks, the certification authority) and are signed off by accountable people.** AI already drafts specifications well enough to move labour, but not well enough to be trusted unreviewed: in an independent test of ten OpenAI and Anthropic models the best caught a median **47%** of expert-identified requirement defects ([arXiv 2609.03230, 3 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03230)), and DeepMind's AlphaProof Code still had its researchers guide and approve the trusted statements, about **26,000 lines** including machine semantics, beneath about 6.5 million lines of machine proof ([DeepMind paper, 10 Nov 2026](https://deepmind.google/documents/36/Formal_Verification_of_Signal_4.pdf)). Value is therefore moving less to the writers of specifications than to the owners of judges and of accountable sign-off. The entry-point picture differs from the quality ranking: Synopsys looks attractive, Cadence fair, Siemens AG, Arm and the Chinese EDA vendors stretched, and the strongest software-proof companies (AbsInt, AdaCore, Harmonic) cannot be bought. Research date: 12 November 2026. US and European prices are closes of 11 November 2026; Asian prices are closes of 12 November 2026. This is analysis for exploration, not personal financial advice.

## 1. Field map: certified incumbents own the chip verdicts, small specialists own the software ones

The field divides by who issues the final verdict. In chips it is a sign-off engine that a foundry has certified tool by tool, node by node, against silicon data only the foundry holds. **TSMC's public certification grids for A14, A16, N2P, N3C and 3DFabric (as of 1 Oct 2026) list only Cadence, Synopsys and Siemens EDA**, even though its EDA Alliance has 11 members, including China's Primarius; the test method and fees sit behind a customer login ([TSMC OIP EDA Alliance](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/oip/eda_alliance)). Within that trio the judges are split. Analyst estimates give Synopsys PrimeTime 90%+ of timing sign-off and Design Compiler 84-85% of synthesis, Siemens Calibre 85%+ of physical verification, and Cadence Palladium 55-60% of a two-vendor emulation split that omits Siemens Veloce ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)). In software the verdict comes from open-source proof kernels (Lean, Rocq) kept by non-profits such as the Lean FRO, whose largest donor is Amazon ([Lean FRO, read 12 Nov 2026](https://lean-lang.org/fro/about)), and, where regulation bites, from tools a certification regime accepts: AbsInt's CompCert became the first compiler for which DO-178C certification credit was claimed, in March 2026 ([AbsInt, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.absint.com/releases/260320.htm)). Specifications with force behind them belong to owners of compliance suites: Arm's architecture compliance kit, which Qualcomm's engineers run as roughly 48,000 tests ([EE Times, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.eetimes.com/two-dollars-and-a-test-suite/)), and RISC-V's Sail model, adopted by RISC-V International while its certification programme is still forming ([sail-riscv](https://github.com/riscv/sail-riscv)).

Several organisations issue or supply the verdict but are deliberately left out of the ranking. **TSMC** is the judge of the judges, and Samsung's SAFE programme a second, more permissive one: it has certified Primarius's SPICE simulator at 3-8nm, which TSMC's grids do not show ([Primarius, Jun 2024](https://www.primarius-tech.com/en/aboutus/news/Primarius-NanoSpice-Simulator-Certified-for-Samsung-Foundry-3nm-4nm-Process-Technology.html)). Rapidus has no public certification regime yet; an analyst found its design kit "does not yet exist in shippable form" ([Futurum, 31 Aug 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/?p=95024)). Certification authorities (FAA, EASA, TÜV, Common Criteria labs), standards bodies and the Lean FRO define what "correct" means but are not companies in this field. The model makers (Google DeepMind, Microsoft, Anthropic, OpenAI, Amazon Web Services) are treated as suppliers and competitors rather than ranked: none reports verification revenue, only AWS sells verification-based features (Automated Reasoning checks at $0.17 per 1,000 text units per policy) ([AWS Bedrock pricing, 12 Nov 2026](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/)), and their effect enters through transition (7) in section 3. Nvidia is an investor and partner (a $2bn Synopsys stake at $414.79 a share) rather than a vendor ([Synopsys, 1 Dec 2025](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-12-01-NVIDIA-and-Synopsys-Announce-Strategic-Partnership-to-Revolutionize-Engineering-and-Design)). Ranking any of these on tests built for verification vendors would be a category error, and no valuation work was done on them.

Galois, Runtime Verification, Imandra, Theorem, &Truth, Math Inc., Logical Intelligence and Code Metal are labour-bound, pre-product or unproven as sellers of proof. Normal Computing, Cognichip, Ricursive, Hiveware and VerifAIX have no verdict engine or no named customers. Tessolve, HCLTech, MosChip and Cyient are labour or chip-product businesses, with LTTS kept as the listed reference. No Japanese, Korean or Taiwanese vendor holds an advanced-node certification, and GUC and Alchip are production pass-through businesses. UniVista, X-Epic and S2C could not be assessed for lack of data; Semitronix's verdict concerns yield rather than design correctness. Keysight and Silvaco were not assessed for quality.

**Why Cadence and Synopsys cannot be separated.** Synopsys wins the position test outright. It owns the timing judge plus extraction and power-integrity sign-off, certified for TSMC A14 ([Synopsys-TSMC, 23 Sep 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-23-Synopsys-and-TSMC-Partner-to-Accelerate-AI-Systems-Innovation-with-Agentic-AI-and-Advanced-Design)), and OpenAI agreed to license its tools, which the joint release calls "the underlying, ground-truth engineering tools", with a revenue share ([Synopsys/OpenAI, 30 Sep 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-30-OpenAI-and-Synopsys-Announce-GPT-Synopsys-Frontier-Intelligence-to-Revolutionize-Chip-Design)). Cadence wins on lead, finances and path. Its core EDA revenue grew **20%** in Q3 2026 ([Cadence Q3 call](https://www.webull.com/news/15818016766808064)) while Synopsys's EDA grew **8.5%** ([Synopsys Q3 FY2026 call, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.webull.com/news/15497781661541376)) and industry revenue **12.2%** ([ESD Alliance, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/electronic-system-design-industry-posts-5-7-billion-in-revenue-in-q2-2026--esd-alliance-reports-302912151.html)); no Cadence customer reaches 10% of revenue ([Cadence FY2025 10-K](https://s206.q4cdn.com/597110084/files/doc_downloads/2026/Mar/CDNS-FY-2025-Form-10-K.pdf)). Synopsys, after the Ansys purchase, carries about $10bn of notes against $3.6bn of cash ([Synopsys 10-Q, Q3 FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)), and its 2025 IP shock was partly self-inflicted: the CEO said "some decisions made on our roadmap and resource allocations did not deliver the expected results" ([SiliconANGLE, 9 Sep 2025](https://siliconangle.com/2025/09/09/shares-synopsys-crater-export-restrictions-derail-china-sales/)). Against that, Cadence is only a certified challenger in the two final sign-off verdicts. The growth gap may reflect China, licence-recognition timing or real share loss; the sources do not say which. The offsets are of similar weight, so neither is placed above the other.

**Why Siemens EDA ranks above Arm.** Calibre is the most secure single judge in the field: foundries write their rule decks in its format, TSMC requires "Calibre-clean" DRC/LVS ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)), it was certified for TSMC A14 in September 2026 alongside a DRC-fix agent co-developed with TSMC that one analyst called the clearest example of foundry-endorsed agentic trust ([Futurum, 24 Sep 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/tsmcs-oip-forum-turns-agentic-chip-design-demos-into-production-flows/)), and Rapidus built its 2nm design kit on it ([New Electronics, Jun 2025](https://www.newelectronics.co.uk/content/news/rapidus-collaborating-with-siemens-on-2nm-semiconductor-designs)). Arm's judge is broader and its royalty model better, but a Delaware jury found in October 2026 that Arm breached Qualcomm's architecture licence by withholding compliance-kit patches, and Arm's CEO agreed at trial that about two-thirds of revenue would come from chips by 2030 ([EE Times, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.eetimes.com/two-dollars-and-a-test-suite/)). Siemens's weaknesses (no standalone disclosure, seat pricing, and a CEO who left to become Synopsys's chief revenue officer with no successor named) sit at the edge of its judge ([Synopsys, 19 Nov 2025](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-11-19-Synopsys-Appoints-Mike-Ellow-as-Chief-Revenue-Officer)). Arm's sits at the centre of its own: a compliance kit is a judge only while licensees trust that its owner is neutral.

**Why AbsInt and AdaCore tie, and Harmonic follows them.** Both own judges that certification regimes already accept, sold to buyers whose budgets do not depend on AI capital spending. AbsInt's judges cover C, a mainstream language rather than a niche one, and CompCert's March 2026 credit is the strongest single certification event found, though the authority is unnamed ([AbsInt, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.absint.com/releases/260320.htm)); a German tool vendor resells Astrée, which is outside confirmation of the channel ([QA Systems](https://www.qa-systems.com/)). AdaCore's evidence is more independent: Nvidia published the Ada/SPARK ISO 26262 process itself ([NVIDIA spark-process](https://github.com/NVIDIA/spark-process)), and the French entity reported EUR 7.1m of net income on EUR 15.6m of revenue in FY2022 ([French registry](https://annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr/entreprise/403325657)). Harmonic has the purest usage model and a shipped product, but no certification acceptance and no evidence yet of paid volume, so it ranks below both and above the Chinese vendors.

**Why the rest fall where they do.** Empyrean and Primarius tie: Primarius's device models are the most judge-like Chinese position and travel abroad, while Empyrean has the broader portfolio, the stronger balance sheet and state backing; both lose money before subsidies, and Empyrean's own risk factor ties its position to the urgency of domestic substitution ([Empyrean 2025 annual report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-28/1225217315.PDF)). Certora leads the one clearly paying unregulated market for verified code, but gave its engine away and still earns mainly from audits. ChipAgents has the most traction of the chip-verification entrants but owns no verdict. Axiom has capital and mathematical results but no code product on its site as of 12 Nov 2026 ([Axiom homepage](https://axiommath.ai/)). LTTS is the strongest-financed of the labour sellers and ranks last because the position test puts verification labour at the bottom and it has not attempted the move from labour to tools.

**Resilience across the three scenarios.** In **steady growth (about 55%)**, everyone above the line gains: more custom-chip tape-outs mean more sign-off runs and emulation hours, Arm's data-centre royalties keep compounding (they doubled year on year in the September 2026 quarter) ([Arm Q2 FYE27](https://newsroom.arm.com/news/arm-q2-fye27-results)), and more AI-written code needs checking. In a **financing-led pause around 2028 (about 25%)**, history points to the up-front lines. Under time-based licensing Synopsys grew 1.7% in FY2009, while Cadence, still selling much up-front, fell 17.9%, with product revenue down 22% and services down 20% ([SEC XBRL, Cadence](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000813672.json); [SEC XBRL, Synopsys](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK0000883241.json)). Today the exposed lines are emulation hardware after three record years (Cadence's inventory rose from $303.5m to $448.8m in nine months of 2026), royalty-bearing IP, Arm's new chip business, services labour, and the private marks of companies without revenue (Axiom at $1.6bn+, Harmonic at $1.45bn). The least exposed are AbsInt and AdaCore, whose buyers are aerospace, defence and automotive programmes, and Siemens, which calls fiscal 2027 "a strong renewal year" for EDA ([Siemens speech, 12 Nov 2026](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:b0adfeab-2c49-4993-9319-7b7169cff52d/Presserede-Q4-2026-Presseformat-EN.pdf)). In **faster acceleration (about 20%)**, agent-driven exploration raises consumption of certified engines but also speeds seat compression and the competence of open tools at mature nodes, where about three-quarters of design starts will sit by 2030 on IBS data shown by Cadence ([DVCon 2026 paper](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/Broadening-the-Adoption-of-Hardware-Assisted-Verification-with-Next-Generation-Emulation-Appliance.pdf)); proof search is commoditised fastest, which hits Axiom, Certora's audits and Harmonic's search edge. Export controls touch most of the ranking: China was 10.3% of Synopsys's revenue (Q3 FY2026), 16% of Cadence's (Q3 2026) and about 18% of Arm's (FY2026), Empyrean is on the Entity List, and the suspension of the US affiliates rule runs only to **10 January 2027** ([Federal Register, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/09/2026-22793/extension-of-the-suspension-of-expansion-of-end-user-controls-for-affiliates-of-certain-listed)).

## 3. Paths forward: the incumbents are funded, the entrants are not yet paid

### Cadence (1=): an acquisition became a production product within a year (coherent)

Cadence faces transitions (1) labour to tools, (2) seats to usage, (3) simulation to formal proof, (4) open-source engines, (5) certification and (7) model makers. Its plan is to sell agents that call its certified engines more often, priced "like a virtual engineer or agent" on top of the subscription base; CEO Anirudh Devgan argues the business is "exploration limited", not seat-limited ([Q4 2025 call, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/17/cadence-cdns-q4-2025-earnings-transcript/)). Actions match words. Cadence agreed in November 2025 to buy ChipStack, a Seattle start-up that had raised a little over $7m ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/cadence-to-acquire-seattle-startup-chipstack-to-boost-chip-design-automation/)), put the ChipStack AI Super Agent into early access in February 2026 and announced a "Level-5" version with Nvidia around 1 June ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260531072918/en/Cadence-Announces-ChipStack-AI-Super-Agent)). By October it reported more than 100 engagements and said MediaTek had "deployed chip stack into its production digital verification flow" (company claim; MediaTek has not confirmed it in the sources read) ([Q3 call, 26 Oct 2026](https://www.webull.com/news/15818016766808064)). A trade report says Nvidia is the first customer using ChipStack to verify its own designs autonomously (secondary) ([ITBrief, 2 Jun 2026](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/nvidia-unveils-tools-for-enterprise-ai-agents)). Its licence terms already meter agents in "coins" ([Cadence agentic AI card terms, Sep 2026](https://www.cadence.com/content/dam/cadence-www/global/en_US/documents/terms-and-conditions/Cadence-agenticaicard-2026Sep.pdf)), and its flows were re-certified in 2026 for TSMC A16 and A14 with a TSMC executive quoted ([Cadence/TSMC, 23 Sep 2026](https://finviz.com/news/394879/cadence-collaborates-with-tsmc-to-deliver-certified-tool-solutions-and-industry-leading-silicon-proven-ualink-ip-on-tsmc-advanced-processes)) and for Intel 18A-P and 14A ([HPCwire, Jul 2026](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/cadence-certifies-ai-driven-reference-flows-for-intel-18a-p-and-intel-14a/)). The plan is self-funded (R&D of $1.77bn, 33% of 2025 revenue; free cash flow of about $1.76bn a year), and the CFO's "We're not assuming a sudden step function in AI monetization in the guide" ([Q1 call, 27 Apr 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/04/27/cadence-cdns-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/)) makes 2027-28 the plausible window. The gaps are formal proof (Jasper exists, but no formal roadmap was found and the fastest-growing verification line, emulation, is simulation-based) and the lack of a preferred frontier-lab partner comparable to GPT-Synopsys. What must go right: agents must add engine consumption rather than cannibalise licences (the February 2027 guide is the first test); certification must stay the gate for AI-generated advanced-node designs; hardware must normalise without a sharp fall; China access must survive 10 January 2027; Hexagon must become accretive in 2027; and Cadence must keep the export compliance that its 2025 settlement makes "a condition to our continued ability to export products" ([Cadence FY2025 10-K](https://s206.q4cdn.com/597110084/files/doc_downloads/2026/Mar/CDNS-FY-2025-Form-10-K.pdf)). **Verdict: coherent**, because products, pricing instruments and certifications line up with the stated plan and the plan pays for itself.

### Synopsys (1=): the moat is real, the monetisation is still announced (partly coherent, leaning coherent)

Synopsys faces all seven transitions, most acutely (2), (3), (4) and (7). Its plan is to make its certified engines the "ground truth" that agents and frontier models must call, through three routes (its own platform, its agents inside customers' AI platforms, and frontier models tuned for engineering), monetised "through subscription and consumption-based models across tools, agents, and its platform", with licence-plus-royalty terms in IP ([Investor Day, 30 Sep 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-30-Synopsys-Details-Growth-Strategy-and-Long-term-Financial-Model-at-2026-Investor-Day)). Done: the GPT-Synopsys agreement with OpenAI (revenue share, no price or availability date), the Amazon IP deal, Nvidia's $2bn equity purchase ("not exclusive"), certified flows at TSMC A14, at Intel 18A-P and 14A ([Synopsys/Intel, 27 Jul 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-07-27-Synopsys-and-Intel-Foundry-Fast-Track-Customer-Readiness-from-Silicon-to-Systems-on-Intel-14A)) and at Samsung's 2nm generations, term loans repaid ahead of plan, buybacks restarted with a $1bn accelerated repurchase, and Design IP back to growth (+11% in Q3 FY2026). Not done: the AgentEngineer verification workflow shown at DAC was still at "Customers are currently evaluating", with availability "planned for the second half of 2026" ([DAC release, 26 Jul 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-07-26-Synopsys-Showcases-Comprehensive-Autonomous-Engineering-Workflows-from-Silicon-to-Systems,-Developed-with-NVIDIA-Technology)); no consumption revenue has been disclosed; and formal proof was not mentioned on the Q3 call, where the agent story is framed around simulation coverage ([Q3 FY2026 call](https://www.webull.com/news/15497781661541376)). Funding is ample: about $2.6bn of free cash flow in FY2026 and $3.1bn guided for FY2027. The timing is plausible for FY2027-28 renewals. What must go right: a paid agent and consumption line in FY2027; EDA growth back to double digits (management expects double-digit organic EDA growth for Q4 and FY2026, which the 8-8.5% run rate of Q2-Q3 does not yet show); IP compounding at the 17%+ the Investor Day targets; the promised $400m of Ansys cost synergies; certifications held at A14 and 14A; no escalation of export controls after 10 January 2027; and customers accepting consumption on top of enterprise agreements rather than renegotiating those agreements down. **Verdict: partly coherent, leaning coherent**: the plan rests on a real moat and is funded, but agent monetisation is announced rather than demonstrated and there is no formal-proof leg for AI-edited designs.

### Siemens EDA (3): coherent at the judge layer, unproven in front-end and pricing (partly coherent)

Siemens EDA's critical transitions are (2) seats to usage, where it lags; (4) open source plus AI, which it is hedging by owning the interface; (5) certification, its strength; and (3), through Questa One's formal engines. Done: the Questa One Agentic Toolkit (27 Feb 2026, early access) exposes Siemens engines over MCP to GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, Cursor and Cline, with quotes from MediaTek and Tsavorite ([Siemens](https://news.siemens.com/fr-fr/questa-one-agentic-ai-toolkit/)); Calibre and Solido were certified for Intel 14A and 18A-P ([Siemens/Intel, 27 Jul 2026](https://news.siemens.com/en-us/siemens-intel-dac-2026)) as well as TSMC A14 and Samsung's next 2nm generation; and Siemens agreed to buy Precision Innovations, which "since 2019 has provided the bulk of software engineering and user support for OpenROAD" ([Schnitger, 20 Jul 2026](https://schnitgercorp.com/2026/07/20/siemens-to-acquire-precision-innovations-for-ai-powered-chip-planning/)), plus Defacto, Canopus AI and, in November 2026, Lauterbach. Pricing lags: named-user subscriptions continue into FY2027, and the CFO said "we plan and we will introduce an agentic surcharge on really this underlying software subscription", with credit-based models to "follow" ([Siemens analyst call, 12 Nov 2026](https://in.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/earnings-call-transcript-siemens-posts-record-orders-in-q4-2026-as-growth-quickens-93CH-5698664)). Not done: usage pricing, outside evidence of front-end share wins, packaging certifications (CoWoS-L and COUPE still under way), and a named successor to Mike Ellow. Funding is not a constraint: the parent generated EUR 12.1bn of free cash flow in FY2026 ([Siemens FY2026 release](https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:11b1a70b-f540-45bb-824f-0b3fbb927e1b/dHQCOPR202611097494EN.pdf)). What must go right: Calibre keeps primacy at A14 and beyond and extends into advanced packaging; the toolkit wins measurable share from VCS and Xcelium once agents choose tools; the surcharge captures agent use without eroding named-user revenue; leadership stabilises; and the export-control suspension holds. **Verdict: partly coherent**: coherent and funded where Siemens owns the judge, but announcements outrun proof in front-end verification and pricing.

### Arm (4): a coherent specification franchise inside a strategy that contests it (partly coherent)

Arm's revenue already scales with use, so transition (2) is behind it. Its live transitions are (4), where the open alternative is RISC-V with a Sail golden model adopted by RISC-V International and a certification programme whose first requirements document was approved on 10 Mar 2026 (search excerpt of the RISC-V wiki), and (7), where model makers so far use Arm's semantics rather than replace them: AlphaProof Code's trusted base includes about 8,000 lines of AArch64 semantics following Arm's machine-readable specification ([DeepMind paper](https://deepmind.google/documents/36/Formal_Verification_of_Signal_4.pdf)). The transition that matters most is not on the list: from neutral owner of the specification to chip vendor. Arm says it expects "up to $2B" of revenue from its own AGI CPU over FYE27-FYE28, with purchase orders through 2027 (company claim) ([Arm Q2 FYE27](https://newsroom.arm.com/news/arm-q2-fye27-results)); it published aslref v1.0, its reference implementation of the architecture specification language, on 3 Apr 2026 ([opam](https://ocaml.org/p/aslref)). On 13 Oct 2026 a jury awarded Qualcomm $2 in damages ([Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/news/privacy-compliance/144989/arm-violated-qualcomms-license-but-will-pay-only-2/)), but the remedy under Section 8.3 of the licence could cut Qualcomm's royalties on qualifying products to zero for five years; the ruling date is unknown and an FTC inquiry is reported ([EE Times, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.eetimes.com/two-dollars-and-a-test-suite/)). Funding is ample (net cash of about $3.1bn, free cash flow of $1.2bn TTM). What must go right: the chip business must not alienate licensees or invite remedies that neutralise the compliance kit, and RISC-V certification must not mature into a credible free judge. **Verdict: partly coherent.**

### AbsInt (5=): it said it would qualify a verified compiler, and did (coherent)

AbsInt is already formal, so transition (3) is behind it; certification (5) is its barrier to entry, much as foundry certification is for chip sign-off; open tools (4) and AI provers (7) are the long-run threats. It claimed and delivered CompCert's qualification for the ATR 42/72 flight-control computer under DO-178C, DO-333 and DO-330 ([AbsInt, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.absint.com/releases/260320.htm)), published CompCert 3.18 in August 2026 ([CompCert](https://compcert.org/)) and keeps shipping product releases (26.10) ([AbsInt](https://www.absint.com/)). It is self-funded and private, and its accounts for FY2006-FY2025 are filed but sit behind a paywall ([North Data](https://www.northdata.com/AbsInt+Angewandte+Informatik+GmbH,+Saarbr%C3%BCcken)). What must go right: further qualifications (other aircraft programmes, ISO 26262, and rail, where vendors say EN 50716 now makes formal methods "highly recommended" at all safety levels ([Verifysoft](https://www.verifysoft.com/en_EN_50716_Railway_Applications.html))), and a channel into AI-assisted development, since AI-written C still needs sound analysis and a trusted compiler. **Verdict: coherent**, with the caveat that its scale and finances cannot be seen.

### AdaCore (5=): hedging an Ada niche into Rust before free tools get there (coherent)

AdaCore is already in formal proof (3); its exposures are (4), where Rust plus open verifiers threaten an Ada-centred franchise, and (7). Its buyers in (6) are safety-critical makers and defence primes. It said it would broaden from Ada to Rust and to AI-assisted development, and it has shipped GNAT Pro for Rust, announced a collaboration with Carnegie Mellon's Verus team on formally proven Rust (3 Nov 2026) ([AdaCore](https://www.adacore.com/press/adacore-and-cmu-researchers-announce-collaboration-around-formal-verification-of-rust-for-high-integrity-systems)), released a demonstrator for trustworthy AI-assisted development (14 Sep 2026) ([AdaCore](https://www.adacore.com/press/adacore-releases-demonstrator-for-trustworthy-development-with-ai)), completed the CodeSecure merger and appointed a chief revenue officer ([AdaCore, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.adacore.com/press/adacore-appoints-radhika-holowko-as-chief-revenue-officer-to-drive-next-stage-of-growth)). Battery Ventures invested in March 2024 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240328498013/en)); a secondary source says it took a majority. What must go right: SPARK-style proof must carry over to Rust and to AI-written code before open tools do the same for free, and CodeSonar must hold its ground against AI code reviewers. **Verdict: coherent.**

### Harmonic (7): shipped, usage-priced and funded, but not yet shown to be paid (coherent, early)

Harmonic is already purely usage-priced (2) and sells proof (3); its open questions are (6), who pays, and (7), whether labs commoditise proof search. Its plan is to make Aristotle a tool that coding agents call: Aristotle proposes properties, engineers review and commit them, and proofs run on every commit ([Harmonic, 27 Oct 2026](https://harmonic.fun/news/aristotle-software-hardware-verification)). It shipped C and SystemVerilog verification with documentation, an SDK and an MCP server; its Tensordyne case study covers a 12.2-million-gate block with 312 pages of specifications and 241 properties, 100 of them written by Aristotle, of which 185 were proved and 56 refuted with counterexamples (company claim, with the customer's engineer quoted) ([Harmonic](https://harmonic.fun/aristotle/case-studies/tensordyne/)). It proved a RISC-V multiply-divide unit against the Sail model while being driven by Claude Code or Codex ([Harmonic, 3 Nov 2026](https://harmonic.fun/news/ibex-mul-div-verification)). Reuters reported usage-based pricing and the DIII-D fusion facility as a user, but no chip-design customer ([Reuters via Investing.com, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/robinhood-ceos-ai-startup-harmonic-expands-into-software-chip-design-verification-4976694)). Its own documentation states the limit: "Behaviors not listed as properties are not verified" ([Aristotle docs](https://aristotle.harmonic.fun/docs/overview)). It raised no new round in 2026, after $120m at $1.45bn in November 2025 ([Harmonic](https://harmonic.fun/news/series-c)). What must go right: credit revenue must grow faster than general models close the gap. A simple agent loop already matches specialised proving workflows given the same models ([arXiv 2610.35962, 25 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35962)), and both Anthropic and DeepMind verified large codebases themselves in October and November 2026, so customers must come to trust Harmonic's semantic models rather than its search alone. **Verdict: coherent (early).**

### Empyrean (8=): funded and buying, but drifting toward labour and leaning on policy (partly coherent)

Empyrean must pass (5) without TSMC or Samsung design kits, (1), where it is moving the wrong way, and (4). It plans to complete a domestic digital flow, partly by buying point-tool developers. It launched four digital verification and sign-off tools in 2025, spent RMB 859m on R&D (65% of revenue, all expensed) ([2025 annual report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-28/1225217315.PDF)), bought 6.90% of place-and-route developer X-Times for RMB 149m because domestic place-and-route "still lags the international level" ([Empyrean filing, 8 Oct 2026](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-08/1225597712.PDF)), and agreed to take a majority of Easy-Logic for RMB 218.8m ([Empyrean filing, 2 Nov 2026](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-11-02/1225650496.PDF)); its attempt to buy Xpeedic ended in July 2025. Technical services grew 54% in H1 2026 while EDA software grew 4.9% ([2026 interim report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-08-29/1225525615.PDF)). State shareholders anchor the funding (China Electronics 21.03%, Big Fund I 6.97%), with RMB 1.31bn of cash and trading assets but negative operating cash flow over nine months ([Q3 2026 report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-30/1225641220.PDF)). What must go right: accredited digital sign-off at Chinese advanced nodes, adopted by leading Chinese AI-chip makers, and controls that persist; if the affiliates-rule stay lapses, majority-owned acquisitions such as Easy-Logic could need US licences (a reading of the rule, not legal advice). **Verdict: partly coherent.**

### Primarius (8=): a global modelling niche diluted by an IP pivot (partly coherent)

Primarius passes (5) better than any Chinese peer, as a TSMC Alliance member with Samsung SPICE certifications at 3-8nm, but services doubled in 2025, against transition (1) ([2025 annual report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-16/1225108676.PDF)). Its new plan is an "EDA tools + semiconductor IP" dual engine: it bought 100% of the physical-IP firm 锐成芯微 for RMB 1.90bn and 45.64% of 纳能微 for RMB 274m, completed in July-August 2026 ([Caiwen, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.caiwennews.com/article/1552968.shtml)), lifting goodwill to RMB 1,226m and the share count by 22% ([Q3 2026 report](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-31/1225648713.PDF)), while cutting H1 2026 R&D by 7.5%. A Chinese outlet notes the main target had failed two IPO attempts and that the earn-out is loose ([Jiemian](https://m.jiemian.com/article/13434279.html)). The company itself says its product breadth "clearly lags" the big three. What must go right: integration of the IP businesses, core profitability, and a device-model niche that stays global. **Verdict: partly coherent**, because the pivot moves value away from verification.

### Certora (10): it built the agent that will eat its own audit revenue (partly coherent)

Certora faces (1), (2), (4), (6) and (7). It chose open source by releasing its prover under GPL v3 in February 2025 ([Certora](https://certora.com/blog/certora-goes-open-source)), shipped the AutoProver beta in July 2026, and in November priced it at $0 (one seat), $120 a month or from $3,000 a month, with runs paid in credits ([Certora, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.certora.com/blog/ai-formal-verification-smart-contracts-costs-use-cases)). On Aave V4, AutoProver generated 104 rules and would have caught one of two notable issues; Certora concedes that AI-written specifications may omit requirements. Revenue still looks labour-based, with about 150 audits in 2025 according to a commissioned release ([Crypto Briefing, 21 Jan 2026](https://cryptobriefing.com/certora-secures-nearly-200-billion-in-defi-value-in-2025-preventing-over-700-vulnerabilities-across-leading-protocols)); support for Rust, C/C++, Go and Java is "in development"; and no equity has been raised since a $36m round in 2022 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/147066/certora-announces-36-million-series-b-funding-round-led-by-jump-crypto)). What must go right: protocols must buy verification as credits rather than audits, and Certora must reach beyond Solidity and crypto before AI-native rivals arrive. **Verdict: partly coherent.**

### ChipAgents (11): traction without a verdict (partly coherent)

ChipAgents is itself transition (1) in action and is exposed to (4), (5) and (7). It has raised more than $134m, mostly in three Series A tranches between October 2025 and July 2026, with Micron, MediaTek and Ericsson as strategic investors and Rambus added in October ([Business Wire, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260729819576/en/); [ChipAgents newsroom](https://chipagents.ai/newsroom)), runs its own fine-tuned model, Renoir ([Crypto Briefing, 14 Oct 2026](https://cryptobriefing.com/nvidia-chipagents-ai-chip-design-evaluation/)), and is, by Nvidia's account, fine-tuning Nemotron models for "debug, formal verification, coverage and more" ([NVIDIA, 26 Jul 2026](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Expands-NVIDIA-Agent-Toolkit-With-NVIDIA-PhysicsNeMo-and-CUDA-X-Libraries-to-Transform-How-the-World-Engineers-Designs-and-Builds/default.aspx)). Pricing and revenue are undisclosed, customer evidence sits on its own pages, and no benchmark has been reproduced. The structural risk is that it drives engines whose owners now meter agent use (Cadence coins, a Siemens surcharge), and Cadence's purchase of ChipStack shows the absorption route. What must go right: it must stay the neutral multi-vendor layer and publish customer-verified outcomes. **Verdict: partly coherent.**

### Axiom Math (12): capital and results, no code product yet (partly coherent, weakest)

Axiom reports formalising the Classification of Finite Simple Groups in 43.1 million lines of Lean in 24 days ([Axiom, 26 Oct 2026](https://axiommath.ai/research/the-enormous-theorem-formalized/)) and the Stacks Project in about 16 million lines; the Stacks Project's founder asked that AI-generated pull requests not go upstream "for the time being" ([Axiom, 4 Nov 2026](https://axiommath.ai/research/the-stacks-project-formalized/)). It gives its engine away ([AXLE, 5 Mar 2026](https://axiommath.ai/research/releasing-axle)) and raised $200m at $1.6bn+ in March 2026 ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/03/12/verifiable-ai-startup-axiom-raises-200m-prove-ai-generated-code-safe-use/)). Its homepage shows no product, API, price or customer as of 12 Nov 2026, and Sacra describes the valuation as reached "despite generating no revenue" ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/axiom-math)). Its distinctive skill, proof search, is the one model makers are commoditising. What must go right: a paid code-verification product with named customers before model makers bundle verification into coding agents. **Verdict: partly coherent (the weakest in the table)**: funded, with a clear aim, but no commercial path shown.

### LTTS (13): moving from hours to outcomes, not from labour to tools (not coherent as a verification business)

LTTS has not attempted transition (1): it has no tool and no licence revenue. Its plan is AI-enabled fixed-price and outcome-based work that keeps part of the productivity gain. The CEO was plain about the pressure: "the tools are being used as part of our bids to our customers, and we are having to pass on some productivity improvement. Absolutely. Otherwise, we will not win it" ([Q1 FY27 call, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.ltts.com/system/files/2026-07/LTTS-Q1FY27-Earnings-Call-Transcript.pdf)). It has raised the fixed-price share to 35.6% from 32.1% a year earlier, lifted gross margin to 32.4% from 29.8% and started a data-centre engineering line, but semiconductors sit in the Tech segment, which fell 8.5% year on year at a 12% margin ([Q2 FY27 release, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/LTTS-Q2FY27-Investor-Release.pdf)). About Rs 4,175 crore of cash and investments funds the business but is not aimed at tools. What must go right: clients must keep letting LTTS retain part of the gain. **Verdict: not coherent as a verification business** (partly coherent as an engineering-services business).

**How much of each price rests on seats and services.** Seat exposure is concentrated in the two US incumbents, whose licences are mostly two-to-three-year terms sized by engineer seats or token pools: time-based licences were **49.5%** of Synopsys's FY2025 revenue and 40.5% in Q3 FY2026, with maintenance and service another 22-33% ([Synopsys FY2025 supplement](https://s201.q4cdn.com/778493406/files/doc_earnings/2025/q4/supplemental-info/Synopsys-Q4-FY2025-Financial-Supplement.pdf)), and Cadence's recurring revenue is about 80% with services at 9%. Neither has yet seen seat pressure in revenue: Cadence's recurring revenue grew 18% in Q3 2026 and its CFO said "we don't see AI forcing a wholesale change from subscriptions to consumption" ([Q4 2025 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/17/cadence-cdns-q4-2025-earnings-transcript/)). The market has priced the risk instead: in the January-February 2026 software sell-off on fears that "AI agents could make traditional software and per-seat pricing obsolete", Synopsys fell 15.1% and Cadence 10.9% in eight trading days ([MarketBeat, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.marketbeat.com/articles/schneider-just-paid-up-for-ptc-is-the-saaspocalypse-overdone/)), and both now trade at or below the bottom of their five-year forward-earnings ranges despite raised guidance. Siemens's EDA is also sold per named user, but EDA is a sliver of Siemens AG's value. Services carry the price of LTTS (all labour, 64% of it hourly), a rising share of Empyrean's and Primarius's revenue, and most of Certora's. Arm and Harmonic carry no seat exposure.

**Where the cycle stands, and the dates that could change the picture.** The chip market is at an extreme, memory-led high: WSTS forecast 2026 growth of 90% to $1.51 trillion ([WSTS](https://www.wsts.org/76/Recent-News-Release)), and September 2026 sales were up 146.8% year on year ([SIA, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.semiconductors.org/global-semiconductor-sales-increase-24-1-from-q2-2026-to-q3-2026/)). Share prices have begun to price a pause: the US chip-stock ETF fell 39.6% from 22 June to 5 November 2026, while over the same dates Synopsys fell only 5.2% and Cadence 19.9%, and the 5 November drop followed midterm results that raised "fears of a slowdown in the data center build-out" ([CNBC, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/06/tech-executives-ai-data-center-selloff-midterms.html)). DeepMind's AlphaProof Code announcement moved both EDA shares only 1.5% on 10 November. Meta has not yet set its 2027 budget ([Meta Q3 2026](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)), and PC makers are already adjusting 2027 memory purchases ([TrendForce, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261111-13303.html)). Because EDA revenue is mostly recurring and backlog-backed, a slowdown would show first in bookings, emulation hardware and IP. The dates to watch are Synopsys's FY2026 results and FY2027 outlook on **8 Dec 2026** ([PR Newswire, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/synopsys-announces-earnings-release-date-for-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2026-302931192.html)), the **10 Jan 2027** end of the US affiliates-rule suspension, Cadence's 2027 guidance in February 2027, the pending Arm-Qualcomm remedy ruling, and the Siemens Healthineers transfer in late spring 2027.

**Access to private and hard-to-reach names.** The best software judges (AbsInt, AdaCore) and the leading AI-native firms (Harmonic, Axiom, ChipAgents, Certora) cannot be bought by outside investors; the realistic public route is a future acquisition by a listed vendor, as with Defacto and Lauterbach going to Siemens.

# Contracts and Permits Outrun Demonstrated Power

As of 14 November 2026, Fervo Energy is the only one of the seven companies selling electricity from a new commercial plant: **its first 33 MW (net) GeoBlock at Cape Station, Utah, declared commercial operation on 30 September 2026** ([Fervo 8-K, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026064103/exhibit991pressrelease10126.htm)), against **1,054 MW of binding power purchase agreements** ([Fervo 10-Q, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026072482/frvo-20260930.htm)). The fission developers hold regulatory approvals but no operating power reactor: the NRC issued construction permits for **TerraPower's Natrium on 9 March 2026** ([NRC, Mar 2026](https://www.nrc.gov/node/2156776)) and for **Dow's four-unit X-energy plant at Long Mott on 9 November 2026** ([ANS, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8573/nrc-issues-construction-permit-for-dowxenergy-long-mott-project/)), while **Oklo's first Aurora is being authorized by DOE rather than licensed by the NRC, and its first-powerhouse target moved to "2028-2029"** ([Oklo 10-Q, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849056/000162828026071319/oklo-20260930.htm)). The fusion developers have demonstrated components (CFS's 20 T magnet, published by its own team; Helion's company-reported D-T fusion at 150 million °C; Thea's coil tests), but **no magnetic-confinement device anywhere has exceeded a scientific gain of 0.64, set by JET in 1997** ([Wurzel & Hsu, ARPA-E, Jan 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10954)). Records the companies do not control (NRC orders, CPUC resolutions, USAspending entries, counterparties' SEC filings) mostly confirm that permits, grants and deals exist; they rarely confirm performance, and **Microsoft, Constellation, Dow and Amazon do not mention their deals with Helion or X-energy in the SEC filings reviewed for this report**. Five of the seven have moved their headline demonstration or first-plant dates by roughly two years or more; Fervo met its 2026 first-power date, and Thea's 2030 date for Eos, set in December 2025, has not moved. Across the fission group the hardest documented constraint is fuel: **the only U.S. HALEU cascade had produced about 1.9 t in total by mid-June 2026** ([POWER, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.powermag.com/centrus-signs-900m-doe-contract-pivots-sole-u-s-haleu-cascade-to-commercial-operation/)), against an unsourced trade-press estimate of 15–20 t for a single Natrium first core ([Neutron Bytes, 9 Jan 2026](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/01/09/terrapower-in-mega-deal-with-meta-for-eight-natrium-345-mw-advanced-nuclear-plants/)). The sections below set out each company's facts and science with dates and sources, test the main claims against outside records and against each other, and list what could not be verified. Nothing here ranks the companies or offers an investment view.

## Fusion developers have built hardware, not net energy

All three fusion companies are measured against the same record. The best magnetic-confinement result is still **a scientific gain (Q) of 0.64, set by JET in 1997**; TFTR reached 0.27, and the ARPA-E compilation of these results notes that practical magnetic fusion likely needs Q of about 20 or more ([Wurzel & Hsu, Jan 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10954)). JET's last campaign produced a record **69.26 MJ of fusion energy in 5.2 seconds on 3 October 2023** ([DIFFER, Feb 2024](https://www.differ.nl/news/JET-energy-record)). Only laser fusion has passed breakeven: the National Ignition Facility's record shot on 7 April 2025 yielded **8.6 MJ from 2.08 MJ of laser energy, a target gain of 4.13**, which counts energy on target, not the facility's electricity use ([LLNL, accessed 14 Nov 2026](https://lmf.llnl.gov/science/achieving-fusion-ignition)). Two independent timelines frame the companies' dates. The National Academies recommended in February 2021 that DOE and industry produce net electricity in a U.S. pilot plant in **2035–2040** ([NASEM, Feb 2021](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/25991/RH-fusionpilotplant.pdf)). ITER's July 2024 re-baseline moved deuterium-tritium (D-T) operation to **2039**, with about €5 billion of extra cost under review ([Physics World, Jul 2024](https://physicsworld.com/a/iter-fusion-reactor-hit-by-massive-decade-long-delay-and-e5bn-price-hike/)).

### Commonwealth Fusion Systems: a published magnet, a later SPARC

CFS makes its own HTS magnets in Devens, Massachusetts, where it is assembling the SPARC demonstration tokamak; it calls itself "the world's largest and leading private fusion company" ([CFS, 12 Jan 2026](https://cfs.energy/news-and-media/commonwealth-fusion-systems-names-stephane-bancel-to-board-of-directors/)). Progress figures come almost entirely from CFS. On 9 April 2026 it said **about three-quarters** of the work was done, a measure blending engineering, procurement, manufacturing and assembly ([CFS blog, 9 Apr 2026](https://blog.cfs.energy/our-sparc-fusion-facility-is-now-about-75-done-take-a-virtual-tour-of-the-progress/)). The CEO said **"about 80% complete"** on 30 July 2026 ([POWER, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.powermag.com/commonwealth-fusion-systems-raises-another-1-billion-as-work-on-commercial-power-plant-continues/)) and repeated that figure on 13 November 2026 ([Semafor, 13 Nov 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/13/2026/commonwealth-fusions-ceo-sees-uae-primed-for-new-energy-tech)). The only physical count is magnets. On 6 January 2026 CFS installed the first of 18 toroidal-field (TF) magnets and said it expected to install **all 18 "by the end of summer"** ([TechCrunch, 6 Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/commonwealth-fusion-systems-installs-reactor-magnet-lands-deal-with-nvidia/)). On 22 September 2026 it posted that it was **"up to eight installed"** ([CFS on X](https://x.com/CFS_energy/status/2102395361390149897)); the post's text was read from a search excerpt and its date decoded from the post ID, and no later count was found. At the April–September pace of roughly one magnet a month, the other ten would take about nine more months before vessel closure, cool-down and commissioning (arithmetic, not a forecast). No independent progress assessment exists.

SPARC's demonstration dates have moved about two years. In September 2021 SPARC was "targeted for completion in 2025" ([MIT News, 8 Sep 2021](https://news.mit.edu/2021/MIT-CFS-major-advance-toward-fusion-energy-0908)) and, per CFS's Series B announcement, expected to show net energy in 2025 ([NEI, 8 Dec 2021](https://www.neimagazine.com/news/commonwealth-fusion-systems-raises-funds-to-commercialise-fusion-9309117/)). By December 2024 first plasma was set for 2026 ([MIT News, 17 Dec 2024](https://news.mit.edu/2024/commonwealth-fusion-systems-unveils-worlds-first-fusion-power-plant-1217)). The CEO told Congress Q>1 was "aiming for 2027" ([Mumgaard testimony, 18 Sep 2025](https://www.congress.gov/119/meeting/house/118606/witnesses/HHRG-119-SY20-Wstate-MumgaardB-20250918.pdf)). On 2 November 2026 CFS said "we expect to turn it on next year," with Q>1 to follow "rapidly" and no date given ([CFS blog, 2 Nov 2026](https://blog.cfs.energy/how-cfs-will-rev-up-our-sparc-tokamak-for-its-q1-fusion-moment/)). The public milestone is also lower than the published mission: SPARC's 2020 physics overview set a mission of **Q>2** ([Creely et al., J. Plasma Phys., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022377820001257)), while CFS's 2026 messaging targets Q>1.

ARC, the first commercial plant, is designed for **400 MWe net from about 1.1 GW of fusion power** ([CFS, 4 Jun 2026](https://cfs.energy/news-and-media/commonwealth-fusion-systems-builds-on-learnings-from-sparc-to-publish-five-peer-reviewed-papers-validating-the-physics-of-the-arc-fusion-power-plant/)). It would occupy 25–40 acres of a 94-acre Dominion-owned parcel at 1201 Battery Brooke Parkway, Chesterfield County, Virginia, which CFS holds under a lease option ([Richmond BizSense, 21 Aug 2025](https://richmondbizsense.com/2025/08/21/nuclear-fusion-plant-gets-permit-endorsement-from-chesterfield-planning-commission/)). The county approved a conditional-use permit on **17 September 2025** ([Chesterfield County, 18 Sep 2025](https://www.chesterfield.gov/m/newsflash/Archive/Item/6473?arcId=16945)). CFS filed a PJM interconnection request in April 2026 and says such studies take four to six years ([CFS blog, 28 Apr 2026](https://blog.cfs.energy/going-to-the-grid-cfs-applies-to-plug-our-first-arc-fusion-power-plant-into-pjm/)). As of 30 July 2026 the county had **no site plan or building permit** on file ([Richmond BizSense, 30 Jul 2026](https://richmondbizsense.com/2026/07/30/commonwealth-fusion-secures-1b-capital-raise-to-set-stage-for-chesterfield-power-plant/)). While SPARC's dates moved later, CFS's stated ARC construction start moved earlier: "late 2020s" in August 2025 ([Richmond BizSense, 21 Aug 2025](https://richmondbizsense.com/2025/08/21/nuclear-fusion-plant-gets-permit-endorsement-from-chesterfield-planning-commission/)), 2028 in September 2025 testimony ([Mumgaard testimony](https://www.congress.gov/119/meeting/house/118606/witnesses/HHRG-119-SY20-Wstate-MumgaardB-20250918.pdf)), and 2027 in April 2026 ([Reuters via AJOT, 22 Apr 2026](https://www.ajot.com/news/first-commercial-fusion-plant-nears-construction-in-us-commonwealth-ceo-says)). POWER paraphrased the CEO in July 2026 as saying the grid interconnection is secured ([POWER, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.powermag.com/commonwealth-fusion-systems-raises-another-1-billion-as-work-on-commercial-power-plant-continues/)), which does not match CFS's own description of a filed request facing a multi-year study; no executed interconnection agreement or queue position was found. CFS has given no ARC cost beyond "several billion dollars" ([TechCrunch, 6 Jan 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/commonwealth-fusion-systems-installs-reactor-magnet-lands-deal-with-nvidia/)). **The science under CFS.** Its central demonstrated result is magnet hardware. On 5 September 2021 the MIT–CFS toroidal-field model coil reached **20.1 T peak field on conductor at 40.5 kA**, with nearly 1 GPa of stress in the case and joints of 0.5–2.0 nΩ ([Hartwig et al., arXiv:2308.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12301)); the results were published in six peer-reviewed papers in IEEE Transactions on Applied Superconductivity in March 2024. In later 2021 tests, an intentional open-circuit quench at 31.5 kA melted a corner of one of the coil's 16 pancakes, "a few percent" of coil volume according to MIT's Dennis Whyte, and led to design changes in SPARC's magnets. That damage became public about two years after the test ([MIT News, 4 Mar 2024](https://news.mit.edu/2024/tests-show-high-temperature-superconducting-magnets-fusion-ready-0304)). The magnet work is peer-reviewed but authored by MIT and CFS teams, and no other organization has replicated it at that scale. No primary results for CFS's central-solenoid model coil were found.

SPARC's plasma prediction extrapolates established tokamak scaling to higher field. At 12.2 T and 8.7 MA, SPARC is projected to reach **Q≈11 by empirical scaling and Q≈9 by physics-based modelling, with Q>2 "even with adverse assumptions"** ([Rodriguez-Fernandez et al., J. Plasma Phys., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022377820001075)). These papers have co-authors from outside institutions, including Chalmers and ORNL ([Creely et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022377820001257)). The step is large. SPARC's projected triple product, 6.2×10²¹ keV·s·m⁻³, is about eight times the best tokamak value on record in the ARPA-E compilation's uniform convention (TFTR's 7.9×10²⁰ in 1995), and Q≈11 would be about 17 times JET's 0.64 ([Wurzel & Hsu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10954)).

Several things remain undemonstrated. **Disruptions:** SPARC's disruption loads are "comparable to, and in some cases higher than, ITER," no passive solution exists for the thermal-quench heat flux on the divertor, and runaway-electron models disagree ([Sweeney et al., J. Plasma Phys., 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-plasma-physics/article/mhd-stability-and-disruptions-in-the-sparc-tokamak/908C6788C0D625C5DDF335DBD9A17476)). A November 2026 preprint finds that a passive coil "can reduce the RE current by over an order-of-magnitude," but only in simulation ([Datta et al., arXiv:2611.04906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04906)). **HTS under neutrons:** whether the tape tolerates irradiation at cryogenic temperature is disputed in 2025 work ([Fischer et al., 2025](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1450988/contributions/6259629); [MIT DSpace, 2025](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/164909)). **Tritium breeding:** ARC's breeding ratio of at least 1.1 in molten FLiBe exists only in calculation ([Sorbom et al., 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3540)). MIT measured a breeding ratio of 3.57×10⁻⁴ in a 100 mL surrogate salt ([Delaporte-Mathurin et al., Nucl. Fusion, 2025](https://iris.polito.it/bitstream/11583/2996739/1/Delaporte-Mathurin_2025_Nucl._Fusion_65_026037.pdf)), improving six-fold at 1 L ([arXiv:2509.26174, 30 Sep 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26174)). EPRI's Andrew Sowder said in August 2026: "Until there is actual evidence of net energy gain from a demonstration, none can be called a winner," calling blanket technology "immature globally" ([Utility Dive, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/what-a-billion-dollar-funding-means-for-commonwealth-fusion-systems-and-its/828515/)).

On whether the science stands on solid ground: CFS's concept rests on decades of tokamak confinement data and a published 20 T magnet whose quench damage was also reported. That is solid ground for building SPARC. For a power plant it is not yet solid: a tokamak gain above 1, disruption mitigation at SPARC's current, HTS that survives neutrons, and tritium breeding are all untested.

### Helion: an operating prototype with unpublished performance data

Helion describes its approach as pulsed, non-ignition fusion in a field-reversed configuration (FRC), fuelled by deuterium and helium-3, with electricity "captured directly" as the plasma expands against the magnetic field and "No steam cycle" ([Helion, Polaris page, accessed 14 Nov 2026](https://helionenergy.com/polaris)). Its seventh prototype, Polaris, has run in Everett, Washington, since the end of 2024. The company lists it as 19 m long with a "50+ MJ" capacitor bank and fields of "15+ T." On 13 February 2026 Helion announced that Polaris had produced **"measurable" D-T fusion and plasma temperatures of 150 million °C** in January ([Helion, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.helionenergy.com/newsroom/helion-achieves-new-fusion-energy-milestones)). It gave **no neutron yield, gain or electricity figure**, and CEO David Kirtley declined to say whether Polaris had reached scientific breakeven ([TechCrunch, 13 Feb 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/13/fusion-startup-helion-hits-blistering-temps-as-it-races-toward-2028-deadline/)). Two outside scientists commented after seeing data, Ryan McBride (University of Michigan) and DOE's Jean Paul Allain; neither described a formal verification ([POWER, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.powermag.com/helion-announces-fusion-milestone-moves-closer-to-commercial-deployment/)). Helion's own measurement note says its scintillators **saturated** on the first D-T pulse, diamond detectors recorded 14.1 MeV neutron peaks, and "Ongoing analysis will refine the exact yield numbers" ([Helion blog, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.helionenergy.com/blog/how-we-conducted-and-measured-d-t-fusion)). The Polaris web page still presents recovering "a portion" of fusion energy to the capacitor bank as a goal ([Helion, Polaris page](https://helionenergy.com/polaris)). Scientific American reported in May 2026 that "No published results have confirmed net power generation from Polaris" ([Scientific American, 19 May 2026](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/helion-energy-is-building-a-fusion-power-plant-can-its-technology-deliver/)).

Helion's dated predictions have not been met. Retired PPPL physicist Daniel Jassby quoted three early claims: "profitable fusion energy in 2019" (2014); "net energy gain within 24 months, and 50-MWe pilot plant by 2019" (2015); and breakeven "in less than three years" (2018) ([APS Physics & Society, Apr 2019](https://engage.aps.org/fps/resources/newsletters/april-2019)). At its November 2021 Series E, Polaris was to show net electricity "as early as 2024" ([New Atlas, 8 Nov 2021](https://newatlas.com/energy/helion-net-electricity-nuclear-fusion-polaris/)). In June 2023 Kirtley told Congress Polaris construction would finish "in early 2024" ([Kirtley testimony, 13 Jun 2023](https://republicans-science.house.gov/_cache/files/7/8/78df2714-ff8f-484f-99c3-0f0308f83e68/72AFD82F88650354FE96FD00A7D605431FB3020F7F12323163E80E02722DA395.2023-06-12-kirtley-testimony.pdf)); it began operating at the end of 2024. In July 2025 Helion hoped to show electricity from Polaris "as soon as this year" ([E&E News, 31 Jul 2025](https://www.eenews.net/articles/startup-begins-work-on-major-us-fusion-power-plant-yes-fusion/)); no such result had been announced by 14 November 2026. The goal language has narrowed from "net electricity" (2021) to "production of electricity" (2023) to recovering "a portion" of fusion energy (2026). Helion's FAQ now says the 2021 term "didn't align with the commercial-driven goals" ([Helion, Polaris page](https://helionenergy.com/polaris)).

Orion is Helion's first commercial plant. **The science under Helion.** FRC formation, merging and compression have a real experimental lineage. The founders' predecessor device was published in Nuclear Fusion in 2011 ([Slough et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1088/0029-5515/51/5/053008)), and TAE's FRC program has peer-reviewed results ([Schmitz et al., Nat. Commun., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13860)). Helion's own published record is thin. It consists of one peer-reviewed company paper, a 2023 modelling study with no experimental data ([Kirtley & Milroy, J. Fusion Energy, 13 Jul 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10894-023-00367-7)), and two abstracts at the 2024 APS plasma physics meeting, where CFS, TAE and Zap submitted dozens each ([Bloomberg via Energy Connects](https://www.energyconnects.com/news/renewables/2024/july/altman-s-37-billion-fusion-startup-leaves-scientists-puzzled)).

Two independent peer-reviewed papers in 2026 dispute the physics of Helion's gain case, which relies on ions staying much hotter than electrons. A formal comment by Max Planck and EPFL physicists, published on 7 February 2026, finds that at reactor conditions **collisional transfer of power from ions to electrons exceeds fusion power by more than 10 times at an ion temperature of 100 keV and by more than 100 times at 40 keV**; the authors conclude that a D-He3 FRC reactor is "not ruled out" but faces much tighter requirements on plasma lifetime, losses and conversion efficiency ([Lackner et al., J. Fusion Energy, 7 Feb 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10894-026-00554-2)). A CNRS–EPFL paper of 25 March 2026 finds that **confinement longer than 10 ms is needed for peak fields below 25 T** ([Nicolas et al., J. Fusion Energy, 25 Mar 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10894-026-00565-z)); Helion's own figures for its sixth prototype, Trenta, as relayed by Wikipedia, give confinement of "up to 0.5 ms" ([Wikipedia, citing Helion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helion_Energy)). Helion replies that the critics ignore its pulse cadence, and that the question is "whether the pulse evolves quickly enough" ([Scientific American, 19 May 2026](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/helion-energy-is-building-a-fusion-power-plant-can-its-technology-deliver/)).

The fuel itself raises the bar. D-He3 needs a triple product of **2.2×10²² keV·s·m⁻³ at 63 keV just to reach fuel breakeven** ([Wurzel & Hsu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10954)). That is about 28 times the best D-T tokamak value ever recorded and roughly 100,000 times the best published FRC value (TAE's C-2W, 2.3×10¹⁷). Helion calls about 200 million °C (about 17 keV) its "sweet spot" ([TechCrunch, 13 Feb 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/13/fusion-startup-helion-hits-blistering-temps-as-it-races-toward-2028-deadline/)). The 2026 figure of 150 million °C is not identified as an ion or electron temperature. Helion's 2021 Trenta figure was a "total" ion-plus-electron temperature, a point seen only in a search summary of the release ([Helion, 22 Jun 2021](https://helionenergy.com/newsroom/helion-energy-achieves-100-million-degrees-celsius-fusion-fuel-temperature-and-confirms-16-month-continuous-operation-of-its-fusion-generator-prototype)).

Efficiency and durability are also unverified. Helion claims over 95% energy-recovery efficiency ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/helion-energy-is-building-a-fusion-power-plant-can-its-technology-deliver/)), while its own blog cites "circuit efficiencies above 90%" ([Helion blog, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.helionenergy.com/blog/measuring-electricity-production-from-fusion-electrical-diagnostics)). No independent measurement exists. Operating at the planned one pulse per second means about 32 million pulses a year. Trenta logged a company-reported 10,000 pulses over 16 months ([New Atlas, 8 Nov 2021](https://newatlas.com/energy/helion-net-electricity-nuclear-fusion-polaris/)), so this is a gap of roughly 3,000-fold (arithmetic). ORNL's Troy Carter says direct recovery is "a real advantage… if it works," but "They don't publish" ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/helion-energy-is-building-a-fusion-power-plant-can-its-technology-deliver/)).

On whether the science stands on solid ground: the plasma configuration is established physics. The path to net electricity is not. No one has demonstrated D-He3 fusion at the needed conditions, nor the required ion-electron imbalance, the claimed recovery efficiency, or pulse durability. Helion's performance data are unpublished, and the only peer-reviewed analyses of its gain case dispute it.

### Thea Energy: peer-reviewed designs and coil tests, no plasma and no site

Thea was founded in 2022 as Princeton Stellarators, a spin-off of PPPL and Princeton University. It is built on planar-coil technology developed at PPPL under ARPA-E's BETHE program ([Thea, history page](https://thea.energy/history/)). In January 2025 it moved into a headquarters of about 15,000 sq ft in Kearny, New Jersey ([Thea, 21 Jan 2025](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-announces-new-headquarters-to-support-core-technology-development-and-manufacturing/)). Thea has no plasma device; its hardware results are magnet tests. The **Canis 3×3 HTS coil array**, operated at 20 K and up to ±140 A, reproduced field shapes within 1% of simulation (announced 20 March 2025) ([Thea, 20 Mar 2025](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-demonstrates-performance-and-controllability-of-small-and-simple-magnets-for-fusion-energy/)) and was published in IEEE Transactions on Applied Superconductivity ([IEEE TAS, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASC.2025.3594533)). A **full-size Eos-spec shaping coil reached above 6 T at 20 K** on 13 May 2026, with no current, data or publication released and quench testing still pending ([Thea, 13 May 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-announces-successful-operation-of-full-scale-planar-shaping-coils/)). The large encircling coil has not been tested; MIT's Plasma Science and Fusion Center will test a full-scale one, with no date given ([Thea, 4 Nov 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-collaborates-with-mits-plasma-science-and-fusion-center-on-planar-coil-magnet-program/); [ANS, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-11-06/article-8563/thea-energy-mit-collaborate-on-testing-stellarator-magnet-technology/)).

DOE's January 2025 record lists two Thea milestones under its Milestone program as met: down-selecting a family of stellarator equilibria, and building and operating a single HTS coil ([DOE, 16 Jan 2025](https://www.energy.gov/articles/us-department-energy-announces-selectees-107-million-fusion-innovation-research-engine)). On 13 January 2026 Thea announced that DOE had certified its **Helios** preconceptual pilot-plant design. The review used an expert panel, a site visit and a 200-page report, and the only panelist named is PPPL's Felix Parra-Diaz ([Thea, 13 Jan 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/u-s-department-of-energy-certifies-thea-energys-fusion-pilot-plant-preconceptual-design/)). No DOE-issued announcement was found. USAspending does show a "Continuation" action on 16 March 2026 and new obligations of $6.2M (9 Jun 2026) and $11.515M (28 Sep 2026) ([USAspending](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_DESC0024881_089)).

Helios is designed as a 1.1 GW-thermal plant with **390 MWe net and an 88% capacity factor**, using 12 encircling and 324 shaping coils ([Swanson et al., arXiv:2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)). Thea's releases round these to "about 400 MWe" and "above 85%" ([Thea, 15 Dec 2025](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-completes-fusion-power-plant-preconceptual-design/)). The design appeared as a 16-paper special issue of Fusion Engineering and Design, with every paper first-authored by Thea staff and external guest editors overseeing the issue ([Thea, 9 Sep 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-publishes-the-most-practical-fusion-power-plant-design-and-a-clear-path-toward-energy-on-the-grid/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/special-issue/10L70JMWRNC)).

In May it still expected to choose "later in 2026" ([Thea, 27 May 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-raises-100-million-series-b-funding-to-build-scalable-fusion-power-plants/)). On 18 August it had made no selection ([Thea, 18 Aug 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-extends-series-b-funding-to-deploy-efficient-and-economical-fusion-power/)), and its press index shows none through 4 November 2026 ([Thea press releases](https://thea.energy/press-release/)).

Thea has **no customer, offtake or utility agreement**, only "advanced talks" ([Thea, 18 Aug 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-extends-series-b-funding-to-deploy-efficient-and-economical-fusion-power/)). CEO Brian Berzin and CTO David Gates co-founded the company. With no site, Thea holds no permits. It filed a comment on the NRC's proposed fusion rule on 27 May 2026, whose content could not be retrieved ([Regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov/comment/NRC-2023-0071-0098)).

**The science under Thea.** Thea's bet is on magnets, not new plasma physics. Optimized-stellarator confinement has strong independent support. In 2025 Wendelstein 7-X set a long-pulse triple-product record over 43 seconds, sustained 1.8 GJ of energy turnover over 360 seconds, and reached a plasma pressure ratio (β) of 3%, against the 4–5% a plant would need ([IPP, Jun 2025](https://www.ipp.mpg.de/5532945/w7x)).

The unproven element is replacing twisted modular coils with flat ones. Thea's own analysis says the shaping coils supply about 3% of the field and must be accurate to about 1%, which works out to about 3×10⁻⁴ of the total field. That is looser than the conventional 10⁻⁴ tolerance and works only with configurations optimized to tolerate field errors ([Canis preprint, Mar 2025](https://thea.energy/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Preprint_Prototyping-and-Test-of-the-Canis-HTS-PlanarCoil-Array-for-Stellarator-Field-Shaping.pdf)). Canis showed ±1% control around a 1.8 T bore field. Eos shaping coils must work inside a background field above 10 T, with more than 14 T on the conductor, according to the same document. The single coil Thea has announced reached above 6 T.

The peer-reviewed description of Eos is a **"sub-breakeven, deuterium-deuterium, beam-target"** neutron source producing about 2.5×10¹⁷ neutrons a second ([Swanson et al., Nucl. Fusion, Feb 2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ada56a)). Thea's website gives Eos a triple product of 10²¹ keV·s·m⁻³ ([Thea, Eos page](https://thea.energy/eos/)). That figure is about 16 times the best published W7-X value in the ARPA-E compilation ([Wurzel & Hsu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10954)), and Thea's papers do not explain it. Thea's own Helios overview flags four open problems: divertor heat flux above an assumed 10 MW/m² limit, about 6.6% of alpha-particle energy lost to the wall, an assumed confinement enhancement (ISS04×1.4) that "should be justified," and a vanadium first wall with uncertain behaviour under irradiation ([arXiv:2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)).

On whether the science stands on solid ground: the stellarator physics Thea borrows is increasingly well supported by experiment. Its planar-coil architecture has not yet confined any plasma, and Helios is a reviewed paper design, not an experimentally validated one.

### Limits every fusion plan shares

Several plant-level prerequisites are undemonstrated for every D-T concept. Measured tritium breeding in litre-scale molten-salt experiments is at the 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ level ([arXiv:2509.26174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26174)). That compares with calculated breeding ratios of 1.1 for ARC ([Sorbom et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3540)) and an idealized 1.3 for Helios ([arXiv:2512.08027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027)). Materials testing under fusion-spectrum neutrons awaits IFMIF-DONES in Granada. Preparatory works for its main building began in September 2026, with substantial completion targeted for 2029 ([IFMIF-DONES](https://ifmif-dones.es/?p=22362)). Tritium for start-up depends on output from CANDU reactors, which is sufficient for ITER but "largely uncertain" for later plants ([Pearson et al., Fusion Eng. Des., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fusengdes.2018.04.090)).

Helion's D-He3 route still handles tritium. It plans to make helium-3 from D-D reactions and from tritium decay ([SiliconANGLE, 5 Jun 2026](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/05/fusion-power-startup-helion-valued-15-5b-465m-funding-round/)). With tritium's 12.32-year half-life, only about 5.6% of a stockpile decays each year (arithmetic).

In the United States, fusion machines are regulated under byproduct-material rules rather than reactor licensing, with Agreement States such as Massachusetts and Washington issuing the licenses ([ANS, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-09/article-8374/fia-white-paper-guides-agreement-states-on-fusion-regulation/)). The Federal Register held no final version of the NRC's proposed rule as of 14 November 2026 ([Federal Register API](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions%5Bregulation_id_number%5D=3150-AL00)). Under the Nuclear Energy Innovation and Modernization Act (NEIMA), the statutory deadline is 31 December 2027 ([Federal Register, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-26/html/2026-03865.htm)). DOE's Milestone approvals for CFS and Thea review paper designs and roadmaps, not plasma performance ([CFS blog, 5 Aug 2026](https://blog.cfs.energy/sparc-progress-doe-milestone-approvals-reflect-cfs-power-plant-maturity/)). The National Academies panel that set the 2035–2040 pilot-plant goal included MIT's Dennis Whyte, a CFS co-founder ([NASEM, Feb 2021](https://nap.nationalacademies.org/resource/25991/RH-fusionpilotplant.pdf); [MIT News, 17 Dec 2024](https://news.mit.edu/2024/commonwealth-fusion-systems-unveils-worlds-first-fusion-power-plant-1217)).

## Fission developers hold permits, not yet fuel or operating licenses

An NRC construction permit establishes that a plant can be built under its stated design basis; operation requires a separate operating license, or, for Oklo's first unit, DOE authorization. The historical record behind each reactor type is mixed. Small sodium-cooled test reactors worked: in tests on 3 April 1986, EBR-II shut itself down from full power, without operator action, after a loss of flow and after a loss of heat sink ([Chang et al., ANL/OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/5428837)). Larger sodium reactors outside Russia had very low lifetime load factors, among them Superphénix at under 7% and the UK's PFR at 20.6%, with recurrent sodium leaks and fires ([IPFM, Feb 2010](https://fissilematerials.org/library/rr08.pdf)). Russia's BN-600 averaged a 74.25% load factor through 2017, after 27 sodium leaks and 14 fires by 1997 ([IPPE, Dec 2018](https://gif.jaea.go.jp/webinar/Series24/gifiv_webinar_pakhomov_19_dec_2018_final.pdf)), and BN-800 averaged 66.4% over 2016–2025 ([WNA/IAEA PRIS data](https://world-nuclear.org/nuclear-reactor-database/details/BELOYARSK-4)). Pebble-bed gas reactors have a similar split. Germany's AVR ran at core temperatures higher than predicted, detected only about a year before shutdown because "a pebble bed core cannot yet be equipped with instruments" ([Moormann, Jülich, 2008](https://hdl.handle.net/2128/3136)), and THTR-300 closed in 1989 after 423 full-load days ([GRS, 11 Oct 2024](https://www.grs.de/en/news/pebble-bed-reactor-shidaowan-nuclear-power-plant-inherently-safe-reactor)). China's HTR-PM has been in commercial operation since 5 December 2023 and passed loss-of-cooling tests from 200 MWt in 2023 ([Zhang et al., Joule, Jul 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joule.2024.06.014)), but posted **load factors of 15.6% in 2024 and 43.8% in 2025**, at a reference rating of 150 MWe against a 200 MWe design ([WNA/PRIS data](https://world-nuclear.org/nuclear-reactor-database/details/Shidaowan-HTR-PM-1)).

### TerraPower: Natrium is under construction without a lead contractor

Natrium is a **345 MWe (840 MWt) sodium-cooled fast reactor** paired with molten-salt storage that can deliver 100–500 MWe for up to about 5.5 hours. The first unit, Kemmerer Unit 1, sits beside PacifiCorp's Naughton plant in Wyoming ([PacifiCorp, Oregon PUC filing, 21 Oct 2025](https://edocs.puc.state.or.us/efdocs/HAA/haa340991027.pdf); [POWER, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/)). The NRC review ran from submission on 28 March 2024 to construction permit CPAR-1 on **9 March 2026**, issued to TerraPower's subsidiary US SFR Owner ([NRC, Mar 2026](https://www.nrc.gov/node/2156776)). The permit came three months after the NRC's accelerated end-2025 goal but five months ahead of its original schedule, and ANS called it the first approval of a commercial non-light-water reactor in more than 40 years ([ANS, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/article-7818/nrc-approves-terrapower-construction-permit/)).

The Commission's order describes an **uncontested proceeding**. It records four exemptions granted by NRC staff, covering HALEU fuel and criticality-accident rules, emergency core cooling analysis, financial qualifications, and use of the NEI 18-04 safety-classification method, and a proposed permit condition requiring annual R&D reports on the sodium-to-salt heat exchanger ([NRC CLI-26-5, 4 Mar 2026](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2606/ML26063A399.pdf)).

Construction has begun but is early. Non-nuclear works started in June 2024, and official nuclear construction began on **23 April 2026** ([POWER, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/)). By 15 October 2026 the reactor excavation was "about halfway to its 120-foot depth" ([Cowboy State Daily, 16 Oct 2026](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/10/16/bill-gates-tours-kemmerers-natrium-nuclear-facility-and-sees-something-profound/)). No safety-related concrete pour had been reported.

**Bechtel, the engineering-procurement-construction (EPC) contractor since 2020, left the project in September 2026.** A WARN notice dated 15 September 2026 said the two firms "were unable to reach an agreement on the next phase" ([ANS, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-21/article-8417/terrapower-bechtel-part-ways-on-natrium-project/)). TerraPower is rebidding the contract and says the schedule holds ([ENR, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.enr.com/articles/63689-terrapower-reopens-epc-competition-for-4b-natrium-nuclear-project)). As of mid-October no successor had been named, and Kemmerer's plans to house up to 1,600 construction workers were "in limbo" ([Cowboy State Daily, 13 Oct 2026](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/10/13/kemmerer-plans-for-1-600-nuclear-plant-workers-in-limbo-after-contractor-backs-out/)).

The operating date has moved three years. In November 2021 the target for commercial operation was **2028** ([POWER timeline, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/)). In December 2022 TerraPower announced "a minimum of a two-year delay" because non-Russian HALEU was unavailable ([NEI Magazine, Dec 2022](https://www.neimagazine.com/news/terrapowers-natrium-reactor-project-delayed-by-lack-of-haleu-10443311/)). The public target is now construction complete in 2030 and commercial operation in 2031 ([ANS, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-21/article-8417/terrapower-bechtel-part-ways-on-natrium-project/)). The NRC record says US SFR Owner "expects to complete construction by February 28, 2031" ([CLI-26-5](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2606/ML26063A399.pdf)). The offtaker, PacifiCorp, projects commercial operation in fall 2031, and its resource-plan modelling uses 2032 ([PacifiCorp, 21 Oct 2025](https://edocs.puc.state.or.us/efdocs/HAA/haa340991027.pdf)).

The cost has been stated as **about $4 billion since 2021, with the government providing $2 billion** ([Reuters Events, 23 Jun 2026](https://efifoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2026/06/Reuters-US-cost-sharing-thrusts-next-gen-nuclear-into-construction-phase.pdf)). That is roughly $11,600 per kWe on a total-project basis (computed). DOE's March 2023 Liftoff report put a well-executed first-of-a-kind cost at about $6,200/kW ([DOE Liftoff, Mar 2023](https://liftoff.energy.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/20230320-Liftoff-Advanced-Nuclear-vPUB-0329-Update.pdf)), but the two figures are not like-for-like, because Natrium's includes the storage island and first-of-a-kind engineering.

The federal share is reported three ways. GAO said DOE awarded "nearly $2 billion" ([GAO-22-105394, Sep 2022](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-22-105394)), POWER said "up to $1.6 billion" ([POWER, 22 Feb 2024](https://www.powermag.com/doe-kairos-unveil-milestone-based-funding-agreement-for-advanced-nuclear-demonstration-project/)), and most other coverage says up to $2 billion. A USAspending query on 14 November 2026 found no Advanced Reactor Demonstration Program (ARDP) award under TerraPower's or US SFR Owner's name. It found only a $2.47M ARPA-E agreement and a $0.49M DOE-NE agreement ([USAspending](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_DEAR0001612_089); [USAspending](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_DENE0008924_089)), so the amounts disbursed cannot be checked there. GAO's 2022 priority recommendation that DOE institutionalize oversight of these demonstrations remained open at its last update ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-22-105394)).

Fuel is the weakest documented link. DOE gave TerraPower a conditional HALEU allocation in April 2025, of undisclosed quantity ([WNN, 10 Apr 2025](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/doe-selects-first-recipients-of-haleu)). The only disclosed first-core contract is with ASP Isotopes' unit Quantum Leap Energy (QLE). In May 2025 TerraPower agreed to lend it up to **$22.0M**, with disbursements conditional on South African enrichment licences, and signed two supply agreements: one for "the required first fuel cores" of the Wyoming plant, the other for up to 150 t of HALEU in 2028–2037 ([ASP Isotopes 10-Q, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1921865/000119312526352603/aspi-20260630.htm)).

The supplier's own filings qualify that contract. On 15 October 2026 ASP Isotopes said it **"has not applied its enrichment technologies to the enrichment of U-235."** The same filing says its Pelindaba joint venture with South Africa's nuclear agency, Necsa, had reached only a heads-of-agreement stage ([ASP Isotopes 8-K, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1921865/000119312526422325/aspi-ex99_1.htm)). Centrus's FY2025 10-K does not mention TerraPower ([Centrus 10-K, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1065059/000162828026007117/leu-20251231.htm)). Wyoming's governor's office said in October 2026 that the fuel problems "have all been solved" ([Cowboy State Daily, 16 Oct 2026](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/10/16/bill-gates-tours-kemmerers-natrium-nuclear-facility-and-sees-something-profound/)), a claim not supported by the supplier's filings.

Fabrication is planned at a GNF-A facility in Wilmington, North Carolina, which has received more than $200M through ARDP. Framatome made the first metallic uranium "pucks" for Natrium in November 2025 ([POWER, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/)).

TerraPower is private and discloses no revenue, margins, cash or debt. Its known equity rounds are a minimum **$750M raise in 2022** that included $250M from SK Inc. and SK Innovation ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/)) and a **$650M round on 18 June 2025**, with NVentures (NVIDIA) as the new investor and no valuation disclosed ([TerraPower, 18 Jun 2025](https://www.terrapower.com/terrapower-announces-650-million-fundraise)). KHNP became a shareholder in January 2026 by buying part of SK Innovation's stake, after a review by the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) closed in December 2025 ([WNN, 21 Jan 2026](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/khnp-takes-stake-in-terrapower)). Estimates of cumulative capital conflict: more than $1.4B ([Barron's, via syndication, Jun 2025](https://www.itiger.com/news/2544545907)) versus more than $2.2B since 2022 ([POWER, Apr 2026](https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/)). No Form D under the TerraPower name was found ([EDGAR, 14 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?company=terrapower&type=&dateb=&owner=include&count=40&action=getcompany)). Bill Gates has chaired the company since 2006 and Chris Levesque is CEO ([WNN, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/terrapower-starts-construction-of-first-us-utility-scale-advanced-nuclear-plant)). Eric Williams became COO and Steven Hellman, formerly of UBS, became CFO in 2025 ([TerraPower, 21 May 2025](https://www.terrapower.com/terrapower-announces-expansion-of-executive-leadership-team)).

The only executed offtake contract found is with PacifiCorp. Its filing describes **a 40-year PPA for KU1's output that is "fully executed" but not effective until state commission approvals**, which it says must arrive by 31 December 2026 to meet ARDP and NRC financial-qualification requirements. It warns that delay "would jeopardize the federal funding and potentially terminate the project entirely" ([PacifiCorp, 21 Oct 2025](https://edocs.puc.state.or.us/efdocs/HAA/haa340991027.pdf)). Prices are redacted. PacifiCorp's 2023 resource plan had selected two more Natrium units by 2033 ([PacifiCorp, 31 Mar 2023](https://www.pacificorp.com/about/newsroom/news-releases/additional-Natrium-reactors.html)); its final 2025 plan keeps only Kemmerer ([Clearing Up, 2025](https://www.newsdata.com/clearing_up/supply_and_demand/pacificorp-files-final-2025-integrated-resource-plan/article_361f3863-8037-4288-87b2-b00956408238.html)).

Meta's own announcement of 9 January 2026 says its funding "supports the development of two new Natrium units," up to 690 MW as early as 2032, with "rights for energy from up to six other Natrium units," 2.1 GW by 2035. It gives no dollar figure or site ([Meta, 9 Jan 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/01/meta-nuclear-energy-projects-power-american-ai-leadership/)). TerraPower's headline described an "Agreement for 8 Natrium Advanced Nuclear Plants" ([TerraPower via PR Newswire, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/terrapower-and-meta-enter-agreement-for-8-natrium-advanced-nuclear-plants-302657043.html)). Its other agreements are earlier-stage: non-binding memoranda of understanding with Utah ([Deseret News, 25 Aug 2025](https://www.deseret.com/utah/2025/08/25/nuclear-utah-energy-cox-terrapower/)) and with Kansas and Evergy ([Axios Kansas City, 3 Oct 2025](https://www.axios.com/local/kansas-city/2025/10/03/kansas-could-get-next-gen-nuclear-power)), and an EPC framework under which Hyundai E&C would build up to eight units with "completion, price, and performance guarantees" ([ANS, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-08-17/article-8297/xenergy-terrapower-share-updates-on-advanced-reactor-projects/)).

TerraPower has two other lines of business. Its Molten Chloride Reactor Experiment at INL is expected to run in 2028 ([ANS, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.ans.org/news/2025-12-08/article-7602/inl-makes-first-fuel-for-molten-chloride-reactor-experiment/)), after an earlier target of "as early as 2027" ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/fast-spectrum-molten-chloride-reactor-test)); DOE's 2023 plan to fuel it with highly enriched uranium drew criticism ([Science, 2023](https://www.science.org/content/article/u-s-planning-test-reactor-run-weapons-grade-uranium)). TerraPower Isotopes broke ground on a $450M actinium-225 plant in Philadelphia on 6 May 2026, targeting production to pharmaceutical (cGMP) standards in 2029, with $10M of Pennsylvania grants ([ANS, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-03-19/article-7853/terrapower-announces-second-ac225-production-facility/)).

The legal and regulatory record is otherwise light. UK regulators accepted Natrium into their Generic Design Assessment on 19 February 2026 ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/terrapowers-kemmerer-1-enters-construction-timeline-of-the-natrium-projects-road-to-first-power/)). A 2015 plan to build a prototype in China with China National Nuclear Corporation was abandoned in January 2019 under U.S. technology-transfer limits ([Caixin, 5 Jan 2019](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2019-01-05/nuclear-power-trial-in-china-will-not-proceed-101366789.html)). No lawsuits were found.

**The science under Natrium.** The reactor physics and fuel type draw on a real record. Metallic uranium-zirconium and uranium-plutonium-zirconium pins reached 15–20% burnup in EBR-II and FFTF, made possible by a 75% smear density that leaves room for the fuel to swell ([PNNL-hosted review](https://www.pnnl.gov/sites/default/files/media/file/Irradiation%20Behavior%20of%20Metallic%20Fast%20Reactor%20Fuels.pdf)). Natrium's first "Type 1" fuel deliberately resembles those pins. The NRC has given only "feedback," not approval, on TerraPower's fuel-qualification topical reports ([NRC Natrium pre-application page, updated 15 Sep 2026](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/new-reactors/advanced/who-were-working-with/pre-application-activities/natrium.html)).

The NRC's Advisory Committee on Reactor Safeguards (ACRS) supported the permit on 16 November 2025. It credited two passive heat-removal paths and diverse shutdown systems, and noted that safe shutdown relies on neither electrical power nor operators ([ACRS letter, 16 Nov 2025](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2531/ML25311A150.pdf)). It also deferred eight areas to the operating-license stage: functional containment, reactivity accidents, passive-cooling validation, sodium-fire protection, seismic design, the completed risk assessment, uncertainties and safety margins. ACRS called the sodium-to-salt heat exchanger "**one of the least mature elements of the plant**." It said TerraPower's argument that core-disruptive accidents are incredible must still be justified, and that "analysis of the margin to eutectic melt, a key metric for fuel failure, is needed."

Two outside records bear on the design's newer elements. Sodium-bonded spent fuel "is not suitable for direct disposal" ([NASEM, 2023](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26500/chapter/3)); DOE's 2006 estimate to treat the remaining legacy EBR-II fuel was about $88,000/kg ([IPFM](https://fissilematerials.org/library/rr08.pdf)). And molten-salt storage has failed before: Nevada's Crescent Dunes solar plant averaged a 20.3% capacity factor in 2018 against 51.9% planned, after leaks in its hot salt tank ([Reuters Events](https://www.reutersevents.com/renewables/node/61289)).

Natrium therefore stands on solid ground for reactor physics and basic fuel type. The integrated plant does not yet: its heat exchanger is novel, sodium reactors outside Russia have a poor reliability record, and its fuel supply is undemonstrated.

### X-energy: a permit arrived before the customer's decision

The Xe-100 is a helium-cooled pebble-bed reactor of **80 MWe (200 MWt)**, normally built in four-unit, 320 MWe plants, and fuelled by TRISO-X pebbles of HALEU enriched to 15.5% ([prospectus, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)). The same prospectus says the design is in "the second of three engineering design phases." It adds: "We have not yet delivered a commercial Xe-100 or achieved final investment decisions (FIDs) for any deployments." X-energy licenses technology and sells fuel and services; it says it will not own or operate plants or take construction risk.

Dow's four-unit Long Mott station in Seadrift, Texas was docketed on 12 May 2025 and given an environmental assessment rather than a full environmental impact statement (18 May 2026) ([NRC Long Mott page, updated 12 Nov 2026](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/new-reactors/advanced/who-were-working-with/applicant-projects/long-mott)). Its final safety evaluation followed on 30 October 2026 ([WNN, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/texas-smr-plant-moves-to-final-permitting-stage)) and its construction permit on **9 November 2026**; the NRC dashboard lists the decision as 8 November ([ANS, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8573/nrc-issues-construction-permit-for-dowxenergy-long-mott-project/)). NRC Chairman Ho Nieh said "We delivered on the 18-month schedule" ([ANS, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8573/nrc-issues-construction-permit-for-dowxenergy-long-mott-project/)), while X-energy said the permit came "over a month ahead of schedule" ([X-energy Q3 release, 12 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456633/xe-ex99_1.htm)). The permit arrived earlier than X-energy's own guidance of the first quarter of 2027, given in April 2026 ([prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)), and the first half of 2027, given in August 2026 ([ANS, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-08-17/article-8297/xenergy-terrapower-share-updates-on-advanced-reactor-projects/)). ANS separately reported that an Atomic Safety and Licensing Board agreed to hear challenges to the application ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/article-7771/nrc-board-to-hear-challenges-to-dows-long-mott-application/)); the outcome of those challenges was not found.

**The customer has not decided to build.** Dow says the permit "does not represent a final decision by Dow to proceed with construction," that construction "could begin later this decade," and that startup would be "early next decade" ([Dow, 9 Nov 2026](https://corporate.dow.com/en-us/news/press-releases/dows-long-mott-generating-station-receives-nrc-construction-permit.html)). In April 2026 X-energy had said "construction is expected to begin in 2027" ([prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)). In November it said: "We expect Dow to consider a preliminary investment decision in 2027" ([Q3 2026 call transcript, 12 Nov 2026](https://fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/12/x-energy-xe-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript)). Dow's Q3 2026 10-Q does not mention Long Mott or X-energy ([Dow 10-Q, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29915/000175178826000160/dow-20260930.htm)).

Federal money is the main funder so far. Under the ARDP agreement signed in February 2021, DOE reimburses half of **$2.4 billion of eligible costs, or $1.2 billion**. X-energy had been reimbursed **about $618M by 30 September 2026**. DOE has notified it of up to roughly $1B more, which "remains subject to execution of a subsequent contract award modification." The program's funding outside date falls "in or around 2030," before most nuclear construction would occur ([X-energy 10-Q, 12 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456659/xe-20260930.htm)). ANS's description of the project as having "received roughly $2.15 billion" ([ANS, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8573/nrc-issues-construction-permit-for-dowxenergy-long-mott-project/)) therefore overstates cash actually reimbursed. Canary Media noted that $2.1B of federal money implies at least $4.2B of total spending ([Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/nuclear/federal-funding-x-energy-small-nuclear-reactor)). No plant capital cost has been disclosed.

The other projects are earlier-stage. Energy Northwest's Amazon-backed Cascade project in Washington, planned for up to 12 units, has been in NRC pre-application since August 2024, with no application filed as of 23 September 2026 ([NRC Cascade page](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/new-reactors/advanced/who-were-working-with/pre-application-activities/energy-northwest)). X-energy's target for filing slipped from the end of 2026 to the first half of 2027 ([ANS, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-08-17/article-8297/xenergy-terrapower-share-updates-on-advanced-reactor-projects/)). Energy Northwest says it has committed $10–15M to equipment and expects construction "within the next four years" ([Tri-Cities Area Journal of Business, 12 Nov 2026](https://www.tricitiesbusinessnews.com/articles/smr-project-update)). AEP signed a joint development agreement in October 2026 to evaluate a 960 MW plant in Indiana, with Amazon development funding and a final investment decision still required ([WNN, 19 Oct 2026](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/aep-amazon-and-x-energy-plan-12-smr-plant)). Centrica's joint development agreement for up to about 6 GWe in the UK and Talen's letter of intent are both described as non-binding ([prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)). X-energy's "144 reactors… approximately 11.5 gigawatts" pipeline assumes "each customer exercises its contingent rights in full" ([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456633/xe-ex99_1.htm)). Amazon's Q3 2026 10-Q does not mention X-energy ([Amazon 10-Q, 30 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000040/amzn-20260930.htm)).

The fuel plant is further along. The NRC issued TRISO-X a 40-year **Category II fuel-fabrication license (SNM-7007) on 13 February 2026**, the first of its kind, for a TX-1 plant sized at 5 t of uranium a year, about 700,000 pebbles, or fuel for up to 11 Xe-100s ([POWER, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.powermag.com/triso-x-secures-first-ever-nrc-category-ii-license-for-commercial-advanced-nuclear-fuel-fabrication/); [ANS, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/article-7762/nrc-grants-license-for-trisox-fuel-manufacturing-using-haleu/)). POWER put the construction phase at $768M over five years. X-energy says vertical construction finished on schedule in the third quarter of 2026 and targets operation in the first half of 2028 ([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456633/xe-ex99_1.htm)).

For HALEU, X-energy cites a first DOE tranche of about 4 t, with about 7.6 t expected in total, "enough for X-energy's first Xe-100 plant with Dow" ([prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)). Centrus confirms "a signed definitive contract" for enrichment services with X-energy prepayments, quantities undisclosed ([Centrus 8-K, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065059/000162828026053897/ex991_080626x-energypr.htm)). Unconditional purchase obligations, mainly graphite and enrichment, totalled $312.2M on 30 September 2026 ([X-energy 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456659/xe-20260930.htm)).

X-energy is the only one of the three fission companies with sizable reported revenue, nearly all of it government cost-share. FY2025 revenue and grant income was **$109.1M, 87% from the U.S. government**, against a **net loss of $389.8M** that included a $223.5M non-cash loss on warrants ([prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)). For the first nine months of 2026, revenue was **$164.4M and the net loss $355.2M** ([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456633/xe-ex99_1.htm)). Direct costs exceeded revenue in both periods, so gross margin is negative ($161.4M versus $109.1M in 2025; $245.6M versus $164.4M in 9M 2026, computed). Liquidity was **$1,805.5M with no debt** on 30 September 2026.

The company's funding path ran through a SPAC deal with Ares that was signed in December 2022 and terminated on 31 October 2023 ([Ares 8-K, 31 Oct 2023](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1829432/000119312523266738/d397916d8k.htm)). A $700M Series D led by Jane Street followed in November 2025 ([Latitude Media, 24 Nov 2025](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/smr-developer-x-energy-secures-a-700-million-series-d/)), then an April 2026 IPO of 44.25 million shares at $23 ([prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)). The shares had been marketed at $16–19 and closed their first day at $29.20 ([TechCrunch via Yahoo Finance, 24 Apr 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/x-energy-stock-pops-27-201054076.html)). IPO proceeds net of underwriting of $1,103.2M imply the over-allotment option was fully exercised, for about $1.17B gross (computed from the [Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456633/xe-ex99_1.htm)).

After the IPO, **Amazon held 23.4% of the Class A shares**, and founder and chairman Kam Ghaffarian held 70.9% of the Class B shares, about 23% of combined votes (computed). Two directors come from Ares. Clay Sell has been CEO since 2019 ([prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000110465926049515/tm2527015-22_424b4.htm)). X-energy reports no material litigation ([X-energy 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2088896/000119312526456659/xe-20260930.htm)).

**The science under the Xe-100.** The fuel rests on DOE's Advanced Gas Reactor (AGR) program. AGR-1 particles showed **no failures after 300 hours at 1,600 °C and 1,700 °C** ([OSTI 1325505](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1325505)). In 2020 the NRC approved EPRI's topical report on this fuel, but only for particles within a defined property envelope, noting that "further fuel qualification efforts… could be within the scope of future submittals" ([NRC safety evaluation, 2020](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2021/ML20210M241.pdf)). The later AGR-5/6/7 tests showed low failure fractions, but also one apparent silicon-carbide layer failure after more than 200 hours at 1,600 °C ([INL](https://inl.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/preliminary-results-from-post-irradiation-heating-tests-of-low-an/)) and "fairly high" silver release ([Stempien, INL, Jul 2024](https://art.inl.gov/2023%20Meetings/2024%20DOE-NE%20ART%20GCR%20Program%20Review/Day%201%20Presentations%20-%20July%2016/11_AGR-567%20Compact%20Destructive%20Exams%20and%20Safety%20Testing_John%20Stempien.pdf)). X-energy says interim irradiation results for its own pebbles "have met all of our expectations," a claim not independently verified ([Q3 2026 call transcript](https://fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/12/x-energy-xe-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript)).

X-energy says "the fuel itself becomes the primary containment system" and that the Xe-100's maximum accident temperature stays "well below" 1,600 °C ([X-energy, 10 Jun 2026](https://x-energy.com/?p=11536)). That margin depends on modelled core temperatures, the same type of prediction AVR got wrong. GRS calls HTR-PM "not an inherently safe reactor, but a reactor with inherently safe characteristics" ([GRS, 11 Oct 2024](https://www.grs.de/en/news/pebble-bed-reactor-shidaowan-nuclear-power-plant-inherently-safe-reactor)). The National Academies add that these reactors "will produce much larger volumes of spent fuel" and graphite waste ([NASEM, 2023](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26500/chapter/3)).

The Xe-100's fuel therefore stands on NRC-accepted experimental evidence. The reactor system's operating record is weaker: pebble-bed availability has been low and pebble handling has caused problems before, and the Xe-100 itself has never operated.

### Oklo: DOE authorization replaced the NRC path for unit one

Oklo plans to own and operate Aurora "powerhouses," sodium-cooled, metal-fuelled fast reactors designed "to initially produce 15-75 megawatts electric," and to sell their power ([Oklo 10-Q, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849056/000162828026071319/oklo-20260930.htm)). Its latest annual report says it has "not yet constructed any powerhouses or entered into any binding power purchase agreement" ([Oklo 10-K, 17 Mar 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849056/000162828026018698/oklo-20251231.htm)).

The NRC denied Oklo's 2020 combined license application, then for a roughly 1.5 MWe design, on 6 January 2022, without prejudice. The NRC said Oklo had failed to provide information on "the maximum credible accident" and on safety classification, and that it "made no findings on the safety of the design" ([Federal Register, 11 Jan 2022](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-01-11/html/2022-00339.htm); [WNN, 7 Jan 2022](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/nrc-denies-oklo-licence-application)). Since then the design has grown to 75 MWe, and the NRC has approved only Oklo's principal design criteria report, in April 2026 ([Oklo, May 2026](https://oklo.com/newsroom/news-details/2026/Oklos-NRC-Principal-Design-Criteria-Topical-Report-Approved-for-Aurora-Powerhouse-in-Idaho/default.aspx)). Oklo had filed no new application as of 6 November 2026 and is "evaluating the timing and form" of one ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849056/000162828026071319/oklo-20260930.htm)).

The first unit, Aurora-ID at Idaho National Laboratory, is instead proceeding under DOE's Reactor Pilot Program. Oklo was selected on 13 August 2025 and broke ground in September 2025. DOE then approved its nuclear safety design agreement and, on 11 June 2026, its preliminary documented safety analysis, which Oklo calls "two of five steps" ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849056/000162828026071319/oklo-20260930.htm); [ANS, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-06-12/article-8119/oklo-secures-doe-pdsa-approval/)). Oklo has not given a project cost.

**DOE's pilot program set a goal of three reactors reaching criticality by 4 July 2026; four other companies met it, and Oklo did not** ([DOE slides, NRC ADAMS ML26198A212](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2619/ML26198A212.pdf); [Neutron Bytes, 8 Jul 2026](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/07/08/four-micro-reactors-hit-criticality-milestones-crossing-does-finish-line-on-july-4th/)). Oklo reports that its low-power Groves isotope test reactor in Texas reached first criticality on **5 August 2026**, 32 days after the goal. No independent confirmation was found ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849056/000162828026071319/oklo-20260930.htm)).

After Kerrisdale Capital's short report of 20 November 2024, a plaintiff firm announced an "investigation," but no filed suit was found ([Pomerantz, 8 Jan 2025](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/shareholder-alert-pomerantz-law-firm-investigates-claims-on-behalf-of-investors-of-oklo-inc---oklo-302343985.html)). **The science under Aurora.** Aurora's case rests on EBR-II, which produced 62.5 MWt / 20 MWe on HEU metal fuel ([IPFM](https://fissilematerials.org/library/rr08.pdf)). At typical conversion efficiencies of 32–40%, a 75 MWe Aurora implies about 190–235 MWt, roughly three to four times EBR-II's thermal power; this is a computed estimate, because Aurora's thermal rating is not published. The fuel also differs, HALEU now rather than HEU.

Independent review is limited. The NRC has not reviewed Aurora's accident analysis, the issue behind the 2022 denial. The DOE reviewing the first unit is also its host site, its fuel supplier and the program sponsor. And the record contains a direct technical inconsistency within the sodium-reactor camp: TerraPower's safety case, as summarized by the NRC's advisory committee, credits "No addition of plutonium in fuel" with reducing void reactivity worth ([ACRS, 16 Nov 2025](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2531/ML25311A150.pdf)), while Oklo is negotiating to use surplus plutonium.

Oklo's recycling claims face independent findings. Oklo says recycling can "tap up to 90% of the energy remaining" in spent fuel with "reduced long-term radiotoxicity" ([Oklo](https://oklo.com/fuel-recycling)). The National Academies concluded that "Regardless of the fuel cycle, a geologic repository will be required," and that separating weapons-usable material raises proliferation risk ([NASEM, 2023](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26500/chapter/3)). Former Energy Secretary Ernest Moniz and members of Congress criticized the plutonium program ([Arms Control Today, Jul/Aug 2026](https://www.armscontrol.org/node/15388)).

Aurora's evidence base therefore rests on a heritage argument across a scale-up and a fuel change, with no NRC review of its safety case. Its near-term advantage is a first core already secured from EBR-II material.

### HALEU is the shared bottleneck

All three fission designs need high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU), and supply is thin. Centrus's 16-machine cascade in Piketon, Ohio, the only one operating in the United States, produces about 900 kg a year; cumulative output passed **1,900 kg by mid-June 2026**, according to Centrus, and the first new capacity is expected "by 2029" ([POWER, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.powermag.com/centrus-signs-900m-doe-contract-pivots-sole-u-s-haleu-cascade-to-commercial-operation/)). IPFM says it is "not clear" that the second ~1,000 kg was actually produced ([IPFM, 1 Jul 2026](https://fissilematerials.org/blog/2026/07/centrus_finalizes_haleu_c.html)). Centrus's CEO says converting enriched uranium into oxide or metal fuel feedstock "does not exist commercially" (same POWER report). Waivers for Russian imports end on 1 January 2028 ([WNN, May 2024](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/doe-unveils-process-for-russian-leu-import-waivers)), and Russia has restricted enriched-uranium exports to the United States since November 2024 ([S&P Global, 15 Nov 2024](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/electric-power/111524-russia-temporarily-bans-exports-to-us-of-enriched-uranium-after-us-curbed-imports)). The National Academies' 2023 finding still describes the position: "There is no current domestic capacity to supply high-assay low-enriched uranium" for projected needs ([NASEM, 2023](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26500/chapter/3)). At about 0.9 t a year, enriching the 15–20 t that a trade blog estimates for one Natrium first core ([Neutron Bytes, 9 Jan 2026](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/01/09/terrapower-in-mega-deal-with-meta-for-eight-natrium-345-mw-advanced-nuclear-plants/)) would take roughly 17–22 years (computed). The near-term cores therefore depend on DOE material (TerraPower; X-energy's ~7.6 t), on EBR-II-recovered HALEU (Oklo's 5 t), or on plants not yet built. Centrus has contracts with both X-energy and Oklo, and its 10-K does not mention TerraPower.

## Fervo's first block runs while Cape's reservoir data stay unpublished

Fervo builds, owns and operates enhanced-geothermal plants. It drills horizontal well pairs or triplets into hot crystalline rock, stimulates them in multiple stages with oilfield techniques, and runs standardized air-cooled binary power units it calls GeoBlocks ([Fervo 10-Q, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026072482/frvo-20260930.htm)). It is headquartered in Houston and had 199 employees at the end of 2025 ([Fervo S-1, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026025821/fervoenergy-sx1.htm)).

On 30 September 2026 Fervo reported **36 MW in operation**: about 3 MW (gross) from the Project Red pilot in Nevada, online since 2023, and **33 MW net from GeoBlock 1 at Cape Station in Beaver County, Utah**. GeoBlock 1 synchronized to the grid on 24 September 2026 and declared commercial operation on 30 September, one day before its contractual date, "meeting the expected production threshold under the PPA" ([Fervo 8-K, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026064103/exhibit991pressrelease10126.htm)). GeoBlock 2 synchronized on 4 November 2026 and GeoBlock 3 was in cold commissioning; both are guided to commercial operation by 1 January 2027. Drilling for the next eight 50 MW blocks, "Cape Google 400," was about 18% complete ([Fervo Q3 release, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026071911/exhibit991earningsrelease1.htm)).

Fervo's portfolio table lists 466 MW under construction, 550 MW "ready to build," 3,000 MW in advanced development and 54,700 MW in early development, on about 670,000 net acres ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026072482/frvo-20260930.htm)). For Cape it cites "approximately 8.4 gigawatts of capacity potential," up from 4.3 GW. That figure takes heat-in-place estimates from DeGolyer & MacNaughton, a consultant engaged by Fervo, and applies Fervo's own recovery factor (same 10-Q). The prospectus says such figures "should not be viewed as a measure of reserves" ([Fervo 424B4, 14 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm)).

Fervo met its headline 2023 promise of first power at Cape in 2026 ([Power Engineering, Sep 2023](https://www.power-eng.com/renewables/geothermal/fervo-energy-breaks-ground-on-next-gen-geothermal-project/)), and it beat its August 2026 guidance of first power in the fourth quarter ([Fervo Q2 release, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026055942/exhibit991earningsrelease8.htm)). In September 2024 it forecast "up to 90 MW by 2026" ([JPT, 16 Sep 2024](https://jpt.spe.org/fervo-and-forge-report-breakthrough-test-results-signaling-more-progress-for-enhanced-geothermal)), and its June 2025 financing release said Phase I "will deliver 100 MW starting in 2026" ([ThinkGeoEnergy, Jun 2025](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/fervo-secures-206m-for-cape-station-development/)). Fervo's permits are uneven. Cape's federal approval from the Bureau of Land Management (October 2024) covers up to 2 GW ([BLM, via POWER, search result](https://www.powermag.com/blm-approves-major-2-gw-geothermal-project-in-utah/)). In Nevada, Fervo has never obtained an Underground Injection Control permit and operates on temporary permits re-issued every 30 days ([424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm)).

**The science under Fervo.** Project Red is the only multi-year record. Fervo's April 2026 data release reports more than 614 days of production since 30 October 2023 at an average 347 °F (175 °C), **an average of about 2.1 MW gross and 1.4 MW net against the 3.5 MW headline of 2023**, about 70% of injected fluid recaptured, tracer moving between the wells in under a day, and no observable thermal decline for more than 500 days followed by a drop of about 2.5 °F ([Fervo, 13 Apr 2026](https://fervoenergy.com/enhanced-geothermal-has-been-proven-at-scale-heres-what-two-years-of-production-data-show/)). The prospectus a month later said Project Red "has not experienced the kind of premature thermal decline that has long plagued traditional geothermal projects" ([424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm)).

Outside readings of the same pilot are more negative. Nevada regulator data analysed by Heatmap show production temperature "falling about 2 degrees per month from November 2025 to May 2026," with water loss of 20–30% a month ([Heatmap, 21 Oct 2026](https://heatmap.news/energy/fervo-short-seller-report)). That trend annualizes to about 24 °F a year (computed). A short seller holding a position against Fervo alleges the same rate, plus a 90 °F drop in June 2026 ([Morpheus Research, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.morpheus-research.com/fervo/)). GeoExpro's reviewers cite about 1.5 °F a year as typical for pumped Nevada fields ([GeoExpro, 23 Apr 2026](https://geoexpro.com/has-enhanced-geothermal-been-proven-at-scale/)). Experts quoted by Heatmap, one of them from a consortium that Fervo advises, called Project Red a research pilot not designed for long-term production, and a former ConocoPhillips CTO said the short seller misread some monthly data.

Cape's public flow data are thinner still. Fervo's September 2024 30-day test reported 107 kg/s maximum flow "enabling over 10 MW" ([Business Wire, 10 Sep 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240910997008/en)). GeoExpro's reviewers note the only published flow plot stops at 24 hours, while still declining. From those 24 hours they estimate about **6 MW net from a three-well triplet, roughly 2 MW net per well**, and a productivity index at the low end of the conventional-geothermal range; the authors call their estimate approximate ([GeoExpro, 1 Oct 2025](https://geoexpro.com/critical-look-at-fervo-cape-station-data-raises-questions-over-sustainability/); [GeoExpro, 18 May 2026](https://geoexpro.com/the-low-productivity-of-enhanced-geothermal-systems/); [GeoExpro, 27 Jul 2026](https://geoexpro.com/the-reservoir-will-always-have-its-final-say/)). Fervo reports 90% water retention at that triplet and models 99% field-wide ([Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/energy/fervo-short-seller-report)), but has published no Cape flow, temperature, water or capacity-factor data from operation. No peer-reviewed journal paper on Fervo's field results was found; its Project Red paper remained a preprint in April 2026.

Independent support for the technique is real. DOE's Utah FORGE site next door, run by the University of Utah, ran a month-long circulation test in 2024 that recovered more than 90% of injected fluid at about 370 °F, with a largest induced quake of magnitude 1.9 ([JPT, 16 Sep 2024](https://jpt.spe.org/fervo-and-forge-report-breakthrough-test-results-signaling-more-progress-for-enhanced-geothermal); [Utah FORGE, 23 May 2024](https://utahforge.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Stimulation-and-Circulation-May-23-2024-Press-Release-FINAL.pdf)). Fervo's reported drilling learning curve was 59 days to 21 days per well and $9.4M to $4.8M per well, as of February 2024 ([Business Wire, 12 Feb 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240212763499/en)). DOE repeated those figures, and noted a parallel rate-of-penetration gain of more than 500% at FORGE since 2017 ([DOE Liftoff, Mar 2024](https://apps.openei.org/GeoCLUSTER/assets/pdfs/DOE%20Report%202024_Pathways%20to%20Commercial%20liftoff.pdf)). Fervo has not disclosed per-well cost or drilling days for its newer Generation 3.0 wells.

**Seismicity has risen as Cape expanded.** Cape's traffic-light protocol sets amber at magnitude 2 and red at magnitude 3 ([Utah FORGE traffic-light system v2](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1706/TLS_V2_final.pdf)). A query of the USGS catalogue for University of Utah network events of magnitude 2 or more within 25 km of Cape, run on 14 November 2026, returned these counts for the box around Cape and FORGE ([USGS ComCat query](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/query?format=csv&starttime=2022-01-01&endtime=2026-11-14&latitude=38.50&longitude=-112.90&maxradiuskm=25&minmagnitude=2.0&orderby=time)):

| Period | Events of magnitude ≥2 |
|---|---|
| 2022–2023 | 0 |
| 2024 | 12 |
| 2025 | 12 |
| January–April 2026 | 17 |
| May–August 2026 | 15 |
| 1 September–14 November 2026 | 82 |

The two largest events, **ML 3.13 on 12 September 2026 and ML 3.12 on 7 November 2026**, reached the red threshold ([USGS event uu80156586](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/uu80156586); [USGS event uu80163521](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/uu80163521)). The catalogue cannot by itself attribute events to Fervo rather than FORGE. A Stanford-led 2026 workshop paper found that seismicity at Cape began near intersections between hydraulic fractures and a fault ([Kim et al., Stanford Geothermal Workshop 2026, seen via search summary](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Kim2.pdf)). Fervo did not respond to Heatmap's seismicity questions ([Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/energy/fervo-short-seller-report)), and no public account of its response to the red-threshold events was found. For scale, the 2017 Pohang earthquake in South Korea, linked to an EGS project, was magnitude 5.5 ([Science News, Apr 2018](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/pumping-water-underground-power-may-have-triggered-south-korean-quake)), and released roughly 4,000 times the energy of an ML 3.1 event (computed).

Cost and resource use set the scaling test. Fervo's $7,000/kW is about 1.4–1.5 times DOE's 2030 "liftoff" target of $4,700–5,000/kW and about 1.9 times its 2035 target of about $3,700/kW ([DOE Liftoff, Mar 2024](https://apps.openei.org/GeoCLUSTER/assets/pdfs/DOE%20Report%202024_Pathways%20to%20Commercial%20liftoff.pdf)). At Project Red's 70% recapture, unrecovered fluid works out to roughly 6–10 gallons per net kWh (computed from Fervo's flow and output figures and the short seller's cumulative-loss figure), against DOE's planning assumption of 0.3–0.73 gal/kWh ([DOE Liftoff, Mar 2024](https://apps.openei.org/GeoCLUSTER/assets/pdfs/DOE%20Report%202024_Pathways%20to%20Commercial%20liftoff.pdf)). Fervo says most of that fluid pressurizes the reservoir rather than being permanently lost ([Fervo, 13 Apr 2026](https://fervoenergy.com/enhanced-geothermal-has-been-proven-at-scale-heres-what-two-years-of-production-data-show/)).

An independent open-source model of a 500 MWe Cape-like field, run by a modeller unaffiliated with Fervo, assumes redrilling about every eight years. It counts 376 wells over the field's life, above the reported 320-well federal authorization, and gives $85/MWh at $5,600/kW. Those results depend on 1% water loss and 107 kg/s per well sustained, neither confirmed in the field ([GEOPHIRES Cape-5 case study, Feb 2026](https://softwareengineerprogrammer.github.io/GEOPHIRES/Fervo_Project_Cape-5.html)).

On whether the science stands on solid ground: the core technique of connecting horizontal wells through stimulated fractures in hot granite is shown at two Fervo sites and independently at FORGE, and a 33 MW plant met its contract date. The commercial-scale claims are not yet public-record facts. Sustained net output per well, decline rate, water retention near 99%, cost on DOE's trajectory, and control of rising magnitude-3 seismicity can all now be measured, because Cape is operating, but none has been published.

## Outside records confirm deals and permits more often than performance

Government records corroborate most of the events the seven companies report. The NRC's own pages and orders confirm the Natrium and Long Mott construction permits ([NRC CLI-26-5](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2606/ML26063A399.pdf); [NRC Long Mott page](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/new-reactors/advanced/who-were-working-with/applicant-projects/long-mott)) and the 2022 Oklo denial ([Federal Register, 11 Jan 2022](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-01-11/html/2022-00339.htm)). The same records hold most of the cautions. ACRS deferred eight Natrium safety areas to the operating-license stage ([ACRS, 16 Nov 2025](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2531/ML25311A150.pdf)). The NRC bounded its TRISO approval to a particle-property envelope ([NRC safety evaluation, 2020](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2021/ML20210M241.pdf)). The pattern is consistent. Where a counterparty has a financial statement to make, it confirms an amount or a stake, as Eni, Nucor and Centrus do. Where the deal is an option on a plant not yet built, the large buyers do not mention it in the SEC reports reviewed. Statements about schedule (Dow, PacifiCorp, Energy Northwest) are more cautious than the developers'.

The published literature splits the same way. CFS's magnet work and SPARC physics are peer-reviewed, with outside co-authors on the physics, and no independent critique of SPARC comparable to the Helion critiques was found. Helion's one modelling paper has drawn two independent rebuttals. Thea's design record is extensive but almost entirely self-authored. Fervo's field results have no peer-reviewed paper, and the critical re-analyses appear in a trade magazine (GeoExpro), which notes that no critiques of Fervo's well tests have appeared in geothermal conference proceedings ([GeoExpro, 27 Jul 2026](https://geoexpro.com/the-reservoir-will-always-have-its-final-say/)). For fission, the independent technical voices are ACRS, the National Academies and the national laboratories' fuel programs. The Union of Concerned Scientists argued in 2021 that non-light-water designs are "not likely to be significantly safer than today's nuclear plants" ([UCS, 18 Mar 2021](https://www.ucs.org/resources/advanced-isnt-always-better)).

## Conclusion

The record on 14 November 2026 divides cleanly. Independent records confirm the existence of permits, public money and signed agreements. They rarely confirm the performance those agreements assume: plasma gain, reactor yields, reservoir behaviour, fuel availability, plant availability. The two partial exceptions are CFS's peer-reviewed magnet and Fervo's SEC-filed statement that a 33 MW block met its contract threshold. Wherever independent scientists or regulators have examined a company's core claim, their reading is more conditional than the company's: the critiques of Helion's gain case, the re-analyses of Fervo's well tests, ACRS on Natrium, and the National Academies on HALEU and recycling.

The binding constraint also differs by technology, and the dated record shows where it has moved. For fusion it is still a physics demonstration, since no magnetic device has shown Q>1. For fission it has shifted from NRC review, which delivered both 2026 permits within or ahead of their original review schedules, to fuel supply and customers' investment decisions. For Fervo it is reservoir performance, water, transmission and seismicity at a scale of hundreds of megawatts. The next 26 months contain dated tests that will turn several claims into public records, whichever way they resolve. They are PacifiCorp's state approvals for the Natrium contract (needed by 31 December 2026), Fervo's GeoBlocks 2 and 3 against SCE's 1 January 2027 delivery date, SPARC's turn-on and Dow's preliminary investment decision in 2027, the NRC's statutory deadline for a fusion rule (31 December 2027), and four 2028 events: TX-1 fuel production, TerraPower's planned operating-license application ([WyoFile, 21 Sep 2026](https://wyofile.com/lead-contractor-pulls-out-of-gates-natrium-nuclear-power-plant-project-in-wyoming/)), Helion's Orion date for Microsoft, and the opening of Aurora-ID's "2028-2029" window. Until then, the clearest independent signal of how large buyers weigh these contracts is that Microsoft, Constellation, Dow and Amazon do not mention them in the SEC filings reviewed.

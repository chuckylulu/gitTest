# Chokepoints hold quality while prices reset

**Verdict.** On business quality alone, **TSMC ranks first, ASML second and KLA third** among the 35 companies ranked here (the 25 named plus ten added), and the gap between those three and the state-backed challengers at the bottom is wide. TSMC took **72.5% of foundry revenue in 2Q26** ([TrendForce, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260909-13225.html)) and earned a **58.7% operating margin in 3Q26** with NT$2.99tn of net cash ([TSMC 3Q26 management report, 15 Oct 2026](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/f83780000ddc0c3b48e57a87eab6814c4ab35058/3Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf)); ASML is still the world's only maker of EUV scanners ([ASML 2025 Annual Report](https://ourbrand.asml.com/m/8ab959d4926657b/original/asml-2025-annual-report-strategic-report-section.pdf)); KLA held **56.5% of process control in 2024** on Gartner data it reproduces ([KLA Investor Day, 12 Mar 2026](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_e2be8b99fd19637d99dedbc7b3bd0329/klatencor/db/1167/10630/presentation/2026+Investor+Day_Khan_Final_IR+copy.pdf)). Behind them comes a band of leading or near-sole suppliers with filed operating margins of roughly 25-46% and net cash (Hoya, Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron, Disco, Lam Research, Lasertec, ASM International), then the memory makers, whose record 2026 profits are mostly price, and at the bottom Intel, SMIC, the Chinese toolmakers, Nikon, Rapidus and SMEE, none of which has a paying leading-edge position that an outside customer has confirmed. Most leaders pay for their 2027-2030 plans from their own cash flow; the paths of Rapidus, Intel's foundry, Nikon's lithography and JSR depend on customers, loans or a sale not yet signed or closed. Quality and price have come apart: 23 of the 30 listed shares sit 30-64% below their 52-week highs while guidance is still rising, so entry in stages now looks sensible for TSMC, ASML, Hoya, Tokyo Electron, Disco, ASM International and Tokyo Ohka Kogyo, only fair for KLA and Applied Materials, and poor for the three memory makers (price-to-book above 16-17 of their last 20 quarter-ends), Intel and the Chinese shares. The two strongest short candidates are **Micron and Intel**, each with a serious timing problem; the dearest shares on paper (AMEC and SMIC's Shanghai line) cannot practically be borrowed. Of the inherited claims, two are out of date (Intel's 14A pause, "over 90%" in Taiwan), two are imprecise (ASML's 65 tools, the A16 slip) and one is half right (constraints AI demand cannot remove); the one-in-four pause probability was never derived, and my reading is about 30% for an outright fall in AI capital spending in 2028-29 and about one in two for a stall. Research date 14 November 2026; prices are closes of 13 November 2026; this is analysis for exploration, not personal financial advice.

## 1. Quality ranking: six tests put TSMC, ASML and KLA ahead of 32 others

### What makes a company high quality in this field

A company is high quality here when the leading edge cannot route around it, when that position shows up as high returns across a cycle rather than at one peak, and when an outsider can check both. I used six tests. **(a) Chokepoint:** is the company the sole supplier, one of two or one of many for something leading-edge production cannot do without, and does a customer's filing, a teardown or a named market-share study confirm it, or does it rest on the company's word? **(b) Economics through the cycle:** gross and operating margin, return on capital and cash conversion, read against the last downturns as well as the latest quarter. **(c) Delivery:** roadmap and guidance promises against outcomes, write-downs and compliance failures. **(d) Self-funding:** can the 2027-2030 plan be paid from its own cash flow and balance sheet? **(e) Exposure it cannot control:** customer and site concentration, China revenue that export rules can remove, dependence on one supplier or on the state. **(f) Verifiability and control:** what is disclosed, whether disclosure is shrinking, and who owns it. Tests (a) and (b) carry most weight, a failure on (d) keeps a company out of the top half, and share price, size and strategic importance play no part.

The second test decides where the memory makers land. Their latest operating margins run from 55% to 81%, but in 2023 SK Hynix and Micron ran margins of about -36% ([SK Hynix operating-margin series](https://companiesmarketcap.com/sk-hynix/operating-margin/); [Micron series](https://companiesmarketcap.com/micron-technology/operating-margin/)), and Micron's own filing says annual changes in average memory prices ranged from about +200% to about -50% over its last five fiscal years ([Micron 10-K, filed 9 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm)). A ranking on this year's numbers would put them first; a ranking through the cycle puts them mid-table. The first test decides the bottom of the list: a state can fund a fab or a toolmaker, but it cannot supply the outside confirmation that customers pay for the product at the leading edge.

### Ten companies were added and more than twenty were left out

Ten companies outside the original list are ranked because the evidence shows the leading edge depends on them. **KLA** is added for process control, where its 56.5% share is the best-documented position in equipment. **Lasertec** is added because, by its own account, its actinic EUV mask-inspection tool still has no competing product ([Lasertec FY6/2026 briefing transcript, Aug 2026](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/6920/ir_material_for_fiscal_ym5/210010/00.pdf)). **SK Hynix and Micron** are added because memory, not logic, is the tightest item in AI supply today: SK hynix said on 28 Oct 2026 that DRAM and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) were "effectively all sold out for the next three years, through 2029" (company claim) ([Kyunghyang Shinmun, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.khan.co.kr/en/article/202610281734027)). **BE Semiconductor (BESI)** is added for hybrid bonding, with 83% of advanced die placement in 2025 on TechInsights data it cites ([BESI investor presentation, Nov 2026](https://www.besi.com/fileadmin/data/Investor_Relations/Investor_Presentations/Investor_Presentation_November_2026.pdf)), and **Disco** for the thinning and dicing that stacked memory and advanced packaging require. **SUMCO and Entegris** are added for wafers and contamination control; TSMC alone was 16% of Entegris's 2025 sales ([Entegris 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101302/000110130226000012/entg-20251231.htm)). **Dai Nippon Printing and AGC** are added, with a caution, for masks and EUV mask blanks: AGC calls itself "one of the market's leading two players" in EUV blanks (company claim) ([AGC semiconductor briefing transcript, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.agc.com/en/ir/library/bizbriefing/pdf/2026_0602esemiconductor_transcript.pdf)) and DNP is Photronics' 49.99% partner in its Taiwan and Xiamen ventures ([Photronics 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/810136/000114036125045801/ef20057458_10k.htm)), but in both groups the relevant business is 2-5% of sales, so their rank says more about the group than about the field.

Considered and left out of the ranking: **SiCarrier**, owned by the Shenzhen government and on the US Entity List since 2 Dec 2024, because no financial figure and no confirmed funding round could be found ([Wikipedia: SiCarrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SiCarrier); [89 FR 96830](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-12-05/html/2024-28267.htm)); **Gigaphoton**, Komatsu's unlisted excimer-laser subsidiary, because no revenue, profit or share data were found ([Gigaphoton company page](https://www.gigaphoton.com/company)); the Huawei-linked fabs (Pengxinwei, Pensun, SwaySure), which are state-owned, sanctioned and almost wholly undisclosed; and **CXMT**, which reached 10% of DRAM revenue in 2Q26 ([Counterpoint, Sep 2026](https://counterpointresearch.com/insights/global-dram-and-hbm-market-share)) and is treated here as a competitor to the memory makers, not assessed in its own right. A further group appears in the research only as named competitors or partners with no data sheet and is not ranked: GlobalFoundries, UMC and Hua Hong (foundries); Mitsui Chemicals (pellicles, and JSR's agreed buyer); IMS Nanofabrication, NuFlare and JEOL (mask writers); ASMPT, Onto Innovation, Camtek, Hitachi High-Tech, SEMES and ACM Research (equipment); GlobalWafers, Siltronic and SK Siltron (wafers); Sumitomo Chemical, Fujifilm, DuPont/Qnity, Dongjin and S&S Tech (resists and blanks); and xLight (an EUV light-source start-up).

### Why the order falls this way

**Why TSMC edges ASML.** Both pass every test, and the gap is small. ASML has the harder chokepoint: no rival EUV scanner has outside confirmation of production-capable performance, and the Chinese prototype reported by Reuters in December 2025 generates EUV light but has made no working chips ([EE Times, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.eetimes.com/china-euv-breakthrough-and-the-rise-of-the-silicon-curtain/)). TSMC does have rivals in volume production at the 2nm class, Samsung's SF2 and Intel's 18A ([Wikipedia: 2 nm process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_nm_process)), yet its N3 and N2 nodes alone were 41% of wafer revenue in a US$46.7bn quarter while Samsung Foundry's revenue across all nodes was US$3.26bn in 2Q26, and I found no on-the-record case of a customer moving a leading-edge product away from it in 2026. TSMC ranks first on delivery. It promised N2 volume production for the second half of 2025 and delivered it in 4Q25, and every 2026 quarter met or beat the top of its revenue guidance ([TSMC 4Q25 call, 15 Jan 2026](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-01/51d09df96cd89ac19d65af39032b038dc2896a24/TSMC%204Q25%20Transcript.pdf); [3Q26 call, 15 Oct 2026](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/eba4fac72873bf546b669bd2b5d973df5c93215d/TSMC%203Q26%20Transcript.pdf)). ASML cut its 2025 sales forecast in October 2024, when the shares fell 15% in a session ([The Register, 21 Oct 2024](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2024/10/21/asml-faces-turbulence-amid-stock-drop-customer-delays/715788)), said in July 2025 that it could not confirm growth for 2026 ([ASML Q2 2025 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828025034992/pressreleasequarterlyresul.htm)), and has seen TSMC defer High-NA to about 2030 on cost ([TrendForce, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/06/04/news-tsmc-rejects-high-na-euv-investment-concerns-confirms-purchase-for-rd-use-flags-cost-driven-production-timing/)). Against that, TSMC carries the one exposure in this list with no ceiling: every 2nm and 3nm wafer it makes comes from Taiwan. A reader who weights that tail above the delivery record should reverse the first two places.

**Why KLA is third and Hoya fourth.** KLA is the only one of the three large US toolmakers that ties a numerical share to a named outside dataset, it has the highest gross margin of the three (61-62% against about 50% at Applied and Lam), and its revenue fell least in past downturns: it grew through 2019 and fell 6.5% in fiscal 2024, when Lam fell 14.5% ([StockAnalysis revenue history, KLA](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/klac/revenue/); [Lam](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lrcx/revenue/)). Its blemishes are a January 2026 forecast of mid-single-digit growth for a year now running at about 25%, and two niches where others lead: its e-beam business is "about a $400M business" and its actinic EUV mask inspector is at "early demo stage" by its own deck. Hoya ranks fourth on the strength of where profit pools sit in the mask chain. Its Information Technology segment (mask blanks, disk substrates, display masks and optics) earned a 55.1% margin in July-September 2026, against 24.6% at Tekscend and 21.1% at Photronics, and Tekscend says it is absorbing blank price increases ([Tekscend Q3 FY2025 Q&A, Feb 2026](https://contents.xj-storage.jp/xcontents/AS83405/d2a4790d/4761/4b8e/968c/38be9bd786df/20260224194704552s.pdf)). The limit on Hoya is evidence, not economics: it publishes no blank share, and AGC says its own customers are in mass production down to 2nm with phase-shift blanks, which conflicts with Hoya's description of rivals as confined to mature binary grades. Its position is leader of a duopoly, not sole supplier.

**How the big toolmakers separate.** Applied Materials sits above Tokyo Electron because its rankings are reproduced from TechInsights and its margins are higher, though it converts the least profit into cash of the US three and settled an export-control case for $252.5m in February 2026 ([Applied 8-K, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/6951/000162828026007444/ex991pressrelease.htm)). Tokyo Electron's 90%+ share of coater/developers is the nearest thing to a monopoly among the broad toolmakers, but it is an unaudited self-estimate; the company also missed its 35% margin target, did not dispute an analyst's figure that its etch share fell about five points in 2025 ([TEL FY2026 Q&A](https://www.tel.co.jp/ir/library/report/jngpc500000000l6-att/FY26Q4_EarningCall_QA_J_v1.pdf)), and saw its Taiwan unit fined on 27 April 2026 for failing to supervise a former employee who took TSMC secrets ([Focus Taiwan, 27 Apr 2026](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202604270011)). Disco earns the best margins in equipment, with gross margin above 70% for two years and an internal profit target met ten years running, but no named source confirms its share, so it ranks on economics alone. Lam has the highest return on capital and the largest exposure to the two things it cannot control, memory capex and Chinese domestic demand. Lasertec is a true sole supplier by its own account and all three leading-edge manufacturers keep buying from it, yet three customers were 62% of fiscal 2026 sales and its orders have swung by more than 40% in each of the last four years. ASM International leads single-wafer ALD on its own data, but its chief executive says there is "publicly more than one supplier" at 1.4nm ([ASM Q2 2026 call](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/ams/ASM/transcripts/549315-q2-2026/)).

**Why an irreplaceable supplier can still rank mid-table.** Carl Zeiss is as hard to replace as ASML itself, and Trumpf says no one else can build the EUV drive laser. Both are captive. Zeiss sells optics to one customer under "a variable pricing model based on annual financial performance of both companies", and that customer finances its expansion with interest-free loans ([ASML 20-F for 2025, related parties](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026011378/R37.htm)); the rest of the Zeiss group contributes little, since the SMT holding's profit of €1.29bn exceeds the whole group's consolidated profit of €0.95bn (the two are on different accounting bases). Trumpf's EUV unit is 18% of a machine-tool group and lost 23% of its sales in a single year when ASML ordered fewer lasers. A chokepoint that cannot set its own price or volume is worth less than its position suggests.

**Why the memory makers sit in the middle, and in this order.** All three are single-product earners at a price-driven peak. Micron's DRAM revenue rose 252% in fiscal 2026 while industry bit shipments grew by a percentage in the mid-20s, which by my arithmetic makes roughly nine-tenths of the increase price ([Micron 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm); [Micron prepared remarks, 30 Sep 2026](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). Samsung ranks first of the three because it stayed profitable in 2023 when the other two lost money ([Samsung cash-flow history](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/krx/005930/financials/cash-flow-statement/); [SK Hynix ratios](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/krx/000660/financials/ratios/)), leads DRAM again and holds KRW 198tn of net cash; its foundry and device businesses subtract from that. SK Hynix has the best margin and still leads HBM, but lost 14 points of HBM share and 14 points of DRAM share in a year. Micron has the best-documented contract cover and the smallest HBM position.

**Why the challengers fill the bottom.** Intel ranks above SMIC because its frontier capability is confirmed by outsiders (products on 18A in the shops, ASML's statement on High-NA) and its filings are detailed, but its Foundry segment still loses about $1.9bn a quarter with 94% of its revenue coming from Intel itself, and its own 10-K says a 14A serving only Intel products would be uneconomic. SMIC is profitable at 97% utilisation yet earns under 5% on equity, and its advanced-node path is capped by lithography it cannot buy. Naura and AMEC are growing fast inside a protected market, but Naura's profit has been flat for seven quarters and 61% of AMEC's nine-month profit came from investment gains; I place Naura a notch higher because it generates operating cash, while noting that AMEC alone is off the Entity List. SUMCO sits just above them as a supplier the leading edge needs but one that cannot earn its cost of capital on contracts signed in 2020-21. Nikon, Rapidus and SMEE are last because each lacks the thing the first test asks for: Nikon sold three immersion tools in a year, Rapidus has reportedly yet to produce a working memory cell, and SMEE has no immersion tool that a customer or outside analyst has seen in production. Rapidus is above SMEE only because IBM and a ministry committee have confirmed parts of its progress and its funding is on the record.

### Foundries and memory makers

**TSMC (1).** The technology hurdles look smaller than the others. A14 is due in volume in 2028 at a higher capital cost per wafer than N2, and High-NA EUV is planned from 2030 (company statements) ([TSMC 4Q25 call, 15 Jan 2026](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-01/51d09df96cd89ac19d65af39032b038dc2896a24/TSMC%204Q25%20Transcript.pdf); [3Q26 call, 15 Oct 2026](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-10/eba4fac72873bf546b669bd2b5d973df5c93215d/TSMC%203Q26%20Transcript.pdf)). The harder part is building "nearly 20 fabs" at once, absorbing overseas margin dilution that widens from about 2 points in 2026 to 3-4 points, and keeping US trade policy, Taiwan's electricity supply and AI capital spending on side. What it has done: N2 reached 8% of wafer revenue by 3Q26; 2026 capex was raised from US$52-56bn to US$60-64bn; the US commitment was raised to US$265bn in July 2026, with a stated end-state of about 30% of 2nm-and-beyond capacity in Arizona (company claim, undated); and the first overseas N3 line was pulled in to 3Q27. It is paid for: trailing operating cash flow of NT$3.13tn covers capex of NT$1.65tn about 1.9 times ([StockAnalysis cash-flow data](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/tpe/2330/financials/cash-flow-statement/)), and subsidies of NT$167.6bn booked in the first nine months of 2026 are a supplement, not a prop ([Focus Taiwan, 14 Nov 2026](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202611140005)). The concentration risk met its first real test on 12 November 2026, when Nvidia said its chief executive had resigned ([Focus Taiwan, 13 Nov 2026](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202611130010)); reports of order slowdowns rest on unnamed sources, and TrendForce had "not observed significant order cuts" ([TrendForce, 13 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/13/news-nvidia-without-huang-new-tests-ahead-as-amd-eyes-gains-tsmc-finds-a-cushion-and-memory-giants-weigh-risks/)). **Verdict: coherent and self-funded; the constraints that bind are external.**

**Samsung Electronics (15).** In memory the plan is working: its share of HBM revenue rose from 15% in 2Q25 to 33% in 2Q26 ([Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/insights/global-dram-and-hbm-market-share)), it says 2027 HBM supply is sold out and that it has multi-year contracts with ten major customers (company claims) ([Q3 call summary](https://www.itiger.com/news/1179661800)), and the 2026 capital budget of about KRW 84.4tn ([Q3 2026 release](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-announces-third-quarter-2026-results)) is covered by trailing free cash flow of KRW 203tn. The foundry is funded but unproven. It has one filed anchor contract, with Tesla, worth US$16.5bn to 2033 ([Half-year report](https://images.samsung.com/is/content/samsung/assets/global/ir/docs/2026_Half_Interim_Report.pdf)), whose first product slipped to late 2027 by a press account ([Electrek, 12 Mar 2026](https://electrek.co/?p=459364)); its Texas fab is to begin operating in 2027; and its own chief told staff that profitability was "more likely in 2028" (press) ([AnySilicon, 17 Jun 2026](https://anysilicon.com/news/samsung-foundry-targets-return-to-profitability-in-2028/)). Estimated foundry and chip-design losses of about KRW 10.7tn across 2025-26 are small beside group profit ([TrendForce, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/09/30/news-samsung-foundry-loss-reportedly-seen-shrinking-42-yoy-in-2026-as-hbm4-base-die-4nm-demand-rises/)). **Verdict: memory coherent and self-funded; foundry funded but not yet credible as a second source at 2nm until the Texas start, Tesla volume and a second customer naming Samsung arrive.**

**SK Hynix (16).** It has to hold its HBM lead while Samsung recovers and while its largest HBM customer changes leadership. It reports long-term agreements with 14 customers, typically five years with price ranges, covering more than half of 2027 and 2028 capacity, with its M15X fab at full capacity by end-2026 and the first Yongin clean room opening in early 2027 (company claims) ([SK Hynix Q3 2026 release, 28 Oct 2026](https://news.skhynix.com/q3-2026-business-results/)). Net cash of KRW 98.7tn funds both the build-out and about KRW 100tn of 2026 shareholder returns. It gives no capex figure for 2027, and the gap between Q2 net income of KRW 93.9tn and Q3 of KRW 39.8tn is unexplained in its release. **Verdict: coherent and self-funded, on thin disclosure.**

**Micron (17).** Its cover is the best documented of the three: 26 multi-year take-or-pay agreements estimated at more than 35% of revenue through 2030, US$12.7bn of customer cash deposits, and grants of up to US$6.4bn ([Micron 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm)). Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow of US$89.7bn paid for net capex of US$27.4bn three times over ([Micron 8-K, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000018/a2026q4ex991-pressrelease.htm)). Capex is guided at about US$25bn for the first half of fiscal 2027 and higher in the second, mostly for clean rooms whose output starts in mid-2027 (Idaho), late 2028 (a second Idaho fab and Hiroshima) and 2030 (New York) ([Micron prepared remarks](https://s25.q4cdn.com/621799436/files/doc_financials/2026/q4/Q4-FY26-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). **Verdict: coherent and self-funded; the new supply lands in 2028-2030, when demand is least certain.**

**Intel (28).** It has to turn "multiple potential customers" for 14A into named commitments inside the window it set itself (second half of 2026 to first half of 2027), reach 14A risk production in the second half of 2027, and bring a Foundry segment that lost US$1.85bn in Q3 2026 to break-even, which the finance chief has said may slip past the end-2027 target ([cnyes transcript of 26 Aug 2026 conference](https://hao.cnyes.com/post/265357)). It must do so while the PC market shrinks (its own estimate: -12% in 2026 and about -10% in 2027) and AMD guides to higher quarterly revenue than Intel for the first time ([Tom's Hardware, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/cpus/amd-expects-to-outsell-intel-in-q4-for-the-first-time-in-history-usd17b-guidance-would-exceed-intels-projections)). What it has done is real: 18A chips were nearly a third of client CPU revenue in Q3 2026 (company claim), the 14A design kit reached version 0.9 in October 2026, and it raised US$22.6bn net of new equity at US$95 a share in August 2026 on top of US$15.9bn from the US government, SoftBank and Nvidia in 2025 ([Intel 10-Q, Q3 2026](https://www.intc.com/filings-reports/all-sec-filings/content/0000050863-26-000191/0000050863-26-000191.pdf); [Q3 2026 prepared remarks](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_5bcd8acc7e1b5c73a62283d752dd0b96/intel/db/887/9273/prepared_remarks/Q3%2726_Prepared_Remarks.pdf)). With US$54.0bn of cash and investments and US$15bn of undrawn credit lines, the plan through 2028 is funded from identified sources, about half of the cushion coming from that equity raise rather than from operations. **Verdict: partly coherent; the money is there, the outside volume that makes 14A economic is not confirmed by any customer.**

**Rapidus (34).** By press accounts it needs about ¥4tn to start 2nm production and about ¥5tn to run stably ([ASCII.jp, 15 Sep 2026](https://ascii.jp/limit/group/ida/elem/000/004/434/4434616/); [Tom's Hardware, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/rapidus-fab-roadmap-examined)). The state has committed about ¥2.6tn to March 2027; private equity is ¥167.6bn from 32 companies against a goal of about ¥1tn ([Rapidus, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.rapidus.inc/en/news_topics/information/rapidus-secures-267-6-billion-yen-in-funding-from-japan-government-and-private-sector-companies/)); and the up-to-¥2tn of bank loans with a state guarantee were reported as an intention in December 2025, not a signed facility ([NHK, 11 Dec 2025](https://news.web.nhk/newsweb/na/na-k10015001571000)). It was due to show working memory cells by summer 2026 and, per Bloomberg on 10 November citing unnamed sources, has not, which puts the March 2028 production deadline in doubt ([Japan Times/Bloomberg, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/11/10/companies/rapidus-behind-schedule/)); no company reply was found. Of some 60 companies in talks, "not one has yet signed a volume agreement". **Verdict: not yet coherent or funded beyond the fiscal year to March 2027.**

**SMIC (29).** Management says capacity stays tight through 2027 and possibly into the second half of 2028, with tool lead times of 16-24 months pushing 2026 deliveries into 2027 ([TrendForce, 13 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/13/news-smic-sees-capacity-tightness-through-2027-possibly-into-2h28-equipment-lead-times-stretch-to-16-24-months/)). Volume growth is paid for by state co-investors (minority interests of RMB 88.1bn) and bank debt (US$14.9bn), not by free cash flow. The advanced-node path is narrower: outside analysts expect one more step (N+4, roughly TSMC N5 density) and nothing denser this decade ([SemiAnalysis, 14 Jun 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/steel-smic-n3-teardown); [Epoch AI, 4 Sep 2026](https://epoch.ai/publications/huaweis-roadmap-to-2031)), and the pending US MATCH Act would bar the sale and servicing of the immersion tools SMIC runs on. **Verdict: partly coherent; a funded volume plan, an advanced-node plan capped by lithography.**

### The lithography chain

**ASML (2).** It has to raise output without overbuilding, get High-NA adopted, and keep a China business that policy can remove. On output it plans 30% more low-NA EUV and immersion capacity in 2027 inside its existing footprint and is studying more than a further 30% for 2028, having said on 14 October 2026 that supply as planned "for both 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet the demand of our customers" (company claim; no bookings are published to check it against) ([ASML Q3 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/937966/000162828026065813/pressreleasefinancialresul.htm)). It has agreed price increases with key customers that take partial effect in 2027 and full effect from 2028 ([ASML Q3 2026 call](https://tickertrends.io/transcripts/ASML/Q3-earnings-transcript-2026)), and broke ground on a new campus in September 2026 ([NL Times, 8 Sep 2026](https://nltimes.nl/2026/09/08/asml-celebrates-start-campus-construction-will-open-extra-20000-jobs)). On High-NA the record is slower than first planned: ten systems recognised since 2024, Intel the only user in volume production, TSMC at about 2030. On China, about 20% of 2026 sales, the US MATCH Act would ban the immersion tools still allowed and the servicing of installed ones; it cleared a House committee in April 2026 ([Congress.gov, H.R. 8170](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/8170)) and its later status could not be verified. Everything is paid from cash flow, with enough left over to lend Zeiss €1.9bn and buy back €6.3bn of shares in nine months. Its 2030 model (revenue of €44-60bn) is stale, since 2026 guidance of €44-45bn already equals the low case; a reset is likely at the investor day on 10 June 2027. **Verdict: coherent and self-funded.**

**Carl Zeiss (12).** Its semiconductor unit must expand optics output in step with ASML and deliver High-NA optics whose market penetration Zeiss itself says "various market dynamics are delaying" ([ZEISS Annual Report 2024/25](https://asset-downloads.zeiss.com/catalogs/download/cco2/c026d158-e8ca-4cbe-ac7f-8ef3541ed925/ZEISS_Gruppe_Gescha_ftsbericht_2024-25_EN_826455_0.pdf)). It has added production space at Oberkochen, Wetzlar, Roßdorf and Jena without giving amounts ([ZEISS SMT release, 24 Jul 2026](https://www.zeiss.com/semiconductor-manufacturing-technology/news-and-events/smt-press-releases/2026/smt-capacity-expansion.html)), and ASML says "We do not see Zeiss today as a bottleneck" (company claim). The money comes partly from its customer: ASML had €1,907.5m of loans and €1,191.9m of advance payments outstanding to Zeiss SMT at end-2025. The group around it is weaker. It launched a cost programme worth "several hundred million euros" a year in June 2026 and called its dependence on the semiconductor unit a core challenge ([ZEISS half-year release, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.zeiss.com/corporate/en/about-zeiss/present/newsroom/press-releases/2026/half-year-figures.html)). **Verdict: coherent, but financed and priced by its one optics customer.**

**Trumpf (18).** It forecasts double-digit growth in EUV orders and sales for the year to June 2027, consistent with ASML's capacity plan, alongside a group margin that will "decline significantly", and it is moving into defence lasers and data-centre equipment (company statements) ([TRUMPF Annual Report 2025/26](https://www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_Master/Corporate/Annual_report/Current/TRUMPF-Annual-Report-2025-26.pdf)). Free cash flow of €356m and a net financial position of +€776m pay for it. The risk is the one already seen: one customer whose ordering cut EUV sales by 23% in a year. **Verdict: coherent and self-funded.**

**Lasertec (9).** The next step is specific. Its new inspection tool for wafer fabs, the A200HiT, is in customer evaluation with qualification expected "around the end of this year… gradually early next year", and the order outlook for the year to June 2027 was raised from ¥300-400bn to ¥400-500bn on customers' forecasts for which, in management's words, "we haven't received the actual orders yet" ([Lasertec Q1 FY6/2027 briefing, 30 Oct 2026](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/6920/ir_material_for_fiscal_ym5/216849/00.pdf)). Beyond that it needs a first memory-fab adoption and volume use of High-NA, which it calls "still some way off". Funding is internal: capex is 1-2% of sales, there is no debt, and customer advances of ¥87.1bn finance work in process ([Q1 FY6/2027 results](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/6920/tdnet/2902313/00.pdf)). The fraud and write-down allegations a short-seller made in June 2024 have not appeared in audited accounts through June 2026, though the committee that cleared the inventory was commissioned by the company and published only a summary ([investigation summary, 9 Aug 2024](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/6920/tdnet/2490411/00.pdf)). **Verdict: coherent and self-funded; one qualification away from proof.**

**Nikon (33).** Its plan to 2030 targets revenue of ¥1tn and operating profit above ¥80bn, with semiconductor lithography told to "focus on expanding business in existing models" ([Nikon Medium-Term Management Plan, 8 May 2026](https://www.nikon.com/content/dam/web-assets/nikoncom/company/local/global/en/ir/management/midtermbusiness/pdf/2026/0508e.pdf)). The one leading-edge item, a next-generation immersion platform promised in November 2025 as a prototype "in FY28", was put under "reassessment" on 5 November 2026 ([Nikon Q&A, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.nikon.com/content/dam/web-assets/nikoncom/company/local/global/en/ir/ir_library/result/qa/2027_2q/20261109_e.pdf)). The plan is to be paid from operating cash flow that has not covered investment in any of the last three years; the company drew ¥49.5bn of new long-term borrowing in the latest half ([1H FY3/2027 results](https://www.nikon.com/content/dam/web-assets/nikoncom/company/local/global/en/ir/ir_library/result/pdf/2027/27_2qf_c_e.pdf)). Its claim of evaluations by new major customers is unconfirmed. **Verdict: not coherent at the leading edge; a legacy position with an option on tight immersion supply.**

**Canon (26).** The plan is coherent for what it is: more i-line and KrF share, a new KrF scanner in December 2026, capacity up more than 20% at its Utsunomiya plant, and Industrial sales rising to ¥600bn by 2030, paid from group cash flow ([Canon Phase VII notes, 15 Jan 2026](https://global.canon/en/ir/conference/pdf/phase7-e-note.pdf)). Its leading-edge options are unproven. Nanoimprint, launched in October 2023, is "making steady progress toward its adoption in mass production beginning in 2027" at an unnamed customer (company claim), and ArF is still a plan ([Canon Q3 2026 notes](https://global.canon/en/ir/conference/pdf/conf2026q3e-note.pdf)). It projected 308 lithography tools for 2025 and sold 234. **Verdict: coherent and funded, for mature nodes, memory periphery and packaging.**

**SMEE (35).** No plan can be assessed because nothing is published. Outside timelines put a commercially viable 7nm-capable Chinese immersion tool in the mid-2030s, and the best-attested domestic immersion effort is no longer SMEE's but a SiCarrier-linked firm's tool, reported in low-volume production in July 2026 ([Center for Technology & Statecraft, Sep 2026](https://techstatecraft.org/duv/)). **Verdict: cannot be assessed; evidence of China's ceiling, not an asset.**

### Front-end equipment and process control

All of these companies describe the same limit for 2027: clean-room space and components, not orders. Lam and KLA both moved their estimate of 2026 wafer-fab-equipment spending from about $135bn in January to the mid-$150bn range in October and see more than $200bn in 2027 (company claims) ([Lam Q1 FY2027 call, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/21/lam-research-lrcx-q1-2027-earnings-call-transcript/); [KLA shareholder letter, 28 Oct 2026](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_e2be8b99fd19637d99dedbc7b3bd0329/klatencor/db/1117/10679/letter_to_shareholders/KLA_Earnings_Shareholder_Letter_-_Q1_FY27.pdf)). **KLA (3).** Its March 2026 model for 2030 (revenue of $26bn plus or minus $2.5bn, gross margin 63.5%, a share of wafer-equipment spending near 9%) assumed a $215bn market in 2030; KLA now expects the market to exceed $200bn in 2027, three years ahead of that assumption, so the model will be superseded, not missed ([KLA Investor Day, CFO deck](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_e2be8b99fd19637d99dedbc7b3bd0329/klatencor/db/1167/10643/presentation/2026+Investor+Day_Higgins_Final_IR+copy.pdf)). It has put more than $250m into supplier capacity. Free cash flow of $4.0bn over twelve months against capex under $0.5bn pays for all of it. The checks that matter are gross margin against 63.5% and China falling from 34% of September-quarter revenue to the "low 20s" in 2027. **Verdict: coherent and self-funded.**

**Applied Materials (5).** It plans to double systems output from the October 2026 quarter's level by 2028, and its 2030 targets are a gross margin of about 54%, an operating margin of about 40% and two more points of share (company claims) ([Applied investor deck, 13 Oct 2026](https://ir.appliedmaterials.com/static-files/2e2e3831-97bb-439c-a469-14b855b6d4f0)). Capex has risen from $1.2bn in fiscal 2024 to $2.67bn in fiscal 2026 and headcount by about 5,000 in fiscal 2026, the year after it cut about 4% of staff in October 2025. Operating cash flow of $8.87bn covered capex, dividends and $3.18bn of buybacks with net cash intact ([Applied Q4 FY26 release, 12 Nov 2026](https://ir.appliedmaterials.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-materials-announces-fourth-quarter-and-fiscal-year-2026)). It has also agreed to lift its BESI stake to about 20%. The open question is not solvency but whether margin targets hold while capex, hiring and receivables (up 74% in a year) grow this fast. **Verdict: coherent and self-funded.**

**Tokyo Electron (6).** Its five-year plan from fiscal 2025 commits at least ¥1.5tn to R&D and ¥700bn to capex, with a new etch centre in Miyagi due in summer 2027 that ultimately triples etch capacity, and price revisions whose full effect arrives in the year to March 2028 (company claims) ([TEL Integrated Report 2026](https://www.tel.co.jp/ir/library/ar/oohl7200000000kk-att/ir2026_chapter1.pdf); [Q1 FY2027 Q&A](https://www.tel.co.jp/ir/library/report/negqvb0000000eqw-att/FY27Q1_EarningCall_QA_J.pdf); [Q2 FY2027 Q&A](https://www.tel.co.jp/ir/library/report/kuoaek00000000iu-att/FY27Q2_EarningCall_QA_J.pdf)). It must win back etch share, where it is a challenger at 400-layer NAND, and live with China at about a third of sales. Operating cash flow of ¥539.7bn in the year to March 2026 covered capex and a record ¥437.4bn of shareholder returns, with no debt ([TEL FY2026 results materials](https://www.tel.co.jp/ir/library/report/jngpc500000000l6-att/fy26q4transcript-j.pdf)). A new medium-term plan is due in spring 2027. **Verdict: coherent and self-funded.**

**Lam Research (8).** Lam says it is "ahead of the model" it gave in February 2025 and promised an update that had not appeared by 14 November 2026. Its near-term drivers have end dates. The NAND upgrade cycle that lifted 2025-26 would finish in 2027 on management's own account, and China will be "meaningfully down" in the December quarter ([Lam Q1 FY2027 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/21/lam-research-lrcx-q1-2027-earnings-call-transcript/)). Its technology claims (dry resist in HBM production, four tool-of-record wins for backside power) name no customer. Capex of about $1bn a year against trailing free cash flow of $6.5bn leaves room for the $25bn repurchase authorisation added on 5 November 2026 ([Lam release, 5 Nov 2026](https://investor.lamresearch.com/2026-11-05-Lam-Research-Corporation-Announces-25-Billion-Share-Repurchase-Authorization-and-Declares-Quarterly-Dividend)). **Verdict: coherent and self-funded; the most exposed of the US three to memory capex and Chinese domestic demand.**

**ASM International (10).** It now guides 2027 revenue above €5.7bn, which was its target for 2030, led by 1.4nm and 2nm logic; that leaves no published plan for 2028-2030 until an investor day expected in the second half of 2027 ([ASM Q3 2026 release, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/27/3389491/0/en/asm-reports-third-quarter-2026-results.html)). Management calls the MATCH Act its main uncertainty and assumes no regulatory change in the guide. Supply is tight, a new Arizona site opens in early 2027, and the finance chief changes on 1 December 2026. Nine-month free cash flow of €620m, with no debt, pays for the plan and a €300m buyback. Growth of about 35% in 2026 and 34% guided for 2027 matches forecast industry growth, so the outperformance its per-node claims imply is not yet visible. **Verdict: coherent and self-funded.**

**Screen Holdings (19).** Management says its next plan, from April 2027, may stay an "investment phase": capex was raised from ¥43bn to ¥58bn for a new site due in January 2028, and first-half free cash flow was -¥16.9bn ([Screen Q2 results](https://www.screen.co.jp/download_file/1a82bbc0-6a46-4f89-afed-362489d8cde5/317); [Q&A](https://www.screen.co.jp/download_file/4db3efd0-f722-4954-af39-5f753a4128cf/317)). Net cash of ¥189bn covers it. What it must show is delivery: unchanged guidance needs second-half operating profit 87% above the first half, after two quarters below market expectations, and its explanation for losing eight points of single-wafer share in 2025 (a memory-heavy year) is untested. **Verdict: coherent and funded from net cash; delivery still to be shown.**

**Kokusai Electric (20).** Revenue guidance was raised 21% in August 2026, and management's "direction" for the year to March 2028 is growth of about 40% with an adjusted margin of 30% or more. Its three plants can support about ¥500bn of revenue; beyond that a large new site is needed and no plan or cost has been set ([Kokusai Q&A, Nov 2026](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/6525/ir_material_for_fiscal_ym/217728/00.pdf)). The order book is the caution: orders reaching into the year to March 2029 are mostly from Chinese customers securing delivery slots, first-half orders had "a special-demand element", and a ban on tool exports to China is the downside a broker names. **Verdict: coherent to March 2028; the capacity plan beyond is uncosted and the orders lean on China.**

**Naura (31) and AMEC (32).** Both grow by replacing imports inside China, where domestic tools are below 15% of the market at 14nm and under and domestic parts below 8% ([Jiwei via Tencent News, 16 Sep 2026](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260916A09VKF00)). Naura, on the Entity List, blames "tight supply of some categories of materials" for a third quarter in which revenue grew 1.3%, gives no guidance, funds itself with cash flow and rising debt, and in October 2026 agreed to buy a loss-making hybrid-bonding start-up from a fund in which its own platform holds 16.45% ([iFeng](https://original.ifeng.com/c/8xmPL6TPxwe)). AMEC reports record orders and plans floor space for more than RMB 70bn of annual output (company claims) ([Sina, 8 Nov 2026](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/zqgd/2026-11-08/doc-inixyrxh7491095.shtml)), but capex exceeds operating cash flow and its founder is about 82. **Verdict for both: partly coherent; growth is policy-led, profit conversion (Naura) and profit quality (AMEC) are not yet there.**

**Disco (7).** Disco publishes no multi-year plan and guides one quarter ahead, so its path is read from its record: a sixth straight year of record sales to March 2026, shipments running ahead of sales, and customer advances up from ¥50.0bn to ¥79.6bn in six months ([Disco H1 results, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.disco.co.jp/jp/ir/library/doc/fr/fr20261022.pdf)). **Hoya (4).** It has to qualify new film materials for A14 and A10, bring 12-inch High-NA blanks to market, and keep second-source evaluations from turning into awards. **Tekscend Photomask (23).** It is starting four lines at once for the 2027-28 demand window: Singapore in the first half of 2027, an advanced line at Round Rock in the United States in the second half, EUV mass production in 2027 and a third Korean plant in 2028 (company plan) ([Tekscend H1 briefing, 11 Nov 2026](https://contents.xj-storage.jp/xcontents/AS83405/6d05be1b/6fe1/4d9b/a123/eb86e87f98ca/140120261111572776.pdf)). **AGC (27).** It must regain EUV-blank volume against Hoya at 2nm and High-NA after a 2025 dip caused by one customer, while group earnings weaken: on 5 November 2026 it cut its 2026 operating-profit forecast from ¥150bn to ¥120bn ([AGC forecast revision](https://ssl4.eir-parts.net/doc/5201/tdnet/2904829/00.pdf)). On 9 November 2026 Mitsui Chemicals agreed to buy the company for an enterprise value of ¥1,840bn, paid with a ¥1,640bn bridge loan that takes the buyer's leverage "temporarily above 4x", with closing targeted for October 2027 to March 2028 and subject to competition and foreign-investment clearances ([Mitsui Chemicals disclosure, 9 Nov 2026](https://jp.mitsuichemicals.com/content/dam/mitsuichemicals/sites/mci/documents/release/2026/261109.pdf)). ## 3. Entry point: a 30-60% fall has priced a shallow pause, not a deep one

This section stands apart from the ranking. It asks one question of each company: is the 13 November 2026 price a sensible place to start? A high rank does not earn a favourable answer, and a low rank does not rule one out.

### What the market has and has not priced

The sector has already had a bear market inside a boom. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index peaked at 14,634.72 on 22 June 2026, fell 38% to 9,011.14 on 5 November, a day on which it lost 12.25% for its second-largest one-day fall on record ([24/7 Wall St, 6 Nov 2026](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/06/chip-stocks-just-had-their-second-worst-day-on-record/)), and closed at 10,146.32 on 13 November, still up 43% in 2026 ([Yahoo Finance index data](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/%5ESOX?period1=757382400&period2=1794700000&interval=1d)). Of the 30 listed shares here, 23 sit 30-64% below their 52-week highs, and forward price/earnings ratios are below the five-year quarter-end median for 23 of them ([StockAnalysis statistics and ratio pages, 13 Nov 2026 close](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/amat/statistics/)). The fall was a change of multiple, not of estimates: Applied Materials' forward multiple went from 49 times on 30 June to about 19 times on 6 November while its fiscal 2027 earnings consensus rose from $16.40 to $18.56 ([TIKR, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.tikr.com/blog/b-riley-cut-its-applied-materials-target-to-475-it-still-sees-28-upside); [TIKR, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.tikr.com/blog/applied-materials-is-up-59-in-a-year-even-after-halving-from-its-high-double-our-output)).

What the market fears is a pause in AI capital spending, and the evidence gives it reason. The four largest US buyers spent about 65% of their operating cash flow on capital projects in 2025 and about 100% in January-September 2026, by a calculation on their filed figures ([SEC XBRL data](https://data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK0001018724/us-gaap/PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets.json)), and the five largest issued more than $370bn of bonds in 2026 to 4 November ([Finimize, citing LSEG](https://finimize.com/content/big-techs-ai-bond-binge-pushed-us-issuance-to-a-record)). Two mainstream forecasters already pencil in a stall or a fall for 2028: UBS has hyperscaler spending up 6% ([Reuters via Express Tribune, 17 Jul 2026](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2618784/among-ai-crowd-some-investors-position-for-slower-hyperscaler-spending-growth)) and Capital Economics has AI investment down about 10% ([Fortune, 8 Nov 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/11/08/stock-market-outlook-sp500-ai-capex-gdp-growth-productivity/)), while Morgan Stanley sees US data-centre capacity additions down 4% in 2028 and parts of the supply chain in "a traditional downcycle" ([investingLive, 9 Nov 2026](https://investinglive.com/stocks/morgan-stanley-says-buy-the-ai-dip-sees-3-4-trillion-in-hyperscaler-spending-through-2028/)). Against that, every large buyer guided 2027 higher in late October and supply contracts now run to 2028-2030. My reading, with low confidence, is about 30% for an outright fall in the big buyers' spending in 2028 or 2029 and about one in two for growth below 10%, a stall that tool, wafer and memory suppliers would feel as flat or falling orders.

History says how much of that is in the price. In the two shallow downturns (2019 and 2023) equipment makers' revenue fell 0-15% and their shares 26-57%; in the two deep ones (2001-03 and 2008-09) revenue fell 37-58%, profits became losses and shares fell 65-90% ([Applied Materials 10-K for fiscal 2003](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/6951/000089161804000093/f95058e10vk.htm); [Lam 10-K for fiscal 2010](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/707549/000119312510194304/d10k.htm)). Applied, Lam and KLA fell 56-57% from their June highs to their 5 November lows with revenue still rising. A shallow pause is therefore arguably priced; a deep one is not. Consensus has not moved to match. It still assumes revenue growth of 38% at TSMC and 29% at ASML in 2027, and of 38-52% at the three US toolmakers in their fiscal years ending in 2027 ([StockAnalysis forecast pages](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lrcx/forecast/)), and several target-price pages were last updated before the sell-off.

The calendar argues for building any position in stages. Nvidia reports on 17 November, its first results under an interim chief ([TrendForce, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/11/09/news-nvidia-faces-leadership-test-ahead-of-earnings-ceo-probe-congressional-briefing-request-and-key-risks-to-watch/)). Futures priced about an 87% chance of a US rate rise on 9 December, with the ten-year yield near 5.13% ([CNBC, 13 Nov 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-live-updates.html)). The US-China truce, the suspended US affiliates rule and China's rare-earth pause all run out on 10 January 2027 ([Federal Register, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/09/2026-22793/extension-of-the-suspension-of-expansion-of-end-user-controls-for-affiliates-of-certain-listed)). TSMC gives its first 2027 capital-spending figure in mid-January, ASML its first 2027 guidance on 27 January ([Simply Wall St event feed](https://simplywall.st/stocks/us/semiconductors/nasdaq-asml/asml-holding/future)), and the large buyers their 2027 budgets in late January and early February. Single-day moves of 10% or more clustered on market-wide dates: 15 and 22 October, 5-6 November and 9-11 November.

I call an entry sensible when four things hold: the company is in the top third for quality; its forward multiple sits in the lower part of its own five-year range on estimates that do not need 2028 to be another boom year; the balance sheet or a buyback gives support; and no unresolved yes-or-no event sits just ahead. For the memory makers and for loss-makers I use price-to-book instead of price/earnings, because in this industry a low price/earnings ratio has marked the top, not the bottom: Samsung traded on 6.8 times trailing earnings at the end of 2022, just before its net income fell 74% ([Samsung ratio history](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/krx/005930/financials/ratios/)), whereas price-to-book marked the turns, with troughs near 0.8-1.1 times and earlier year-end peaks near 1.6-2.3 times ([Micron price-to-book series](https://companiesmarketcap.com/micron-technology/pb-ratio/)). One vendor, StockAnalysis, supplies nearly all the valuation data below, and its share counts and net-cash figures were not reconciled to filings.

### Where quality and entry point pull apart

**Top quality at below-average prices.** TSMC, ASML, Hoya, ASM International, Tokyo Electron and Disco combine a top-ten quality rank with a forward multiple in the lower part of their own five-year range, above no more than 7 of the last 20 quarter-ends. ASML's is the starkest: 23.3 times is higher than only one of its last 20 quarter-ends, although the trailing multiple of 38 times sits near its ten-year average and the forward figure is low only because consensus has earnings rising 36% in 2027. For TSMC the same caveat applies with more force, since its 66% gross margin is ten points above its own long-run target and the forward multiple of 16.5 times becomes about 21 times if 2027 earnings merely hold. These are entries that make sense for a holder who can sit through a 2028 stall, not bargains that are safe against one. Hoya asks least of the cycle: consensus needs earnings growth of only 12-15%, the share is up 1% on the year, and ¥125bn of buyback authority runs to March 2027.

**Top quality at an average price.** KLA is third for quality and only fair as an entry. At 24.5 times forward earnings it sits exactly at its five-year median and carries the highest multiples of the US three on every measure, and in 2026 its fall from the peak was as deep as its peers', unlike 2018 and 2022 when it fell less ([Trefis, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.trefis.com/stock/amat/articles/623842/applied-materials-stock-at-373-bargain-or-warning/2026-11-11)). Applied Materials is in the same position with a little more margin: after first closing 50% below a peak in 2018 and 2022 its shares were 105% and 69% higher twelve months later, but only 14% higher after 2008, following a further 28% fall.

**Lower quality at low prices.** Screen (19th) trades on 10 times forward earnings with net cash equal to about an eighth of its market value, and Tekscend (23rd) trades at its listing price on 5 times EV/EBITDA. Both are cheap for stated reasons: Screen has lost share and missed two quarters, and Tekscend's 46.5% holder has been free to sell since April 2026. They are the clearest cases of the reverse the question anticipates, a middling business that can be a reasonable entry once a named event passes.

**Mid-table quality at peak prices.** The memory makers look cheapest on earnings and are the poorest entries. A forward multiple of 2.4-4.4 times is the market saying earnings will fall; the measure that marked past turns, price-to-book, stands at 2.16 times for Samsung, 2.87 for SK Hynix and 6.36 for Micron against year-end peaks of roughly 1.6-2.3 times in earlier cycles. Samsung is the least stretched and the only one with businesses beyond memory, which is why it is "not yet" where the other two are "no". Entegris is a quieter version of the same problem: mid-table quality, the smallest fall from its high (21%) and a gain of 75% this year.

**Shares that are not a way to own the field.** Canon, Dai Nippon Printing and AGC are priced as diversified groups, correctly. Intel at 33-46 times 2027 earnings, depending on the consensus source, and the Chinese shares, from 38 times forward earnings at Naura to about 110 times trailing earnings for SMIC in Shanghai, are priced for outcomes their own results do not yet show. SMIC's two lines price the same earnings 2.6 times apart, so the question of whether its price is sensible has two answers, and for a US person neither can be acted on because the company is on the Treasury's investment-ban list ([OFAC NS-CMIC list](https://www.treasury.gov/ofac/downloads/ccmc/nscmiclist.pdf)).

## 4. Short candidates: Micron and Intel, each conditional on timing

Two candidates can be justified, but not with equal confidence, and neither is a high-conviction short today. The sector has already fallen 30-60% from its June highs, short interest is low everywhere, and the cycle evidence says a shallow pause is in the price. What is left to short is whatever the price has not yet discounted. For Micron that is a return on equity with no precedent; for Intel it is an earnings recovery that needs customers nobody has named. Micron is the stronger of the two. If only one thesis is wanted, it is the memory price cycle, and Micron is its cleanest expression.

### Micron: the highest price-to-book on the most price-driven profits

**Reasoning.** Micron's fiscal 2026 revenue rose 256% to $133.2bn at a 74.6% operating margin, and roughly nine-tenths of the increase was price, not volume. Prices are still rising but at a falling rate: quarterly DRAM contract increases went from about 80% in 2Q26 to about 40% in 3Q26 and about 20% expected for 4Q26 (press) ([TrendForce, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026-10-20/news-dram-prices-seen-rising-20-qoq-in-4q26-despite-slower-gains-ai-demand-tight-supply-support-outlook/)); a benchmark NAND contract price was flat in October after 21 straight monthly rises ([Tech Insider, citing DRAMeXchange](https://tech-insider.org/dram-record-high-nand-flat-21-months-october-2026/)); notebook shipments fell 15.8% in 3Q26 and PC makers are trimming memory orders as inventories build ([TrendForce, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261111-13303.html)); and China's CXMT went from 4% to 10% of DRAM revenue in a year. Micron is also guiding to more than $50bn of capex in fiscal 2027 by my addition of its half-year figures, mostly for clean rooms that arrive from late 2028. Of the three memory makers it has the smallest HBM share and the highest book multiple, and its US listing makes it mechanically the easiest of the three to short.

**What the price already reflects.** At $778.31 the share is 38% below its June high but still up 173% in 2026. The forward multiple of 4.4 times, lower than at any of the last 20 quarter-ends, says investors already expect earnings to fall steeply after fiscal 2027-28. What is not reflected is on the other side of the ledger: price-to-book of 6.36 times stands above every year-end value of 2016-2025 (1.02-6.04 times), consensus has revenue up another 107% this fiscal year, and 45 of 49 analysts rate the share a buy with an average target twice the price ([StockAnalysis forecast page](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/forecast/)).

**Main risks to the position.** The first is time. If consensus earnings of $176.50 a share arrive in fiscal 2027, equity of $138.4bn more than doubles and today's market value falls below three times book within a year (my arithmetic on figures in the [8-K of 30 Sep 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000723125/000072312526000018/a2026q4ex991-pressrelease.htm) and the consensus above), so the short loses its valuation argument unless prices crack soon. The second is contract cover: 26 take-or-pay agreements, $12.7bn of customer deposits, and a "vast majority" of 2027 HBM supply contracted at higher prices (company claims). The third is the company's own buying, with $35.16bn of repurchase authority usable from 9 December 2026. The fourth is violence in both directions: the share fell 17.5% on 5 November and rose 11.9% on 11 November. Short interest is only 2.07% of float, so the position is not crowded.

**What would prove it wrong.** Contract prices that keep rising through 2027; a fiscal first-quarter report (expected mid-December) with gross margin at or above the guided 86% and customer deposits growing; take-or-pay floors that hold when spot prices fall, a test these contracts have not yet faced; and 2027 budgets from the large buyers, due by early February, that match the growth they signalled in October. Any two of these would say the cycle has another year, which is long enough for book value to catch up with the price.

### Intel: a recovery multiple without the customers the recovery needs

**Reasoning.** Intel trades at about 43 times forward earnings and 2.76 times book, a price-to-book higher than at 18 of its last 20 quarter-ends, while its guidance points down: fourth-quarter revenue of $15.0-16.0bn and a non-GAAP gross margin of 38.0% against 44.1% in the third quarter, a PC market it expects to shrink about 10% in 2027, and 2027 capex "up strongly" ([Intel Q3 2026 prepared remarks, 29 Oct 2026](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_5bcd8acc7e1b5c73a62283d752dd0b96/intel/db/887/9273/prepared_remarks/Q3%2726_Prepared_Remarks.pdf)). A press summary of the call has the finance chief not expecting positive free cash flow in 2026 ([investingLive, 29 Oct 2026](https://investinglive.com/stocks/intel-earnings-call-18a-progress-and-data-centre-growth-offset-by-pc-slump-and-cash-burn/)). The share rose from $19 in August 2025 to $141 in June 2026, yet the foundry business is still almost entirely prospective. External foundry revenue was $878m in nine months, most of it a former subsidiary and one non-recurring contract; no 14A or 18A-P customer has been named; and Intel's policy is not to name them. The share count is up 23% since 2024, with 132m more shares in escrow for the US government.

**What the price already reflects.** A great deal: the share is 58% below its June peak, 37% below the $95 at which Intel sold $23bn of stock in August, and fell 28.6% on 30 October alone ([Nasdaq historical prices](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/intc/historical)). The fourth-quarter guide and the PC slump are in the price. What is not: consensus still has earnings recovering, to between $1.31 and about $1.79 a share in 2027 depending on the source ([Nasdaq/Zacks analyst data](https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/intc/analyst-research); [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/intc/forecast/)); 34 of 49 analysts sit at hold with an average target of about $91; and nothing in the price allows for the customer window closing empty in mid-2027, foundry break-even slipping into 2028, or an impairment at Mobileye, where goodwill has less than 10% headroom.

**Main risks to the position.** This short turns on timing, not solvency. Intel has $54.0bn of cash and a plan funded through 2028, so there is no balance-sheet event to wait for. A single announcement can move the share violently: it rose 23.6% on one results day in April 2026, and a Japanese summary of the October call, which I could not check against a transcript, says management expects to announce new foundry customers "within the next two quarters" ([Business Insider Japan](https://www.businessinsider.jp/article/2611-earnings-intel-corporation-q3-2026-earnings-call-summary/)). The US government, Nvidia and SoftBank bought shares in 2025 and have reasons to help. Server processors are in shortage, with average prices up 54% year on year in the third quarter, and Intel says supply stays tight through 2027. Short interest is about 3% of the shares.

**What would prove it wrong.** A named outside customer for 14A or 18A-P that confirms a volume commitment itself; a fiscal 2026 annual report (due about late January 2027) that withdraws the 14A risk factor for that reason; gross margin back above 44% by mid-2027; or a Foundry operating loss falling well below the current $1.9bn a quarter without help from transfer prices.

### Why not the others

**SK Hynix** is the same thesis as Micron with a lower book multiple (2.87 times), a share already 59% off its high and about KRW 100tn of 2026 shareholder returns; its Nasdaq depositary shares can be borrowed, so it is the alternative for anyone who prefers the HBM leader losing share. **Samsung** is weaker again as a short: lowest book multiple, other businesses, KRW 55-75tn still to return. The dearest shares against their own economics are Chinese: **AMEC** at 97 times 2026 earnings with 61% of nine-month profit from non-recurring gains, **SMIC's Shanghai line** at about 110 times, **Naura** at 58 times 2026 earnings on flat profit. They fail on mechanics. Securities-lending short balances in the three A-shares are 0.01-0.05% of float ([exchange data via Eastmoney](https://datacenter-web.eastmoney.com/api/data/v1/get?reportName=RPTA_WEB_RZRQ_GGMX&columns=ALL&source=WEB&sortColumns=DATE&sortTypes=-1&pageNumber=1&pageSize=3&filter=%28SCODE%3D%22688012%22%29)), too small to suggest shorting at size is practical, and whether stock can be borrowed through Stock Connect was not verified. SMIC's Hong Kong line can be shorted, with reported short positions of 0.88% of the Hong Kong shares on 6 November ([SFC short-position data](https://www.sfc.hk/en/Regulatory-functions/Market/Short-position-reporting/Aggregated-reportable-short-positions-of-specified-shares)), but it trades at a 61% discount to Shanghai, is down 32% this year, and is closed to US persons. **Entegris** has the smallest fall and real leverage, but sales are growing 25% and its margins are rising. **Kokusai** has an order book its own management calls partly one-off, yet at 15 times next year's consensus after a 50% fall the pay-off is thin. **Lasertec** is a coin-toss on one qualification. **Nikon** is weak but trades below book with an 18.5% strategic holder cleared to go to 20%. Published short interest is low across the whole list (1.7-3.1% of float for the large US names and no disclosed position of 0.5% or more in Tokyo Electron, Lasertec, Screen, Disco, Shin-Etsu, Hoya or Tokyo Ohka), and borrow cost and availability were not obtained for any market.

Three consequences follow. First, the order of tightness today is memory, then powered data-centre space, then leading-edge wafers and EUV, then packaging, which is easing. That is why SK Hynix and Micron belong in the list and why TSMC is reported to be raising wafer prices by 3-6% from January 2027 ([TrendForce, 14 Sep 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026-09-14/news-tsmc-reportedly-targets-22-2nm-16-3nm-capacity-boost-by-mid-2027-cowos-to-double-by-2028/)) while a benchmark DRAM contract price went from $8.70 in October 2025 to $48.00 in September 2026 ([Tech Insider, citing DRAMeXchange](https://tech-insider.org/dram-record-high-nand-flat-21-months-october-2026/)). Second, "cannot remove" has a date on it. TSMC's 2nm capacity is to more than double in 2027, ASML's output rises 30% a year, and the memory makers' new clean rooms open from late 2028; if demand stalls in 2028-29, those additions become the excess. Third, the earlier work called the pause "financing-led". On this evidence power, permits and late delivery are as likely a trigger: Microsoft's first shortfall against its own capex guidance came because third-party capacity arrived late, not because demand weakened ([secondary write-up of the October call](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/11/07/microsoft-fy2027-q1-earnings-analysis)), and after the 3 November midterm elections Morgan Stanley put about a fifth of US data-centre projects planned for 2028 at risk of slipping a year ([investingLive, 9 Nov 2026](https://investinglive.com/stocks/morgan-stanley-says-buy-the-ai-dip-sees-3-4-trillion-in-hyperscaler-spending-through-2028/)). For chip, tool and materials makers the trigger matters little, because orders slip either way.

## Conclusion

The pattern that separates these 35 companies is not the node they work at but who sets their price and their volume. Profit sits one step upstream of wherever capacity is being added: with the blank maker, not the mask shops; with the scanner maker, not the optics and laser suppliers it finances; with the foundry that customers have not left, not the fabs that states have paid for. Public money has bought buildings, tools and time for Intel's foundry, Rapidus, SMIC and the Chinese toolmakers. It has not bought the one thing the first test asks for, and in every case the event that would move a company up this ranking is a customer putting its own name to an order: an outside 14A customer, a Rapidus volume contract, a domestic immersion tool counted on SMIC's lines, a second EUV-blank award at 2nm. Those are observable, and most fall due between now and mid-2028.

The earlier work's central idea needs a date and a different subject. "Constraints AI demand cannot remove" is a statement about roughly the next two years, and the constraint that binds hardest has moved from logic and lithography to memory and power. That is where this year's extraordinary profits are, where the price-to-book ratios have no precedent and where the short case sits, while the true chokepoints (TSMC, ASML, KLA, Hoya) have been marked down to the lower part of their own valuation ranges before a single revenue line has fallen. The market has priced a shallow pause in the toolmakers and something worse in memory earnings, but not in memory book values. Ten weeks of dated tests, from Nvidia's results on 17 November through the 10 January policy deadline to the first 2027 budgets in late January, will show whether "supply-limited" survives contact with the buyers' own financing. Until then the sensible posture is the one the evidence supports: own the confirmed chokepoints in stages, wait for named events at the companies one qualification or one review away from proof, and treat this year's memory margins as a price, not a moat.

# Seven quantum builders outrun their independent verification

As of 14 November 2026, none of the seven companies examined (IBM Quantum, Google Quantum AI, Quantinuum, IonQ, QuEra, PsiQuantum and IQM) has built a fault-tolerant quantum computer or shown a commercially useful quantum advantage that outsiders have confirmed, and almost every headline performance figure they publish is self-measured. The physics under all four hardware approaches (superconducting circuits at IBM, Google and IQM; trapped ions at Quantinuum and IonQ; neutral atoms at QuEra; photons at PsiQuantum) is established, and no source reviewed identifies a fundamental physical barrier. What remains unproven is engineering at 100 to 10,000 times today's scale, where clock speed, photon loss, chip-to-chip links, atom loss and correlated errors set the limits. Outside checks are sparse: DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative began testing hardware only in October 2026, with **IBM, IonQ and PsiQuantum in Stage C, Quantinuum and QuEra in Stage B, Google in Stage A and IQM not participating** ([DARPA, list as of 8 Oct 2026](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/quantum-benchmarking-initiative)). Across the seven, component milestones (gate fidelities, chip fabrication) mostly arrive on time while system and deployment milestones slip by months to roughly two years. The money tells seven different stories. IonQ guides to **$505–515 million** of 2026 revenue, but **$83.5 million of its $173.8 million third quarter came from an acquired chip foundry** ([IonQ Q3 2026 results, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1824920/000119312526449779/ionq-ex99_1.htm)). Quantinuum guides to **$31–34 million** and holds **$1.94 billion** of cash ([Quantinuum Q3 2026 results, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026071000/q32026earningspressrelease.htm)). IQM guides to **€42–47 million**, which needs a fourth quarter larger than its first nine months combined ([IQM Q3 2026 interim report, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.manilatimes.net/2026/11/05/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/interim-report-for-q3-2026/2462482)). IBM, Alphabet, QuEra and PsiQuantum disclose no quantum revenue. Governments now act as customers, lenders, shareholders and referees at once, and the loudest advantage claims remain contested: one instance from IBM's July 2026 "quantum advantage era" papers was reproduced classically in **37.3 minutes on 256 GPUs** ([Manabe, Gu, Pan, arXiv 2608.13110, 13 Aug 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13110)).

## Independent verification covers a sliver of the claims

Three kinds of evidence exist for these companies, and they are not equally plentiful. Company-reported figures (press releases, spec sheets, company-authored preprints) cover nearly everything. Peer-reviewed papers cover a minority of flagship results, and most of those are company-authored. Measurements by parties with no stake (government testers, unaffiliated academics, customers publishing their own data) are rare. The only government body that has publicly sorted these firms is DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (QBI). Stage A is a six-month review of concepts, Stage B a one-year review of R&D plans, and Stage C a hardware test by a government independent verification and validation team, with per-company ceilings of up to $1 million, $15 million and $300 million respectively ([DARPA QBI page, accessed 14 Nov 2026](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/quantum-benchmarking-initiative); [DARPA, 3 Apr 2025](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/companies-targeting-quantum-computers)). On 7 October 2026 DARPA moved Atom Computing, Diraq, IBM and IonQ into Stage C, joining Microsoft and PsiQuantum, and programme manager Micah Stoutimore said **"Stage C is where we begin moving from plans to proof"** ([DARPA, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2026/four-more-teams-enter-quantum-benchmarking-initiative-final-stage)). DARPA's verdicts so far therefore judge designs and plans, not measured performance. DARPA says firms not yet promoted are not ruled out and gave no funding amounts.

Beyond DARPA, independent measurement of these machines is thin. The one cross-vendor hardware benchmark, run by Jülich Supercomputing Centre and Purdue on 24 processors, certified **Quantinuum's H2-1 at 56 fully connected qubits and 4,620 two-qubit gates**, IonQ's Forte Enterprise at 28 qubits and IBM's ibm_fez at 20 qubits on fully connected problems; IBM's Heron held a coherent signal at 100–156 qubits on a one-dimensional chain to depth p≈300, and IQM's Garnet stayed coherent to about p=50. It also found that vendor-quoted layer-fidelity metrics "do not consistently predict algorithmic performance" ([Montañez-Barrera, Michielsen, Bernal Neira, arXiv 2502.06471 v2, 28 May 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06471v2)). Google, QuEra and PsiQuantum hardware was not tested. A Jülich follow-up ranked IQM's 54-qubit Emerald above its 20-qubit Garnet but found IQM devices degrade faster with circuit depth than IBM's ([Montañez-Barrera & Michielsen, arXiv 2610.05928, 5 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05928)). Outside groups have run error correction on IBM's cloud Nighthawk r2 ([arXiv 2610.23840, 16 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23840)), and Sandia National Laboratories has run encoded arithmetic on Quantinuum's Helios-1 ([arXiv 2610.14202, 9 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14202)). The UK's £30 million testbed programme reprints supplier-written performance sections rather than its own measurements, and its evaluation phase runs to Q2 2027 ([NQCC QCTI Phase 1 report, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.nqcc.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NQCC_Quantum-Computing-Testbed-Initiative_Phase-1-report.pdf)). Customers that host machines (RIKEN, AIST, ORNL, LRZ, IT4I, EPB, the Basque Government) confirm installation but publish no acceptance tests or fidelity data ([ORNL/OLCF, 8 Jul 2026](https://www.olcf.ornl.gov/2026/07/08/ornl-deploys-new-iqm-quantum-computer/); [EuroHPC JU, 12 Feb 2026](https://eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/inauguration-euro-q-exa-expanding-european-quantum-computing-infrastructure-2026-02-12_en)).

The headline numbers also measure different things. "Qubits" can mean physical qubits, error-corrected logical qubits, error-detected logical qubits kept only when no error is flagged, or IonQ's "algorithmic qubits", a company-defined benchmark score. Two-qubit "fidelity" can mean one best pair in a laboratory, a median over a commercial chip, or an average with all qubits operating at once. Error-correction "records" variously measure memory per round, logical gates or magic-state preparation. Google's Willow spec sheet itself combines error-correction figures from one physical chip with random-circuit-sampling figures from another ([Willow spec sheet, 9 Dec 2024](https://quantumai.google/static/site-assets/downloads/willow-spec-sheet.pdf)). An independent procurement review concluded that vendor roadmaps are **"procurement signals, not procurement evidence"** ([Krasnok, arXiv 2604.04761, 6 Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04761v1)). ## Seven dossiers: what each company makes, earns, promised and has proven

Each dossier follows the same order: products and stage; roadmap and the record against earlier promises; money; customers, capacity and people; legal and regulatory matters; the science, including who outside the company has checked it; the physical and engineering limits; and an assessment of whether the science stands on solid ground. "My arithmetic" marks calculations made from cited figures that the company itself has not stated.

### IBM Quantum: gate counts on schedule, modules and deployments behind

**Products and stage.** IBM's quantum business sits inside IBM Research, whose director since 1 October 2025 has been Jay Gambetta, IBM's long-time quantum lead, reporting to CEO Arvind Krishna ([IBM, 30 Sep 2025](https://newsroom.ibm.com/blog-ibm-names-new-director-of-ibm-research)). It builds superconducting transmon processors and sells cloud access, on-premises "System Two" installations and software (Qiskit and the renamed IBM Quantum Functions). The flagship is **Nighthawk r2: 120 qubits on a square lattice with 218 tunable couplers**, generally available from September 2026. IBM says it runs circuits of more than 7,500 operations at up to 100,000 samples per second (25 times Heron) and that clients ran more than 34 billion circuits on it with gains of "up to 19x" over earlier IBM processors ([IBM blog, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/qdc-2026); [IBM newsroom, 11 Nov 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-11-11-ibm-quantum-nighthawk-demonstrates-new-levels-of-speed-and-quality,-with-plans-to-scale-beyond-1,000-qubits-in-2027)). IBM's documentation lists the chip as "120 programmable, 458 physical" qubits ([IBM docs, accessed 14 Nov 2026](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/guides/processor-types)); 458 equals 120 qubits plus 218 couplers plus 120 reset elements, which is my arithmetic rather than IBM's explanation. The 156-qubit Heron r3 remains in service. In November 2025 only **57 of its 176 couplings had two-qubit errors below 1×10⁻³** ([IBM blog, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/qdc-2025)), and the joint IBM–RIKEN release for the Kobe Heron gave a two-qubit error of 3×10⁻³, best 1×10⁻³ ([IBM–RIKEN, 23 Jun 2025](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-06-23-ibm-and-riken-unveil-first-ibm-quantum-system-two-outside-of-the-u-s)). IBM's 2026 announcements emphasise throughput and gate counts and give **no fleet-wide two-qubit error, layer fidelity or coherence figure for Nighthawk**. The experimental Loon chip, carrying the long on-chip "c-couplers" and multilayer routing IBM's error-correction design needs, has been built; IBM says only that its performance is "comparable to Nighthawk r2" ([IBM blog, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/qdc-2026)). IBM counts more than 90 systems deployed via cloud and on site, used by more than 340 organisations ([IBM FAQ, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/10-billion-investment-faq)). On-premises System Two machines run at RIKEN in Kobe (full operation 24 June 2025), in San Sebastián for the Basque Government (inaugurated 14 October 2025, running at "a quarter of its capacity" at year-end inside a €120 million regional programme through 2028, per [Onda Vasca, 31 Dec 2025](https://www.ondavasca.com/el-ordenador-cuantico-basq-podria-alcanzar-la-mitad-de-su-capacidad-en-enero/)) and in Chicago, where go-live was announced on 6 November 2026 with Nighthawk r2 rather than the Heron promised in December 2024 ([XenoSpectrum, Nov 2026](https://xenospectrum.com/en/ibm-quantum-system-two-chicago-nighthawk/)).

**Roadmap and record.** IBM's top targets are unchanged: Starling, a large-scale fault-tolerant machine running **100 million operations on 200 logical qubits, in 2029** at Poughkeepsie, then Blue Jay with 2,000 logical qubits and a billion operations ([IBM, 10 Jun 2025](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-06-10-IBM-Sets-the-Course-to-Build-Worlds-First-Large-Scale,-Fault-Tolerant-Quantum-Computer-at-New-IBM-Quantum-Data-Center)). Blue Jay is dated "2033+" on the roadmap page but "mid-2030s" in IBM's HRL release ([IBM roadmap, accessed 14 Nov 2026](https://www.ibm.com/roadmaps/quantum/); [IBM, 26 Aug 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-08-26-ibm-completes-acquisition-of-hrl-laboratories-to-accelerate-the-future-of-quantum)). In November 2026 IBM added Nighthawk r3 (520 programmable qubits, 2,034 physical, ten chip-to-chip "l-couplers") and a two-chip 1,040-qubit system for 2027. It also cut Starling's Josephson junctions per module from about 5,000 to about 2,000, moved to a code needing five connections per qubit instead of six, and said Starling needs more than 100 l-couplers. Two linked Nighthawk r1 chips reached **99% average and 99.3% best state-transfer fidelity, against a 99.9% target** ([IBM blog, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/qdc-2026); [IBM newsroom, 11 Nov 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-11-11-ibm-quantum-nighthawk-demonstrates-new-levels-of-speed-and-quality,-with-plans-to-scale-beyond-1,000-qubits-in-2027)). **Kookaburra**, the 2026 module meant to combine a logical processing unit with quantum memory, remains on the roadmap page but appeared in none of the 11 November materials, and no delivery has been announced. IBM has scheduled a "Quantum Day" for 10 December 2026 ([IBM Q3 2026 call, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/21/ibm-ibm-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).

**Science and verification.** IBM's experiments are reproducible as experiments; its claims of being beyond classical reach have repeatedly been answered by classical simulation. The 2023 "utility" experiment on the 127-qubit Eagle (Nature 618) was matched within days by a Flatiron/NYU tensor-network method "significantly more accurate and precise than the results obtained from the quantum processor" ([Tindall et al., arXiv 2306.14887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14887)) and by Caltech's sparse Pauli dynamics on "a single core of a laptop" ([Begušić & Chan, arXiv 2306.16372](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16372)). Peaked-circuit candidates on IBM's advantage tracker were overtaken classically within days in November 2025 and simulated "in an hour to seconds" by February 2026 ([IBM, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/quantum-advantage-tracker)). On 30 July 2026 IBM declared "We are now firmly in the quantum advantage era" on the strength of three preprints ([IBM newsroom, 30 Jul 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-07-30-ibm-and-the-university-of-chicago-demonstrate-quantum-advantage,-establishing-trusted-quantum-computation-on-logical-circuits)). An independent fact-check found **none was peer-reviewed**, and all three remain "active candidates" on the tracker rather than adjudicated results ([PostQuantum, 1 Aug 2026](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/ibm-three-quantum-advantage-papers/)). The strongest, IBM and the University of Chicago's doped-Clifford sampling on Heron, used 97 physical qubits (70 data, 27 syndrome), 2,869 CZ gates and 468 T rotations applied as virtual frame updates. It kept 2,051 samples from about 3.5 million shots (0.059% acceptance) in 16.1 minutes of processor time, with a certified fidelity lower bound of 0.284, and its "logical" qubits are error-detected, not fault-tolerant (same fact-check). Manabe, Gu and Pan then computed exact amplitudes for all 2,051 published batches in **37.3 minutes on 32 nodes of eight NVIDIA H100s**, with a cross-entropy score "compatible with IBM's fidelity lower bound" ([arXiv 2608.13110, v2 29 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13110)). The Qedma paper (up to 74 qubits) rests on the failure of the classical methods tried plus heuristic extrapolation; the Algorithmiq paper (56 qubits) rests on cross-device consistency while three classical groups disagreed with one another. IBM's own blog concedes that announcing advantage "does not close the case" ([IBM, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/quantum-advantage)). On error correction, IBM's bivariate-bicycle "gross" code is peer-reviewed theory and simulation: **12 logical qubits in 288 physical qubits preserved for about a million cycles at 0.1% physical error, against about 3,000 qubits for a surface code**, with a 0.8% threshold and a need for six-way connectivity across two wiring layers ([Bravyi et al., Nature 627, 2024; arXiv 2308.07915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07915)). No hardware demonstration of a below-threshold qLDPC memory was found. The Relay-BP decoder is supported by simulation ([arXiv 2506.01779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01779)) and IBM's FPGA latency benchmark, with no outside replication. The most useful outside data come from third parties running surface codes on IBM's cloud: on Nighthawk r2, logical error per round fell from 0.53% to 0.24% between distances 3 and 6 (Λ ≈ 1.7–1.9), but **"the distance advantage weakens" over 150 rounds** ([arXiv 2610.23840, 16 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23840)). Another group reported that monolithic fabrication "produces unavoidable heterogeneity" that forces weak qubits and couplers out of the schedule ([arXiv 2610.11658, 8 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11658)). HSBC's September 2025 "world-first" bond-trading result on Heron (a relative gain of up to 34% in a backtest) appeared with hardware noise and vanished in noiseless simulation; the paper calls the method "a complementary explorative tool", and Scott Aaronson called it "yet another 'qombie'" ([arXiv 2509.17715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17715); [Aaronson, 25 Sep 2025](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=9170)).

**Limits.** IBM's own numbers show the gaps its 2029 plan must close. Chip-to-chip l-couplers run at **0.7–1% infidelity against a 0.1% target**, and Starling needs more than 100 of them. The gross-code simulations assume 0.1% physical error, while about two-thirds of Heron r3's couplings were at or above that level in November 2025 (my arithmetic from IBM's 57-of-176 figure). Error detection pays heavily in postselection: the July 2026 experiment's sampling rate fell 860-fold against unencoded runs ([PostQuantum, 1 Aug 2026](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/ibm-three-quantum-advantage-papers/)). ORNL's figures put IBM about 230 times short of DOE's late-2028 hard-operation target. Cosmic-ray bursts, documented on Google hardware, break the independent-error assumption behind error correction on superconducting chips generally ([McEwen et al., Nature Physics 18, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05219)).

**Assessment.** IBM's hardware stands on solid ground as a reproducible experimental platform, and outside groups now run below-threshold error correction on it; its qLDPC theory is peer-reviewed. Its beyond-classical claims do not: three rounds (2023, 2025–26, July 2026) drew classical matches within days to weeks, and the July 2026 claims await adjudication. The 2029 fault-tolerance plan rests on hardware IBM has not yet shown working together (long c-couplers, more than 100 l-couplers at 99.9%, a qLDPC memory below threshold, closed-loop real-time decoding), and Kookaburra, the 2026 module meant to show it, has not been reported.

### Google Quantum AI: peer-reviewed error correction, no product, DARPA Stage A

**Products and stage.** Google Quantum AI is a research division inside Google (its scientists publish as "Google Research, Google Quantum AI team"), founded in 2012 by Hartmut Neven and based in Goleta/Santa Barbara with its own fabrication facility ([Google Research, 13 Jan 2026](https://research.google/blog/dynamic-surface-codes-open-new-avenues-for-quantum-error-correction/); [Google, 9 Dec 2024](https://blog.google/technology/research/google-willow-quantum-chip/)). Its flagship remains **Willow, a 105-qubit superconducting chip announced 9 December 2024**; no successor had been announced by 14 November 2026. The spec sheet reports two different chips: the error-correction chip has a mean CZ error of 0.33% ± 0.18%, measurement error of 0.77%, T1 of 68 ± 13 µs and 909,000 error-correction cycles per second (1.1 µs per cycle); the random-circuit chip has an iSWAP-like error of 0.14% ± 0.052% and T1 of 98 ± 32 µs ([Willow spec sheet](https://quantumai.google/static/site-assets/downloads/willow-spec-sheet.pdf)). On 24 March 2026 Google added a neutral-atom hardware programme in Boulder, Colorado, led by JILA's Adam Kaufman. It said neutral atoms are "easier to scale in qubit count" (arrays of about 10,000 atoms, millisecond cycles) and superconducting processors "easier to scale in circuit depth", and set its next superconducting goal at "tens of thousands of qubits" ([Google, 24 Mar 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/neutral-atom-quantum-computers/)). In October 2025 it absorbed Atlantic Quantum, an MIT spin-out that puts control electronics in the cold stage; Google said the company "joins", while the seller's counsel called it an "acquisition" ([Gunderson Dettmer, 2 Oct 2025](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/client-news/gunderson-dettmer-represented-atlantic-quantum-in-its-acquisition-by-google); [TQI, 3 Oct 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/10/03/atlantic-quantum-joins-google-quantum-ai/)). **Google sells no quantum hardware or compute.** The only outside access found is a grant-funded programme with the UK's National Quantum Computing Centre, whose first award went to King's College London on 28 May 2026 ([NQCC, 12 Dec 2025](https://www.nqcc.ac.uk/updates/nqcc-and-google-quantum-ai-launch-new-initiative-to-accelerate-quantum-science-in-the-uk/); [TQI, 28 May 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/05/28/kings-college-london-awarded-access-to-googles-willow-processor-through-nqcc-initiative/)). **Roadmap and record.** Google publishes a six-milestone roadmap but has mostly avoided dated interim targets. Willow's best logical error, 1.43×10⁻³ per cycle at distance 7, is about **1,430 times above that target**; at the measured Λ of 2.14, closing the gap would need a distance of about 27, roughly 1,457 physical qubits per logical qubit (my arithmetic, assuming constant Λ). Executive timelines converge on about 2030: Neven said real-world applications "within five years" ([TQI, 5 Feb 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/02/05/google-quantum-ai-head-sees-commercial-quantum-within-five-years/)); hardware director Julian Kelly said "about five years out" ([TQI, 26 Mar 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/03/26/google-executive-says-quantum-applications-could-arrive-in-five-years/)); Sundar Pichai put "useful" machines 5 to 10 years away ([Bloomberg, 12 Feb 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-02-12/google-ceo-sees-useful-quantum-computers-5-to-10-years-away)); and Neven wrote that "commercially relevant" superconducting machines would arrive "by the end of this decade" ([Google, 24 Mar 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/neutral-atom-quantum-computers/)). **Science and verification.** Google's error-correction record is peer-reviewed and quantified. Willow's below-threshold surface-code memory (Nature 638, 2025) cut logical error by **Λ = 2.14 ± 0.02 per distance step**, reached 0.143% per cycle at distance 7, outlived its best physical qubit 2.4-fold, and decoded in real time at distance 5 with 63 µs mean latency for up to a million cycles; repetition codes to distance 29 hit a floor of rare correlated errors about once an hour ([arXiv 2408.13687](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13687)). The central effect has been reproduced on independent hardware: China's USTC reported Λ = 1.40 at distance 7 on Zuchongzhi 3.2 (PRL, 22 Dec 2025) ([QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/ustcs-zuchongzhi-3-2-achieves-below-threshold-qec-milestone/)). Scott Aaronson called Willow "a very important threshold that's now been crossed" while stressing that it stored one logical qubit and ran no logical operations ([Shtetl-Optimized, 10 Dec 2024](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=8525)). Follow-ups are smaller steps: color codes with Λ = 1.56 and lattice-surgery teleportation at 86.5–90.7% fidelity ([Lacroix et al., Nature 645, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14256)); dynamic surface codes (Nature Physics, January 2026); and **magic-state cultivation on hardware** (Science, 15 October 2026), which reached 99.99% logical T-state fidelity with about 40-fold error reduction but kept only about 8% of attempts, at distance 3 grafted into distance 5, with fidelity bounded by a "self-referential" protocol and no outside commentary found ([Google, 15 Oct 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/quantum-computing/magic-state-cultivation-quantum-computing/); [QCR, 15 Oct 2026](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/google-quantum-ai-demonstrates-magic-state-cultivation-on-willow-processor-in-science-milestone/)). Google's beyond-classical record is mixed. The 2019 Sycamore claim (200 seconds versus 10,000 years) was answered by IBM's 2.5-day estimate ([IBM, 22 Oct 2019](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/on-quantum-supremacy)) and then by an executed simulation taking about 15 hours on 512 GPUs ([Pan, Chen, Zhang, PRL 129, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03011)). Willow's "5 minutes vs 10^25 years" sampling claim stands unrefuted, but Aaronson notes that any check is "indirect, based on extrapolations from smaller circuits" ([Shtetl-Optimized](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=8525)). Quantum Echoes (Nature 646, October 2025) claimed a "verifiable" 13,000-fold speedup using 65 qubits. Nature's three referees were "split" over how fully advantage was shown ([Scientific American, 23 Oct 2025](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/google-measures-quantum-echoes-on-willow-quantum-computer-chip/)); Aaronson called it "a decent candidate" and MIT's Aram Harrow "pretty convincing" ([Science News, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/quantum-echoes-google-computer)); and Google's own Thomas O'Brien said the molecular results "aren't beyond classical yet" ([IEEE Spectrum, 22 Oct 2025](https://spectrum.ieee.org/quantum-echoes)). Google defines "verifiable" as repeatable on "any other [quantum computer] of the same caliber" ([Google, 22 Oct 2025](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/quantum-echoes-willow-verifiable-quantum-advantage/)); no non-Google machine has done so, and the main published argument that tensor networks cannot simulate the experiment comes from Google-affiliated authors ([Bermejo et al., arXiv 2604.15427, 16 Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15427)).

**Limits.** A million physical qubits is about 9,500 times Willow. Willow's 63 µs decoder latency is about six times the 10 µs reaction time assumed in Google's own RSA estimate, and its error-correction chip's 0.33% CZ error is about 3.3 times the 0.1% that estimate assumes (my arithmetic). Cosmic-ray and radioactivity bursts "simultaneously and severely limit" the coherence of all qubits on a chip ([McEwen et al., arXiv 2104.05219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.05219)), and in the standard surface-code circuit time-correlated errors persist for up to 40 cycles ([Google Research, 13 Jan 2026](https://research.google/blog/dynamic-surface-codes-open-new-avenues-for-quantum-error-correction/)). At 8% retention, cultivation needs about 12.5 attempts per magic state, and its ~10⁻⁴ error sits about 100 times above the 10⁻⁶ level Google sets for Milestone 3.

**Assessment.** Google's error-correction science stands on solid ground: it is peer-reviewed with quantified uncertainties, an independent group has reproduced the central effect on separate hardware, and outside experts accept it as genuine. Its scope is narrow: distances 3–7 on one 105-qubit chip, memory and single-logical-qubit operations, and no below-threshold logical two-qubit gate. The beyond-classical claims are weaker: one overtaken, one unverifiable by construction, one contested and unreplicated. As a business, Google Quantum AI has no product, no disclosed revenue and the earliest DARPA stage.

### Quantinuum: 99.92% gates at 98 qubits, $31–34 million of revenue, a slow clock

**Ownership and capital.** Quantinuum was formed on 29 November 2021 by combining Honeywell Quantum Solutions with Cambridge Quantum ([424B4 prospectus, 5 Jun 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm)). **Science and verification.** Quantinuum documents its physical-layer numbers in detail. The Helios paper reports average errors of 2.5×10⁻⁵ (single-qubit), **7.9×10⁻⁴ (two-qubit, randomized benchmarking)** and 4.8×10⁻⁴ (state preparation and measurement), but random circuits across all 98 qubits show an effective two-qubit error of 2.00×10⁻³, about 2.5 times the isolated-gate figure ([arXiv 2511.05465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05465)). Outsiders have used the hardware: the Jülich benchmark certified H2-1 at 56 fully connected qubits ([arXiv 2502.06471](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06471v2)); Sandia found that error-detected one-bit adders on Helios-1 failed at an estimated (6.0 ± 5.9)×10⁻⁵ after filtering burst errors against (3.5 ± 1.3)×10⁻³ unencoded, while four- and five-bit adders ran "just above threshold" ([arXiv 2610.14202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14202)); and a second Jülich study of error-correction operations was reported as finding that "Quantinuum processors stand out" ([TQI, 8 Oct 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/?p=2395758)). Peer-reviewed results with outside co-authors include non-Abelian topological order on a 27-qubit lattice ([Iqbal et al., Nature 626, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03766)); certified randomness with JPMorgan, Argonne, ORNL and UT Austin, which produced 71,313 certified bits secure only "under a restricted class of realistic near-term adversaries" and needed 1.1×10¹⁸ FLOPS of supercomputer verification ([Liu et al., Nature 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20498)), and which JPMorgan itself describes only as "a potential useful cryptographic primitive" ([JPMorganChase, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/about/technology/blog/certified-randomness)); a logical magic state made by code switching, with 82.58% acceptance and infidelity of at most 5.1×10⁻⁴ ([Daguerre et al., PRX 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14169)); and Microsoft–Quantinuum logical qubits with error rates 11 to 800 times better than physical baselines, using codes "combined with a scalable method of error detection and post-selection" ([Nature 654, 10 Jun 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10628-y)). The 2026 error-correction headlines are company-only preprints. The **"48 logical qubits at 2:1"** claim rests on error-detecting "iceberg" codes and a distance-4 concatenated code ([arXiv 2602.22211, 25 Feb 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22211); [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-research/quantinuum-94-logical-qubits/)). A [[20,2,6]] "Helix" code showed 4.6×10⁻⁵ error per logical qubit per cycle "without relying on postselection" ([arXiv 2609.03194, 2 Sep 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03194)). Color codes at distances 3, 5 and 7 reached **about 5×10⁻⁶ error per round with Λ = 12** over 20 rounds, and 7×10⁻⁵ per logical two-qubit Clifford at distance 5, but the memory data were decoded offline and all 23 authors work for Quantinuum ([arXiv 2610.24077, 16 Oct 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24077)). The accompanying release claimed error "100 times lower than the best published result across other hardware modalities", benchmarked against Google's 2024 paper, and its one outside quote came from Sandia's Robin Blume-Kohout, a co-author of Quantinuum's 2025 magic-state paper ([Quantinuum, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.quantinuum.com/press-releases/quantinuum-breaks-error-correction-world-record-by-10x-validating-its-leading-path-to-fault-tolerant-quantum-computing-by-2029)). One beyond-classical claim on Quantinuum hardware has already fallen: a "peaked circuit" run on H2 was fully contracted in about an hour on a single GPU, about half the hardware runtime ([Kremer & Dupuis, arXiv 2604.21908, 23 Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21908)).

**Limits.** Speed, not fidelity, binds. On Helios a two-qubit gate takes about 70 µs, ground-state cooling about 3 ms, and a full 98-qubit circuit layer about **55 ms**, dominated by ion transport ([arXiv 2511.05465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05465)); a million layers would take about 15 hours (my arithmetic). Analyst Olivier Ezratty estimates gates about 100,000 times slower than superconducting gates, which "will become a handicap for FTQC" ([Ezratty, 12 Nov 2025](https://www.oezratty.net/wordpress/?p=24056)). In one published cost model, trapped ions at 1 ms cycles need 2.7–3.1 million qubits for a one-month RSA-2048 run against about 94,000–98,000 superconducting qubits at 1 µs ([PostQuantum, updated 29 Oct 2026](https://postquantum.com/post-quantum/pinnacle-architecture-break-rsa-2048-critical/)). Sol is reported to carry about 192 physical qubits ([Ezratty](https://www.oezratty.net/wordpress/?p=24056)); 100 logical qubits at the distance-5 color code's 37 physical qubits each would need about 3,700, so Sol must rely on high-rate codes such as Helix (about 10 per logical qubit) or error detection, and the definition of "logical qubit" behind the target is unstated (my arithmetic). Apollo additionally needs two-dimensional junction transport, integrated photonics, cryogenic scale-up (still "validating that our cryogenic system can be deployed at the required scale"), real-time decoding of non-Clifford circuits and onshored fabrication ([Q3 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026071000/q32026earningspressrelease.htm)).

**Assessment.** Quantinuum's physical fidelities and quantum-volume record stand on solid ground: standard methods, a consistent series from H1 to Helios, public QV data and independent users at Jülich and Sandia, with the caveat that component averages overstate full-system performance. Its peer-reviewed physics with outside co-authors is solid within stated limits (postselection, restricted adversaries). The 2026 error-correction records are strong but not yet established, since they are company-only preprints with offline decoding and memory-level metrics. Sol and Apollo are unproven targets, and clock speed is the constraint no published result yet addresses.

### IonQ: acquisitions drive revenue; its record physics remains laboratory-scale

**Products and stage.** The IonQ computers anyone can rent on public clouds are still the 25-qubit Aria and the 36-qubit Forte and Forte Enterprise, which use ytterbium ions and laser gates ([Microsoft Learn, updated 29 Aug 2026](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/quantum/provider-ionq); [Amazon Braket, accessed 14 Nov 2026](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/braket/latest/developerguide/braket-devices.html)). The only peer-reviewed full-device benchmark, by IonQ authors on Forte, found a **median two-qubit error of 46.4×10⁻⁴ (99.54%) across 435 pairs, a best pair of 99.72% and a worst of about 91.2%**, two-qubit gates of 770–1,103 µs, and circuits failing after about 200 two-qubit gates without error mitigation ([Chen et al., Quantum 8, 1516, 7 Nov 2024](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2024-11-07-1516/pdf/)). The fifth-generation, barium-based Tempo is sold for on-premises use. IonQ announced an algorithmic-qubit score of #AQ 64 on it on 25 September 2025, "three months ahead of schedule", without giving the qubit count, mitigation settings or fidelities, and its product page still lists **"target qubits: 100" and "target fidelity: 99.9%"** ([IonQ, 25 Sep 2025](https://ionq.com/news/ionq-achieves-record-breaking-quantum-performance-milestone-of-aq-64); [IonQ Tempo page, accessed 14 Nov 2026](https://ionq.com/quantum-systems/tempo)). **Science and verification.** IonQ's present-generation performance is its best-supported claim: the Forte paper agrees with the independent Jülich benchmark (Forte Enterprise certified to 28 qubits; Aria 2 indistinguishable from random at 23) ([arXiv 2502.06471](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06471v2)) and with Quantinuum's competitor measurement of an algorithmic-qubit score of 9 for Forte without error mitigation and 99.35% two-qubit fidelity ([Quantinuum, 1 Mar 2024](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/debunking-algorithmic-qubits)). Oxford Ionics' 99.97% all-electronic two-qubit gate in a trap of up to 10 qubits is peer-reviewed ([Löschnauer et al., PRX Quantum 6, 040313, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07694)). The **"world record" 99.99% gate** is a preprint on two calcium-40 ions forming one pair in a cryogenic trap, with state preparation and measurement errors "assumed negligible". Its 8.4×10⁻⁵ headline comes from fits truncated at 200 Cliffords, while **the full 500-Clifford dataset gives 1.16×10⁻⁴ (about 99.988%)**, and multi-pair operation is "not demonstrated" ([Hughes et al., arXiv 2510.17286, 20 Oct 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17286v1)). IonQ's error-correction "breakeven" preprint used a stationary chain of 40 barium-133 ions: its best code's logical lifetime was 3.95 ± 0.68 s against a physical 3.3 ± 0.9 s, the largest code discarded 74.8% of shots for leakage, and larger codes did worse ([arXiv 2606.06455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06455); [PostQuantum, 5 Jun 2026](https://postquantum.com/quantum-research/ionq-qldpc-breakeven-trapped-ion/)). Critic Amit Hagar notes that the physical baseline omitted dynamical decoupling, that the margin is about half an error bar and that distance 3 beat distance 5 ([Hagar, 8 Oct 2026](https://amithagar.substack.com/p/breakeven-is-not-a-milestone)). The "Walking Cat" fault-tolerant architecture is theory (110 logical qubits on 2,514 physical qubits at about a million T gates a day) ([arXiv 2604.19481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19481)), and the April 2026 photonic link is "a directional signal rather than a quantitative proof point" ([PostQuantum, 20 Apr 2026](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/ionq-photonic-interconnect-networked-quantum/)). Continuity across ion species is unproven: commercial machines use ytterbium, the breakeven work barium, the 99.99% gate calcium, and Superion's species is undisclosed.

**Limits.** Commercial two-qubit gates take 0.6–1.1 ms, and a 25-qubit, depth-100 benchmark job took about 18,000 s on Aria 2 against 0.5 s on IBM's ibm_fez ([arXiv 2502.06471](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06471v2)). Long ion chains crowd their motional modes (about 10 kHz spacing in Forte's 36-ion chain), and in a published shuttling-based demonstration runtime split into about 2% gates, 27% transport and 68% cooling ([Chen et al.](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2024-11-07-1516/pdf/); [IonQ blog citing Pino et al. 2021](https://www.ionq.com/blog/accelerating-towards-fault-tolerance-unlocking-99-99-two-qubit-gate)). Academic ion-photon links reach 182–250 entangled pairs per second at about 94–96% fidelity ([O'Reilly et al., PRL 133, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16167)), against IonQ's untested proposal of more than 1,000. The roadmap's jumps are steep: 256 to 10,000 physical qubits in one year (about 39-fold) and logical error from about 10⁻² to below 10⁻⁷ (five orders of magnitude) (my arithmetic). A commentator noted that de Masi's statement that IonQ expects the logical-qubit count "required to challenge RSA 2048" in 2028–2029 conflicts with IonQ's own 2030 full-fault-tolerance timeline ([PostQuantum, 7 May 2026](https://postquantum.com/security-pqc/ionq-q-day-claim-rsa-2048-earnings/)).

**Assessment.** IonQ's current-generation component performance stands on solid ground, being peer-reviewed and consistent with independent measurement. Its laboratory gate physics is moderately supported (one peer-reviewed 99.97% result, one single-pair 99.99% preprint). Its system-scale claims (Tempo at specification, the #AQ 64 details, photonic links, logical qubits, Superion performance, the 2027–2030 roadmap) rest on releases and company preprints and have not been checked. A growing share of IonQ's reported revenue is acquired, and part of it (CMOS foundry work, radar satellites) is not quantum computing. DARPA's Stage C, running through 2029, is the first scheduled independent test of the roadmap.

**Legal and regulatory.** No litigation involving QuEra was found; cases involving the similarly named Quantum Computing Inc. are a different company ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/?q=%22QuEra+Computing%22&type=r)). No export licence, CFIUS review or foreign-investment record was found.

**Science and verification.** QuEra's headline science comes from Harvard-led Nature papers whose senior authors are QuEra's co-founders and Chief Scientists, so it is partner-affiliated evidence rather than independent validation. The December 2023 "48 logical qubits" ran in [[8,3,2]] error-**detecting** codes with postselection ([Bluvstein et al., arXiv 2312.03982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03982)); an outside physicist noted that acceptance fell "as low as 0.04%" at the largest sizes and will "shrink exponentially for larger circuits" ([Leykam, 20 Dec 2023](https://dleykam.blogspot.com/2023/12/towards-fault-tolerant-quantum.html)), yet QuEra's FAQ calls it an "error-corrected quantum computer" ([QuEra FAQ](https://www.quera.com/faq)). Logical magic-state distillation ran on Gemini with distance-3 and distance-5 color codes (Nature 645, July 2025) ([TQI, 15 Jul 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/07/15/quera-harvard-and-mit-researchers-demonstrate-logical-level-magic-state-distillation-on-a-neutral-atom-quantum-computer/)). More than 3,000 atoms were held for over two hours with reloading at 300,000 atoms per second (Nature 646, 2025), although a referee contested the word "continuous" ([MIT CUA](https://cua.mit.edu/publications/continuous-operation-of-a-coherent-3000-qubit-system)). The 448-atom architecture paper (Nature 649, online 10 November 2025) showed surface-code error **2.14 times lower at distance 5 than at distance 3 over a four-round circuit**, 0.62% per round at distance 5, without postselection but decoded offline ([arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1)). "Algorithmic fault tolerance", claiming 10–100-fold runtime cuts, is simulation (Nature, September 2025). The 2026 outputs are preprints: a raw CZ fidelity of **99.854%** (99.941% after discarding atom-loss events, with loss about 60% of the error) ([arXiv 2604.25987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25987)); roughly 2:1 high-rate codes "verified only for memory, not computation" in simulation ([arXiv 2604.16209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16209); [review, arXiv 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1)); a Shor estimate of about 10,000–26,000 atoms, with P-256 broken "in a few days" at 26,000 ([Cain et al., arXiv 2603.28627, 30 Mar 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28627)); and circuits found with an Anthropic AI model, which are "resource estimates ... rather than runs on hardware" ([TQI, 10 Nov 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/11/10/quera-harvard-anthropic-ai-quantum-circuit-resource-requirements/)). Independent groups have mirrored the platform's capabilities but not QuEra's specific logical experiments: Caltech trapped 6,100 atoms for about 13 seconds (Sep 2025), Tsinghua about 11,022 (June 2026 preprint), USTC reported a 99.84% CZ gate (2025) ([review, arXiv 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1)), and Atom Computing ran a toric code for up to 90 cycles with mid-circuit atom replacement, noting that in neutral atoms "to date there has been no demonstration of repeated error correction scalable to arbitrary depth" ([arXiv 2606.04079, 2 Jun 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04079)).

**Limits.** Atom loss accounts for about half of the errors in each error-correction round in the 448-atom work; circuit depth there was limited by "a depleting atomic reservoir" and by 27 layers of waveform memory; and the authors say a further 3–5-fold reduction in physical error is still needed ([arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1)). Gates are separated by about 400 µs in repeated stabilizer rounds, and an independent review finds two-qubit fidelity above 99.9% "is not achieved", no measured error-correction cycle time, typical experimental cycles of about 100 ms and room-temperature trap lifetimes of about 10–60 s ([arXiv 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1)). At 1 ms cycles one cost model puts a one-month RSA-2048 run at 9.5–13 million atoms ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/post-quantum/pinnacle-architecture-break-rsa-2048-critical/)), against about 10,000 atoms over weeks to months in Cain et al.'s atom-specific design. Laser power and crosstalk for 10⁴–10⁵ atoms are not quantified in any source found.

**Assessment.** QuEra's peer-reviewed physics stands on solid ground within its stated scope, which is narrower than the headlines (error detection, four rounds, offline decoding), but it comes from a partner group, not independent validators. Platform-level capabilities (thousands of atoms, reloading, 99.5–99.85% gates, error suppression with distance) are now corroborated by independent groups. QuEra's delivered products have no published performance data, its 2026 hardware target slipped about two years, and Libra's 10⁻⁶ error at 256 logical qubits depends on real-time decoding, active reloading and high-rate codes working together at scale, which no one has shown.

**Science and verification.** PsiQuantum's architecture, fusion-based quantum computing (FBQC), is peer-reviewed but company-authored: it tolerates **10.4% photon loss per fusion, equivalent to 2.7% per photon**, in its original linear-optical scheme ([Bartolucci et al., Nature Communications 14, 912, 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-36493-1)), and newer adaptive schemes raise loss thresholds at higher resource cost ([arXiv 2506.11975, Jun 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11975)). The Omega paper itself sets a target of "about 10% total accumulated optical loss". No outside group has replicated Omega or published a rebuttal. DARPA's evaluation since 2023, with "over 50 experts'" worth of effort reviewing designs, is the main outside technical check, but its findings are unpublished ([DARPA, 6 Feb 2025](https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/quantum-computing-approaches)), as are those of Australia's technical advisory group. Outside comment ranges from USTC's Chao-Yang Lu, who in 2024 saw "no technologically feasible pathway" ([Physics World, 7 May 2024](https://physicsworld.com/a/australia-raises-eyebrows-by-splashing-a1bn-into-us-quantum-computing-start-up-psiquantum/)), to Scott Aaronson in 2026: "It is very hard for an outsider to evaluate" ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/14/1140356/psiquantum-plan-massive-quantum-computer-out-of-light/)).

**Limits.** Photon loss is the binding constraint. In decibels (my conversions), 2.7% loss is 0.12 dB and 10% is 0.46 dB, while Omega's chip-to-fibre coupling (52–127 mdB) and barium titanate switch insertion (about 100 mdB) each consume 1–3% of a photon's budget; one coupler plus one switch already exceeds the original per-photon allowance before sources, filters and detectors. The demonstration's 26% heralding efficiency equals about 5.9 dB of loss ([Nature 641, full text](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)). Photon sources are probabilistic (about 5% pair probability) and fusions fail 25–50% of the time, so large multiplexing and fast low-loss switch networks are needed; the paper lists these as not yet built. Superconducting detectors need about 2 K today (the Linde plant targets about 4 K), and helium systems are among the largest capital costs ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/14/1140356/psiquantum-plan-massive-quantum-computer-out-of-light/)). Peng says Moreton Bay will use "an order of magnitude" less energy than a large AI data centre, but its footprint "has not been established"; a June 2026 ORNL study put fault-tolerant machines at "single- to double-digits" of megawatts ([POLITICO, 11 Nov 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/psiquantum-chief-insists-nothing-changed-180000977.html)).

**Assessment.** PsiQuantum's component claims stand on solid ground: peer-reviewed, open-access, with error bars, from a commercial 300 mm process. Its central claim, that photonics can reach fault tolerance, rests on peer-reviewed theory but is experimentally unproven: there is no logical qubit, no below-threshold fusion network and no loss-inclusive system benchmark, and the published loss figures sit far outside the original architecture's budget, so viability rests on newer, costlier loss-tolerant schemes and further component gains. Its public timelines have been reframed repeatedly (2025 to 2027 to 2030). DARPA's Stage C status shows it passed design reviews, not that the machine works.

**Science and verification.** IQM's peer-reviewed results are device-level: the "unimon" qubit with 99.8–99.9% single-qubit gates on three qubits (Nature Communications, 2022) and a 99.81% CZ gate across a long-distance coupler (PRX Quantum, 2023) ([arXiv 2208.09460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.09460)). The independent evidence is relative, not absolute: Jülich's October 2026 study scored Emerald 0.672 against Garnet's 0.564 on a depth-3 optimisation benchmark, found IQM "still degrades faster with circuit depth than ... the IBM QPUs", and found its feed-forward control "restricted by the conditional-control architecture", so one-dimensional chains with more than two data qubits "cannot be implemented" and no error-correction memory test could be run ([arXiv 2610.05928](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05928v1)). A TU Munich compilation study found the star architecture's resonator moves add "non-negligible overhead" ([TUM, DATE 2026](https://www.cda.cit.tum.de/files/eda/2026_date_compilation_for_superconducting_bus_resonator_architectures.pdf)). Hosts publish no fidelity figures.

**Limits.** The barbell simulations assume physical noise of 10⁻⁴, about 50 times below IQM's published median two-qubit error of about 5×10⁻³; the Halocene target of 99.7% still implies about 3×10⁻³ (my arithmetic). Production coherence of about 40 µs sits far below the 1 ms shown on a single test device. VTT's 300-qubit machine scales through classical, not quantum, links between processors. Defects matter at this scale: on a 120-qubit square-lattice array, excluding defective components cut distance-5 logical error per round from 4.49% to 1.62% ([Drouet et al., arXiv 2607.12118, 13 Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12118)).

**Assessment.** IQM's device physics stands on solid peer-reviewed ground, and its systems demonstrably exist at public-sector sites. Every quantitative system figure, however, is company-measured, outside evidence is relative or architectural, and no error-correction experiment beyond detection has been shown. Its dated hardware targets slipped by 9–22 months, its 2026 revenue guidance hinges on fourth-quarter acceptance of its first 150-qubit system, and its error-correction roadmap assumes noise far below what its chips now deliver.

## The physics holds; speed, loss and scale set the limits

All four hardware approaches rest on established physics, and none of the sources reviewed claims a fundamental physical barrier. The open questions are engineering and scale. Jens Eisert and John Preskill describe four transitions the field has still to make: from error mitigation to active error detection and correction, from rudimentary correction to scalable fault tolerance, from heuristic to verifiable algorithms, and from exploratory simulators to credible simulation advantage ([Eisert & Preskill, arXiv 2510.19928, v3 21 May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19928)). The approaches' binding constraints differ in kind, so comparing them by qubit count alone misleads.

Error suppression with growing code size has now been shown in three of the four approaches, but the reported Λ values are not like-for-like: Google and USTC quote Λ per distance step of two on symmetric surface codes, the IBM third-party figure covers distance 3 to 6 in one basis, and Quantinuum's Λ = 12 is for color codes on a platform whose rounds take milliseconds rather than a microsecond. **No source found shows, on any platform, a complete algorithm that combines real-time decoding, non-Clifford logical gates fed by a magic-state factory, and many rounds at logical error of 10⁻⁶ or below.** Also undemonstrated: large qLDPC codes with real-time decoding (the largest qLDPC code demonstrated is reported as [[30,8,3]]), month-scale fault-tolerant operation, and any photonic logical qubit ([PostQuantum, updated 29 Oct 2026](https://postquantum.com/post-quantum/pinnacle-architecture-break-rsa-2048-critical/)). Many headline counts of dozens of logical qubits, in both trapped ions and neutral atoms, use distance-2 to distance-4 codes with error detection and postselection, which do not scale to deep circuits.

### Resource estimates shrink on paper, not in hardware

Published estimates for breaking RSA-2048 fell from 20 million qubits running about eight hours (2019) to **fewer than a million noisy qubits running under a week** ([Gidney, arXiv 2505.15917, 21 May 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15917)), then to fewer than 100,000 with qLDPC codes in Iceberg Quantum's "Pinnacle" design ([arXiv 2602.11457, Feb 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11457)), which is simulation-only, not peer-reviewed, needs beyond-nearest-neighbour connectivity and leaves decoder latency unresolved. Atom-specific designs need about 10,000–26,000 reconfigurable atoms ([Cain et al., arXiv 2603.28627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28627)), and Google estimates fewer than 500,000 superconducting qubits to break 256-bit elliptic-curve keys in minutes ([arXiv 2603.28846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28846)). Every one of these assumes about 10⁻³ physical error across 10⁴–10⁶ qubits, plus real-time decoding and reaction times near 10 µs, and none of those conditions has been met at scale. Gidney noted that qLDPC reaction-time assumptions are "harder to meet than for surface codes", and Aaronson separated what is shown "in simulation" from what "remains to be proven in hardware" ([TQI, 31 Mar 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/03/31/q-day-just-got-closer-three-papers-in-three-months-are-rewriting-the-quantum-threat-timeline/)). Clock speed drives large differences between approaches. In Pinnacle's cost model for a one-month RSA-2048 run (May 2026 version), superconducting qubits at 1 µs and 0.1% error need about 94,000 qubits, trapped ions at 1 ms and 0.01% about **2.7 million**, and neutral atoms at 1 ms and 0.1% about **9.5 million** ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/post-quantum/pinnacle-architecture-break-rsa-2048-critical/)). The US Government Accountability Office reported on 7 October 2026 that most experts expect a cryptographically relevant machine "possibly as soon as the 2030s" and that none of the 24 major federal agencies is fully prepared for post-quantum cryptography ([ExecutiveGov on GAO-27-108740](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/gao-federal-agencies-quantum-cryptography-readiness)). For chemistry, the benchmark FeMoco problem was estimated in 2021 at about 4 million physical qubits and under four days at 1 µs cycles and 0.1% error ([Lee et al., PRX Quantum 2, 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.03494)).

### Supply chains: helium-3, refrigerators and lasers

Superconducting machines need dilution refrigerators at millikelvin temperatures, and photonic machines need 2–4 K plants for their detectors. Global helium-3 output is roughly **22,000–30,000 litres a year**, priced at about $2,500–3,000 per litre, and each dilution refrigerator needs 10–100 litres ([MP-IDSA, 21 May 2026](https://idsa.in/publisher/issuebrief/a-helium-shock-to-indias-quantum-technology-ambitions)). Even if all of it went to refrigerators, that would supply only about 220–3,000 new units a year (my arithmetic), while Bluefors alone ships about one system a day from a market "dominated by three suppliers" with 6–9-month lead times ([War on the Rocks, 20 Oct 2025](https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-supply-chain-chokepoints-in-quantum/)). Bluefors has contracted up to 10,000 litres a year from Interlune for a decade from 2028 ([MP-IDSA](https://idsa.in/publisher/issuebrief/a-helium-shock-to-indias-quantum-technology-ambitions)), and Interlune says it can extract pure helium-3 from US helium plants and holds nearly $500 million of binding purchase agreements ([TQI, 20 Jul 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/20/interlune-produces-pure-helium-3-from-domestic-helium-using-novel-cryogenic-technology/)). Supplier economics are thin: Oxford Instruments sold its quantum cryogenics business, which earned about £1 million of adjusted operating profit on about £59 million of revenue in FY25, for £60 million ([Oxford Instruments RNS, 10 Jun 2025](https://www.ticker.app/lse/OXIG/rns/2025-06-10/sale-of-oxford-instruments-quantum-business/1656M)). Helium-4 supply is also exposed: Qatar provides about a third of world helium, and Iranian drone strikes shut a Qatari LNG and helium facility in March 2026 ([MP-IDSA](https://idsa.in/publisher/issuebrief/a-helium-shock-to-indias-quantum-technology-ambitions)). Ion and atom systems depend on lasers and optics whose rare-earth inputs China dominates (about 90% of processing) and on lithium niobate, of which China makes 60–70% of high-quality boules ([War on the Rocks](https://warontherocks.com/cogs-of-war/the-supply-chain-chokepoints-in-quantum/)). Power estimates for fault-tolerant machines are sparse: a 2023 extrapolation put a superconducting code-breaking machine at about 125 MW, using older, larger qubit counts ([Parker & Vermeer, arXiv 2304.14344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14344)).

### Advantage claims keep meeting classical rebuttals within months

Independent researchers broadly accept that random-circuit-sampling results after 2019 (Google, USTC and Quantinuum) have not been reproduced classically, but these produce no useful output and rest on "a proxy of a proxy of the quantum fidelity"; fewer than half of an expert audience polled by Dominik Hangleiter thought advantage had been achieved ([Quantum Frontiers, 6 Jan 2026](https://quantumfrontiers.com/2026/01/06/has-quantum-advantage-been-achieved/)). Amit Hagar counted more than 30 advantage-class announcements and found that, with one exception, every flagship claim from the noisy pre-fault-tolerant era was classically reproduced, shown to have tractable structure or closed by a simulability theorem within about 18 months ([Hagar, arXiv 2607.07530, Jul 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07530)). An outside example confirms the pattern: D-Wave's 2025 Science claim of dynamics "beyond the reach of classical computation" was reproduced for "hundreds of qubits" with "modest computational resources" in Science in 2026 ([Tindall et al., arXiv 2503.05693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05693)). A US National Quantum Initiative workshop report calls establishing advantage "inherently adversarial" ([arXiv 2508.13973](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13973)). **No independent evidence of commercially useful quantum advantage was found for any of the seven companies.** IQM's own filing says no quantum computer has reached broad advantage ([DEFM14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002052161/000119312526259964/d900375ddefm14a.htm)), and Quantinuum's says advantage claims "whether made by us or our competitors, are subject to scrutiny" ([S-1, 8 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002110105/000162828026032836/quantinuum-sx1.htm)).

**Customers rarely say what vendors say.** No customer in scope claims, in its own channel or co-authored paper, an advantage over the best classical methods. HSBC's release came closest with "world-first" and "empirical evidence", but its paper calls the work exploratory ([HSBC, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-demonstrates-worlds-first-known-quantum-enabled-algorithmic-trading-with-ibm)). AstraZeneca's only quoted words call its IonQ work "an important step towards" its goal ([IonQ, 9 Jun 2025](https://ionq.com/news/ionq-speeds-quantum-accelerated-drug-development-application-with)), and Cleveland Clinic's team "stopped short of claiming quantum advantage" on IBM hardware ([Barron's via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2633996871)). The speedup multiples vendors attach to customers (E.ON 15x, AstraZeneca 20x, Cleveland Clinic 33x) compare against earlier quantum or hybrid runs, not classical baselines. Several "customers" are also investors or ecosystem partners: JPMorgan owns Quantinuum shares; NVIDIA's venture arm invested in Quantinuum, PsiQuantum and QuEra, and its NVQLink integration names IonQ, IQM, Quantinuum and QuEra but not IBM, Google or PsiQuantum ([NVIDIA, 28 Oct 2025](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-nvqlink-quantum-gpu-computing)); AMD works with IBM on decoding and is investing in PsiQuantum; GlobalFoundries makes chips for PsiQuantum and traps for Quantinuum; and IonQ now owns SkyWater, a foundry that also serves D-Wave and has served PsiQuantum ([QCR, 14 Oct 2026](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/skywater-and-d-wave-extend-quantum-manufacturing-partnership-through-multi-year-agreement/)).

**Government records confirm only fragments of announced contracts.** USAspending confirms two AFRL awards to PsiQuantum ($22.46 million and $10.84 million), one to IonQ ($13.4 million), a $3.98 million Army award to Quantinuum (2025–27) and small DARPA awards to QuEra and IBM. No DARPA QBI company award appears under any company's name; the only QBI-labelled records are $8.70 million to the University of Maryland's ARLIS, DARPA's evaluator, and $1.0 million for an Illinois proving ground ([USAspending HR001126FE003](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_HR001126FE003_9700_HQ003424D0003_9700); [USAspending W911NF253A002](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_W911NF253A002_097)). Other Transaction agreements and contract ceilings explain much of the gap, so absence is not evidence that a deal is fictitious, but **most headline government values are vendor-stated ceilings that cannot be confirmed in public spending data**. Filed concentration figures, by contrast, check out: RIKEN's 60% share of Quantinuum's 2025 revenue and IQM's 99.5% public-sector share appear in audited or registered filings, and no customer source contradicts them.

**Rivals critique each other mostly through metrics.** IBM's 2019 rebuttal of Google's first supremacy claim remains the sharpest direct exchange ([IBM, 22 Oct 2019](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/on-quantum-supremacy)). Quantinuum's March 2024 "Debunking algorithmic qubits" measured IonQ's Forte at an algorithmic-qubit score of 9 without mitigation against IonQ's claimed 29–36 with it, and showed compilation shrinking benchmark circuits from 992 to 141 entangling gates ([Quantinuum](https://www.quantinuum.com/blog/debunking-algorithmic-qubits)). Quantinuum's S-1 contrasts its 2:1 overhead with superconducting overheads of "up to 100:1" citing a Google study. Google's March 2026 move into neutral atoms adopts the modality of its portfolio company QuEra, while Neven said demonstrating deep circuits with many cycles remains an "outstanding challenge" for atoms ([HPCwire, 3 Apr 2026, search summary](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/04/03/google-expands-quantum-efforts-to-include-neutral-atom-systems/)). Of the seven, only IonQ, which has made twelve acquisitions since December 2024, has faced published short-seller reports (2022, 2025, 2026); none was found on the two other listed companies, Quantinuum and IQM.

## Governments now fund, own and police the sector

On 21 May 2026 the Commerce Department signed nine CHIPS letters of intent worth **$2.013 billion**, each conditional on "a minority, non-controlling equity stake": IBM $1 billion for its Anderon foundry, GlobalFoundries $375 million, about $100 million each for Quantinuum, PsiQuantum, Atom Computing, D-Wave and Infleqtion, up to $100 million for Rigetti and up to $38 million for Diraq ([Commerce/NIST, 21 May 2026](https://www.nist.gov/document/quantum-chips-press-release)). IonQ, QuEra and IQM were not among them. The finalised awards differ in structure. Quantinuum issued parent-company stock (2,369,528 shares at $42.20) that Commerce keeps even if funds are clawed back; IBM's award went to its subsidiary, which issued Commerce a minority interest in Anderon rather than IBM; and PsiQuantum's equity terms are undisclosed ([Quantinuum Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026072454/qnt-20260930.htm); [IBM Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/51143/000005114326000095/ibm-20260930.htm); [PostQuantum, 9 Sep 2026](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/chips-act-quantum-equity-ota/)). Among the seven, IBM, Quantinuum and PsiQuantum hold both a CHIPS award and DARPA QBI status, QuEra holds DARPA Stage B status only, IonQ holds Stage C only, Google holds Stage A only, and IQM holds neither. The Department of Energy renewed its five quantum research centres for up to $625 million in November 2025 ([TQI, 4 Nov 2025](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/11/04/doe-announces-625-million-to-advance-national-quantum-information-science-research-centers/)). States add incentives: Illinois for PsiQuantum and IBM, Maryland $5 million plus $20 million toward a $50 million commitment for IonQ's headquarters ([TQI, 29 Apr 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/04/29/maryland-doubles-down-on-quantum-with-ionq-hq-funding-and-startup-infrastructure/)), and New Mexico $1.5 million for a Quantinuum photonics hub ([QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quantinuum-secures-1-5m-in-city-and-state-leda-funding-to-expand-rd-center-in-albuquerque/)).

**Export controls have not changed since 2024 in the US, but have spread abroad.** The core US rule remains the 6 September 2024 interim final rule creating ECCN 4A906, which controls quantum computers worldwide by qubit count and average CNOT error (34–99 qubits at ≤1×10⁻⁴, 100–199 at ≤1×10⁻³, 200–349 at ≤2×10⁻³, rising to ≤6×10⁻³ at 1,100–1,999 qubits, and all machines of 2,000 or more), with a licence exception for countries with equivalent controls ([Federal Register, 6 Sep 2024](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/09/06/2024-19633/commerce-control-list-additions-and-revisions-implementation-of-controls-on-advanced-technologies-consistent-with-controls)). A Federal Register search found no BIS document mentioning quantum after 16 September 2025, when the Chinese Academy of Sciences' National Time Service Center joined the Entity List; in March 2025 BIS had listed Chinese suppliers of dilution refrigerators ([govinfo, 28 Mar 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-03-28/html/2025-05426.htm)). The EU added quantum computers and cryogenic items to its dual-use list, in force 15 November 2025 ([European Commission, 8 Sep 2025](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/news/2025-update-eu-control-list-dual-use-items-2025-09-08_en)); Finland (2024), the UK (2024, revised December 2025) and Japan (September 2024) have national controls. China's June 2026 countermeasure lists named none of these companies ([Arnold & Porter, 2 Jul 2026](https://arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/07/china-imposes-export-control-and-government-procurement-restrictions-on-designated-us-companies)). No company has published a classification or disclosed a specific licence. On a plain reading of the bands against published averages, none of today's flagship averages falls inside the 4A906.a performance thresholds (Helios 7.9×10⁻⁴ at 98 qubits against ≤1×10⁻⁴; Willow's 3.3×10⁻³ at 105 against ≤1×10⁻³; Emerald's ~5×10⁻³ at 54 against ≤1×10⁻⁴), while a 520-qubit Nighthawk r3 would be captured at an average error of ≤4×10⁻³, and a 260-qubit atom array would be captured if the 2026 preprint's 99.85% held chip-wide. Components and technology are controlled separately, and the rule's "C-NOT error" may not equal vendors' native-gate benchmarks, so this is my reading, not a legal determination.

## Conclusion

The evidence points to a consistent asymmetry rather than a simple gap between hype and reality. Component physics is real, frequently peer-reviewed and increasingly reproduced across groups: error suppression with code size now appears in superconducting, trapped-ion and neutral-atom hardware, and two-qubit fidelities of 99.5–99.9% are now reported across all three. What no company has shown is the integration that turns components into a computer: real-time decoding in closed loop, logical error near 10⁻⁶ sustained inside a computation, a steady supply of magic states, and links between modules at target fidelity. The seven companies' schedules mirror that split, with component milestones on time or early and system, deployment and integration milestones months to two years late. The approaches also fail in different places (wiring, cryogenics and correlated errors for superconducting chips; clock speed for ions and atoms, which in one cost model multiplies the qubits needed for the same task 30 to 100 times; photon loss for photonics), so no single figure, whether qubits, fidelity or revenue, ranks them.

The next three years bring dated, externally checkable tests rather than further announcements: IBM's 10 December 2026 Quantum Day and Kookaburra's status; IQM's acceptance-dependent fourth-quarter 2026 revenue; Quantinuum's Sol integration in Q1 2027 and Singapore commissioning in spring 2027; IonQ's Superion deliveries and PsiQuantum's cryoplant in 2027; the NQCC's evaluation phase ending in Q2 2027; QuEra's Libra and IBM's 1,040-qubit system by 2027–2028; and DARPA's Stage C hardware verification of IBM, IonQ and PsiQuantum through 2029. Several of the bodies that will deliver those verdicts also have financial ties to the companies: Commerce holds equity in Quantinuum and Anderon and is to receive a stake in PsiQuantum under its award, public buyers supply nearly all of IQM's revenue, and DARPA both funds and tests. As of 14 November 2026, hosts such as RIKEN, ORNL, LRZ and AIST had not published acceptance data for the machines they run.

# Gated platforms lead automated security, at stretched prices

**Research date: 12 November 2026.** US share prices are 11 November closes; Tokyo, Seoul, Taipei, Shanghai, Shenzhen, European and Indian prices are 12 November closes. "Company claim" marks a statement only the company has made. Implied-growth figures are the research team's own arithmetic unless labelled consensus. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.

On quality alone, the strongest businesses in automated security are **CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks, joint first because the evidence does not separate them, followed by Fortinet**. CrowdStrike and Palo Alto are the only vendors confirmed inside all three gated frontier-model programmes, Anthropic's Glasswing (now its Cyber Verification Program), OpenAI's Daybreak and Google's Fairwind, and each owns a privileged enforcement point with large telemetry, mostly recurring revenue and strong cash generation ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/glasswing); [Help Net Security, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/08/11/openai-daybreak-cyber-models/); [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/fairwind-program/)). CrowdStrike has the better outside evidence and revenue model; Palo Alto converts more revenue into cash after stock pay but is digesting about $25 billion of 2026 acquisitions. Fortinet pairs firewall test results as strong as any rival's with the field's best margins and the largest installed base of the enforcement points that shield devices which cannot be patched, but about 37% of its revenue is product, mostly hardware, sold late in a refresh cycle. On position alone **Anthropic and OpenAI**, which own the gated models and the gates, would rank above all three; their security businesses cannot be separated from AI labs whose finances fail the resilience test, so they are scored beside the ranking rather than in it. Quality and price diverge at the top: CrowdStrike trades at **about 43 times trailing sales against a five-year median of 22**, Palo Alto at **24 times against 12**, both stretched; Fortinet is fair after a 22% intraday fall on 29 October; and the clearest attractive entry among the ranked listed companies is **Check Point**, a middle-ranked business priced below its own five-year range ([StockAnalysis CRWD](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/crwd/financials/ratios/); [StockAnalysis PANW](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/panw/financials/ratios/); [StockAnalysis CHKP](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/chkp/financials/ratios/)).

The open question has a split answer. No independent study measures the net balance, but **in the short run attackers gain at least as much as defenders, because discovery has outrun repair, not because exploitation has surged.** The US National Vulnerability Database published **96,496 CVEs in 2026 to 11 November, twice 2025's full-year total of 48,150**, and only **8.4% of the 6,157 flaws Anthropic had disclosed to maintainers were patched by 2 October 2026** (company figure), with the unpatched backlog up 3.8-fold since late May ([NVD API](https://services.nvd.nist.gov/rest/json/cves/2.0); [SIG, updated 15 Oct 2026](https://www.softwareimprovementgroup.com/blog/mythos-project-glasswing-security/); [Cloud Security Alliance](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/project-glasswing-ai-discovery-outpaces-open-source-patching-capacity)). Observed exploitation has not followed yet: additions to CISA's catalogue of exploited flaws rose 36% year on year (to 10 November) against 136% for new CVEs, and AI-found flaws are exploited at about the 1.3% baseline rate ([CISA KEV](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json); [VulnCheck, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.vulncheck.com/blog/state-of-exploitation-1h-2026)). Defenders win where the loop closes to a deployed fix or a shield. Open-weight models trail the gated frontier by four to eight months at about a hundredth of the cost per task, so the gate buys months, not a monopoly ([CSA, 31 Jul 2026](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-open-weight-cyber-capability-gap-20260731/); [NIST CAISI, 1 May 2026](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/05/caisi-evaluation-deepseek-v4-pro)). Access to the gate is concentrated: **Palo Alto Networks and CrowdStrike in all three programmes, Cisco in two**, a wider ring of OpenAI-only partners (Fortinet, Sophos, Akamai, Cloudflare, NCC Group, Cato Networks, Darktrace), and a thin non-US layer that Washington can close, as the US Commerce Department did for every foreign national on Anthropic's Mythos 5 from 12 to 30 June 2026 ([Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/did-the-us-government-just-set-an-ai-export-precedent-by-blocking-mythos/); [CNBC, 30 Jun 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/30/anthropic-says-trump-admin-has-lifted-export-controls-on-claude-fable-5-and-mythos-5.html)). Chinese vendors are barred and build on domestic open models.

Across the field, value is moving to whoever owns the judge of a fix (the release gate, the enforcement point, the proof checker) and away from billable testing hours and seat-priced scanners. Penetration-testing and bug-bounty platforms, India's IT-services cyber practices and China's state-bound vendors sit at the bottom on quality. The AI-native finders (Horizon3.ai, Pentera, XBOW) sit mid-table because their finances cannot be verified, not because the evidence shows them weak. Several background claims needed correction; the most material is that UK human penetration-testing day rates rose in 2026 rather than falling first (section 6).

## 2. Quality ranking: two gated platforms lead; missing evidence, not weakness, holds the AI-native finders back

**How the order was set.** Each company was scored on the five tests from outside evidence. A company's audited figures (revenue, margins, cash) count; its descriptions of capability or customers count only when an outside party confirms them; and a test that cannot be verified counts against the company rather than for it. Position (a) and lead (b) carry the most weight, because they decide whether the business keeps its place once finding and fixing run at machine speed; revenue model, financial strength and resilience (c–e) decide whether it can pay for the transition. A coherent path was required for the top group (ranks 1–5). Below that, verified evidence outranks unverified claims, which is why the private AI-native firms, most of which have coherent strategies, sit below listed firms whose paths are only partly coherent; their places are provisional. Valuation, share-price moves and size played no part. Where the evidence does not separate companies, they share a rank.

**Why the model makers are scored beside the ranking, not in it.** Anthropic and OpenAI hold the strongest position in the framework, gated frontier cyber models plus the gates that decide who else gets them, and Microsoft and Alphabet run large security franchises with their own models. Tests (b) to (e) could only be scored on the parent, which would rank an AI lab or a hyperscaler rather than a security business. ## 3. Path forward: the leaders' plans are funded; the finders' gap is fixing, and everyone's gap is the gate

The six transitions are numbered as in the brief: (1) billable testing hours to automation; (2) human triage to machine-speed finding and fixing; (3) patching endpoints to shielding devices that cannot be patched; (4) dependence on gated frontier models, and the model makers' own security products; (5) liability and regulation for device makers; (6) software verified when it is written. Transition (5) differs sharply by jurisdiction, so it is summarised once here.

The plan is funded by $5.0 billion of cash and FY2027 free-cash-flow guidance of at least 30%, and the timing is plausible because net new ARR grew 51% in the July quarter ([CrowdStrike release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1535527/000153552726000029/crwd-20260826xex991.htm)). What must go right: acceleration must persist while stock pay falls as a share of revenue, so that cash flow becomes owner earnings; Delta's suit (in discovery) and the DOJ and SEC information requests "relating to the Company's recognition of revenue and reporting of ARR" must close without material loss; and the model makers must not commoditise endpoint triage.

**Palo Alto Networks — coherent, with the highest integration risk of the leaders.** Transitions (2), (3), (4) and (5) all apply. Done: Cortex XSIAM ARR above $700 million (+70%) and Prisma AIRS ARR above $100 million within four quarters (company figures, FY2026) ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9063501/palo-alto-networks-inc-panw-q4-2026-earnings-call-highlights-record-rpo-surpasses-20-billion-ngs-arr-soars-63)); membership of all three gated programmes and early use of GPT-6 Cyber ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/openai-launches-gpt-6-cyber-for-daybreak-red-customers/)); Device Security covering OT and medical devices; and CyberArk ($21.1 billion), Chronosphere ($3.0 billion) and four tuck-ins closed or agreed in 2026, with talks for Clutch Security (about $500 million) reported but unconfirmed ([PANW 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1327567/000132756726000023/panw-20260731.htm)). Said, not shown: an OT capability that builds and deploys protections in under four hours against an industry norm of 55 days (company claim). The plan is funded by about $7.9 billion of cash and investments and an FY2027 adjusted free-cash-flow margin guided at 38% ([PANW release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001327567/000132756726000019/ex991q426earningsrelease.htm)). What must go right: CyberArk must integrate (Chronosphere and CyberArk added $930 million of revenue and a $797 million operating loss from their closing dates); organic next-generation ARR must keep growing above 20% after a "nine-figure" benefit from one large AI-lab customer runs out in the first quarter; memory costs must be passed on; and OT customers' long testing cycles must shorten.

**Fortinet — coherent.** Transition (3) is its home ground: intrusion prevention and virtual patching in front of devices that cannot be patched, and OT segmentation, with OT billings up nearly 40% in Q3 2026 (company claim) ([Motley Fool transcript, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/29/fortinet-ftnt-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Transition (5) cuts both ways: the EU's Cyber Resilience Act creates demand and also makes Fortinet a regulated manufacturer. Transition (2) is partial (FortiAgent SOC; security-operations billings +15%), and (4) is covered by OpenAI's Daybreak but not by Anthropic's or Google's programmes, even though its own call named no model partner. Done: FortiGuard Inline AI Security, FortiAIGate for GPU clusters and FortiAgent SOC shipped, a seven-figure expansion with a frontier AI developer (company claim), and $2.84 billion of free cash flow in nine months ([Fortinet 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1262039/000126203926000027/ftnt-20260930.htm)). What must go right: the 2026 refresh must normalise without a 2027 air pocket (Jefferies flags pull-forward weighing around mid-2027) ([Investing.com, 29 Oct 2026](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/fortinet-earnings-analysis-questions-answered-and-next-catalysts-93CH-4984773)); memory costs must keep being passed on (product gross margin fell from 67.7% to 64.4%); and its custom-chip advantage must survive the next hardware step, enforcement built into switches and DPUs such as Cisco's Hypershield. That last risk is the background's "gap-bridging hardware" pattern in miniature.

**JFrog — coherent.** Transitions (2) and (4) dominate, and (5) adds SBOM and CRA demand (inference). Done: AI asset scanning, an agent guard, support for Microsoft's Agent Package Manager, "zero-touch remediation" and a Wiz partnership; a $76 million multi-year telecom deal bundled with AI security; FY2026 revenue guidance raised three times, to $680–682 million; and Anthropic and OpenAI confirmed as customers on the Q3 call ([JFrog 8-K, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001800667/000119312526449797/frog-ex99_1.htm); [Finviz/Earnings Whispers](https://finviz.com/news/418104/jfrog-beats-on-accelerating-cloud-growth-and-raises-again-but-q4-outlook-signals-a-margin-pause)). It passed the February test operationally: the shares fell about 25% on the day Anthropic previewed Claude Code Security, then revenue accelerated to +31% ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001535714)). The plan is funded by $881 million of cash and no debt. What must go right: the security and AI attach, which management declined to quantify until year-end, must become disclosed and measurable; consumption commitments must keep converting; the labs must keep buying third-party package gates rather than building their own; and stock pay (about 23% of revenue) must fall.

**Tenable — coherent, moderate confidence.** Its bet is to be the system of record that ingests everyone else's machine-found flaws, which addresses (2) and (4) directly: it says it integrated Anthropic's Mythos into Tenable One and uses OpenAI's cyber models, the latter in a release quoting OpenAI's head of global cyber partnerships ([Tenable 8-K, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001660280/000166028026000050/q32026financialresults-ear.htm); [Tenable, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.tenable.com/press-releases/tenable-uses-openai-gpt-cyber-models-to-advance-agentic-security-review-in-the-cyberagents-exchange)). Done: Vulcan Cyber (remediation orchestration, $148.5 million) and Apex ($47.8 million) closed; Hexa AI agents and an agent exchange launched. Funded: about $290 million of unlevered free cash flow in 2026 and an $800 million convertible running to 2031. What must go right: per-asset pricing must hold as discovery commoditises (net expansion is 107%); bundlers such as Google-owned Wiz must not absorb exposure management; and the model makers whose findings Tenable ingests must stay suppliers rather than rivals. Transition (6) is a long-run headwind.

**Qualys — partly coherent.** Its answer to commoditised discovery is to own the fix: TruRisk Eliminate 2.0 (14 Oct 2026) adds patch-reliability scoring and wave-based autonomous remediation (company description), and current billings grew 14% in Q3 ([Qualys 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001107843/000110784326000043/qlys-20261109xex991.htm); [Qualys newsroom](https://www.qualys.com/company/newsroom/)). That is the right destination, since repair is the bottleneck, and it is funded with no debt and $808 million of cash. It is only partly coherent because it has disclosed no answer to (4) (no frontier-model partnership and no acquisitions in 2026) while AWS Continuum, Microsoft's Perception and Google's CodeMender automate the same remediation step. What must go right: remediation automation must hold prices up; Qualys must gain or build frontier capability; its channel and managed-operations partners must scale.

**Claroty — partly coherent, leaning coherent.** Transition (3) is the core and (5) the demand driver (FDA premarket rules, the CRA, NIS2); (2) is still mostly said. Done: a $150 million Series F in January 2026; xDome, on-premises detection, secure access and edge discovery; segmentation features; and a second year as a Gartner CPS Leader ([Claroty, 9 Mar 2026](https://claroty.com/blog/claroty-named-a-leader-in-2026-gartner-magic-quadrant-for-cps-protection-platforms); [SecurityWeek, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.securityweek.com/claroty-raises-150-million-in-series-f-funding/)). Said: an IPO "as early as 2027". Its shielding depends on integration with other vendors' enforcement points, and its three Gartner co-Leaders now sit inside ServiceNow, Mitsubishi Electric and Accenture, which can bundle them. What must go right: protection rather than visibility alone, profitability (undisclosed), and an IPO window in 2027; no gated-model access was found.

**Check Point — partly coherent.** The pivot to "preemptive security" covers (2), (3), (4) and, through AI-evaluation start-ups, part of (6), backed by about $392 million of disclosed acquisitions (Veriti, Lakera, Cyclops, Cyata) plus Deepchecks, and by a reported selection for OpenAI's Daybreak ([Check Point 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1015922/000117891326001932/zk2634942.htm); [Channel Insider](https://www.channelinsider.com/ai/openai-daybreak-cyber-program-security-tools/)). Email, AI security and exposure-management ARR grew over 38% (company claim). But Q3 2026 revenue fell 1.1%, calculated billings 5% and products 19%, and the chief executive said "the recovery is taking longer than anticipated" ([Check Point release, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.checkpoint.com/press-releases/check-point-software-reports-2026-third-quarter-financial-results/)). Funding is ample ($4.1 billion of cash; about $1.15 billion of 2026 free cash flow), though $1.4 billion a year of buybacks competes with M&A. What must go right: new sellers must convert, products must stabilise against Fortinet, AI security must scale, Israel risk must stay contained, and the February 2027 Capital Markets Day must confirm more than $1.5 billion of 2027 subscriptions.

**Chainguard — coherent.** It answers (2) by rebuilding, more than 2,500 container projects a day (company claim), so fixes ship as rebuilt images rather than tickets; it adds (5) through federal demand and part of (6) through attested provenance ([Chainguard, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.chainguard.dev/unchained/building-a-category-chainguard-named-a-leader-in-the-inaugural-gartner-magic-quadrant-for-software-supply-chain-security)). Done: Gartner SSCS Leader; pricing moved to a catalog fee; ARR about $100 million by mid-2026 (third-party estimate) ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/chainguard/)). Funded with $892 million raised. What must go right: it must grow into a $3.5 billion valuation and become agents' default trusted source before hyperscalers bundle hardened images.

**Horizon3.ai — coherent, with remediation still a promise.** NodeZero exploits weaknesses inside customers' own networks, an environment-specific judge that source-code scanners do not replicate, and the company claims FedRAMP High authorisation, 7,000+ organisations and ARR growth of 120% from an undisclosed base ([Horizon3.ai, 3 Aug 2026](https://horizon3.ai/news/press-release/horizon3-raises-250m-series-e-at-2b-valuation-to-lead-the-ai-vs-ai-cybersecurity-era/)). Said: "autonomous blue-team agents that remediate findings". Funded by a $250 million Series E at more than $2 billion. What must go right: the remediation agents must ship; it must stay ahead of AWS's testing agent (marketed as cutting tests "from weeks to hours") and the labs' red-team tiers ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/security-agent/)); and a valuation that tripled in about 14 months must become disclosed revenue.

**Pentera — coherent.** Transitions (1) and (2): it replaced scheduled human tests with automated validation, added remediation workflows (Resolve) and claims more than $100 million of ARR across 1,200+ enterprises (January 2026); it listed on AWS Marketplace and hired a CFO in April 2026 ([TechIntelPro](https://techintelpro.com/news/cybersecurity/ai/pentera-hits-100m-arr-as-first-aev-centaur-company)). It also bought a human red-team service, a partial step back towards labour. Funding is modest ($60 million in 2025, "approaching breakeven" then, company claim) ([Calcalist, 12 Mar 2025](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ryvxvby21x)). What must go right: the breakeven claim must hold, acquisitions must lift ARR towards the $200 million target, and LLM-native attack depth must not leapfrog it.

**XBOW — partly coherent; the research notes rated it coherent.** Transitions (1), (2) and (4). Done: the single most important outside proof in the field, in which Google reproduced a three-bug Chrome chain and approved a $250,000 reward on 31 August 2026, although one researcher steered the work and the model was OpenAI's public GPT-5.6 Sol ([CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/google-pays-250k-bounty-for-ai-assisted-chrome-exploit-built-in-six-days/); [The Hacker News, 14 Oct 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/researchers-used-public-openai-model-to.html)); $155 million raised in 2026 with Accenture, NVIDIA, Samsung and SentinelOne as strategic investors; Samsung reselling it in Korea; 250+ staff ([Pulse 2.0, 7 May 2026](https://pulse2.com/xbow-35-million-added-to-series-c-to-expand-autonomous-offensive-security-platform/)). It earns only "partly coherent" here for two reasons the plan does not address: it finds but does not fix, and its supplier now sells the same work, since OpenAI's Daybreak Red tier offers purpose-trained models for vulnerability research, exploit validation and red teaming ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/08/11/openai-daybreak-cyber-models/)). Funded with about $272 million raised. What must go right: a lasting lead over its own model supplier, a remediation product, and disclosed ARR that supports a valuation above $1 billion.

**Zscaler — partly coherent.** The architecture suits agents (inline control of agent traffic), and (3) is covered through Airgap and Zero Trust Branch for OT, with a seven-figure OT deal at a healthcare-equipment maker (company claim). But (2) is early, since Agentic SecOps on Red Canary launched on 9 September 2026 with a meaningful contribution expected only in FY2028; (4) shows no confirmed gated access; and the Red Canary MDR book churned ([Zscaler call, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/09/09/zscaler-zs-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Seat-to-metered pricing has started (metered products about 30% of new contract value, company claim). Funded with $3.5 billion of cash and investments. What must go right: the sales-leadership change must settle in the first half of FY2027, capex must normalise, metered pricing must scale, and free cash flow after stock pay must turn positive.

**Cisco (security segment) — partly coherent.** It has the best gated access among network vendors (Glasswing launch partner, Daybreak partner, GPT-6 Cyber user), segmentation built into its N9300 smart switches (Hypershield) and Cyber Vision inside industrial switches (company descriptions), and it closed Astrix in 2026 ([Cisco Hypershield](https://www.cisco.com/site/us/en/products/security/hypershield/index.html); [Cisco 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/858877/000085887726000106/exhibit991pressrelease-q4f.htm)). But security grew only 2% in FY2026 (14% in Q4), Gartner places it a Visionary, and CyberRatings rated its virtual firewall "Caution" at 66.10% effectiveness ([CyberRatings](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cyberratingsorg-and-nss-labs-announce-2026-cloud-network-firewall-test-results-302892181.html)). What must go right: switch-embedded enforcement must show up both in security growth and in independent tests.

**Trend Micro — partly coherent.** It owns the Zero Day Initiative and Pwn2Own, a vendor-recognised judge whose Ireland 2026 contest paid $1.26 million for 98 zero-days ([CyberInsider, 9 Oct 2026](https://cyberinsider.com/google-pixel-10-hacked-as-pwn2own-ireland-wraps-with-1-26-million-in-rewards/)); it announced Glasswing participation on 3–4 June 2026, days before the US directive suspended all foreign access ([Uravation](https://uravation.com/media/hitachi-trendmicro-claude-mythos-project-glasswing-japan-2026/)); it is building Vision One agents (Vision One ARR +49%, company claim); and it holds TXOne and VicOne for (3). But on 12 November 2026 it dropped its "Road to 2028" plan, which targeted a 25–27% operating margin, and moved to annual guidance only, after cloud costs rose 57% in nine months ([Trend Micro Q3 deck](https://www.trendmicro.com/content/dam/trendmicro/global/en/about/investor-relations/reports-presentation/2026/financial-2026-presentation-negi-q3.pdf)). What must go right: cost-to-serve must fall, Vision One must outgrow the legacy decline, ZDI must remain the venue of record as AI floods supply, and its Mythos access must be restored and disclosed.

**Anthropic — coherent, contingent on Washington.** It is the supplier in transition (4) and covers (1) and (2) itself. Glasswing grew from 12 launch partners and 40+ organisations on 7 April 2026 to about 200 organisations in June, was folded into a three-tier Cyber Verification Program on 6 October, and Mythos 5.2 (9 November) is sold to verified organisations at $8 and $40 per million input and output tokens; Claude Security, powered by Mythos, is in beta for every Enterprise customer at token cost with free credits of up to $25,000 a month for two months through 31 December 2026 ([Anthropic, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2); [Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/14661296-use-claude-security); [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/anthropic-expands-cyber-verification-program-to-three-access-tiers/)). Not done: general availability and any disclosed security revenue. Funded by an IPO raising up to $64 billion plus a reported $26 billion private placement ([CNBC, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-sets-price-range-of-70-to-78-in-upcoming-ipo.html)). What must go right: relations with Washington, which closed the gate for 18 days in June; gate integrity, after unauthorised users reached Mythos through a Discord channel in April (secondary report) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Mythos)); and remediation throughput, since only 8.4% of its disclosed findings were patched by October.

**OpenAI — coherent, commercialisation unproven.** It shipped a run of cyber-specific models in 2026 (GPT-5.4-Cyber in April, GPT-5.6-Cyber by August, GPT-6 Cyber in November), the Daybreak tiers and partner programme, a $1 billion "Daybreak for Frontline Defenders" grant scheme, Codex Security (still a research preview with no separate price) and the kernel run ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/openai-launches-gpt-6-cyber-for-daybreak-red-customers/); [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/codex-security-cloud)). No security acquisition has been completed; talks with Trail of Bits were reported in one secondary source and are unconfirmed. What must go right: maintainers must accept its patches, Washington must not gate it as it gated Anthropic, partners must not defect, and the November dispute over training on Daybreak Blue session data must not cost trust ([CyberScoop](https://cyberscoop.com/openai-gpt-6-cyber-security-model/)).

**Microsoft — coherent.** It routes about 90% of security tasks to its own MAI-Cyber-1-Flash and buys frontier capability for the rest, claiming the model beats Mythos "at half the cost" (unconfirmed); MAI Cyber runs over 40% of the scans in its agentic code scanner at 18% lower cost, and GitHub Advanced Security revenue grew over 50% (company figures, 28 Oct 2026) ([Microsoft FY26 Q4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4); [Microsoft FY27 Q1](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2027/earnings-fy-2027-q1)). What must go right: consumption pricing (Security Copilot, Perception) must offset the cannibalisation of per-seat suites as agents replace human users.

**Alphabet / Google — coherent in product, partly coherent commercially.** It owns the widest set of judges of any model maker: Fairwind's gated models, CodeMender's validation stack, Wiz's exposure graph and, from 10 November 2026, AlphaProof Code's two machine-checked security proofs for compiled libsignal code (company claim; human-reviewed specifications; no independent replication) ([DeepMind, 10 Nov 2026](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/); [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/safety-security/fairwind-program/)). Mandiant still sells hours, and no security revenue is disclosed.

**Amazon / AWS — partly coherent.** Continuum covers code vulnerabilities and penetration testing with "multiple frontier cyber models", and Kiro's requirements analysis pairs an LLM with an SMT solver, the clearest commercial step towards transition (6) ([AWS Continuum](https://aws.amazon.com/continuum/); [GeekWire, May 2026](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/aws-targets-ai-slop-with-new-spec-check-in-kiro-coding-tool-amid-scrutiny-of-agent-reliability/)). It is a distributor that depends on others' gates, with no published price or general-availability date.

The largest change in understanding is about where scarcity sits. The background treated the gated models as the scarce asset and finding as the frontier. The 2026 evidence says discovery is now abundant (CVEs doubled, findings in the hundreds of thousands) and the scarce assets are repair capacity and the authority to accept a fix: the maintainer who merges the patch, the release gate that blocks the package, the enforcement point that shields the device. That favours owners of gates and systems of record over pure finders, makes the US government a co-owner of the most important gate, and suggests the pricing pressure in testing arrives through volume and routine-finding prices before it reaches the day rates of skilled humans.

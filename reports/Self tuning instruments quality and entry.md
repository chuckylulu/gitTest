# Owning the judge beats owning the agent

On quality alone, the three strongest companies in self-tuning instruments on 12 November 2026 are **KLA, Thermo Fisher Scientific and Keysight Technologies**, with Waters a close fourth: each owns the measurement that decides whether a set-up is right (the "judge"), sits on a large installed base, and is already carrying out a funded plan for agent-run set-up. KLA holds about **57% of chip process control** (2025, secondary data); Thermo Fisher microscopes recorded **97.5% of the electron-microscope structures** released to the Protein Data Bank in 2025; and Keysight has shipped an agent server that runs vendor-defined calibration routines. On the open question, makers are opening instruments to agents on their own terms, with open plumbing (the Model Context Protocol, MCP) but closed dictionaries (vendor-written command sets, safety limits and calibration procedures), so the value stays with the maker that owns the judge and its service contracts rather than with an independent software layer. Users capture value only where they own the control stack or the data: national laboratories build and open-source their own agents, chip fabs hold the device data that fine tool matching needs, and large pharma buyers are pressing, through procurement, for native open standards and one-time API fees on instruments released after 2027. Interfaces opened first where instruments are cheapest (electronic test and LC-MS software, in 2026), while electron microscopes, laboratory X-ray and fab metrology keep paid or proprietary access. Much of the striking evidence behind the original thesis is human-gated, single-step or classical optimisation, and no maker yet ships agent-run alignment of a complex instrument as a standard feature. On entry, judged separately, most leaders look fairly priced after the October–November 2026 sell-off; Oxford Instruments and Anritsu look attractive, Rigaku looks stretched, and the best business, KLA, is only fairly priced. This is analysis for exploration, not advice to buy or sell.

## 1. Field map: across eight sub-segments, makers own the judges and users build the agents

The agents that tune instruments against that measurement are mostly built by users: national laboratories, universities and chip fabs. They use general-purpose models and open control stacks. **Electron microscopy is a hardware oligopoly with one dominant judge.** Thermo Fisher microscopes account for **7,017 of the 7,200** microscope records on PDB electron-microscopy entries released in 2025. JEOL had about 2.5%, and Hitachi and Zeiss were effectively zero. The first Chinese 300 kV cryo-microscope (Shuimu) appears on five entries in 2025–26. These are the researchers' counts from the RCSB Search API on 12 Nov 2026 ([RCSB](https://search.rcsb.org/rcsbsearch/v2/query)). Every working agent study found runs on Thermo Fisher interfaces. In LBNL's TEM Agent, Claude Sonnet 4.5 drove the TEAM 0.5 microscope through four MCP servers that wrap Thermo Fisher scripting, a CEOS corrector gateway and Gatan software ([arXiv 2511.08819](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08819v1)). CEOS correctors and Gatan detectors are therefore choke points for any outside agent; both are private or undisclosed. Zeiss took a **€54m** goodwill impairment in its research-microscopy unit in FY2024/25, leaving that unit's goodwill at zero, on "lower expectations for the future business prospects" ([ZEISS Annual Report 2024/25](https://asset-downloads.zeiss.com/catalogs/download/cco2/c026d158-e8ca-4cbe-ac7f-8ef3541ed925/ZEISS_Gruppe_Gescha_ftsbericht_2024-25_EN_826455_0.pdf)). Shimadzu completed its purchase of TESCAN on 10 Jul 2026 for a final $711m, as reported by trade press ([Yakuji Nippo, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.yakuji.co.jp/?p=136531)).

**X-ray and synchrotron work splits between closed lab instruments and open facility stacks.** DECTRIS detectors appear on **86.8% of 2025 X-ray PDB entries**, up from 21.8% in 2015, and DECTRIS publishes an open stream API ([DECTRIS documentation](https://github.com/dectris/documentation)). Lab diffractometers from Rigaku and Bruker already automate alignment by classical means. Every synchrotron agent found was built by a public facility on open or simple control stacks (Bluesky, EPICS, BLISS, SPEC or a lab-built API). Every one that moves hardware keeps a human confirmation step.

**Mass spectrometry already self-tunes; the new frontier is agent access to methods.** LCGC's staff review of ASMS 2026 found AI features in data interpretation, predictive maintenance and help functions, not in autonomous operation or self-calibration ([LCGC, 5 Jun 2026](https://www.chromatographyonline.com/view/a-look-at-the-major-technical-themes-of-asms-2026)). Waters is the only major found that exposes method creation and acquisition to outside agents.

**Chip measurement is concentrated class by class.** The share data come through TechInsights via CSET and are transcribed by an aggregator page that says it was written "with Claude". The underlying CSET view could not be opened, so treat these shares as secondary ([chipsupplychain.org, updated 29 Oct 2026](https://chipsupplychain.org/metrology-and-inspection/)). Hitachi's CD-SEM share is consistent across three sources: about 76% (company), 70% (trade press) and 71.9% (TechInsights via the aggregator). The main independent connectivity and analytics layer, PDF Solutions, had revenue of **$219m** in 2025, with three customers making up 53% ([PDF Solutions 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1120914/000143774926005366/pdfs20251231d_10k.htm)). That is small beside KLA's $3.13bn service book.

**Electronic test opened first because it was always open.** SCPI, VISA and IVI have been open standards for decades, and the IVI Foundation moved its specifications to GitHub in 2025 ([IVI 2026](https://www.ivifoundation.org/downloads/IVI%20Generations/IVI%202026.pdf)). The 2026 money flowed to hardware for new speed nodes. Keysight's communications revenue grew 56%, and Siglent's network analyser, source meter and power supply revenue grew 507% in Q3 2026 on data-centre demand ([Siglent Q3 2026](https://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-10-27/1225626059.PDF)).

**Quantum control is being squeezed from both sides.** Processor makers build control in-house; a listed peer notes that "many quantum hardware developers ... continue to build their own control electronics" ([SEEQC prospectus, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1779977/000121390026074258/ea0278139-08.htm)). Calibration software is being given away: Quantum Machines' QUAlibrate is BSD-licensed, and NVIDIA's Ising calibration models are open-weight ([NVIDIA, 14 Apr 2026](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-launches-ising-the-worlds-first-open-ai-models-to-accelerate-the-path-to-useful-quantum-computers)).

**Laboratory-automation software is being pressed from above and below.** From above, model suppliers set the protocol. Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard entered a research preview on 27 Aug 2026; its specification, licence and conformance suite were not public ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/model-hardware-standard-research-preview); [noze, 29 Aug 2026](https://www.noze.it/en/insights/model-hardware-standard-agents-lab-instruments/)). From below, makers write their own MCP servers. The independents that are holding up have tied themselves to a large maker: TetraScience to Thermo Fisher, Automata to Danaher and Atinary to Bruker.

**Accelerators run on open stacks, and the labs own the agents.** A survey of 29 accelerators presented at IPAC'26 found no facility at full automation and called LLMs "a marginal topic" ([IPAC'26 WEP6099](https://indico.jacow.org/event/95/papers/13653/files/5105-WEP6099.pdf)). By its developers' account, LBNL's Osprey agent is in production at the Advanced Light Source under a BSD licence, and a human approves every hardware write ([Osprey](https://github.com/als-apg/osprey)).

**What separates the leaders.** KLA ranks first because it scores high on all five tests. Its judge is the strongest, with the largest installed base (>57,000 tools). ## 3. Path-forward notes: six coherent plans and ten partial ones

The five transitions are the same throughout: (T1) from expert to agent-run set-up; (T2) from closed to open control interfaces; (T3) from one-off hardware to software and subscription revenue; (T4) cheaper hardware with heavier computation; and (T5) instruments as data sources for customers' own AI.

**KLA (1): coherent.** KLA faces T1 and T5 in the fab, and it has already crossed T3. Its plan is to sell agent-style automation inside its service contracts rather than open its tools to outsiders.
- **What it says.** Its Investor Day deck lists "Autonomous synchronization", which matches a tool to "25,000 configuration settings" after a service event and up to about 1,250,000 across a global fleet. It also lists "On-demand agentic expertise", a "Digital Mentor" that captures master engineers' knowledge. Both are sold through services, where more than 80% of revenue comes from multi-year subscription contracts with a 95% renewal rate (company claims; no customer named) ([KLA Investor Day, 12 Mar 2026](https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_7791115a123b86b3f10b1a5eb5210224/klatencor/db/1166/10653/file/2026+Investor+Day+Master+Final_IR+copy.pdf)).
- **What it has done.** Services reached **$3.13bn, 23% of FY2026 revenue**, and backlog reached $12.57bn at 30 Jun 2026 ([KLA 10-K FY2026](https://ir.kla.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000319201-26-000027/0000319201-26-000027.pdf)).
- **Funding.** R&D was $395m in the September 2026 quarter, and net debt is about $1.0bn ([KLA 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/319201/000031920126000034/klac-20260930.htm)). No public agent interface was found.
- **What must go right.**
  - Fabs must accept matching run by the tool maker rather than bring it in-house; KLA's own 10-K warns that AI may "accelerate vertical integration or insourcing by customers".
  - KLA needs the fab device data that the finest matching requires. Onto's engineers say matching "requires access to fab-level device data, such as metrology results and functional test outcomes" ([Semiconductor Engineering, 10 Mar 2026](https://semiengineering.com/tool-matching-getting-tougher-across-test-metrology/)).
  - The guided step-down in China, 34.5% of the latest quarter, must be absorbed.

**Thermo Fisher (2): coherent.** Thermo Fisher faces T1, T2 and T5 in electron microscopy, and its microscopes are where the research is happening.
- **Outside evidence.** Tennessee's January 2026 aberration-tuning work drove a Spectra 300 through Thermo Fisher's AutoScript ([arXiv 2601.18972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18972)). The PNNL–Sandia framework plans to drive a Helios FIB-SEM through AutoScript ([Microscopy and Microanalysis, 27 Jul 2026](https://academic.oup.com/mam/article/32/Supplement_1/ozag053.977/8742936)). Thermo Fisher's own staff publish LLM-to-AutoScript prototypes ([M&M 2026](https://academic.oup.com/mam/article/32/Supplement_1/ozag053.1011/8742819)).
- **What it says.** The Krios 5 offers "AI-powered experimental set-up" (company claim). The company's innovation VP calls MCP "valuable" but says the biggest challenge is "the quality and accessibility of the underlying data" ([Pharmaceutical Processing World, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.pharmaceuticalprocessingworld.com/thermo-fishers-vp-of-innovation-on-ai-co-scientists-autonomous-instruments-and-the-data-work-that-has-to-come-first/)). An executive was reported, in a secondary source only, as saying "the first version of fully autonomous instruments" would reach customers in the second half of 2026 ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/what-thermo-fisher-ceo-said-at-investor-day-that-the-market-is-still-processing)). No such launch appears in the Q3 results of 21 Oct 2026 ([Thermo Fisher Q3 2026](https://ir.thermofisher.com/investors/news-events/news/news-details/2026/Thermo-Fisher-Scientific-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).
- **What it has done.**
  - **Interfaces:** they are open by licence and monetised. "Lens current control" is an optional licence on AutoScript TEM ([AutoScript TEM](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/electron-microscopy/products/software-em-3d-vis/autoscript-tem-software.html)).
  - **Products and partners:** it has shipped Smart EPU machine-learning functions and the Vulcan Automated Lab. It has an NVIDIA collaboration whose first agents recommend actions rather than execute them.
  - **Data:** its EU Data Act disclosure takes an irrevocable, time-unlimited, sublicensable licence to non-personal product data ([Thermo Fisher](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/global/policies-notices/product-data-access-use-disclosures-eu.html)).
- **Funding.** Group R&D was $1,397m in 2025.
- **What must go right.**
  - **Deterministic, safe tool layers.** LBNL found that the same prompt produced different action sequences, and that "traditional dedicated software would likely outperform an agent in the near term" ([Berkeley Lab, 29 Oct 2026](https://foundry.lbl.gov/2026/10/29/new-virtual-assistant-streamlines-one-of-the-most-advanced-microscopes/)).
  - **Buyers must keep accepting paid scripting.**
  - **Leverage of about 3.3x must not constrain investment.**

**Keysight (3): coherent.** Keysight faces T1 through T4 and has done something on each.
- **What it has shipped.** Its MCP server for instrument control turns plain language into SCPI commands drawn from Keysight definition files: "The AI doesn't invent the commands" and "Nothing runs without approval". It includes 1-port SOLT calibration ("2-port SOLT is not yet available"). It works through Keysight, NI or Rohde & Schwarz VISA, which makes it a potential cross-vendor gateway ([Keysight KMSIC](https://www.keysight.com/find/kmsic); [release notes](https://helpfiles.keysight.com/kmsic/English/keysight_mcp_for_instrument_control/Content/release-notes.html)). The release date (v0.1, May 2026) is secondary, and no price is stated.
- **Software and services.** These were about 37% of FY2025 revenue, and annual recurring revenue 29% (company) ([Q4 FY2025 remarks](https://s22.q4cdn.com/444849635/files/doc_earnings/2025/q4/transcript/Q4-FY2025-Prepared-Remarks.pdf)). Keysight bought Spirent for $1,564m. It co-led Liquid Instruments' $50m round for FPGA-based, software-defined instruments, a hedge on T4 ([SiliconANGLE, 28 Apr 2026](https://siliconangle.com/?p=741861)).
- **What must go right.**
  - Definition-file coverage must reach the installed base.
  - Customers must loosen per-command approval as trust grows.
  - Keysight must earn more than hardware pull-through from agents.
  - Earnings must survive a pause in AI spending: communications was 54.5% of Q3 FY2026 revenue ([Keysight Q3 FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001601046/000160104626000029/exhibit991-q326pressrelease.htm)).

**Waters (4): coherent.** Waters made the clearest T1 and T2 move in its segment.
- **What it has launched.** AI Link brings MCP to waters_connect, so that "Agents can create methods, run acquisitions, and process results without custom integration". Regulated work moves into Empower, the validated system of record, with its audit trail. Waters joined Anthropic's hardware-standard programme "effective immediately" ([Waters release, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/waters-launches-ai-link-advancing-ai-enabled-lc-ms-workflows-from-research-to-release-302906439.html)).
- **What is missing.** No customers, pricing or validation results were given, and general availability "in November" could not be verified.
- **Regulatory fit.** The design suits the regulation. The draft EU GMP Annex 22 confines LLMs to non-critical uses under human oversight, and it was not in force on 12 Nov 2026 ([European Commission, EudraLex Vol. 4](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medicinal-products/eudralex/eudralex-volume-4_en)).
- **Revenue model.** More than 70% of revenue recurs (company) ([Waters IR](https://ir.waters.com/)), and Waters says AI is "multiplying the sample volume running through our instruments and columns".
- **What must go right.**
  - General availability and real adoption.
  - Safe operation within its safety limits.
  - A validated path from R&D agents into QC.
  - Integration of BD's biosciences and diagnostics businesses while leverage is about 3.1–3.7x.

**Lasertec (5): partly coherent.** Lasertec has crossed T3: services were **¥58.6bn, 25.4% of FY6/26 sales**, up 36.5%, and orders rose to ¥237.5bn from ¥105.2bn ([Lasertec Business Report](https://www.lasertec.co.jp/en/ir/plan/message.html)). In the September 2026 quarter, advances received doubled to ¥87.1bn and services were 39.7% of sales ([Lasertec Q1 FY6/27](https://finance-frontend-pc-dist.west.edge.storage-yahoo.jp/disclosure/20261030/20261030561656.pdf)). No AI, agent or interface programme was found. Its path rests on hardware leadership in EUV mask inspection against KLA (42% vs 45.9%, secondary), funded by ¥110.8bn of cash and no debt. What must go right: wins in EUV and High-NA mask inspection, AI-chip capex holding up, and continued service growth. It is partly coherent because the economics are superb but there is no visible plan for T1, T2 or T5.

**Hitachi High-Tech (6): coherent, on mostly secondary evidence.** Hitachi High-Tech owns the CD-SEM judge, and its plan targets T1 and T5. Trade press reports an AI platform "by FY2027" that would collect data from its SEMs and etchers for recipe optimisation. Proofs of concept are running with unnamed chipmakers, and other makers' tool data would be added later ([Nikkan Kogyo via NewSwitch, 9 Feb 2026](https://newswitch.jp/p/48308)). Hitachi plans "HMAX for Semiconductors" and ¥370bn of cumulative R&D through FY2027 for the sector's "physical AI" business ([Hitachi Integrated Report 2026](https://www.hitachi.com/content/dam/hitachi/global/en/ir/media/library/integrated/2026/ar2026e_12.pdf)). It has already automated its own pre-shipment CD-SEM adjustment, which technicians previously did by hand. The plant now runs six unmanned hours in each 24-hour cycle and monitors 70 adjustment pits remotely ([MONOist, 31 Mar 2025](https://monoist.itmedia.co.jp/mn/spv/2503/31/news128_2.html)). Its interfaces could not be checked because the vendor site blocked access. What must go right: the proofs of concept must convert to paid deals, the CD-SEM share must hold against Applied Materials (27.5% of CD metrology), and fabs must share data.

**Agilent (7): partly coherent.** Agilent has crossed T3: CrossLab was $786m, 41.9% of Q3 FY2026 revenue, at a 34.3% margin ([Agilent Q3 FY2026](https://www.investor.agilent.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Agilent-Reports-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-Year-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)). On T1 its AI is assistive only: GC Assist offers monitoring and guided troubleshooting, and the OpenAI and BCG deal starts with customer experience. No agent interface or move on T2 was found. What must go right: Agilent must open access before buyers force it. A letter of intent signed on 6 Jul 2026 by automation leads at Roche Diagnostics, Bayer CropScience, Takeda, Novo Nordisk, BioNTech, Syngenta, Lonza and the Botnar Institute commits them to "prioritize and, where feasible, mandate" native SiLA 2 or OPC UA LADS standards. It also sets one-time API fees ("Recurring Fees are not an option") for products released after 2027 ([SiLA letter](https://sila-standard.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Letter-of-Intent-on-Open-Standards-for-Life-Sciences_firstSignatures.pdf)).

**Nova (8): partly coherent.** Nova addresses T1 and T4 through software: Nova FIT ("machine learning and modeling solutions") and Nova HPC, the "foundation for Nova's Centralized Fleet Management and Control". Its own risk factor warns that AI "may change the balance between hardware capabilities and software or algorithms needs" ([Nova 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1109345/000117891326000504/zk2634307.htm)). There is no evidence on T2 or T5. Its plan is funded by about $1bn of net cash and R&D at 15% of revenue. What must go right: metrology intensity at gate-all-around (about 30% above FinFET) and HBM demand must persist, access to China (33% of 2025 sales) must survive, and the 9 Mar 2027 investor day must set credible targets.

**Rohde & Schwarz (9): coherent but narrow.** Rohde & Schwarz has done T1 and T2 for one product family. MXO AI Connect, released on 29 Oct 2026, is a local MCP server for its oscilloscopes. It accepts any MCP client and is "available free of charge... No license key is required". It claims agents can iterate "until the measurement goal is reached" (company claim) ([R&S release](https://www.rohde-schwarz.com/jp/about-us/news-press/all-news/rohde-schwarz-introduces-mxo-ai-connect-for-ai-assisted-oscilloscope-workflows_229356-1709313.html)). Its subsidiary Zurich Instruments launched ZQCS on 9 Mar 2026. Its LabOne Q software "provides automation for calibration and tune-up" ([Zurich Instruments](https://www.zhinst.com/de/news/zurich-instruments-launches-zqcs-quantum-control-system-master-long-lived-logical-qubit/)). R&D is "a double-digit percentage" of revenue. What must go right: the approach must extend beyond oscilloscopes, the loop-closing claim must be proven on real tasks, and Zurich Instruments must keep its lead as processor makers bring control in-house.

**Oxford Instruments (10): partly coherent.** T2 is the crux for Oxford Instruments. The PNNL–Sandia framework planned to drive its AZtec analyser software by reading the screen, because APIs were "unavailable or impractical" ([M&M 2026](https://academic.oup.com/mam/article/32/Supplement_1/ozag053.977/8742936)). On 10 Nov 2026 the company said it is "developing customer-facing APIs to enable customers to integrate our tools and analytical software into AI workflows" ([Oxford Instruments H1 FY27](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/oxford-instruments--oxig/half-year-results/9866415)). That is a stated plan, not a shipped product. It has net cash of £74.8m and spends about 9% of revenue on R&D. What must go right: the APIs must ship before column makers absorb analysis into their own software, semiconductor orders must continue, and its "Made in China" detectors must defend share in China.

**Shimadzu (11): partly coherent.**
- **What it says.** Its November 2026 deck sets out an "Autonomous lab", "AI platform partnerships" and "Open connectivity" ([Shimadzu H1 FY3/27 deck](https://www.shimadzu.com/sites/shimadzu.com/files/ir/r272/7zicbmkg9z2n0leo.pdf)).
- **What it has done.**
  - **Products:** it has shipped auto-diagnostics with automatic recovery and AI-based method creation, and its LabSolutions software controls Agilent and Thermo Fisher LC and GC (company claim).
  - **Recurring revenue:** this reached 44% of analytical sales.
  - **TESCAN:** the acquisition brings the 100 kV TENSOR 4D-STEM, whose "alignments run in the background without user intervention" (company claim) ([TESCAN](https://tescan.com/product-portfolio/stem/tensor)). This is the clearest T4 product in electron microscopy.
- **What is missing.** No AI partner or agent protocol is named.
- **What must go right.** TESCAN must lift returns: ROIC fell from 19.6% in FY3/22 to about 12% on a trailing basis. The promised partnerships must also materialise.

**Bruker (12): partly coherent.** Bruker has the most ambitious software strategy among the analytical makers: "Automated AI-Labs" combining Chemspeed robotics with SciY software, "Open for integration of third-party hardware, software, and data stacks" (company claim) ([Bruker JPM 2026](https://s22.q4cdn.com/617463959/files/doc_downloads/2026/04/BRKR-JPM26-Presentation-Final.pdf)). It added a minority stake in Atinary. Its Nion electron-microscope stack, Nion Swift, is open source under the GPL-3.0 licence ([GitHub](https://github.com/nion-software/nionswift)). The plan is under-funded in practice. Bruker took goodwill impairments of $309.4m and generated free cash flow of only $37.5m in the first nine months of 2026, and it is running a $140m cost-out ([Bruker Q3 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001109354/000119312526446176/brkr-ex99_1.htm)). No agent control of its own instruments was found. What must go right: free cash flow must recover, SciY must prove genuinely open, and US academic funding must stabilise.

**Park Systems (13): partly coherent.** Park has done T1 in the form of vendor automation. NX-Wafer automatically receives the defect map, then aligns and scans. FX40 recognises and mounts probes and aligns its laser automatically (company claims) ([Park Systems semi-annual report, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.parksystems.com/content/dam/parksystems/investor/pdf/-Park%20Systems-Semi-Annual%20Report%282026.08.14%29.pdf)). Nothing was found on T2, T3 or T5: services are about 7% of revenue. The plan is funded by a KRW 100bn perpetual bond and R&D at 18.5% of sales. What must go right: the backlog must convert in H2 2026, and Park must either open its SmartScan software to outside agents or earn money from the data.

**JEOL (14): partly coherent.** JEOL has stated plans for T1 and T3. They are "automated and AI-assisted cross-section preparation and structural analysis using FIB/TEM", a next-generation cryo-microscope and a key performance indicator for its maintenance-contract ratio ([JEOL FY2025 briefing, May 2026](https://www.jeol.com/assets/pdf/ir/financial_results/financial_results_briefing_2026_dab6Ky.pdf)). It has done this much: PyJEM Python control is published but distributed through sales representatives ([PyJEM](https://github.com/PyJEM/PyJEM)), and two new microscopes launched with unverified AI content. Its semiconductor electron-microscope business is about ¥6.4bn, and fab evaluations are still "progressing steadily" ([JEOL Q1 briefing, 14 Aug 2026](https://www.jeol.com/assets/pdf/ir/financial_results/financial_results_briefing_2026_1q_en_dibwU8.pdf)). Free cash flow was about zero in FY3/26, after heavy capex and ¥12.8bn of buybacks, mostly a self-tender that bought out Nikon's stake. What must go right: JEOL must win fab TEM and FIB qualifications against Thermo Fisher, mask-writer shipments must recover, and cash conversion must improve.

**Rigaku (15=): partly coherent.** On T1 Rigaku relies on classical automation. SmartLab offers automatic optics alignment and an "intelligent User Guidance expert system", with no API or SDK ([Rigaku SmartLab](https://rigaku.com/products/x-ray-diffraction-and-scattering/xrd/smartlab)), and GitHub shows only community file parsers. Its T4 path runs through Onto, which bought 27% of the company from a Carlyle affiliate for $719.5m on 10 Aug 2026 ([Onto 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/704532/000119312526453859/onto-20260930.htm)). The two are building a CD-SAXS-OCD hybrid engine aimed at memory lines from 1Q FY12/27 ([Rigaku Q3 FY12/26](https://finance-frontend-pc-dist.west.edge.storage-yahoo.jp/disclosure/20261111/20261111573406.pdf)). What must go right: Rigaku must deliver a record fourth quarter, the alliance with Onto must produce products, and it must open interfaces before third parties wrap its graphical interface.

**Anritsu (15=): partly coherent.** Anritsu has a single T1 data point: at CEATEC 2026 it showed an "AI-enabled vector network analyzer that derives optimal measurement methods" (company claim) ([Anritsu 1H FY3/27](https://finance-frontend-pc-dist.west.edge.storage-yahoo.jp/disclosure/20261029/20261029559469.pdf)). No agent interface was found, though the search was incomplete, and it discloses no software or recurring share. What must go right: data-centre test demand must outlast FY3/27, the 6G cycle expected from around 2028 must arrive, and Anritsu needs an agent interface to match Keysight and Rohde & Schwarz.

## 6. Where the evidence contradicts the background

**The dated evidence is real but smaller than the background implies.** Every item resolves to a genuine source. Most are proofs of concept, human-gated, single-step or classical optimisation rather than agents.

| Background item | What the evidence shows on 12 Nov 2026 |
|---|---|
| Stanford/SLAC beamline agent (Nature Machine Intelligence, July 2026) | Peer-reviewed and published online 1 Jul 2026. A human relayed every command to meet facility safety rules. There were few real-beamline trials, which the authors say "should not be interpreted as a statistically established capability". No time saving was measured, and lattice precision "does not yet match the precision typically required". The agent used Claude Opus 4, off the shelf, through an MCP wrapper around SPEC; no instrument vendor was involved ([Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01261-5); [full text](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3818105)) |
| IQM and NVIDIA agent calibrating a 20-qubit chip | A company blog post of 21 Oct 2026 with no timing, no fidelities and no human comparison ([IQM](https://iqm.tech/blog/what-happened-when-we-let-ai-calibrate-a-quantum-chip-using-nvidia-ising/)). The benchmark behind the model was built by NVIDIA and its collaborators, and 186 of its 243 charts are simulated ([arXiv 2604.25884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25884)) |
| Language agent brings up a 112-qubit processor (arXiv 2606.22376) | Single-qubit steps only, with valid T1 fits on 108 of 112 qubits. The setup was a Claude Code harness driving a fine-tuned open Qwen model. The 18–24 h expert baseline is an estimate; a measured 16-qubit subset was 6.3x faster. The agent passed its quality gates yet left the median T2R at 7.9 µs against 14.4 µs for the expert. Not peer reviewed ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22376)) |
| Google AI adjusting >1,000 parameters during error correction | Confirmed and peer-reviewed (Nature). It is reinforcement learning, not a language agent; it achieved ~20% lower logical error than expert tuning on Google's proprietary stack ([arXiv 2511.08493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08493); [The Quantum Insider, 10 Jul 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/10/google-study-shows-quantum-computer-can-learn-from-its-own-errors-while-it-computes/)) |
| Automated aberration tuning, January 2026 | Bayesian optimisation, not an agent. One sample on one instrument, with no comparison against the vendor's own auto-tuning ([arXiv 2601.18972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18972)) |
| Planned agent framework on a Thermo Fisher microscope | Planned, with no results. The analyser layer had to be driven by reading the screen ([M&M 2026](https://academic.oup.com/mam/article/32/Supplement_1/ozag053.977/8742936)) |
| Automated discovery of accelerator commissioning procedures (arXiv 2608.07138) | The title is "Autonomous discovery of accelerator commissioning algorithms". It is simulation only and covers one step (RF beam capture). Injections to capture fell from 207.5 to 20.3, against a baseline the author calls "not the best performance achievable by a human". The judge was gamed three ways: "Agent activity is therefore not evidence of useful search" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07138)) |
| Table-top EUV microscope resolving 43.5 nm half-pitch | Confirmed (Light: Advanced Manufacturing, online 9 Sep 2026). Its "self-calibration" is model-based phase retrieval, not AI or an agent. A scan takes ~15 minutes, and the work is academic CAS research with no industry partner ([Light: AM](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.112)) |

**The priors need revising.**
- **"Little attention".** This is wrong for autonomous labs broadly. Lila Sciences has raised $550m (secondary), Periodic Labs raised a $300m seed round (secondary), the US Genesis Mission issued a $293m funding call ([DOE Office of Science, 27 Mar 2026](https://science.osti.gov/-/media/About/pdf/scac/meetings/2026/DOE-Genesis-Mission-Launched---2026-03-27.pdf)) and selected 278 projects ([DOE, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.energy.gov/articles/secretary-energy-chris-wright-announces-first-genesis-mission-projects-selected-accelerate)), and Keysight, Rohde & Schwarz, Waters and Anthropic all launched products. It is closer to right for agent-run tuning of physics instruments, which remains national-laboratory work.
- **Prior (i), 65%.** This depends on the definition. Read loosely, it is nearly met already: Keysight ships agent-invoked SOLT calibration with engineer approval, and Rohde & Schwarz ships agent control of oscilloscopes. Read strictly, as autonomous alignment of a complex instrument sold as a standard, included feature, nothing qualifies by November 2026 and no maker has a dated plan. Thermo Fisher's "fully autonomous instruments" in H2 2026 remains a secondary quote with no launch. My judgment is that the strict reading deserves well under 65% and the loose reading well over it.
- **Spread to fab fleet matching.** This mostly does not hold. Matching is already automated with standard wafers, statistical process control and machine learning. LLMs appear only as assistants: the clearest SPIE 2026 example is a retrieval-based recipe helper for beginner operators ([SPIE 13981](https://spie.org/Publications/Proceedings/Volume/13981)). The finest matching needs fab device data that fabs hold. SK hynix uses virtual metrology from Gauss Labs, an SK-affiliated company, in production (company claim) ([Gauss Labs](https://www.gausslabs.ai/vm)).
- **Closed interfaces and hardware damage as the main obstacles.** This is only partly right.
  - Interfaces are already open in electronic test, synchrotrons, research accelerators and quantum control. They are closed or paid in electron microscopes, analysers, laboratory X-ray and fab tools.
  - Damage is handled by interlocks below the agent; LBNL's Lightfall puts protection with "the device controllers ... and the independent hardware safety systems" ([arXiv 2606.06711](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06711)).
  - The binding constraints the sources name are elsewhere. One is trusted unattended operation: Lightfall says "fault diagnosis under ambiguous conditions" remains "beyond reliable agent performance today". Others are validation and audit (Annex 22 is not in force), facility safety policy, machine time, access to fab data, certification (the EU Machinery Regulation from 20 Jan 2027, secondary) and cybersecurity.
- **The judge is "fast, faithful and final".** This often fails in practice. Opposite QEC errors can produce identical syndrome statistics ([arXiv 2609.19090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19090)). In a Genentech pilot of Anthropic's standard, Claude misread bubble-caused errors as software faults and produced more foam until humans intervened (secondary report) ([R&D World, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.rdworldonline.com/anthropic-wants-claude-to-run-life-sciences-rd-now-it-is-wiring-ai-agents-into-the-lab/)).
- **Agents vs classical automation.** Much "expert set-up" is already automated by classical means: NMR auto-tuning since 1985 (company claim), MS autotune, alignment-free X-ray optics, Bayesian beam optimisation at SACLA since 2020, and Varian's validated daily self-check. Where agents meet specialised optimisers, they lose or tie. At DESY, LLMs were "still lagging behind other specialised methods" ([DESY](https://desy.de/e18959/e18960/e19156/index_eng.html)), and CERN replaced a reinforcement-learning controller with PID for maintainability ([RL4AA'26](https://indico.ph.liv.ac.uk/event/2025/contributions/10596/)).
- **The background's value logic holds.** Judge-owners keep their position. But value did not reach a new software layer; it went to makers and to users who own the stack.

## Conclusion

The evidence moves the question from "can an agent tune an instrument?" to "who writes the dictionary the agent must use?" Agents are being given away. Rohde & Schwarz's server is free, Keysight states no price, NVIDIA's calibration models are open-weight, and national labs publish their agent frameworks under open licences. What is not given away is the vendor-certified list of safe commands, the calibration routine with accredited traceability, the validated system of record, and the fleet data behind matching. That is why the companies with judges, dictionaries and service books rank highest. It is also why the theme does not yet drive revenue anywhere: buying "self-tuning instruments" today mostly means buying the chip-capex cycle (KLA, Lasertec, Nova) or the pharma replacement cycle (Waters, Agilent, Thermo Fisher).

Four checkpoints in the next fifteen months will show whether that holds:
- **10 Jan 2027:** the Affiliates Rule decision, which affects the China-heavy metrology names.
- **20 Jan 2027:** the EU Machinery Regulation takes effect, which may require certification of AI-based safety functions.
- **2027:** the date by which large pharma buyers want instruments with native open standards and one-time API fees, if procurement teams enforce their letter.
- **Through 2027:** whether Keysight and Waters turn agent access into priced software or keep it as hardware pull-through, and whether Thermo Fisher's promised autonomous instruments appear as a named product.

If buyers' procurement rules and lab-built stacks win, value shifts toward users, and makers' paid scripting comes under pressure. If they do not, the judge-owners consolidate.

## 1. Position: what each sells into the data-centre power chain, how scarce, and whether demand outlasts AI

### Takeaway
The scarce items are large power transformers/GSUs, HV switchgear and HVDC — dominated by Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy and GE Vernova (plus Korean/Chinese makers outside this scope); ABB, Schneider, Eaton and Siemens Smart Infrastructure sit one step downstream (MV/LV distribution, where data centres are a larger and faster-growing share of orders but lead times are 1–2 years, not 3+). Utilities, HVDC interconnectors and replacement still drive most transmission-level orders, so demand is not AI-only — but the 2026 order surges at ABB, Schneider, Eaton and Siemens SI are explicitly data-centre-led.

## 2. Lead: independent evidence on market share, lead times, prices, framework agreements, SF6-free

### Takeaway
Independent data (Wood Mackenzie) show US large-transformer lead times have not shortened in 2026 — the most recent reported readings are longer (GSU and substation units above 160 weeks) even as the estimated US power-transformer deficit narrowed from ~30% (2025) to ~15% (2026). Market-share claims for Hitachi Energy are qualitative ("number one") and I found no independent percentage.

### Cited Findings
- Wood Mackenzie Q2 2025 survey: power transformers 128 weeks, GSUs 144 weeks; up from ~50 weeks in 2021 and ~120 weeks average in 2024. — [POWER, Jan 2026](https://www.powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/); [Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/news/opinion/supply-shortages-and-an-inflexible-market-give-rise-to-high-power-transformer-lead-times/)
- Later readings (secondary reports of Wood Mackenzie data): GSU lead times exceeded 160 weeks by Q1 2026 vs 143 weeks in 2024 (Reuters, 9 Jul 2026); substation transformers ~140 weeks 2023, ~150 in 2025, over 160 in 2026 (Data Center Knowledge, 12 May 2026); Wood Mackenzie analyst expects transformer costs to rise ~4–10% over the next year. I did not open the Reuters or Wood Mackenzie originals. — [explainx summary](https://explainx.ai/blog/transformer-lead-times-160-weeks-ai-data-centers-grid-equipment-shortage-2026)
- High-capacity transformer lead times "as long as four years" (PwC analysts); prices up roughly 80% over five years. — [pv magazine USA, 11 May 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/)
- Price change since 2019: power transformers +77%; distribution transformers +78–95%; distribution-transformer lead times fell to ~30 weeks from >100 weeks at the 2023 peak; switchgear averages 44 weeks, data-centre switchboards 52 weeks+. — [Giga Energy blog citing Wood Mackenzie](https://www.gigaenergy.com/blog/lead-time-delays-for-data-centers)
- Wood Mackenzie (31 Aug 2026): 2026 US market shortage 15% for power transformers and 8% for substations; a US executive order banning Chinese bulk-power equipment "set to worsen" it; bulk-power imports exceeded $22bn since 2025, China almost all of the affected imports; unclear whether the bulk-power definition covers data-centre loads (DOE to interpret within 120 days). — [Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/us-executive-order-banning-chinese-bulk-power-equipment-set-to-worsen-critical-transformer-shortage/)
- Wood Mackenzie 2025 balance: US supply deficits of 30% (power) and 10% (distribution) transformers. — [Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/power-transformers-and-distribution-transformers-will-face-supply-deficits-of-30-and-10-in-2025/)
- Hitachi Energy: Varennes (Quebec) is "the only [plant] in North America that produces transformers of this magnitude" (company claim). — [Hitachi, 30 Sep 2025](https://www.hitachi.com/en/press/articles/2025/09/0930c/)
- GE Vernova's Trench Group target (HV bushings, instrument transformers, coils — components that are themselves bottlenecks) comes with "approximately $5 billion in backlog and framework agreements, some of which reach into the next decade". — [GE Vernova, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.gevernova.com/news/articles/ge-vernova-releases-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-announces-agreement-acquire)
- NKT: €2.5bn TenneT framework not yet in backlog (second-hand figure); Q1 2026 order intake >€4.2bn took HV backlog to €13.5bn. — [Inderes/NKT Q1 2026](https://www.inderes.dk/releases/nkt-as-q1-2026-interim-report-record-high-order-intake-and-ebitda-of-eur-97m)
- SF6-free: Eaton is buying COL Group for "SF₆-free switchgear, grid automation technologies and modular power systems" in Europe; Meidensha reports its "transition to SF6 gas-free products progressed against a backdrop of new and expanded data center construction". — [Eaton Q3 2026 release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261102754211/en/Eaton-Reports-Record-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-with-Strong-Organic-Growth-Accelerating-Record-Orders-and-Backlog-Raises-Organic-Growth-Guidance); [Meidensha Q1 FY2026 filing](https://finance-frontend-pc-dist.west.edge.storage-yahoo.jp/disclosure/20260731/20260730504002.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Are lead times shortening?" No for large power/GSU units (128–144 weeks in mid-2025 → 160+ in 2026); yes for distribution transformers (100+ → ~30 weeks). The series are for different products and partly second-hand, so the direction is more reliable than the exact figures.
- A narrowing deficit (30% → 15%) alongside longer quoted lead times suggests added capacity is absorbing demand growth but queues are still lengthening for the largest units.
- The August 2026 US executive order against Chinese bulk-power equipment is a tailwind for the Western/Japanese/Korean incumbents' US pricing power.

### Gaps
- No independent global or US market-share percentages for transformers, HV switchgear or HVDC were found in this session (e.g. Hitachi Energy HVDC share). Named hyperscaler framework agreements for these companies were not found; SF6-free portfolio comparisons for Hitachi Energy (EconiQ), Siemens Energy (Blue), GE Vernova (g3), Schneider (AirSeT), ABB were not re-verified and are omitted.
- The Wood Mackenzie original for the 2026 lead-time series was not read.

## 5. Order-book firmness: coverage, reservations, cancellations

### Takeaway
Transmission-level backlogs cover roughly 3 years or more and are increasingly backed by prepaid capacity reservations (Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy); distribution-level backlogs (ABB, Eaton, Siemens SI) cover under a year to about a year, so their 2027–28 revenue depends on orders not yet booked. I found no reported 2026 cancellations at these companies, but did not read full transcripts.

## 10. Resilience across scenarios and position in the cycle

### Takeaway
On the evidence gathered, a 2028 financing-led pause would hurt order intake most at the distribution-level suppliers with short backlogs and data-centre-led growth (ABB Electrification, Eaton, Schneider, Siemens SI, Hammond, nVent), and least at the transmission-level suppliers with 3–5 year, partly prepaid backlogs (Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy GT, Prysmian Transmission, GE Vernova Electrification) — but those are also the ones adding the most fixed capacity for 2027–28.

### Cited Findings
- Backlog cover and reservation evidence: see section 5 (Hitachi Energy 3–5 years' visibility; ABB ~40% of backlog converts in the next year). — [Quartr](https://quartr.com/events/hitachi-ltd-6501-hitachi-energy-investor-day-2026_3P84Osoi); [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/abb-q3-fy-2026-earnings-show-record-orders-outpacing-revenue-on-data-centers/)

### Inferences
- Steady AI capex growth: all main companies meet targets; the binding constraint is capacity and labour, so the most capacity-adding (Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, Eaton) grow fastest; ABB grows slowest by choice.
- Financing-led pause around 2028: transmission backlogs carry revenue through roughly 2029–30, but new orders, prepayments and pricing would weaken just as new plants open; record margins and backlog margins (+12pp at Siemens Energy GT since FY22) are the main thing at risk, not volumes. Distribution-level names see revenue impact within about a year.
- Faster acceleration: those with unsold or flexible capacity and behind-the-meter offerings gain most (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Powell); pre-sold capacity at Hitachi Energy limits upside until new plants open in 2027–28.
- Cycle position: this is the largest T&D up-cycle on the measures found (demand doubling to tripling since 2019, ~80% price rises, 3-year-plus lead times). Late-stage features are present — record margins, a bunched capacity wave, large acquisitions at full prices (Trench at ~13x 2028 EBITDA; PTC at $22.6bn), and first YoY order declines in two bellwether grid segments — while end-demand indicators (lead times, deficits) have not yet turned.

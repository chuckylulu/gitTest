## 1. Position: products, export/US share, data-centre orders, local-market dynamics

### Takeaway
The three Korean majors are the only Asian group with large, proven, direct exposure to the US grid and to hyperscalers (HD Hyundai Electric and Hyosung in 345-765 kV transformers; LS Electric in data-centre switchboards), with Taiwan's Fortune Electric a smaller pure-play; Chinese makers are strong at home and in non-US export markets but mostly reach US data centres only through dry-type/medium-voltage products (Jinpan, Igor/Eaglerise), and Indian listed makers are almost entirely a domestic transmission-capex story with small data-centre exposure. Ranking by relevance to the "AI data-centre grid connection": HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung Heavy, LS Electric, Fortune Electric, Jinpan, Sieyuan; then the Indian MNC subsidiaries (quality businesses, different driver); minor: Iljin, Sanil, Cheryong, Taihan, LS C&S, Shihlin, CHEM, Allis, Tatung, China XD, Pinggao, XJ, NARI, TBEA (conglomerate), Huaming (component), Voltamp, Bharat Bijlee, TARIL, Schneider Electric Infrastructure, ABB India.

### Inferences
- Backlog cover (backlog / trailing-12-month revenue, my arithmetic): HD Hyundai Electric about 2.6 years (USD 8.84bn at roughly KRW 1,325/USD = about KRW 11.7tn against TTM revenue KRW 4.52tn); Hyosung heavy-industries about 3 years or more (KRW 18.9tn against a heavy-industries revenue base that is smaller than the consolidated KRW 6.41tn TTM, which includes construction); LS Electric about 1.2 years (KRW 7.04tn / KRW 5.69tn) — shorter-cycle switchboards; Hitachi Energy India about 3.2 years (INR 33,335 crore against roughly INR 10,300 crore TTM).
- Direct hyperscaler orders are now disclosed at all three Korean majors (HD KRW 1.12tn framework; Hyosung KRW 386.5bn; LS Electric several of KRW 50-320bn). The HD contract is a framework with staged call-offs, i.e. softer than a firm purchase order.
- Among Chinese names, only Jinpan has data centres as a dominant share of revenue (39%) and backlog (49%); Sieyuan's North American exposure is small and management itself is cautious. TBEA is a conglomerate (coal, polysilicon, aluminium, T&D), so its equity is a weak proxy for grid equipment.
- Indian names are driven by domestic transmission and HVDC capex; the data-centre angle is company aspiration (Hitachi's "30%") rather than disclosed order share.
- Minor/why: Iljin, Sanil, Cheryong (small scale, distribution transformers, US-concentrated); LS C&S/Taihan (cable, not transformers/switchgear; LS C&S is unlisted inside LS Corp); Shihlin/CHEM/Allis (Taipower "resilient grid" domestic story; CHEM exports mainly to Japan); Tatung (loss-making conglomerate); Pinggao/XJ/NARI (State Grid subsidiaries, negligible US access); Huaming (single component); Voltamp/Bharat Bijlee/TARIL (small domestic); Schneider Electric Infrastructure (thin margins); ABB India (broad electrification/automation, not grid-specific).

## 5. Cross-border restrictions and tariffs

### Takeaway

## 9. Resilience across three scenarios (steady AI capex growth; financing-led pause around 2028; faster acceleration)

### Takeaway
On the evidence, the EHV transformer makers with US plants and multi-year utility backlogs (Hyosung, HD) are the most resilient to a 2028 pause on the operating side; the switchboard/dry-type suppliers tied to data-centre construction schedules (LS Electric, Jinpan, HD's new distribution business) and the highest-multiple equities (Indian names, LS Electric, Fortune) are the least; domestic-grid names (NARI, Pinggao, Shihlin, CHEM, Indian MNC subsidiaries) are largely insulated from AI capex but have their own policy risks.

### Inferences
- Steady growth: all majors grow into 2028 on existing backlog; margin drift (FX, mix, ramp costs, tariffs) is the main risk; HD and Hyosung look inexpensive on current multiples, LS Electric and Indian names need the scenario to hold.

## 1. Position: who owns scarce powered land / interconnection / generation, evidence of the premium, durability, regulatory and local-opposition risk

### Takeaway
The scarce asset is energised (or firmly dated) power, and it is being paid for three ways: PJM capacity at the cap three auctions running, 15-20 year hyperscaler contracts on existing nuclear and gas fleets (Constellation, Vistra, Talen), and powered-shell leases at about $1.8-2.4m per MW-year. The premium is real but politically exposed: Texas (governor-elect moratorium pledge, earlier ERCOT connection halt), PJM price collars, FERC co-location rules, and local opposition (about $130bn of projects cancelled in Q3 2026 alone) now determine who can monetise it.

### Inferences
- Ranking of positional strength (quality only): (1) owners of existing, licensed, already-interconnected baseload with signed 15-20 year investment-grade offtake — Constellation, Talen, Vistra; (2) powered-shell owners with energised sites and hyperscaler-backed leases (TeraWulf, Cipher, Hut 8, Core Scientific, Applied Digital) — real scarcity, but levered and construction-stage; (3) gas-infrastructure owners selling behind-the-meter power (Williams) — speed advantage, turbine-slot dependent; (4) asset managers (Brookfield) — breadth and capital, less pure exposure.
- Durability beyond 2030: the PJM collar caps upside through 2029/30 delivery while uncapped simulations sit 70% higher, so underlying tightness persists into the 2030s; but the premium is a regulated/political variable, not purely a market one.
- Vistra's Texas exposure (uncontracted Comanche Peak MW, ERCOT gas fleet) is now the clearest single-jurisdiction policy risk among the large generators; Constellation and Talen are more PJM-weighted, where the risk is price caps and co-location rules rather than moratoria.
- Minor for this theme (and why): regulated utilities NextEra (ex-Duane Arnold), Entergy, Dominion, AEP, Southern, Duke earn regulated returns on data-centre load growth rather than scarcity rents; Kinder Morgan/Energy Transfer are gas-transport beneficiaries one step removed; Equinix/Digital Realty are landlords whose premium is in buildings and interconnection density more than in owned power (they held up in the October sell-off: -9% and -11% off highs vs -62% for powered-shell names); fusion (Helion, CFS) has no deliverable MW before 2032; Rolls-Royce SMR and EDF/Nuward are mid-2030s.
- Non-US powered-site owners cannot be ranked with confidence from this session's evidence (see Gaps); the two dated datapoints (NextDC, Nscale) both show grid-connection timing, not demand, as the failure mode.

## 2. Lead: independent evidence of contracts, MW energised vs contracted, restart/uprate progress, NRC milestones, Fervo delivery

### Takeaway
Restarts are slipping or holding rather than accelerating: Crane remains "second half of 2027", Palisades has loaded fuel but has no restart date after a fuel-handling incident, Duane Arnold is targeted by Q1 2029. Advanced reactors made real licensing progress in 2026 (TerraPower and TVA BWRX-300 construction permits, X-energy safety review complete, Kairos Hermes 2 under construction), and Fervo delivered the first utility-scale enhanced geothermal block (33 MW) — but delivered new MW remain tiny against contracted GW.

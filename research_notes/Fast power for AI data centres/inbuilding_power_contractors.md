## 1. Architecture shift: AC UPS + 48/54 V rack power to 800 VDC with solid-state transformers — status, timing, who wins and who loses

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026 the 800 VDC shift is real at the rack edge (sidecar / in-row power racks converting 480 VAC to +/-400 or 800 VDC, first small shipments H2 2026) but still "engineering ambition and speculation" upstream; SSTs are at pilot volumes (Eaton 10 pilots; Delta targeting 100 units by end-2026). Independent evidence (Dell'Oro, Vertiv's own guide) does not support "UPS is eliminated" before roughly 2028; the nearer-term effect is content moving out of the IT rack into sidecars (favouring power-supply makers Delta, Lite-On, and facility vendors who own the sidecar) while protection, DC busway and battery placement are unresolved.

### Cited Findings

Open questions: solid-state vs thermal-magnetic breakers, DC PDU design, battery/capacitance location, arc flash, fault-current control. — [Dell'Oro](https://www.delloro.com/inside-ocp-global-summit-2026-cold-plates-800-vdc-sidecars-and-a-buildout-at-full-steam/)

### Inferences
- Likely winners in the 2026-27 "sidecar" phase: rack/in-row AC-DC power makers with NVIDIA/hyperscaler qualification (Delta first, then Lite-On; AcBel, Flex/Axiom, Megmeet as followers) because the sidecar is essentially a scaled-up power shelf plus BBU; and power-semiconductor suppliers (SiC/GaN content rises with voltage).
- Facility incumbents (Vertiv, Schneider, Eaton, ABB, Siemens) are not obviously displaced before 2028: the first-mover upstream path is a transformer + rectifier "DC UPS", which they already make, and protection (solid-state breakers), DC busway and switchgear are their territory. The threat is specific: the double-conversion AC UPS, AC PDUs/RPPs and AC busway are the product lines that a full MV-to-800 VDC SST architecture would shrink; none of the four disclose that mix precisely.
- The gap that the next hardware step could close is the low-frequency MV/LV transformer + AC UPS chain. Whoever fills today's transformer shortage only with conventional units (not covered here) is more exposed than switchgear/protection owners. SST start-ups (Heron, DG Matrix, Amperesand) and Delta/SolarEdge/Sungrow are attacking that layer, but volumes are pilot-scale and standards (OCP SST v0.3) are drafts.
- Two competing standards (+/-400 V Mt Diablo from Microsoft/Meta/Google vs NVIDIA's monopolar 800 V) appear to be converging but are not yet unified; this delays volume and favours vendors able to ship both (Delta explicitly does both).

- Amperesand: nothing retrieved on 2026 funding or orders.

## 9. Resilience across scenarios (steady growth; financing-led pause around 2028; faster acceleration) and prior-downturn history

### Takeaway
Hard historical evidence is thin in what I retrieved: the one clear datapoint is Emerson (Vertiv's former parent) seeing sales fall more than 20% in the second half of fiscal 2001 in the electronics/telecom businesses hit by the telecom/computing bust. Structurally, net-cash, services-bearing and diversified names (Schneider, Eaton, EMCOR with Building Services, Quanta via utilities) should hold up better in a 2028 pause than concentrated ones (Vertiv, Delta's AI half, Comfort Systems at 59% technology, Sterling at 83% E-Infrastructure); in faster acceleration the capacity-adders and labour owners gain most.

### Cited Findings
- Emerson FY2001 annual report: sales declined over 20% in the second half of fiscal 2001 as fibre-optic and embedded power products were first hit by the downturn in computing and communications; fiscal Q1 2002 net earnings fell 28.5%. Network Power is not broken out separately in the snippets. — [Emerson FY2001 annual report](https://ir.emerson.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000032604-01-500032/ex13.txt); [Control Engineering](https://www.controleng.com/emersons-fiscal-1q02-earnings-down-28-5-process-control-sales-up-3/)

### Inferences
- Steady growth: all main names grow; the architecture shift redistributes content toward Delta/Lite-On (rack and sidecar) and SiC/GaN suppliers, with Vertiv/Schneider/Eaton keeping facility share if their 800 VDC products ship on NVIDIA's 2027 timetable.
- Financing-led pause around 2028: orders, not backlog, fall first. Most exposed: (a) companies with new fixed capacity landing in 2027 and hyperscaler-heavy books (Vertiv, Delta AI power, FIX modular, STRL, Korean transformer makers); (b) loss-making pure plays needing 2027-28 ramps (Navitas, SST start-ups). Least exposed: Schneider, Eaton (diversified, though leveraged), Quanta (utility-driven), EMCOR (services + other sectors), Kandenko/SPIE (domestic diversified). Contractors' advance-billing cash flows reverse, but their cost base is variable.
- Faster acceleration: labour and factory capacity are the bottlenecks, so owners of trained electricians and prefab factories (FIX, EME, IESC, Eaton/Fibrebond, Flex/Axiom) and already-qualified 800 V suppliers (Delta) capture pricing; latecomers cannot add electricians quickly (4-5 year training).
- A technology transition during a pause would be the worst combination for legacy AC UPS/PDU lines: customers could skip a generation and restart on 800 VDC.

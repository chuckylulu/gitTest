## 1. Position: bridge or durable beyond 2030? (fuel cells vs batteries)

### Takeaway
Fuel cells (in practice: Bloom SOFC) are winning because of speed and air permitting while grid interconnection takes 3-6 years and turbines are sold out; they remain gas-fed, so durability beyond 2030 depends on gas price, carbon-capture economics and whether on-site power stays a permanent architecture rather than a bridge. Batteries are a commodity in oversupply at the cell level, but in data centres they are acquiring a durable systems role (smoothing GPU load swings, firming gas turbines, enabling flexible interconnection, UPS/BBU) that favours integrators and US-compliant cell makers over generic cell supply.

### Cited Findings

Batteries / BESS in data centres
- Data centres face ~4-year average waits for grid power; behind-the-meter gas turbines do not ramp fast enough for compute load, and batteries fill the gap and reduce turbine wear. BNEF: 4.9 GW of storage announcements co-located with on-site fossil generation at data centres (~32% of announced on-site data-centre battery capacity); storage projected to support 9.8 GWh of gas generation at data centres through 2030. Named projects: xAI Colossus Memphis (Tesla Megapacks beside turbines in a 1.2 GW off-grid plant), Pacifico GW Ranch (1.8 GW storage with 7.65 GW gas), Williams (Tesla batteries with gas), NIPSCO (400 MW storage with 2x1.3 GW gas for Amazon), DTE (six storage systems for a 1.4 GW Oracle site). Fluence says gas-paired projects are its fastest-growing pipeline segment. — [Fortune, 24 Apr 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/04/24/data-centers-ai-batteries-natural-gas-power/)
- Technical caveat: sub-second smoothing accelerates battery ageing; a hierarchy of GPU-level, rack-level (BBU/supercapacitor) and site-level BESS is proposed. — [arXiv 2603.00415](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00415v1)
- Cummins (11 Nov 2026) says it is working on a "multi-gigawatt hour data center campus project for a hyperscaler", its largest BESS deployment. — [Cummins](https://www.cummins.com/en-na/news/2026/11/11/how-battery-energy-storage-can-help-ai-data-centers-prepare-large-load-requirements)
- Commodity context: InfoLink counted 303.4 GWh of global ESS shipments in H1 2026 (+83% y/y) and raised its 2026 forecast to 662 GWh. — [PVTIME/InfoLink](https://www.pvtime.org/?p=26081). China ESS battery sales were 73.5 GWh in September 2026 alone (+104% y/y). — [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/20/catl-q3-2026-earnings/)
- Margin pressure despite volume: Sungrow's storage gross margin fell from ~34% in Q2 to ~21% in Q3 2026; CATL's Q3 net margin fell to 12.86%, lowest since Q1 2024, with analysts citing higher ESS mix. — [Gelonghui](https://m.gelonghui.com/p/7238981); [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/10/20/catl-q3-2026-earnings/); [SCMP](https://scmp.com/business/china-business/article/3371545/chinese-battery-giant-catl-hit-profit-slowdown-ev-headwind-fears-grow)

### Inferences
- Fuel cells: the 2026-2030 demand is primarily a time-to-power bridge product, but 15-20-year offtakes (AEP/Google 20-year PPA) and "primary power" contracts mean installed units will run well past 2030; what is not assured is the order rate once turbine and grid lead times normalise. Durability rests on (a) permitting/speed advantage persisting, (b) CCS or 800 VDC integration becoming real products, (c) SOFC cost falling the 20-25% Rystad expects.
- Implied all-in equipment price from AEP ($2.65bn for 700-900 MW) is roughly $2,900-3,800/kW (my calculation; scope may include more than stacks). This is not "on par" with frame turbines on a $/kW basis unless overbuild, speed and fuel savings are counted.
- Batteries: the data-centre role (load smoothing, gas firming, flexible interconnection) is durable because it is tied to the physics of GPU loads, not to grid lead times; but value accrues to integration, software, grid-forming PCS and compliant supply, while cells remain oversupplied globally.

### Gaps
- No verified $/kW list price or LCOE for Bloom; no independent confirmation of an 800 VDC-native fuel-cell product in service.
- No BNEF/WoodMac figure for fuel cells' share of on-site data-centre power was found.
- "BESS replacing diesel gensets" at hyperscale: no direct source found; evidence is for augmentation, not replacement.

Bloom is the only fuel-cell maker with multi-GW named data-centre contracts and >$1bn/quarter product revenue; every other fuel-cell name is at tens-to-hundreds of MW. ## 3. Revenue model and financials (latest quarter) — QUALITY FACTS ONLY

### Takeaway
Bloom has become a genuinely profitable hardware business (non-GAAP operating margin 23%, ROIC ~27%) but with thin service margins, cash conversion lagging revenue and extreme customer/related-party concentration. ## 6. Cross-border restrictions (tariffs, FEOC/prohibited foreign entity, Section 301, China export controls)

### Takeaway
US rules are progressively closing the US data-centre market to Chinese cells and systems (tax-credit disqualification plus rising tariffs), which is the direct cause of the Korean makers' ESS order surge and of Tesla/Fluence localisation; China's own export controls on LFP cathode technology and battery materials are the counter-risk to anyone building non-Chinese LFP supply. The status of China's suspended October-2025 controls after their 10 Nov 2026 expiry is unresolved.

### Cited Findings
- The 2025 budget law kept storage tax credits through 2033 but disqualifies projects receiving "material assistance" from prohibited foreign entities; non-PFE content thresholds start at 55% (one source says 60% for 2026) for projects beginning construction in 2026, rising to 75% after 2029 (sources differ; check statute/IRS guidance). — [Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/ev-slowdown-creates-potential-lifeline-for-us-energy-storage-amid-feoc-tariffs/); [Carina Energy guide](https://carina.energy/feoc-compliance-guide-bess/)
- A non-Chinese cell plant can become a prohibited foreign entity if it pays royalties under a Chinese technology licence signed/amended after the cut-off. — [China Prospect](https://chinaprospect.com/battery-storage-tax-credit-foreign-entity-rules/)
- DOE FEOC guidance: covers PRC-headquartered firms, any company producing a covered battery component in China, and firms ≥25% PRC-government controlled. — [US DOE](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/manufacturing/foreign-entity-concern-interpretive-guidance)

- Treasury guidance delays have slowed BESS procurement decisions (GridStor). — [Energy-Storage.news](https://www.energy-storage.news/gridstor-warns-of-industry-slowdown-as-us-treasury-guidance-delays-bess-decisions/)

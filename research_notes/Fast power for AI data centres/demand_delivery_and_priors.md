- Research done 12 Nov 2026 by web search plus page fetches. Where a figure was read from the primary page it is marked **[fetched]**; where it comes only from a search-result summary of the linked page it is marked **[snippet]** and should be re-checked before publication.

## 1. Delivered vs announced: on-site generation and data-centre capacity actually energised

### Takeaway
Operating behind-the-meter (BTM) generation at US data centres is still about 2 GW (roughly 2% of a proposed pipeline that has grown from ~48-56 GW in early 2026 to ~90-116 GW depending on the Cleanview snapshot); the earlier "~3 GW by end-2026" figure still stands as Cleanview's projection (2.8-3.2 GW), not a confirmed outturn. For total capacity, brokers report record deliveries and near-zero vacancy (JLL: 25 GW absorbed in North America in H1 2026; 66 GW under construction, 95% pre-committed), while Bernstein rates only ~34% of a 586 GW North American pipeline as credible; the "half of 2026 capacity delayed" claim (Sightline) is disputed by SemiAnalysis.

### Inferences
- The delivered/announced ratio for on-site generation (~2%) is far lower than for data-centre shells (JLL: 95% of capacity under construction is pre-committed). On-site generation is the most announcement-heavy part of the chain; the growth in the proposed figure during 2026 was almost entirely paper.
- Sightline and SemiAnalysis are measuring different populations (announced megaprojects vs. satellite-verified construction). Both can be right: late-stage hyperscaler builds are mostly on time; speculative announced campuses slip heavily. Bernstein's one-third "credible" share is consistent with that reading.
- Company evidence of slippage in Q3 2026 came from third-party (neocloud/colocation) deliveries to Microsoft, not from hyperscaler self-builds.

### Gaps
- No authoritative count of GW energised in calendar 2025 or 2026 year-to-date was found (JLL "absorption" is leasing, not energisation; CBRE covers only primary colocation markets). Synergy H1 2026 delivery figures not found.
- No November 2026 Cleanview update confirming whether end-2026 operating BTM capacity reached ~3 GW. The Earthjustice report "The AI Gas Rush: Risks of BYOP" (Oct 2026) exists but the PDF was too large to read: [Earthjustice PDF](https://earthjustice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/the-ai-gas-rush-risks-of-byop_full-report-with-cover.pdf).
- No global (non-US) tally of operating on-site generation at data centres was found. EIA has not published results from its data-centre pilot survey. LBNL, Grid Strategies and S&P figures specific to BTM data-centre generation were not found.

## 2. Verification of specific figures from the brief

### Takeaway
Most figures hold; three have moved. GE Vernova's gas backlog plus slot reservations is now 122 GW (from 83 GW), but quarterly growth slowed sharply; generator step-up transformer lead times lengthened to over 160 weeks (not shortening for large units); and the "~40% in moratorium states" figure now refers to states whose newly elected governors promised a pause, not states with bills pending.

### Cited Findings
- **IEA:** data-centre electricity consumption roughly doubles from 485 TWh (2025) to 950 TWh (2030), ~3% of global demand (prior IEA estimate 945 TWh); 15-27 GW of on-site gas could be supplying data centres globally by 2030; on-site gas requires overbuilding generation by 30-70% relative to demand; IEA adds that given the gas-turbine supply crunch "it is not clear that onsite generation necessarily promises a faster route to market"; 20-25 GW of battery storage could be installed at data centres by 2030 **[snippet]**. Confirmed, unchanged — [IEA, Key questions on energy and AI](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary); [PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/ai-data-centre-demand-to-more-than-double-by-2030/)
- **US interconnection waits (generation):** LBNL Queued Up 2026 (data to end-2025): ~8,200 active projects, 1,312 GW generation plus ~749 GW storage; median time from interconnection request to commercial operation over 5 years for projects built in 2025 (a secondary summary gives 61 months for 2025 vs 63 months in 2024) **[snippet]** — [LBNL Queued Up 2026](https://emp.lbl.gov/publications/queued-2026-edition-characteristics); [LBNL news release](https://emp.lbl.gov/news/backlog-power-plants-seeking-transmission-grid-connection-eased-somewhat-2025-amidst)
- **US interconnection waits (large load):** secondary sources put active data-centre projects in PJM and ERCOT at 3-4 years, with Northern Virginia approaching 7 years; ERCOT's large-load queue grew from 63 GW (end-2024) to ~226 GW (Nov 2025) and ~410 GW (Mar 2026; 551 applications, 88% data centres) against an all-time peak load of ~85.5 GW **[snippet; later figures from trackers, not ERCOT primary]**. The "4-7 years" range is supported at the upper end only for the most congested zones — [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/ercots-large-load-queue-has-nearly-quadrupled-in-a-single-year/); [BIC Magazine](https://www.bicmagazine.com/industry/powergen/texas-grid-sees-233-gw-data-center-requests-much-real/); [Ascend Analytics](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/large-load-interconnection-queues-data-center-grid-access)

- **GE Vernova gas backlog + slot reservations:** 83 GW (start of 2026) → 100 GW (Q1) → 116 GW (Q2) → 122 GW (Q3, end-Sept 2026); firm backlog rose from 53 to 64 GW while slot reservations fell from 63 to 58 GW; in Q3 it signed 11 GW (8 GW reservations, 3 GW orders), converted 13 GW of reservations to orders and shipped 5 GW; target "at least 130 GW by year-end 2026"; total company backlog $197bn, expected above $200bn in Q4 **[fetched, SEC exhibit]** — [GE Vernova Q3 2026 press release (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000162/gevpressrelease3q26.htm)

### Inferences
- GE Vernova's mix is improving (firm share of the 122 GW rose from 46% to 52% in one quarter) while headline growth slows; "normalisation" and "peak" cannot yet be told apart from one quarter.

### Gaps
- Siemens Energy's firm-vs-reserved GW split needs checking against the company's own FY26 presentation; sources disagree.

## 3. Order-book firmness, cancellations and signs of a financing-led pause

### Takeaway
The first large, documented cancellation of turbine reservations has now happened: SoftBank cancelled unusable reservations (mostly GE Vernova) and wrote off $3.7bn because the fees were not recoverable — which shows that reservation deposits are real and non-refundable, and that the supplier keeps the cash. Hyperscalers have not cut 2027 spending (Alphabet and Amazon say it rises), but credit is tightening for neoclouds (CoreWeave bond yields above 15%), Microsoft trimmed 2026 capex on delivery delays, and GE Vernova's order growth has slowed.

### Cited Findings
- **SoftBank (results for Jul-Sep 2026, reported 11-12 Nov 2026):** net loss ¥1.12tn (~$7.1bn), the largest single item a $3.7bn write-down on power equipment for US AI data centres; advance payments for power-generation equipment had reached $9.5bn by end-September, made "in anticipation of shortages"; when orders were cancelled the fees could not be recovered (chief accounting officer Kazuko Kimiwada, paraphrased via Reuters); SoftBank can claim rebates of up to $2.1bn on future purchases over ten years; CFO Goto denied Musk's "default" claim and said the company is doing "a thorough reassessment" of equipment purchasing **[fetched]** — [The Next Web, 12 Nov 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/softbank-loss-7bn-ai-data-centre-power-equipment-writedown)
- Per the WSJ (as relayed): SoftBank reserved more than 13 GW of gas turbines between late 2025 and early 2026, mostly from GE Vernova, and cancelled the reservations it could not use; one secondary account puts the cancelled amount at about 4 GW (range 3-6 GW), others say all remaining GE Vernova reservations — figures conflict. ### Inferences
- Firmness ranking supported by the evidence: (1) firm orders with shipped/progress-billed equipment; (2) slot reservations backed by 20-25% non-refundable cash — soft as to future revenue, but the supplier keeps the deposit and can resell the slot (GE Vernova is selling 2032 slots); (3) framework agreements and announced pipelines, which carry no cash. SoftBank's case shows category (2) transfers the loss to the buyer.
- The exposure for turbine makers in a pause is price and conversion rate on the reservation layer (58 GW at GE Vernova, ~39 GW at Siemens Energy), not refunds. GE Vernova already guides to lower per-kW pricing next quarter (attributed to mix).

## 4. Outside the US: is power the constraint, and is on-site gas a US-specific phenomenon?

### Takeaway
Grid connection is the binding constraint in Europe's main hubs, Tokyo and Johor, but not in China, where power is abundant nationally and the limit is chips and utilisation. On-site gas at gigawatt scale is overwhelmingly a US phenomenon so far, though Ireland now requires on-site or nearby generation for new connections and the UK has over 100 data-centre requests for gas connections. Coverage of Korea, Taiwan, India, the Gulf and Israel is thin in the English sources reached.

### Cited Findings
- **Europe overall:** Ember puts grid-connection waits in the main markets at 7-10 years, up to 13 years; in Q2 2026 only 37% of new European capacity went to the five main hubs (Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris, Dublin) **[snippet]** — [Ember, Grids for data centres in Europe](https://ember-energy.org/app/uploads/2025/06/Grids-for-data-centres-in-Europe.pdf); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/europe-data-centres-leaving-flap-d-grid-queues-land); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/power-availability-the-defining-constraint-in-emea-data-center-development-report/)

### Inferences
- On-site gas as primary supply at multi-hundred-MW scale is concentrated in the US (cheap pipeline gas, permissive air permitting in some states, long queues). Europe is adopting it in smaller units where regulators require self-supply (Ireland) or queues are longest (UK). The IEA's global 15-27 GW by 2030 against a 47 GW US-only pipeline implies the IEA expects most of the US pipeline not to be built on time.

## 6. Tests of the five priors

### Takeaway
Priors 2 (nuclear under 5 GW before 2032) and 5 (firm backlogs matter in a pause) are well supported by current evidence; prior 1 (power is the main limit through 2030) holds for the US, Europe and Japan but is contested by memory shortages and does not hold in China; prior 3 (1 MW racks in production by end-2030) is plausible but ahead of confirmed product; prior 4 (powered sites hold value better than chips) is supported by leasing data but challenged in late 2026 by rising GPU rental prices and by political risk to sites.

- For: SoftBank cancelled reservations and forfeited non-refundable fees (section 3); GE Vernova's reservation layer shrank from 63 to 58 GW while firm backlog rose to 64 GW; net additions slowed to 6 GW — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/softbank-loss-7bn-ai-data-centre-power-equipment-writedown); [SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000162/gevpressrelease3q26.htm); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-in-9b-deal-to-buy-high-voltage-manufacturer-as-gas-turbine-orde/873223/)
- Qualifying: the supplier kept the cash and reported "no negative or positive impact"; management "noted no slot exits" as of 28 Oct; slots remain scarce enough to be sold for 2032 — so a reservation backed by a deposit behaved more like a firm order than "soft" would imply — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/softbank-loss-7bn-ai-data-centre-power-equipment-writedown); [Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ge-vernova-in-9b-deal-to-buy-high-voltage-manufacturer-as-gas-turbine-orde/873223/)

### Inferences
- Prior 1 is best restated as: in the US, Europe and Japan, energised capacity (power plus the labour and equipment to connect it) is the main physical limit, with memory a parallel limit in 2026-27 and cost of capital the emerging limit for leveraged buyers.
- Prior 4 needs a condition: powered sites with creditworthy tenants in jurisdictions without pending pauses. Power equipment or land held speculatively is not the same asset.
- Prior 5 should distinguish deposit-backed reservations (loss falls on the buyer) from announcements and frameworks (no protection for the supplier's revenue forecast).

### Gaps
- No market data on resale values of powered land or of GPUs was found; prior 4 is tested only indirectly.
- No Wood Mackenzie or BloombergNEF forecast of nuclear capacity delivered to data centres by 2032 was located.

## 7. Local opposition, permitting and market rules

### Takeaway
Political risk rose sharply in November 2026: the midterms produced governors-elect pledging pauses in states holding about 40% of pending US capacity, and dozens of local bans passed. On the federal side FERC acted by show-cause orders rather than a final large-load rule, PJM cleared at its price cap with a shortfall for a second year, and FERC reportedly rejected PJM's large-load curtailment proposal in October 2026.

### Inferences
- Pledged pauses in Texas and Ohio, if enacted, would hit the two states with the most on-site generation proposals; a Florida-style "bring your own power" mandate would instead increase demand for on-site generation. The policy direction therefore cuts both ways for fast-power suppliers.

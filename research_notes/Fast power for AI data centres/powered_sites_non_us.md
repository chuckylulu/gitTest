# Non-US owners of powered sites for AI data centres (as of 12 Nov 2026)

Scope note: about 35 tool calls, mostly search-result summaries plus a handful of page fetches (several primary pages returned HTTP 403/404). Figures marked "search summary" were read from search-result digests, not from the primary document, and should be re-checked against filings before publication. Quality facts are in sections 1-4; valuation facts are only in section 5.

Shortlist that emerged (ordered by how much firm, contracted, powered capacity is evidenced):
1. CDC Data Centres (via Infratil, NZX/ASX) - more than 1 GW contracted, 10-30 year terms.
2. DayOne (ex-GDS International; Nasdaq IPO filed) - 962 MW in service, 4.6 GW including secured-power land.
3. YTL Power (Bursa Malaysia) - a utility that owns the campus and the power; 298 MW contracted, Kulai plan 1.2 GW.
4. AdaniConneX / Adani Enterprises (NSE) - more than 960 MW tied up, incl. 400 MW first phase of Google's Vizag hub.
5. Nebius (Dutch-domiciled, Nasdaq) - 5 GW contracted-power target, but much of the new power is in the US.
6. NextDC (ASX) - the clearest live case of a grid-connection slip destroying and then re-rating value.
7. Reliance (Jamnagar) and Gulf sovereign vehicles (Khazna/G42, Humain) - large, self-powered, but early and (Gulf) not investable.
Cautionary cases: SoftBank (power equipment without tenants) and Nscale (tenant without power).

## 1. Who actually holds scarce energised or contracted MW, and with what signed contracts?

### Takeaway
Outside the US, firm contracted capacity at scale is evidenced at CDC (more than 1 GW), DayOne (962 MW in service), AdaniConneX (more than 960 MW tied up) and YTL Power (298 MW contracted); most other headline numbers (SK Ulsan, Humain, Reliance, Nscale) are plans, with energised MW an order of magnitude smaller. I found no direct published evidence of a land-price premium for powered sites outside the US; the evidence is indirect (stake-building in NextDC, JLL data on capacity migrating away from constrained hubs).

### Inferences
- The names with the strongest claim to "scarce powered capacity already monetised" are CDC, DayOne and (smaller) YTL Power and AdaniConneX: each has hundreds of MW either in service or under long-dated contract. SK Ulsan, Humain, Reliance and Nscale are mostly roadmap.
- YTL Power, Reliance, Adani and OPC share a structural feature the pure developers lack: they own generation as well as the site, so the power is an internal asset and not a queue position.
- The Goodman/Digital Realty raid on NextDC at A$9.50, three weeks after a grid-delay collapse, is the best available non-US evidence that strategic buyers value entitled, partly energised metro sites above what public investors paid during the scare. It is indirect evidence, not a land-price comparison.
- Nscale's Loughton episode shows the reverse: a marquee tenant has no value without a connection date.

## 2. How binding is the power constraint by region, and what opposition or limits apply?

### Takeaway
The constraint is most binding in Europe's core hubs (connection waits of roughly 7-10 years), now demonstrably binding in suburban Australia and in Israel (a 200 MW cap and a connection freeze), and in the UK (a site told to wait until the 2030s). In the Gulf, India and Malaysia the developers are largely the power owners, so the limit is their own build pace; for China I found no evidence either way.

### Cited Findings
- Europe: the IEA is cited as putting waits in Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris and Dublin at up to a decade; other analyses put the core-hub average at 7-10 years (search summary) — [The Register, Feb 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/03/amazon_power_europe/); [Global Data Center Hub](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/grid-interconnection-delays-repricing)
- Only 37% of about 700 MW added in Europe in Q2 2026 went to the five core markets; JLL's mid-2026 EMEA report puts the 2026-28 pipeline an average 175 km from major hubs versus 46 km for 2022-25 projects (search summary) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/europe-data-centres-leaving-flap-d-grid-queues-land)
- Denmark's Energinet paused new large grid-connection agreements in March 2026 after about 60 GW of requests (search summary) — [oenergetice.cz](https://oenergetice.cz/en/data-centers/europe-puts-the-brakes-on-data-centers-power-is-running-short-and-grids-are-hitting-limits)
- Ireland: sources conflict. One says the de facto Dublin moratorium (since 2021) ended in Dec 2025 with a tiered connection framework; another says no new Dublin allocations before 2027; others still describe a moratorium to 2028 (search summary; unresolved) — [Euronews, May 2026](https://www.euronews.com/2026/05/05/europe-is-hungry-for-ai-data-centres-but-its-energy-grid-cannot-feed-them)
- Netherlands/Frankfurt: one Euronews piece says new connections are effectively barred until at least 2030; single-source claim — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/05/05/europe-is-hungry-for-ai-data-centres-but-its-energy-grid-cannot-feed-them)
- UK: Nscale's Loughton site was told not to expect grid power until the 2030s (Telegraph via TNW, 18 Oct 2026) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/microsoft-pulls-out-nscale-loughton-data-centre-ipo)
- Australia: resident opposition to large power poles through suburban streets stopped Jemena's M3 works; a Victorian decision on later M3 stages is expected in 2027, after the 28 Nov 2026 state election — [investingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/nextdc-shares-plunge-44-as-power-delays-expose-ai-data-centre-bottleneck/); [The New Daily](https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/themoneyedition/companies-that-matter/2026/10/21/australian-data-centre-giant-nextdc-loses-40-of-share-value-as-expansion-stalls)
- Israel: the Electricity Authority capped any new data centre at 200 MW after developers sought about 27 GW of grid capacity, and froze new requests of 8 MW or more for 140 days (search summary; dates not captured) — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/163814-israel-caps-data-centres-at-200mw-after-developers-sought-27gw-of-grid-c/); [Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/israel-s-electrical-authority-freezes-new-data-centre-grid-requests-for-140-days)
- China: the only power-related datum found is that GDS margins were pressured by higher utility costs in Q2 2026 — [Bamboo Works](https://thebambooworks.com/gds-lifts-ebitda-outlook-as-ai-driven-bookings-hit-record/)

### Inferences
- Caps and freezes (Israel, Denmark, Dublin) raise the value of connections already granted, but also cap the size of any single owner's prize (200 MW in Israel).
- The NextDC and Nscale cases show the binding step is often the last few kilometres of distribution works and local consent, not national generation adequacy.

### Gaps
- Tokyo, Singapore and Johor rules (Singapore's capacity allocation scheme, Johor water/power tariffs and approvals) were not researched.
- Whether power is a scarce asset in China was not answered; no source found. My prior from before these searches is that chips, not power, are the main limit there, but that is unsourced.
- Ireland's current connection policy needs a primary check (CRU decision).

### Takeaway
Balance sheets diverge sharply: CDC and YTL Power fund growth against long contracts and utility cash flow, NextDC has liquidity but cut guidance by about a third and froze new projects, and the IPO-dependent names (Nscale, DayOne) are loss-making and exposed to a closing equity window.

### Inferences
- SoftBank's writedown implies that turbine reservation slots were not freely resaleable at cost even in a tight market: roughly $2.85bn of deposits was forfeited on the excess over what one project needed. Owning power equipment ahead of a tenant and a site is a different, weaker asset than owning an energised site with a signed lease. No source says whether SoftBank tried to resell the slots.
- NextDC's contracts show that "contracted" is not the same as "take-or-pay regardless": the customer protections (service credits, termination rights) run against the landlord when power is late.
- CDC's 10-30 year terms and 95%-contracted revenue are the firmest contract evidence found, but the counterparty of the 555 MW deal is unnamed ("a US customer"), so tenant concentration cannot be assessed.

## 4. Resilience under three scenarios (steady growth, financing-led pause about 2028, faster acceleration)

### Takeaway
This section is my inference from the facts above; no source modelled these scenarios. Owners with long contracts, in-service capacity and their own generation (CDC, YTL Power, Adani, Reliance, Gulf sovereigns) look most robust to a pause; IPO-dependent, loss-making developers with unbuilt sites (Nscale, to a lesser degree DayOne and Nebius) look most exposed.

### Cited Findings
- AI-infrastructure equity windows were already narrowing in Oct-Nov 2026: DayOne was reported (unconfirmed) to be considering cutting its raise from $5bn to $2.5-3bn or delaying; Nscale met investor pushback on valuation (search summary) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nscale-ipo-valuation-investor-pushback); [Finimize](https://finimize.com/content/dayone-tests-the-us-ipo-market-for-data-centers)
- NextDC shares had fallen for seven straight weeks before the 21 Oct downgrade amid concern about AI spending and rising rates (search summary) — [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2026/10/20/australian-dc-power-delay-exposes-ai-bottleneck)
- CDC's contracted capacity comes online over FY28-FY29, i.e. delivery straddles a possible 2028 pause — [Infratil](https://infratil.com/news/cdc-signs-555mw-data-centre-contract-with-us-customer/)

### Inferences
- Steady growth: CDC, DayOne, YTL Power and AdaniConneX convert backlog to earnings; NextDC recovers if Jemena restarts in 2027.
- Financing-led pause about 2028: capacity already energised under 10-year-plus contracts keeps paying (CDC, GDS/DayOne in-service base); capex-heavy plans without signed tenants stall (Humain targets, SK's 900 MW, Reliance beyond 288 MW, Nebius's 5 GW). Nebius's $20-25bn 2026 capex against $3.0-3.4bn revenue makes it the most financing-sensitive listed name here. SoftBank is the template for what unsupported pre-ordering costs.
- Faster acceleration: the scarce items become granted connections in constrained metros (NextDC's sites, Israeli connections under the cap, any energised European core-hub capacity) and owners with spare generation (YTL Power, Reliance, OPC).

### Gaps
- No lender or rating-agency view on these companies under a pause scenario was collected.

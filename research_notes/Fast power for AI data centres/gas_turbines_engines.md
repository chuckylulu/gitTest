## 1. Position: products, market share, bridge vs durable, upstream constraints

### Takeaway
Heavy-duty turbines are a three-firm oligopoly (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, MHI) whose orders are 80-94% utility/IPP grid plants with multi-decade service tails, so they are durable beyond 2030; data centres are a minority of their books (GEV ~20% of gas customers, Siemens <10% of group backlog by value, MHI ~6% of orders). The on-site "bridge" products are aeroderivatives/small turbines (GEV LM series, Baker Hughes NovaLT, Solar Titan, Siemens SGT-800) and gas reciprocating engines (Caterpillar, Wärtsilä, INNIO Jenbacher, Rolls-Royce mtu, Cummins, Everllence), where data-centre concentration is much higher. Hot-section castings (Howmet, PCC, CPP, Doncasters) are the scarce upstream input.

### Inferences
- Bridge vs durable ranking (most durable first): MHI, GE Vernova and Siemens Energy (grid fleet plus 20-30 year service annuity); Cummins and Rolls-Royce mtu (standby is needed whether or not the grid arrives); Caterpillar and Wärtsilä (mixed, with large non-data-centre end markets); Baker Hughes (LNG/oil and gas dominate); INNIO (most concentrated in on-site prime power, i.e. the purest bridge exposure).
- MHI's CEO openly argues on-site small/mid turbines "can be considerably more expensive" and that grid power will win over time, which is the incumbent's statement of the bridge thesis.
- Vertical moves into castings (GE Aerospace/CPP, SpaceX foundry) suggest the airfoil bottleneck, not assembly, sets industry output.

### Gaps
- No free-access 2025/2026 McCoy Power Reports OEM share table; the latest full league table found is for 2024.

## 3. Revenue model: services vs equipment

### Takeaway
Services are roughly 40-55% of revenue at the turbine majors and engine specialists, and new long-term service agreements are being signed at higher margins; the equipment boom temporarily lowers the service share but enlarges the future annuity.

### Cited Findings
- Siemens Energy Gas Services: services were 55% of FY2026 revenue (about EUR 8bn of EUR 14.5bn, per an analyst's statement on the call); new service contracts carry margins ~5 points above the existing service backlog; service revenue expected to more than double by FY2035 (company claim) — [Q4 FY2026 transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-siemens-energy-shares-jump-on-record-q4-2026-results-93CH-5018439)

## 9. Resilience: the 2017-2019 gas turbine bust and what it implies

### Takeaway
In the last bust, new-unit prices fell ~30%, GE wrote down ~$23bn and cut 12,000 jobs, Siemens cut 6,900 and considered selling or merging the turbine unit; service revenue is what kept the businesses alive. Today's incumbents are better protected (net cash, prepayments, higher-margin backlog, bigger service base), but the capacity plans in section 6 recreate the same overcapacity risk for 2029-30.

### Cited Findings
- Siemens reported turbine prices fell 30% between FY2014 and FY2017 for lack of demand; GE had planned for 30-34 GW of 2018 demand and the market came in below 30 GW (search summary) — [IEEFA](https://ieefa.org/resources/general-electric-misread-energy-transition-cautionary-tale)
- GE took a goodwill writedown of almost $23bn in 2018, mostly tied to the 2015 Alstom acquisition (search summary) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloombergquint.com/businessweek/ge-s-23-billion-writedown-stems-from-a-bad-bet-on-fossil-fuels)
- GE Power's profit fell 45% in 2017; GE announced 12,000 job cuts and Siemens 6,900; Siemens considered selling its gas turbine business or merging it with Mitsubishi's — [Global Energy Monitor (with source links)](https://globalenergymonitor.org/research/leading-three-manufacturers-providing-two-thirds-turbines-gas-fired-power-plants-under); [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/ge-power-falters-on-underperformance-of-alstom-investment/)
- GE also suffered HA-class blade failures in 2018 that hurt service economics — [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/financial-gas-turbine-blade-troubles-plague-ge-power/)
- Mitsubishi was the Americas heavy-duty share leader in 2020 (54% per McCoy) and no. 1 in advanced frames in 2022-23, i.e. it gained share through the downturn — [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/gas/mccoy-power-reports-says-mitsubishi-power-won-54-percent-of-all-heavy-duty-gas-fired-turbine-order-in-2020/); [Turbomachinery International](https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/gas-turbine-market-report-2024-posts-highest-units-mws-in-22-years)
- Global orders in 2020-23 ran at only 30-48 GW/yr per MHI's chart, versus 96 GW in 2025 — [MHI Q&A slide](https://www.mhi.com/finance/library/result/pdf/fy20262q/qa_20262q.pdf)
- Today's cushions: Siemens new-unit backlog margins are 26 points above FY2022; ~85% of FY2027 revenue is already in backlog; GEV says backlog is at improved margins; MHI's backlog revenue runs to FY2030-31 — [Siemens transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-siemens-energy-shares-jump-on-record-q4-2026-results-93CH-5018439); [GEV release](https://www.gevernova.com/news/ge-vernova-reports-strong-third-quarter-2026-financial-performance); [MHI Q&A](https://www.mhi.com/finance/library/result/pdf/fy20262q/qa_20262q.pdf)
- Caterpillar's stated hedge: the same large-engine platform serves gas compression and mining trucks as well as data centres — [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/caterpillar-power-chief-just-revealed-what-the-data-center-boom-really-means-for-cat-stock)

### Inferences
- In a 2028 pause scenario, the first-order hit would be to new-order pricing and reservation conversion, not to 2027-29 revenue, which is largely in firm backlog. The damage in 2017-19 came from fixed costs sized for a market that halved; the companies most at risk of repeating it are those adding the most new-unit capacity relative to their service base (Caterpillar engines, Doosan, INNIO) rather than MHI, which is adding hot-part capacity for a service stream it can already see.
- MHI's record (gained share in the last downturn, cautious on capacity, no reservations tier) is the strongest evidence of resilience among the majors. GE Vernova's and Siemens Energy's turbine units were the casualties last time and their current net-cash positions are partly customer money.
- Engine makers had no comparable bust in 2017-19 because data-centre prime power did not exist as a market; there is no historical test of how INNIO or Wärtsilä Energy behave when a GW-scale customer class pauses.

Source labels used throughout: **[IBM]** = IBM-controlled (newsroom, blogs, docs, filings, IBM-authored papers, IBM executives' remarks even when carried by a third-party transcript); **[Partner]** = controlled by an IBM partner or customer that is party to the claim; **[Ind]** = independent (journalism, analysts, outside academics); **[Gov]** = government. ## 1. Products and stage: processors, systems, metrics, cloud vs on-premises, Loon, Qiskit / Functions

### Takeaway
As of mid-November 2026 IBM's flagship is the 120-qubit Nighthawk r2 (square lattice, generally available from about September 2026), alongside 156-qubit Heron r2/r3 processors; Nighthawk r3 (520 qubits) is announced for 2027, the experimental Loon chip has been built, and Kookaburra (the 2026 fault-tolerance module) had not been announced as delivered. IBM publishes throughput and gate-count claims, but none of the sources I opened gave fleet-wide two-qubit error, EPLG or layer-fidelity numbers for Nighthawk.

### Inferences
- The 458 "physical qubits" on Nighthawk r2 equal 120 qubits + 218 tunable couplers + 120 per-qubit reset elements (120+218+120 = 458); IBM appears to be counting all quantum circuit elements, not computational qubits. This is my arithmetic, not an IBM statement.
- IBM's 2026 public metrics emphasise throughput (samples/s, CLOPS) and gate counts reached with error mitigation rather than per-gate error statistics. Comparing Nighthawk with other vendors' hardware on error rates is therefore not possible from IBM's announcements alone.
- Heron r3's 57/176 couplings below 1e-3 means about 68% of couplings had errors at or above 1e-3 (Nov 2025).

## 2. Roadmap: current published plan, what changed in 2026, and the end-2026 "quantum advantage" claim

### Takeaway
IBM's targets are unchanged at the top: Starling (first large-scale fault-tolerant system, 200 logical qubits, 100 million operations) in 2029 at Poughkeepsie, then Blue Jay (2,000 logical qubits, 1 billion operations) from 2033. In 2026 IBM added a near-term Nighthawk r3 / 1,040-qubit linked system for 2027, changed parts of the Starling design, and said nothing public about Kookaburra. IBM and partners claimed quantum advantage on 30 July 2026 in three preprints. The claim is contested: the public tracker lists them as candidates, and one instance was reproduced classically in 37.3 minutes on 256 GPUs.

### Cited Findings
**Published roadmap (IBM roadmap page, accessed 14 Nov 2026; IBM states all items are "goals and objectives" subject to change)** — [IBM roadmap page, IBM](https://www.ibm.com/roadmaps/quantum/)
- 2026 ("enable the first examples of quantum advantage"): Nighthawk running circuits up to 7,500 gates "across up to three 120-qubit modules"; Kookaburra "demonstrates a single module combining a logical processing unit with quantum memory"; prototype real-time error-correction decoder.
- 2027: no items listed on the page at time of access.
- 2028: Nighthawk up to 15,000 gates on up to 1,080 qubits; demonstrations of multiple fault-tolerant modules and magic-state distillation; complete fault-tolerant instruction-set-architecture prototype.
- 2029: Starling, "200 qubits running 100 million gates".
- 2030: scaling beyond via distributed quantum computing; 2033+: Blue Jay, 2,000 qubits, 1 billion gates.

## 3. Milestones met, re-scoped, dropped or slipped (2020–2026)

### Takeaway
IBM hit its qubit-count headline processors on schedule (Eagle 2021, Osprey 2022, Condor 2023), but pivoted after 2023 from qubit count to gate quality and qLDPC error correction. The 2022 plan for a 4,158+-qubit three-Kookaburra system in 2025 was dropped and the Kookaburra name reassigned. The multi-chip Crossbill and Flamingo milestones became small proofs of concept. The 7,500-gate target slipped one year and was then reported met in 2026, and two customer installations (Chicago, India) slipped roughly one year.

### Inferences
- Count-based milestones (Eagle, Osprey, Condor, Heron) were met on their announced years, but Osprey and Condor were not followed by client-scale products at those sizes. IBM's largest generally available processors in 2026 have 120–156 qubits, far below the 2022 plan of 4,158+ by 2025.
- The modular-scaling milestones (Crossbill, Flamingo, three-Kookaburra system) each shrank to proofs of concept. Inter-chip quantum links reached 99.3% best state-transfer fidelity in 2026, still below IBM's own 99.9% target.
- The gate-count series (5k in 2024, 7.5k in 2026, 10k in 2027, 15k in 2028) has kept to its revised dates. The 7.5k step took two years (2024 to 2026) instead of one, and it relies on error mitigation (PEA) rather than error correction.

## 7. Legal and regulatory

### Takeaway
US export controls effective 6 Sep 2024 (BIS interim final rule, 89 FR 72926) license quantum computers by qubit count and average CNOT error, worldwide, with a License Exception (IEC) for countries that implement matching controls. IBM has made no public statement on how the rule affects it. No litigation or patent disputes involving IBM Quantum were found. The main 2026 regulatory events are the government-funded Anderon CHIPS award and the DARPA verification process.

### Cited Findings
- BIS interim final rule, published and effective 6 Sep 2024 (89 FR 72926; 15 CFR parts 736, 738, 740, 742, 743, 772, 774), adds ECCN 4A906. 4A906.a controls quantum computers with "fully controlled, connected and working physical qubits" and "C–NOT error" (average nearest-neighbour physical CNOT error) at: 34–99 qubits ≤1×10⁻⁴; 100–199 ≤1×10⁻³; 200–349 ≤2×10⁻³; 350–499 ≤3×10⁻³; 500–699 ≤4×10⁻³; 700–1,099 ≤5×10⁻³; 1,100–1,999 ≤6×10⁻³; ≥2,000 qubits regardless of error. 4A906.b covers qubit devices/circuits (incl. superconducting chips and "coherent interconnects") and 4A906.c covers control/measurement components "specially designed" for 4A906.a items. Reasons NS, RS ("worldwide control"), AT; License Exception IEC available; annealers excluded. BIS states the error tolerance "plateaus around 2,000 qubits at an error rate at 10⁻²". Deemed-export provisions grandfather foreign-person employees/contractors with access as of 6 Sep 2024 and set annual reporting (new §743.8) for a general-licence route covering certain deemed exports to Country Group D:1/D:5 nationals; comments on quantum deemed-export policy were due 5 Nov 2024 — [Federal Register, 89 FR 72926, 6 Sep 2024, Gov](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/09/06/2024-19633/commerce-control-list-additions-and-revisions-implementation-of-controls-on-advanced-technologies-consistent-with-controls) (text read from the GovInfo PDF: [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-09-06/pdf/2024-19633.pdf))

## 8. Science: what has been demonstrated, who verified or challenged it, what is unproven, and how solid each claim is

### Takeaway
IBM's hardware experiments are generally reproducible as experiments. Its claims that results lie beyond classical reach have repeatedly been answered by outside or IBM classical simulation: the 2023 utility experiment within weeks, peaked circuits in 2025–26, and the July 2026 doped-Clifford instance within two weeks. IBM's error-correction architecture (bivariate-bicycle/gross codes, Relay-BP) is peer-reviewed or preprint theory and simulation plus an FPGA decoder benchmark. As of November 2026, no hardware demonstration of a below-threshold qLDPC memory was found.

### Cited Findings
**2023 "utility" experiment (Kim et al., Nature 618, 500–505, 2023)**
- 127-qubit Eagle kicked-Ising experiment with error mitigation; IBM later noted it used 2,880 two-qubit gates — [IBM blog, 13 Nov 2024, IBM](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/qdc-2024); [Tindall et al. abstract citing Nature 618, 500 (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14887)
- Rebuttal 1: Tindall, Fishman, Stoudenmire, Sels (Flatiron/NYU), "Efficient tensor network simulation of IBM's Eagle kicked Ising experiment" (arXiv 26 Jun 2023; PRX Quantum 5, 010308, 2024): belief-propagation tensor network "significantly more accurate and precise than the results obtained from the quantum processor", extendable to the thermodynamic limit — [arXiv 2306.14887, Ind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14887)

### Inferences
**Evidence status of each major claim (as of 14 Nov 2026), with reasons**
- 2023 utility experiment, "accurate 127-qubit expectation values with error mitigation": peer-reviewed (Nature) and consistent with outside classical simulations, so the data are sound. The implied "beyond exact/brute-force classical" status was overtaken within about two weeks by two independent methods, one running on a laptop core. Status: utility as a benchmark holds; classical intractability is refuted for that experiment.
- Gross/BB-code overhead (288 vs ~3,000 qubits for 12 logical qubits): peer-reviewed theory and simulation under an assumed 0.1% circuit-noise model. Its hardware prerequisites (degree-6, two-layer couplers, long c-couplers) are only now being tested on Loon, and IBM changed the code in 2026. No hardware logical-memory demonstration found. Status: theoretically supported, unproven in hardware.
- Relay-BP real-time decoding: algorithm accuracy shown in simulation (preprint), and an FPGA latency of <480 ns is reported by IBM. It has not been reported closing the loop on a hardware qLDPC memory, and no outside replication was found. Status: company benchmark, not independently verified.
- HSBC 34%: one backtest on proprietary data with a non-peer-reviewed preprint. The gain appears only with noisy hardware features, not noiseless simulation, which has no established explanation. Status: an unexplained empirical observation, not evidence of quantum advantage (the authors do not claim it).
- July 2026 advantage claims: (a) IBM/UChicago doped Clifford has the strongest verification (a certified fidelity bound), but its specific published instance was reproduced classically in 37.3 GPU-cluster minutes vs 16.1 QPU minutes, so for this instance any runtime separation is small or absent. (b) Qedma rests on the failure of the classical methods tried plus partial cross-platform agreement, with heuristic extrapolation at the largest sizes. (c) Algorithmiq rests on cross-device consistency with heuristic rescaling and no accuracy bound. All three are preprints listed as tracker "candidates". Status: contested; IBM's "advantage achieved" statement is not settled by community adjudication.
- The repeated pattern (2023, 2025–26 peaked circuits, 2026 doped Clifford) is that IBM-hardware results near 50–100 qubits attract classical simulations that match or beat them within days to weeks. IBM's own blogs acknowledge this pattern.

## 9. Physical and engineering limits relevant to IBM's plan

### Takeaway
IBM's plan depends on lowering two-qubit error well below 1e-3 at scale, making long-range on-chip (c-coupler) and chip-to-chip (l-coupler, >100 for Starling) links of usable quality, fabricating multilayer chips with thousands of Josephson junctions per module, managing TLS-defect and correlated (cosmic-ray) errors, and decoding in real time. Disclosed numbers show gaps on several of these: inter-chip links at 99.3% vs a 99.9% target, 2Q errors below 1e-3 on only about a third of Heron r3 couplings, and post-selection costs of about 860x in the 2026 logical experiment.

### Inferences
- The 2026 numbers show inter-chip quantum links worse than IBM's target in infidelity: best-case 0.7% and average ~1%, vs a 0.1% target (7x–10x). Starling needs more than 100 such links working together, so link quality, not qubit count, is a binding constraint on the 2029 plan.
- Error mitigation and post-selection costs grow exponentially with circuit size and error. IBM's 2026 advantage-scale results (16 QPU minutes for 2,051 accepted samples) show why mitigation-based advantage is hard to scale without true error correction.
- IBM's change to a 5-connection code and fewer junctions per module in 2026 is consistent with fabrication and connectivity limits binding harder than the 2024 gross-code design assumed. This is an inference; IBM has not stated a reason.

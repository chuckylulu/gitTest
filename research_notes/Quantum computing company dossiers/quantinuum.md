## 1. Ownership, corporate history, funding rounds, IPO, Honeywell break-up, key people

### Takeaway
Quantinuum Inc. listed on Nasdaq as QNT. The IPO priced at $60 on 3 Jun 2026, trading began 4 Jun and the deal closed 5 Jun. It sold 28.5M Class A shares, including 0.5M from the underwriters' option. Honeywell is no longer the majority owner. It holds 124.6M Common Units, about 47.8% of voting power at the IPO by the prospectus (Honeywell itself says "48%"). Honeywell deconsolidated Quantinuum in Q2 2026, booked a $6.63B gain and now uses the equity method. Cambridge Quantum Holdings, controlled by founder Ilyas Khan, holds about 31.8% of voting power. Khan is no longer an officer or director.

**Corporate history**
- 7 Jun 2021: Honeywell agreed to combine Honeywell Quantum Solutions (HQS) with Cambridge Quantum Computing Ltd (CQC). The deal closed **29 Nov 2021** and HQS contributed its assets at carrying value. Honeywell was the accounting acquirer, and Quantinuum was consolidated by Honeywell as a variable-interest entity (VIE). HQS's legal entity was renamed Quantinuum LLC in 2021 and CQC was renamed Quantinuum Ltd in 2023. — [Quantinuum Inc., Form 424(b)(4) final prospectus, dated 3 Jun 2026, filed 5 Jun 2026 (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm)
- HQS and CQC were both founded in 2014. — [Wikipedia, "Quantinuum," accessed 14 Nov 2026 (IND; citing Next Platform 2019 and Forbes 2021)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantinuum)
- IPO-related legal entities: Quantinuum Inc. was formed 20 Jan 2026 and Quantinuum Holdings, LLC on 21 Apr 2026. The structure is an Up-C. Class A shares are economic. Class B shares carry votes but no economics and are held one-for-one with Common Units by "Continuing Common Unitholders". Each class has one vote per share and they vote together. — [Quantinuum S-1/A Amendment No. 1, 26 May 2026 (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026037917/quantinuum-sx1a.htm); [424B4, 3 Jun 2026 (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm)

### Inferences
- Computed from Q3 2026 share counts: Honeywell's voting power at 30 Sep 2026 is about **46.9%** (124,628,729 ÷ 265,651,920 votes) and CQH's about 31.2%. This assumes their holdings were unchanged after the 13G dates.
- Honeywell's fair value of $6,173M ÷ 124.63M units implies a QNT price of about $49.5 on 30 Sep 2026. At the cited $40.57 close, total equity value on a fully exchanged basis is roughly $10.8B, against about $15.7B at the IPO price.
- The 180-day lock-up runs from the 3 Jun 2026 prospectus and so expires around 30 Nov 2026, about two weeks after this file's as-of date. The 424B4 also allows J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley to release locked-up shares early.
- The Honeywell break-up did not move the Quantinuum stake. The stake sits with the remaining Honeywell, and the IPO, not the spin, cut Honeywell below 50% and triggered deconsolidation. The ion-trap supplier (Aerospace) is now an unrelated third party.
- Khan's look-through economic stake (15% per the press) cannot be checked from the filings. They show only the 31.8% held through CQH entities, which have other shareholders.

## 2. Financials

### Takeaway
Revenue is small and lumpy: $23.0M in FY2024, $30.9M in FY2025, and $22.3M in the first nine months of 2026, with FY2026 guidance of $31–34M. Losses are large: FY2025 net loss $192.6M, and a 9M 2026 GAAP net loss of $851M, inflated by $485M of IPO-related stock compensation and $112M of warrant losses. Cash was $1.94B at 30 Sep 2026 after the IPO. No borrowings line appears. Revenue has depended heavily on RIKEN and on U.S. government customers.

## 3. Products and stage

### Takeaway
Quantinuum is in the early commercial stage. It sells cloud access and research services and has placed one on-premises system (at RIKEN, booked as a sales-type lease). It has three hardware generations: H1 (2020), H2 (2023; 56 qubits from 2024) and Helios (launched 5 Nov 2025: 98 ¹³⁷Ba⁺ qubits, 99.921% two-qubit fidelity). The software stack is Nexus, Guppy, TKET, InQuanto and Quantum Origin. It operates four commercial systems. A Singapore Helios is being assembled, and an Oracle OCI deployment is planned.

### Cited Findings

## 4. Roadmap (with dates), past commitments met or missed, quantum volume history

### Takeaway
The current roadmap is Helios (2025, delivered), Sol in 2H 2027 (targeting about 100 logical qubits at about a 10⁻⁵ logical error rate), Apollo in 2029 (universal fault tolerance, hundreds of logical qubits) and "Lumos", the DARPA QBI utility-scale concept. Honeywell's 2020 pledge of a 10x quantum-volume increase per year for five years was met cumulatively (QV 64 in Jun 2020 to 2^23 in May 2025 and 2^25 in Sep 2025). Helios shipped on time with 98 qubits against a promised 96. The Singapore Helios has slipped toward spring 2027. Sol and Apollo are company targets that no one outside the company has demonstrated.

## 8. Science: what has been demonstrated, who outside verified or challenged it, what remains unproven, and how solid each major claim is

### Takeaway
Quantinuum's physical-layer performance is the best publicly reported for gate-based hardware: 99.92% two-qubit fidelity on 98 qubits, and QV 2^25. It is documented in company papers and is consistent across a long series of results. Several flagship results are peer-reviewed with outside co-authors:
- Microsoft logical qubits (2024), whose "800x" figure depends on post-selection.
- Non-Abelian topological order (Nature 2024).
- 56-qubit random circuit sampling (PRX 2025).
- Certified randomness (Nature 2025), secure only against a restricted class of adversaries.
- Logical magic states via code switching (PRX 2025).

The newest logical-qubit claims are company-only and, as far as I found, not yet peer-reviewed: 48 logical qubits at 2:1 (2026), and color-code memory with Λ = 12 and about 5×10⁻⁶ logical error per round (Oct 2026). I found no independent replication or published classical-spoofing challenge of any of these, but this is a search gap and does not show that none exist.

### Cited Findings
- **First logical qubits better than physical (2021).** Quantinuum cites "the 2021 Ryan-Anderson et al. Study" for being "the first in the industry to implement logical qubits with a higher accuracy than physical qubits". — [424B4 (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm). The paper itself was not reviewed here.
- **Microsoft–Quantinuum logical qubits (Apr 2024):**
  - [[7,1,3]] code logical error rates were 9.8–500× below physical.
  - [[12,2,4]] code logical error rates were 4.7–**800×** below physical.
  - Repeated error correction with more than 100 physical CNOTs per cycle.
  - The authors say the improvements depend on "the judicious use of post-selection". v3 (17 Nov 2024) "fixed [a] misinterpreted physical teleportation baseline".
  - 33 authors from Microsoft Azure Quantum and Quantinuum. No journal reference was listed on arXiv.
  — [Paetznick et al., "Demonstration of logical qubits and repeated error correction with better-than-physical error rates," arXiv:2404.02280, 2 Apr 2024, rev. 17 Nov 2024 (MIX: Microsoft + Quantinuum)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02280)
  - Press summary: "four logical qubits, 14,000 experiments without a single error". — [Wikipedia citing TechCrunch 3 Apr 2024 (IND, secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantinuum)
- **Non-Abelian topological order (Nature 2024):**
  - D4 non-Abelian topological order on a 27-qubit kagome lattice on H2. Per-site fidelity was above 98.4%.
  - Non-Abelian braiding was detected through anyon interferometry, and all 22 ground states were created on a torus.
  - Claimed as "the first unambiguous realization of non-Abelian TO".
  - 18 authors, including theorists Nathanan Tantivasadakarn, Ruben Verresen and Ashvin Vishwanath. Affiliations were not shown on the fetched page; they are academic theorists per general knowledge, not re-verified.
  — [Iqbal et al., arXiv:2305.03766 (May 2023, rev. Feb 2024); Nature 626, 505–511 (2024) (MIX)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03766)
- **56-qubit random circuit sampling (Jun 2024; PRX 2025):**
  - H2 was upgraded to 56 qubits with "arbitrary connectivity" and two-qubit fidelity of 99.843(5)%.
  - The authors say the sampling "appears to be beyond the capabilities of state-of-the-art classical algorithms" and that classical difficulty "is likely limited only by qubit number".
  - 52 authors.
  — [DeCross et al., arXiv:2406.02501; Phys. Rev. X 15, 021052 (2025) (CO-authored; outside co-authors not verified)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02501)
- **Certified randomness (Nature, Mar 2025):**
  - Used the 56-qubit H2-1 "accessed over the internet".
  - Certified **71,313 bits** of entropy "under a restricted class of realistic near-term adversaries and additional assumptions".
  - Classical verification used a "measured combined sustained performance of 1.1×10^18" FLOPS across multiple supercomputers.
  - Relies on the classical hardness of random circuit sampling.
  - 32 authors, led by Minzhao Liu and Ruslan Shaydulin. The brief names partners JPMorgan Chase, Argonne, ORNL and UT Austin; I did not re-verify affiliations on the fetched abstract page.
  — [Liu, Shaydulin et al., "Certified randomness using a trapped-ion quantum processor," arXiv:2503.20498 (26 Mar 2025); Nature (2025), doi:10.1038/s41586-025-08737-1 (MIX)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20498)

- **Logical magic state via code switching (Jun 2025; PRX 2025):**
  - Switched from the 15-qubit Reed–Muller code to the 7-qubit Steane code to make an encoded magic state.
  - **82.58% acceptance probability**; infidelity **≤5.1(2.7)×10⁻⁴**, which is "at least 2.7 times" below the leading physical infidelity.
  - Universality is argued by combining this with separately demonstrated fault-tolerant Clifford operations.
  - Authors: Daguerre, Blume-Kohout, Brown, Hayes, Isaac H. Kim. Kim and Daguerre are academics per general knowledge, not re-verified.
  — [Daguerre et al., arXiv:2506.14169 (17 Jun 2025, rev. 15 Oct 2025); Phys. Rev. X 15, 041008 (2025) (MIX)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14169)

- **Color code below threshold (Oct 2026):**
  - The press release claims **99.9995%** logical fidelity, "ten times lower [error] than September's record [Helix code]" and "100 times lower than the best published result across other hardware modalities", with Google's 2024 Nature surface-code paper as the comparator.
  - The color code needs up to 37 physical qubits per logical qubit, against 10 for the Helix code.
  - Robin Blume-Kohout of Sandia, who was not involved, called it "real, serious performance gains".
  — [Quantinuum press release, "Quantinuum Breaks Error-Correction World Record by 10x…," 19 Oct 2026 (CO)](https://www.quantinuum.com/press-releases/quantinuum-breaks-error-correction-world-record-by-10x-validating-its-leading-path-to-fault-tolerant-quantum-computing-by-2029)
  - The underlying paper is a company PDF, with **all 23 authors at Quantinuum**:
    - Triangular color codes at d = 3, 5 and 7, using 13, 37 and 73 qubits per memory experiment.
    - 20 syndrome-extraction rounds per shot.
    - Logical error "as low as 5(+4/−2)×10⁻⁶" per round, with **Λ = 12(+3/−2)** per distance step.
    - Encoded two-qubit Clifford randomized benchmarking at d = 5 gave 7(+3/−3)×10⁻⁵ per logical Clifford, with Λ = 9(+8/−3).

    - Memory data were decoded **offline** (Tesseract). Neural decoders are "capable of real-time decoding" based on measured latency. The "next step" is a full Clifford+T circuit with real-time decoding.
    - Data were reported both with and without a phase-tracking improvement.
  — [Lavasani et al., "Quantum error correction well below the color code threshold," Quantinuum PDF linked from quantinuum.com/fault-tolerance-by-2029, Oct 2026 (CO)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/669960f53cd73aedb80c8eea/6ad6013f8ae0364b2cd03c53_c8c5526bc88434b2e47f6ffc3b27deca_Quantum-Error-Correction-Well-Below-the-Color-Code-Threshold.pdf)
- **Outside validation channels:** DARPA QBI provides "unbiased third-party verification and validation". Quantinuum is in Stage B, not Stage C, per the list current at 8 Oct 2026. An IV&V solicitation was published 17 Nov 2025. — [DARPA QBI page (IND)](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/quantum-benchmarking-initiative)
- The company's own risk disclosure says experimental results "may not replicate at larger scale, in logical computation, or in commercial use". — [Quantinuum, 19 Oct 2026 (CO)](https://www.quantinuum.com/press-releases/quantinuum-breaks-error-correction-world-record-by-10x-validating-its-leading-path-to-fault-tolerant-quantum-computing-by-2029)

### Inferences
**Claim-solidity assessment** (evidence-based; ordered from most to least solid)
1. **Physical fidelities and QV records: solid.** Standard methods (randomized and cycle benchmarking). The numbers have been consistent from H1 through Helios. QV data are public (GitHub, per Wikipedia), and outside users have run on the machines, such as the certified-randomness team. **Caveat:** all fidelity numbers are vendor-measured, and averages can hide outliers. The Helios paper's 98-qubit RCS gives an *effective* two-qubit error of about 2×10⁻³, roughly 2.5× the isolated-gate figure. Component metrics overstate full-system performance.
2. **Non-Abelian topological order (Nature 2024): solid as physics.** Peer-reviewed with academic theory co-authors. Small system (27 qubits), and the 98.4% is per site. This is quantum simulation, not a computing advantage.
3. **Magic-state code switching (PRX 2025): solid as a primitive.** Peer-reviewed with external co-authors. But it is post-selected (82.58% acceptance), the infidelity is an upper bound with wide error bars, and "universality" is assembled from separate experiments rather than run as one fault-tolerant computation.
4. **56-qubit RCS (PRX 2025): solid as a benchmark, with limited meaning.** Peer-reviewed. The authors hedge ("appears to be beyond"). Historically, RCS advantage claims have been eroded by better classical algorithms. I found no specific spoofing result against H2 (search gap). It produces no useful output, as Ezratty notes.
5. **Certified randomness (Nature 2025): solid experiment, conditional guarantee.** Peer-reviewed with external co-authors. Security holds only against "a restricted class of realistic near-term adversaries and additional assumptions". Verification needed about 1.1 EFLOPS sustained on supercomputers, and 71,313 bits is a small output. The authors present it as a step toward practical use, not a deployed service.
6. **Microsoft 2024 logical qubits ("800x"): moderately solid, often overstated.** It is real better-than-physical performance. The headline figure depends on post-selection, and the baseline was corrected in v3. It does not show scalable fault-tolerant computation.
7. **Color-code QEC (Oct 2026: 99.9995%, Λ = 12): promising, not yet independently established.** All authors are Quantinuum staff and the paper is a company PDF (I found no peer review or arXiv posting). The 99.9995% is a **per-round logical memory** error rate over 20 rounds at d = 7 (73 qubits), decoded offline. It is not a logical gate fidelity in a computation, although logical-Clifford randomized benchmarking at d = 5 gives about 7×10⁻⁵. The "100x better than other modalities" comparison picks one metric (memory per round) against Google's 2024 paper. The one outside quote (Blume-Kohout) is an endorsement, not a replication, and he co-authored Quantinuum's 2025 magic-state paper.
8. **48 logical qubits at 2:1 (Helios; "2026 Dasu et al."): weakly supported in public.** It was announced at launch without a paper (Ezratty), and I could not review the cited study. Concatenated codes at a 2:1 rate typically have small distance. Error-detection results (94 and 50 logical qubits) rely on post-selection and are a different thing from error correction.
9. **"Beyond classical" simulations (superconductivity and magnetism on Helios; "beyond the practical capabilities" of classical, Q2 2026): unverified.** These are company assertions. I found no independent classical-simulation rebuttal or confirmation in this session.
10. **Sol (100 logical qubits at 10⁻⁵, 2H 2027) and Apollo (universal FT, hundreds of logical qubits, up to 10⁻¹⁰, 2029): unproven roadmap targets.** The 10⁻¹⁰ figure is explicitly from literature-based analysis, not data. See Section 9 for limits.

- Pattern: peer-reviewed, externally co-authored results are concentrated in 2023–2025. The 2026 headline error-correction results are company-only so far.

### Gaps
- I found no independent replication or published critique of: the H2 56-qubit RCS hardness, for example tensor-network spoofing; the certified-randomness assumptions or cost, beyond the authors' own caveats; the Helios 98-qubit RCS; the superconductivity and magnetism "beyond classical" claims; or the 48-logical-qubit result. The search budget was exhausted, so absence is not confirmed.
- I did not locate the September 2026 "Helix code" record (code parameters and error rate) or the "2026 Dasu et al." study.
- I did not retrieve the Microsoft–Quantinuum Sep 2024 follow-up (12 logical qubits, per general knowledge; not verified here).
- Author affiliations for the Nature 2025, Nature 2024 and PRX papers were not shown on the fetched pages. The outside-co-author status above is partly from general knowledge and from the assignment brief.

## 9. Physical and engineering limits of trapped-ion QCCD; feasibility of Sol and Apollo

### Takeaway
The binding constraint for Quantinuum's QCCD design is time, not gate fidelity. On Helios a two-qubit gate takes about 70 µs, ground-state cooling about 3 ms, and one full 98-qubit circuit layer about 55 ms, because ion transport (ring rotations) and cooling dominate. That is several orders of magnitude slower per layer than superconducting systems. Scaling to Sol (2D grid) and Apollo depends on unproven engineering: junction-rich 2D traps, integrated photonics, cryogenic deployment at scale, real-time decoding and supply-chain onshoring. The company frames the remaining challenges as "engineering-based… rather than limits imposed by the underlying physics".

### Cited Findings
- Helios timing:
  - Two-qubit gate about **70 µs**; ground-state cooling about **3 ms**; extra cooling after shifts about **300 µs**.
  - **Average depth-1 layer time about 55 ms** for random pairings on all 98 qubits, with ring rotations dominating transport time.
  - Memory (idle) error during transport: linear rate 5(1)×10⁻⁴.
  - Mid-circuit-measurement crosstalk: about 2.1×10⁻⁴ local and 4.8×10⁻⁵ global per operation.
  - Parallel cooling in separate zones is intended to raise clock speed.
  - Heating rates and operating temperature are not reported.
  — [Ransford et al., arXiv:2511.05465, Nov 2025 (CO)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05465)
- Leakage repump takes about 340 µs per cycle. Three cycles fit within the ground-state cooling time ("roughly twice as long"). — [Lavasani et al., Oct 2026 (CO)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/669960f53cd73aedb80c8eea/6ad6013f8ae0364b2cd03c53_c8c5526bc88434b2e47f6ffc3b27deca_Quantum-Error-Correction-Well-Below-the-Color-Code-Threshold.pdf). This implies about 2 ms of cooling, while the Helios paper gives about 3 ms; the figures may refer to different operating points.
- Independent view:
  - Shuttling from storage to the gate zone takes about 40 ms (Ezratty's reading), and a full layer about 40–55 ms.
  - "Gate operations are quite slow, given the ions shuttling required between zones".
  - Gates are about 100,000× slower than superconducting gates, which "will become a handicap for FTQC when error correction kicks-in with very large circuits".
  - The gate zone holds 16 ions with up to 8 parallel two-qubit gates. There are at least seven lasers.
  — [Ezratty, 12 Nov 2025 (IND)](https://www.oezratty.net/wordpress/?p=24056)
- Company position:
  - "Our approach prioritizes accuracy and time-to-solution over raw gate speed."
  - QCCD can scale to "tens of thousands of qubits residing on a die and millions of qubits on a wafer". This is an aspiration, not demonstrated.
  - Ion chains are limited to "approximately 100 qubits"; this is the company's view of competitors.
  - Remaining challenges are "engineering-based, such as integrated optics and packaging density".
  - The design is laser-only, and microwave gates have "significant challenges in crosstalk, gate speed, and power".
  — [424B4 (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm)
- Wiring: a 2D-grid chip demonstration (Apr 2024) cut control wiring from "up to 20 analog wires per qubit to 1 digital wire per qubit". — [Wikipedia citing The Quantum Insider 16 Apr 2024 and Quantum Computing Report 9 Mar 2024 (IND, secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantinuum). Helios uses 273 voltage signals for 98 qubits (2.8 per qubit). — [Ransford et al. (CO)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.05465). A Sol chip prototype validated "broadcasting control signals to multiple qubits simultaneously". — [424B4 (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm)
- Cryogenics and vacuum:
  - Helios uses liquid-helium-4 cryogenics, and its vacuum pump draws under a couple of hundred watts. — [Ezratty (IND)](https://www.oezratty.net/wordpress/?p=24056)
  - Laser-cooled ions avoid He-3. — [424B4 (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026041003/quantinuum-424b4.htm)
  - Apollo still requires "validating that our cryogenic system can be deployed at the required scale". — [Q3 2026 release (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026071000/q32026earningspressrelease.htm)
- Laser and optics integration: Monarch Quantum CEO: "The road to large-scale, trapped-ion quantum computers relies on moving away from complex, sprawling optical setups to scalable, reliable integrated photonics engines." — [CHIPS release, 8 Sep 2026 (CO, partner quote)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026060934/quantinuum-finalizesx100.htm)
- Decoding: real-time decoding at scale is not yet demonstrated in a full fault-tolerant loop. The Oct 2026 memory data were decoded offline, and a Clifford+T circuit with real-time decoding is the stated "next step". — [Lavasani et al. (CO)](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/669960f53cd73aedb80c8eea/6ad6013f8ae0364b2cd03c53_c8c5526bc88434b2e47f6ffc3b27deca_Quantum-Error-Correction-Well-Below-the-Color-Code-Threshold.pdf)
- Company risk language: "Achieving fault tolerance at scale faces significant technical barriers." Roadmap technology "may not become available". — [S-1/A (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026037917/quantinuum-sx1a.htm)
- Sol status: control system validated; trap chip back from fabrication; integration Q1 2027; target 2H 2027. — [Q2 2026 release (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026055699/fy26q2_qntmxearningsxrelea.htm); [Q3 2026 release (CO)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2110105/000162828026071000/q32026earningspressrelease.htm)

### Inferences
- **Speed arithmetic** (computed from the figures above; illustrative only):
  - At about 55 ms per full-width layer, one million layers would take about 15 hours. Large fault-tolerant algorithms need roughly 10⁸–10¹⁰ logical operations, which would take days to years unless per-layer time falls by orders of magnitude.
  - So Apollo's usefulness depends as much on clock-rate gains (parallel zones, faster transport and cooling, junction networks) as on fidelity.
  - I found no published Quantinuum target for Sol or Apollo clock speed or logical-operation rate.
- **Sol feasibility:** the 100-logical-qubit target at 10⁻⁵ is consistent with Helios-era data in fidelity terms: about 7×10⁻⁵ per logical Clifford at d = 5, and about 5×10⁻⁶ per memory round at d = 7. But 100 logical qubits would need about 3,700 physical qubits at d = 5 color-code overhead (37 each) or about 1,000 with the Helix code (10 each). Ezratty reports Sol at about 192 physical qubits, so the target appears to rely on high-rate codes (the 2:1 concatenated or Helix codes) with lower distance, or on error detection. The definition of "logical qubit" behind the Sol target is therefore critical and is not specified in the sources read.
- **Apollo feasibility:** it requires, at once, 2D junction transport at scale, integrated photonics (Monarch), cryogenic scale-up, real-time decoding of Clifford+T circuits and onshored fabrication (GlobalFoundries 300 mm). Each is identified by the company or its partners as work still to do. DARPA had not advanced Quantinuum to QBI Stage C as of Oct 2026, so there is no completed government IV&V of the Apollo or Lumos design yet.

### Gaps
- No published numbers for ion heating rates in Helios traps, junction-transit times and their error contributions, or Helios operating temperature.
- No published Sol or Apollo specifications for physical qubit count (beyond Ezratty's 192 for Sol), clock speed, power or footprint.
- No independent (non-Quantinuum) engineering assessment of Sol or Apollo feasibility was found. DARPA QBI assessments are not public.
- I did not retrieve independent academic analyses of QCCD speed limits, such as resource estimates comparing trapped-ion and superconducting runtimes.

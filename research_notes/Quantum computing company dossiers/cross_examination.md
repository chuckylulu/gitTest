# Cross-examination: independent checks on claims by IBM Quantum, Google Quantum AI, Quantinuum, IonQ, QuEra, PsiQuantum and IQM (status at 14 Nov 2026)

Conventions used below. Source labels: [IND-gov] government body or regulator; [IND-court] court record; [IND-acad] academic paper or independent scientist; [IND-press] independent news or trade press; [IND-analyst] consultancy or analyst; [IND-short] short seller (independent of the company but holds a short position, so it has a financial interest); [IND-partisan] political party; [CC] company-controlled (press release, blog, spec sheet); [CC-SEC] company-authored filing that is legally regulated (SEC 8-K, 10-Q, S-1, 6-K). "(opened)" means I read the page itself. "†" means I saw the item only in search-engine result summaries and did not open the page, so treat it as lower confidence. "Older" marks information from before 2026. Research note: the shared WebSearch budget ran out partway through this task. After that I only opened URLs I had already found. Several items could not be checked and are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Government evaluators: DARPA QBI/US2QC, national labs, testbeds, audits

### Takeaway
DARPA's Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (QBI) is the only government evaluator that has publicly sorted these firms. On DARPA's list "current as of Oct. 8, 2026", IBM, IonQ and PsiQuantum (which entered through US2QC) are in Stage C, Quantinuum and QuEra are in Stage B, Google Quantum AI is in Stage A, and IQM does not take part. DARPA's stage decisions judge plans, not hardware measurements. No government lab or testbed has yet published its own performance measurements of any of the seven companies' machines: the UK NQCC's evaluation phase runs until Q2 2027 and ORNL has published no data. Australian audit bodies have looked only at the process and legal costs around the A$940m PsiQuantum deal.

- Independent cross-vendor benchmark by Jülich Supercomputing Centre (Forschungszentrum Jülich) and Purdue: Montañez-Barrera, Michielsen and Bernal Neira, arXiv 2502.06471 (v1 10 Feb 2025, v2 28 May 2025) — [arXiv 2502.06471v2 (opened)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06471v2) [IND-acad] (older: 2025 hardware; Google, QuEra and PsiQuantum were not tested):
  - It tested 24 QPUs from six vendors: IBM Eagle and Heron; IonQ Harmony, Aria, Forte and Forte Enterprise; Quantinuum H1-1 and H2-1; IQM Spark and Garnet; Rigetti Ankaa-2 and -3; and OriginQ.

  - IQM Garnet stayed coherent to about p=50. IQM Spark fell below the random region by p=30.
  - The vendor metric EPLG "does not consistently predict algorithmic performance, especially at scale."
  - Quantinuum's S-1 cites this study when choosing its peer set.

### Inferences
- Only IBM and IonQ have gone through all of QBI's Stage A → B → C steps. PsiQuantum reached Stage C through the US2QC pilot. In US government terms, these are the only three of the seven that DARPA has formally judged plausible enough to reach the hardware-validation stage. Stage C is the first stage in which DARPA's team tests hardware, so no DARPA judgment so far validates performance claims.
- Being "not advanced" does not mean failing. DARPA says more promotions to Stage C are expected (7 Oct 2026), and Quantinuum says it is negotiating a Stage C scope.
- As of 14 Nov 2026, the independent measurements I found are academic (Jülich 2025) rather than government. The NQCC report reprints supplier-reported figures, including Oxford Ionics' "global records" claim, inside a government document. These should not be cited as NQCC-verified.
- The Australian scrutiny so far is about procurement, legal costs and site delays. None of it assesses PsiQuantum's technology. The technical evaluation of PsiQuantum's plans sits with DARPA, whose findings are not published.

- I found no public statement by DARPA officials naming problems with any specific company's claims. DARPA's published remarks are generic.
- I do not know when PsiQuantum formally moved from US2QC into "Stage C". It was listed as Stage C by 11 Mar 2026.
- I did not obtain DARPA's own statement of Stage C funding. The $300m figure is IonQ's own statement.

## 2. Customers and suppliers: what they say about results, and where they qualify company claims

### Takeaway
Most customer "results" in the public record come from vendor press releases that attribute numbers to customers. Few customers publish independent accounts. The most visible customer claim, HSBC's "world-first" 34% bond-trading improvement on IBM Heron (Sep 2025), was strongly disputed by independent scientists. Customer concentration and financial filings (RIKEN, Oracle, KISTI, SkyWater) show where vendor revenue actually comes from.

### Cited Findings
**Banks and finance**
- HSBC with IBM (announced 25 Sep 2025): HSBC called it a "world-first". Trials on IBM's Heron produced a 34% improvement in predicting whether a European corporate-bond request-for-quote would be filled (AUC back-test). — [CBS News †](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ibm-hsbc-quantum-computing-bond-trading/) [IND-press] (older)
  - The paper's authors say the gains are tied to one dataset and that the 34% figure "remains puzzling". The gain appeared with hardware noise and disappeared in noiseless simulation. — [The Quantum Insider, 28 Sep 2025 †](https://thequantuminsider.com/2025/09/28/bond-trading-quantum-bond-trading-a-deeper-look-at-hsbc-and-ibms-bond-trading-study/) (older)
- Scott Aaronson (UT Austin), 25 Sep 2025, "HSBC unleashes yet another 'qombie'" — [Shtetl-Optimized (opened)](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=9170) [IND-acad] (older):
  - He asks whether they "see a quantum advantage... but only _because_ of the noise".
  - He notes the advantage disappears when the noiseless computation is simulated classically, and warns that "the possibility of selection bias rears its head".
  - He calls the design "fatally flawed from the beginning, optimized for generating headlines rather than knowledge".
  - In comments he says "HSBC (and, alas, IBM) launched a marketing campaign".
- Other coverage: [Global Trading, 30 Sep 2025 (opened; paywalled)](https://www.globaltrading.net/experts-pour-cold-water-on-hsbc-quantum-computing-breakthrough/) says the claim "has been disputed by experts" [IND-press]; [The DESK/FI-Desk †](https://www.fi-desk.com/quantum-computing-breakthrough-has-more-red-flags-than-a-peoples-liberation-army-parade/) headline: "more red flags than a People's Liberation Army parade" [IND-press] (older).

## 3. Competitors, critics, short sellers and analysts

### Takeaway
Rivals mostly contradict each other implicitly, through competing "highest/first/most" claims and chosen metrics, rather than by direct rebuttal. The most specific independent pushback concerns quantum-advantage claims: Google 2019 (IBM), HSBC/IBM 2025 (Aaronson), Google Quantum Echoes 2025 (a "decent candidate" but unverified), and IBM's July 2026 "quantum advantage era" (three non-peer-reviewed preprints that are still candidates on IBM's own tracker). ## 6. Contradictory "first", "record", "largest" and "advantage" claims among the seven

### Takeaway
Most conflicts come from qualifiers that differ: prototype versus commercial, physical versus logical, memory versus magic state, "sold" versus "delivered", "expected" versus measured. The claims can all be literally true at once while each still implies leadership. The only third-party adjudications found are Jülich's 2025 benchmark and the ongoing IBM-hosted advantage tracker.

### Cited Findings
1. **"World record" two-qubit fidelity**
   - IonQ: above 99.99%, "setting a new quantum computing world record", "the first and only quantum computing company to cross the 'four-nines' benchmark". This was on R&D prototypes and beat the "previous record of 99.97%, set in 2024 by Oxford Ionics" — [IonQ, 21 Oct 2025 (opened)](https://www.ionq.com/news/ionq-achieves-landmark-result-setting-new-world-record-in-quantum-computing) [CC].
   - Quantinuum: 99.921% on Helios, "the highest two-qubit gate fidelity among commercially available gate-based quantum computing systems" — [S-1, 8 May 2026 (opened)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002110105/000162828026032836/quantinuum-sx1.htm) [CC-SEC].
   - NQCC report (supplier text): "Oxford Ionics currently holds the global records for single-qubit gate fidelity, two-qubit gate fidelity, and quantum state preparation and measurement" — [NQCC, 21 Oct 2026 (opened)](https://www.nqcc.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/NQCC_Quantum-Computing-Testbed-Initiative_Phase-1-report.pdf).
   - Google: 0.14% mean simultaneous error on Willow Chip 2 — [spec sheet](https://quantumai.google/static/site-assets/downloads/willow-spec-sheet.pdf) [CC].
   - Conflicting evidence: these are not like-for-like (prototype pair versus full-system average). Jülich's independent 2025 test found that vendor metrics "do not consistently predict algorithmic performance" — [arXiv 2502.06471v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.06471v2) [IND-acad].
2. **Logical-qubit and error-correction "records"**
   - Quantinuum: 99.9995% logical fidelity (October 2026), with error "100 times lower than the best published result on other hardware modalities" [CC]. Also "first to achieve an error-correcting overhead in a commercial setting of 2:1" and "first in the industry to implement logical qubits with a higher accuracy than physical qubits" (2021) [CC-SEC].
   - Google: below-threshold surface code (Λ = 2.14) and 99.99% logical T-state fidelity (Science, October 2026) [CC/IND-press].
   - QuEra/Harvard: an encoding rate above 1/2 (about 2:1 for memory), reported April 2026 †.
   - Conflicting evidence: these measure different things (memory, magic-state preparation, encoding rate), so "record" claims overlap rather than contradict. The 2:1 "first" is qualified by "in a commercial setting" because academic neutral-atom work reports similar ratios.
3. **"Quantum advantage achieved"**
   - Google: 2019 supremacy; Willow random-circuit sampling at "5 minutes vs. 10^25 years" ([spec sheet](https://quantumai.google/static/site-assets/downloads/willow-spec-sheet.pdf)); Quantum Echoes, "verifiable quantum advantage", 13,000x (22 Oct 2025).
   - IBM: "We are now firmly in the quantum advantage era" (30 Jul 2026).
   - HSBC/IBM: "world-first" bond-trading improvement (25 Sep 2025).
   - Conflicting evidence:
     - IBM's own 2019 rebuttal of Google (2.5 days; prior knowledge).
     - Quantum Echoes was unverified on another device at publication ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/quantum-echoes-google-computer)).
     - IBM's July 2026 papers are non-peer-reviewed tracker "candidates" ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/ibm-three-quantum-advantage-papers/)).
     - Some peaked-circuit tracker instances were classically simulated ([arXiv 2604.21908 †](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.21908)).
     - Aaronson called HSBC's result a "qombie" ([blog](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=9170)).
     - Quantinuum's own S-1 says such claims "are subject to scrutiny".
4. **"Most systems sold"**
   - IQM: "sold 23 quantum computers worldwide, more than any other quantum manufacturer" (July 2026) [CC].
   - Conflicting evidence: a secondary guide says 18 had been delivered to customer premises †. IBM, IonQ (for example KISTI) and Quantinuum (RIKEN, Singapore) also sell on-premises systems, and I found no independent count. Unverifiable as stated.
5. **"Most powerful" or "highest performing"**
   - Honeywell (June 2020, quantum volume 64, "highest performing") versus IonQ (1 Oct 2020, "world's most powerful", expected quantum volume above 4 million) [CC claims; IND-press coverage †: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2020/10/01/ionq-quantum-computer), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/why-honeywell-cant-claim-the-worlds-most-powerful-quantum-computer)] (older).
   - Conflicting evidence: Axios noted IonQ's figure was "expected", not measured. A UCL researcher said the "most powerful" label was not substantiated (IEEE Spectrum †).
6. **"First fault-tolerant" machine**
   - IBM: "the first fault-tolerant system in 2029" ([IBM, 7 Oct 2026](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-10-07-ibm-advances-to-stage-c-of-darpa-quantum-benchmarking-initiative)).
   - Quantinuum: "the first commercial-scale, fully fault-tolerant quantum computer before the end of this decade" (Apollo, 2029) ([S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002110105/000162828026032836/quantinuum-sx1.htm)).
   - PsiQuantum: a "commercially useful" machine by end-2027 ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/psiquantums-stalled-quantum-plant-to-break-ground-after-location-switch/)).
   - IonQ: 2 million qubits by 2030; IQM: fault tolerance by 2030; QuEra: 2029 ([arXiv 2604.04761](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04761v1)).
   - Conflicting evidence: these cannot all be "first". DARPA is now testing IBM, IonQ and PsiQuantum hardware plans in Stage C; Quantinuum and QuEra are in Stage B, and Google is in Stage A ([DARPA QBI page](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/quantum-benchmarking-initiative)).
7. **"Largest" machines and qubit counts**
   - IBM Nighthawk r3: 520 programmable and 2,034 total physical qubits, as a design target (11 Nov 2026) [CC].
   - Google's neutral-atom announcement noted that neutral-atom arrays have reached about 10,000 qubits, while superconducting circuits have run millions of gates †.
   - QuEra's NQCC platform: more than 200 physical qubits and more than 2,000 register sites (October 2026).
   - Conflicting evidence: "largest" depends on whether one counts physical sites, programmable qubits or logical qubits. None of these counts has been independently measured.
8. **Listing "firsts"**
   - Quantinuum: first traditional IPO by a full-stack quantum company and the largest quantum listing ([The Quantum Insider, 26 May 2026 (opened)](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/05/26/honeywell-backed-quantinuum-files-for-landmark-quantum-ipo/)).
   - IQM: "the first European quantum computing company listed on a major U.S. stock exchange" ([The Quantum Insider, 3 Jul 2026 (opened)](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/03/iqm-begins-trading-on-nasdaq/)).
   - Conflicting evidence: none found. IonQ listed via SPAC in 2021, so it was not a "traditional IPO" and does not contradict Quantinuum's wording.

- The ownership change matters for fidelity records: Oxford Ionics, cited in the NQCC report as the "global record" holder, is part of IonQ. So IonQ's and the NQCC text's record claims belong to the same corporate group.

## 1. Corporate facts, people, ownership, funding (private and government), revenue, debt, cash

### Takeaway
PsiQuantum is a private US company founded in 2015–2016 (sources differ). Disclosed private equity totals about US$1.67B: US$665M through Series D in 2021 and a US$1B Series E in Sept 2025 at a US$7B valuation. AMD announced an equity investment of undisclosed size on 12 Nov 2026. Government support is large but mostly loans, equity, tax credits or conditional:
- **Australia:** A$466.4M each from the Commonwealth and Queensland (about A$940M in total), as equity and loans.
- **US federal:** US$100M from the Commerce CHIPS program, DARPA awards of US$31.8M and US$125M, and a conditional loan of up to US$150M from the Office of Strategic Capital.
- **Illinois:** about US$200M in state incentives.

Since February 2026 the company has been led by Victor Peng, a former AMD president. Jeremy O'Brien is now Executive Chairman. Revenue, cash and most loan drawdown status are not disclosed. The widely repeated "US$1.5B round at US$10.5B in May 2026" appears to be a currency mix-up of the 2025 Series E (A$1.5B at A$10.5B).

## 2. Products and stage: Omega chipset, GlobalFoundries manufacturing, cryogenics (Linde), Brisbane/Moreton Bay and Chicago sites; logical qubit / FBQC / multi-chip demonstrations?

### Takeaway
PsiQuantum has published peer-reviewed component-level benchmarks for its Omega chipset, made at GlobalFoundries (Nature, Feb 2025). It runs a multi-cabinet test system in Milpitas (three cabinets of about 250 chips). No public evidence was found of a logical qubit, a below-threshold fusion-based error-correction demonstration, or a fault-tolerant multi-chip computation.
- **Australia:** The site moved from Brisbane Airport to Moreton Bay Central (announced 20–21 May 2026; ground broken 17–18 June 2026). The Linde cryoplant is due for delivery in H2 2027.
- **Chicago:** The first building shell (>65,000 sq ft) was complete by about May 2026. Hardware move-in is reported to start in 2027.

### Cited Findings
**Omega chipset (component benchmarks)**
- Publication: "A manufacturable platform for photonic quantum computing", Nature 641(8064):876–883, online 2025-02-26, open access, 95 authors. Preprint: arXiv:2404.17570, 2024-04-26. — [Europe PMC record PMC12095036, IND index of CC paper](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%2210.1038/s41586-025-08820-7%22&format=json&resultType=core); [arXiv:2404.17570, CC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17570)
- Headline results (all **conditional on photon detection; loss not accounted for**):
  - Dual-rail SPAM fidelity 99.98% ± 0.01% (99.996% with bright light and off-chip detectors).
  - Hong-Ou-Mandel (HOM) visibility between independent sources 99.50% ± 0.25%.
  - Two-qubit (Bell) fusion fidelity 99.22% ± 0.12%.
  - Chip-to-chip interconnect fidelity 99.72% ± 0.04% over 42 m of telecom fibre. The grating-coupler interface adds "~3 dB" loss.
  - — [Nature paper full text via Europe PMC, PMC12095036, 2025, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
- Sources: spontaneous four-wave mixing (SFWM), heralded and non-deterministic.
  - Single-ring purity limit ~93%. Interferometrically coupled resonator source: purity 99.5% ± 0.1%. Cascaded 24-resonator source: purity ≤99.35%, with 5% pair probability at ~100 pJ pump.
  - Indistinguishability >99% over a ±400 pm resonance shift.
  - HOM test at 125 MHz: coincidence-to-accidental ratio (CAR) 929 ± 4; heralded g²(0) = 0.00358 ± 0.00024; **maximum Klyshko (heralding) efficiency ~26%**.
  - — [same, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
- Detectors:
  - Superconducting nanowire single-photon detectors (SNSPDs) at ~2 K: median on-chip efficiency 93.4%; mean 88.9% ± 3.5%.
  - Photon-number-resolving detectors (PNRDs): median 98.9%, mean 96.2% ± 4.3%, resolving up to 4+ photons.
  - — [same, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
- Losses:
  - Silicon nitride (SiN) waveguide: 1.8 ± 0.2 dB/m single-mode; 0.5 ± 0.3 dB/m multimode.
  - Splitters 0.5 ± 0.2 mdB; crossings 1.2 ± 0.4 mdB.
  - Chip-to-fibre coupling: 127 ± 18 mdB (to SMF-28) and 52 ± 12 mdB (to UHNA4).
  - The paper says component losses are about 2× target, while waveguide losses meet target.
  - — [same, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
- Barium titanate (BTO) electro-optic switch:
  - Pockels coefficient >1,000 pm/V; Vπ·L 0.62 V·cm.
  - Phase-shifter propagation loss 53 ± 3 dB/m (2 mm device); insertion loss ~100 mdB, about 2× target.
  - Speed stated only as "GHz rates".
  - — [same, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
- Fab and packaging:
  - GlobalFoundries, 300-mm wafers, >20 lithography levels.
  - Packages have >1,000 electrical connections and up to 200 optical I/O.
  - Cryostat: text says ~2 K and up to 20 W; Figure 1 caption says ">10 W at 2.2 K".
  - — [same, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)

## 3. Roadmap and milestones: earlier claims vs current targets; DARPA US2QC/QBI

### Takeaway
Public timelines have slipped repeatedly. In 2020 the company spoke of about one million qubits "within five years" or a "handful of years". By 2024 the target was a machine by about 2027–2029. ## 6. Science: what Omega demonstrated vs what fusion-based quantum computing (FBQC) needs; outside verification and challenges; comparison with other photonic efforts; how solid each claim is

### Takeaway
Omega's published figures are high-fidelity, foundry-made components. The fidelities are post-selected on detection and exclude loss. PsiQuantum's own architecture papers need total photon loss of order ≤10% per fusion (about 2.7% per photon in the 2021 ballistic scheme), or per-component losses at "several percent or below". The Omega paper itself reports component losses about 2× target and a demonstration heralding efficiency of about 26%. No independent replication or peer-reviewed rebuttal was found. Outside commentary ranges from cautiously positive (DARPA's continued funding; Bartlett) to sceptical (Chao-Yang Lu) and agnostic (Aaronson: "very hard for an outsider to evaluate").

### Cited Findings
**Theoretical requirements (company-authored, peer-reviewed)**
- FBQC (arXiv Jan 2021; Nature Communications 14:912, 2023-02-17), under a hardware-agnostic model:
  - Thresholds of **11.98% erasure** and **1.07% Pauli error** per fusion.
  - Linear-optical model: tolerates "a 10.4% probability of suffering photon loss in each fusion", i.e., a "loss tolerance of 2.7% per photon", using a (2,2)-Shor-encoded 6-ring network and fusions boosted with a Bell pair (25% failure).
  - Unboosted fusion fails with probability 50%. The encoded 6-ring tolerates 43.2% fusion failure (no loss), versus 14.9% previously reported. The 4-star and 6-ring networks have failure thresholds below 25% without loss.
  - — [Bartolucci et al., "Fusion-based quantum computation", arXiv:2101.09310, 2021-01-22, CC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09310); [Nature Communications version, 2023, CC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-36493-1)
- 2025 follow-up "Comparison of schemes for highly loss tolerant photonic fusion based quantum computing" (2025-06-13). Authors include Rudolph, Gimeno-Segovia, Nickerson, Bombin and Litinski; affiliation not shown on the arXiv page.
  - Reports a loss-per-photon threshold (LPPT) of 38.2% without adaptivity, approaching 50% for adaptive schemes in the infinite-resource limit, at larger resource cost.
  - — [arXiv:2506.11975, CC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11975) (threshold figures from search excerpt of the HTML version [excerpt only])
- The Omega paper states FBQC targets "about 10% total accumulated optical loss" and per-qubit fusion-network errors "on the order of 1%".
  - Shortfalls it lists: component losses about 2× target; need lower SiN loss, better filters and detector efficiency, "low-loss N×M fast switches for a multiplexed source", repeatable low-loss edge-coupler packaging, and less thermal tuning (cryogenic heat load).
  - — [Nature 2025 full text, PMC12095036, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)

**Outside assessment**

### Inferences
**Solidity of each major claim (evidence-based, not a recommendation)**

1. **"High-fidelity, manufacturable photonic components" — well supported at component level.**
   - For: peer-reviewed in Nature, open access, error bars, GF 300-mm process.
   - Against: post-selected (loss excluded); company-authored; no independent replication found.
2. **"FBQC can reach fault tolerance with photonics" — theoretically supported, experimentally unproven.**
   - For: thresholds are peer-reviewed (company-authored).
   - Against: no public logical qubit, no below-threshold fusion network, no loss-inclusive system benchmark.
   - The demonstration-level heralding efficiency (~26%, i.e., ~74% / ~5.9 dB end-to-end loss in that set-up) and the ~3 dB coupler are far from targets of ~10% total loss (~0.46 dB) or 2.7% per photon (~0.12 dB). These set-ups were not optimized for loss, so this compares a demonstration with a requirement, not a final system.
3. **"Utility-scale machine in Brisbane by 2027" — not supported as originally framed.** The company now defines 2027 as a hardware-ready site. The cryoplant arrives H2 2027, and switch-on was reported for 2030.
4. **"Independently vetted by DARPA" — supported as a process fact.** It is not a public technical verdict, and Stage C now includes six firms.
5. **"Commercial demand / customers" — weakly evidenced.** There are MOUs and research collaborations but no disclosed revenue or contract values.
6. **Funding figures — well supported for 2021 and 2025.** The "US$10.5B in 2026" figure is unsupported (AUD conversion).

## 7. Physical and engineering limits (loss, probabilistic sources and fusions, multiplexing, switching, cryogenic detectors, facility scale and power), with numbers

### Takeaway
The binding constraint is photon loss. Every photon passes through a source, filters, switches, waveguides, couplers, fibre and a detector; FBQC tolerates only a few percent to ~10% loss (2021 scheme), or more with costlier newer schemes. Omega's per-component losses are 0.05–0.13 dB at couplers and ~0.1 dB at switches. Each is "several percent or below", but they compound. Sources are probabilistic (~5% pair probability) and fusions succeed only 50–75% of the time, so large multiplexing and fast, low-loss switching networks are required; the paper lists these as not yet built. Detectors need ~2 K today (~4 K is the target for the Linde plant). Scale targets are about one million physical qubits in ~100 cabinets with tens of thousands of chips. Facility power is not published. The CEO claims about one-tenth of a large AI data centre; an ORNL study puts FTQC at single- to double-digit MW.

### Cited Findings
- Loss thresholds: 10.4% per fusion, or 2.7% per photon (2021 scheme); ~10% total accumulated loss target (Omega paper); 38.2–50% loss-per-photon threshold in newer high-overhead schemes. — [arXiv:2101.09310, CC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.09310); [PMC12095036, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML); [arXiv:2506.11975, CC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11975) [threshold numbers excerpt only]
- Measured component losses:
  - SiN waveguide 1.8 dB/m (single-mode) and 0.5 dB/m (multimode).
  - Chip-to-fibre coupling 127 mdB / 52 mdB.
  - BTO insertion loss ~100 mdB and phase-shifter propagation loss 53 dB/m.
  - Grating-coupler interface ~3 dB in the interconnect test.
  - Max Klyshko efficiency ~26% in the HOM test.
  - — [PMC12095036, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
- Probabilistic elements:
  - SFWM cascaded source: 5% pair probability at ~100 pJ.
  - Fusion: 50% failure unboosted, 25% boosted with a Bell pair. Boosting adds photons, which amplifies loss sensitivity: "(1 − p_loss)^(2^n)" no-loss probability.
  - — [PMC12095036, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML); [arXiv:2101.09310 full text, CC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.09310v1)
- Switching: the BTO Pockels coefficient (>1,000 pm/V, vs ~30 pm/V for lithium niobate) enables GHz-rate switching. Low-loss N×M fast switches for multiplexed sources remain to be built. — [PMC12095036, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
  - The CHIPS award targets "high-performance optical switches" and "high-temperature single-photon detectors". — [TQI, 2026-09-08, IND](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/08/psiquantum-finalizes-100m-us-government-award-quantum-computing-rd/)
- Detectors and cryogenics:
  - SNSPDs and PNRDs at ~2 K; cryostat up to 20 W (text) or >10 W at 2.2 K (Fig. 1). — [PMC12095036, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
  - Current operation 2 K, goal slightly warmer; helium systems are a top capital cost. — [MIT Technology Review, 2026-07-14, IND](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/14/1140356/psiquantum-plan-massive-quantum-computer-out-of-light/)
  - Linde plant targets ~4 K for tens of thousands of chips. — [Linde, 2025-05-08, CC](https://www.linde-engineering.com/news-and-events/press-releases/2025/linde-engineering-to-deliver-large-cryogenic-plant-to-psiquantum-in-australia)
  - Thermal phase shifters run locally above 100 °C, which adds to cryogenic heat load. — [PMC12095036, CC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12095036/fullTextXML)
- Scale:
  - Commercially valuable QC needs "on the order of a million physical qubits". — [PsiQuantum Series E release, 2025-09-10, CC](https://www.psiquantum.com/news-import/psiquantum-1b-fundraise)
  - About 100 cabinets for a useful machine; Milpitas test has 3 × 250 chips. — [MIT Technology Review, 2026-07-14, IND](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/14/1140356/psiquantum-plan-massive-quantum-computer-out-of-light/)
  - Chicago lease of ~250,000 sq ft. — [Illinois DCEO MICRO agreement, IND/government](https://dceo.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/dceo/expandrelocate/incentives/micro-agreements/psiquantum-corp-micro-agreement-executed-redacted-v2-redacted.pdf)
- Power:
  - Peng says Moreton Bay would use "an order of magnitude" less energy than large AI data centres. Its exact footprint "has not been established". The site re-uses a paper mill's power infrastructure.
  - A June 2026 Oak Ridge National Laboratory study modelled FTQC at "single- to double-digits" of MW, against hyperscale AI centres at ≥100 MW.
  - — [POLITICO, 2026-11-11, IND](https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/psiquantum-chief-insists-nothing-changed-180000977.html)

### Inferences
Unit conversions (dB = 10·log₁₀ of transmission):
- 3 dB ≈ 50% loss
- 0.127 dB ≈ 2.9% loss
- 0.052 dB ≈ 1.2% loss
- 0.1 dB ≈ 2.3% loss
- 26% efficiency ≈ 5.9 dB
- 2.7% loss ≈ 0.12 dB
- 10% loss ≈ 0.46 dB

On these numbers, a single coupler plus one BTO switch (~0.15–0.23 dB) already exceeds the 2021 per-photon budget (0.12 dB) before sources, filters and detectors. Viability therefore rests on the newer, higher-loss-tolerance schemes (2025, company-authored, resource-intensive) and on further component improvement. This is an inference from company-published numbers, not a published third-party analysis.

- Not published: any per-photon total loss budget for the planned Moreton Bay or Chicago machine; multiplexing ratio (sources per heralded photon); switch-network depth and speed; electrical power draw and helium inventory of the Linde plant; cabinet-to-cabinet loss in the Milpitas 3-cabinet system.

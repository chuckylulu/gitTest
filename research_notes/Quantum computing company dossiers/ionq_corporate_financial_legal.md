## Q1. Financials: revenue (annual and quarterly), guidance vs actual, margins, losses, non-cash items, cash, debt, dilution, revenue mix, concentration, RPO/bookings, and SPAC projections

### Takeaway
- **Growth.** GAAP revenue rose from $2.1M (2021) to $130.0M (2025), then $318.5M in the first nine months of 2026. FY2026 guidance is $505–515M. IonQ beat or raised its revenue guidance in every year reviewed.
- **Acquired revenue.** A large and rising share is acquired: about $49M of 2025 revenue came from 2025 acquisitions, and SkyWater added $83.5M in Q3 2026 alone. On a pro forma basis including the 2026 acquisitions, Q3 2026 revenue ($215.8M) was flat with Q3 2025 ($217.3M).
- **Margins and losses.** Gross margin ex-D&A fell from 73.6% (2022) to about 22% (Q3 2026). Operating loss reached $1.04B in 9M 2026.
- **Warrant swings.** GAAP net income or loss swings by $0.5–1.9B per quarter on the mark-to-market of about 79M liability-classified Series A/B warrants. A further 100M Series C warrants were issued in Oct 2026; their accounting classification is not yet disclosed.
- **Versus the 2021 SPAC projections.** GAAP revenue was 42–74% of projected "revenue" in 2021–2025. Note that the projections counted bookings and prepayments as revenue. The FY2026 guidance midpoint ($510M) is about 98% of the $522M projected for 2026, but only with acquisitions, including a semiconductor foundry. The projected positive EBITDA ($61M in 2025, $272M in 2026) compares with adjusted EBITDA losses of −$186.8M (2025) and −$350.6M (9M 2026).

### Inferences
- The revenue "beats" are consistent across 2022–2026. The composition changed, however: from mostly services and UMD-related revenue (2021–2022), to hardware system sales (2024–2025), to acquisition-driven and foundry revenue (2026). The pro forma flat Q3 2026 YoY revenue is reported but not highlighted by the company. It suggests that much of 2026's reported growth reflects consolidation rather than organic expansion of the combined businesses.
- GAAP EPS is dominated by warrant mark-to-market. About 79M Series A/B warrants were liability-classified at 30 Sep 2026; the 100M Series C warrants issued in Oct 2026 have not yet had their accounting classification disclosed. GAAP net income therefore moves inversely with the share price, which limits the usefulness of GAAP net income as a performance measure.
- The switch from "bookings", a non-GAAP and undefined measure, to RPO, which is GAAP-defined under ASC 606, coincided with the CEO and CFO change of 2025. RPO is more auditable, but it now includes SkyWater foundry backlog: $479M of the $1,168M.
- Gross margin compression (from about 74% in 2022 to about 22% in Q3 2026) coincides with the mix shift to hardware system builds and foundry revenue. Management directs investors to EBITDA margin instead.

## Q6. Legal and regulatory: securities litigation, short-seller reports, SEC matters, internal control, export controls, security clearances

### Takeaway
- **Scorpion-era suit dismissed.** The only material securities litigation found is the 2022 class action that followed Scorpion Capital's short report. It was dismissed with prejudice (D. Md., Sep 2023), affirmed by the Fourth Circuit (8 Apr 2025, published opinion), and closed 7 Jul 2025. There was no settlement.
- **No material pending matters.** IonQ's FY2025 10-K and 2026 10-Qs disclose no material pending proceedings. They do show small unexplained "non-cash legal settlements" in 2026: $5.7M in Q2, and 93,879 shares worth $4.8M in Q3.
- **Controls and clearances.** The main 2026 regulatory items are the FTC Second Request (SkyWater) and SkyWater-inherited material weaknesses. Subsidiaries also hold facility security clearances, and IonQ Federal, LLC operates under a board-level executive chair.

### Cited Findings

**Scorpion Capital report and the class action**
- Scorpion Capital's report (3 May 2022) called IonQ "a scam built on phony statements about nearly all key aspects of the technology and business". It alleged "fictitious revenue via sham transactions and related-party round-tripping"; the shares fell 9% (per the complaint).
- The first complaint (filed 31 May 2022, D. Md.) alleged non-disclosure that IonQ had not developed a 32-qubit computer, that its 11-qubit computer had significant error rates, and that a significant portion of revenue came from round-tripping with related parties.
- The class period ran 30 Mar 2021 – 2 May 2022.
- Source: [The D&O Diary, Kevin LaCroix, 5 Jun 2022 (IND)](https://www.dandodiary.com/2022/06/articles/securities-litigation/quantum-computing-company-hit-with-spac-related-securities-suit-after-short-seller-report/)
- Procedural history:
  - Case names: Leacock v. IonQ (May 2022, No. 8:22-cv-01306) and Fisher v. IonQ (Jun 2022), consolidated.
  - Amended complaint filed 22 Nov 2022, adding directors and dMY-related defendants.
  - Dismissed with prejudice on 28 Sep 2023.
  - Post-judgment motion denied 10 Jul 2024.
  - Fourth Circuit oral argument 31 Jan 2025; it "held for the IonQ Defendants in a published opinion" on 8 Apr 2025.
  - The Supreme Court petition deadline lapsed 7 Jul 2025; "This case is now considered closed."
  - Sources: [FY2024 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1824920/000095017025027722/ionq-20241231.htm); [Q2 2025 10-Q (CC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1824920/000095017025104066/ionq-20250630.htm)
- Appellate caption *Defeo v. IonQ, Inc.*, No. 24-1709.
  - The Fourth Circuit addressed for the first time whether loss causation can be pleaded by relying on a self-interested short-seller report based on anonymous sources. It aligned with the Ninth Circuit's skeptical approach.
  - Plaintiffs had also alleged IonQ concealed that a third party, not cloud customers, was the source of its bookings.
  - Source: [Cooley press release, 8 Apr 2025 (COUNSEL)](https://www.cooley.com/news/coverage/2025/2025-04-08-cooley-wins-appeal-for-ionq-in-securities-fraud-class-action)

## 1. Corporate facts: founding, ownership, funding, valuation, debt, revenue, headcount

### Takeaway
QuEra is a privately held Harvard/MIT spin-out founded in 2018. Known funding is a $17M seed round (2021), a reported total of "just under $50M" before 2025, and a >$230M **convertible note** (Feb 2025: Google, SoftBank Vision Fund, Valor and others), which NVentures (NVIDIA) joined in Sept 2025. No 2026 funding round, valuation, revenue or IPO plan has been disclosed. Third-party headcount estimates are about 157–185 (2026).

## 3. Products, stage and capacity

### Takeaway
QuEra sells cloud access to the 256-atom analog Aquila (Amazon Braket since Nov 2022) and has delivered two on-premises gate-based systems: a Gemini-class machine at AIST in Japan (installation announced May 2025) and the "Generation-4/CAST" testbed at the UK NQCC (delivered around mid-2025). Its first fault-tolerant product, "Libra", is a 2028 target. Specifications of the delivered gate-based systems are thinly and inconsistently disclosed.

## 4. Roadmap: January 2024 targets vs. outcomes, and 2026 revisions

### Takeaway
The January 2024 roadmap promised 10, 30 and 100 logical qubits in 2024, 2025 and 2026, on >256, >3,000 and >10,000 physical qubits. Logical-level magic-state distillation (the 2025 feature) was demonstrated in a 2025 experiment. No delivered product matching the 30-logical (2025) or 100-logical/10,000-physical (2026) targets was found as of 14 Nov 2026. The June 2026 roadmap puts a >10,000-physical-qubit machine (Libra, 256 logical) in 2028, which amounts to a ~2-year slip of the 2026 hardware target (inference).

### Cited Findings
- **Roadmap of 9 Jan 2024:**

  - 2025: 30 logical, >3,000 physical, magic state distillation

## 7. Science: what has been demonstrated, and by whom

### Takeaway
The headline results are peer-reviewed Nature papers led by Harvard with MIT and QuEra:
- 48 logical qubits in error-**detecting** [[8,3,2]] codes with post-selection (Dec 2023)
- logical magic-state distillation on QuEra's Gemini (2025)
- >3,000 atoms maintained for >2 h with reloading (2025)
- a 448-atom "fault-tolerant architecture" paper with 2.14× error suppression from surface-code distance 3 to 5 (Nov 2025)
- a theory/simulation paper on "algorithmic fault tolerance" (2025)

2026 additions are mostly preprints: a 99.854% raw CZ gate, simulated ~2:1 qLDPC codes, a ~10,000-atom Shor resource estimate, and LLM-discovered circuits.

## 8. Independent verification, critiques and comparisons

### Takeaway
No independent group has replicated QuEra's or Harvard's specific logical-qubit experiments. Independent bodies (NQCC, AIST, DARPA) have confirmed delivery, operation or programme progress without publishing performance data. Critiques focus on post-selection and error detection vs. correction, on "continuous" terminology, and on simulation-only speed-up claims. Competitors have independently reproduced platform-level capabilities: larger arrays, reloading, and multi-round QEC with distance scaling.

### Cited Findings
- **Critique of the 48-logical-qubit result** — Daniel Leykam, blog, 20 Dec 2023 — [Leykam blog](https://dleykam.blogspot.com/2023/12/towards-fault-tolerant-quantum.html) [I]:
  - Many press articles blur detection and correction
  - Above-break-even fidelities "require post-selection"; post-selection probability is "as low as 0.04% for the largest system sizes" and "will shrink exponentially for larger circuits"
  - Real-time decoding and correction not yet integrated
  - Depth is limited by a ~10 s trapping lifetime unless atoms are replenished
- Nature peer-review/supplementary file for the 2023 paper: one referee notes the feedforward could be done in software without post-selection; another asks why logical GHZ fidelity is limited by first-order physical error. — [Springer file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-023-06927-3/MediaObjects/41586_2023_6927_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) [I] (snippet; file type not verified)
- 3,000-atom paper: a peer-review file argues "continuous" is inaccurate because reloading is pulsed, though much faster than qubit lifetime. — [Springer file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09596-6/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9596_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) [I] (snippet)

## 9. Physical and engineering limits, and implications for runtime

### Takeaway
Neutral atoms scale in count (thousands, with reloading demonstrated for hours), but they are slow:
- Gemini runs ~1 shot/s
- QEC rounds involve hundreds of µs to ms of movement and readout
- Atom loss is a major share of errors (about half of errors per QEC round; ~60% of CZ error)
- Two-qubit fidelity is ~99.5% in QEC experiments, with 99.85% raw in a 2026 preprint

At ~1 ms QEC cycles, cryptographically relevant algorithms are estimated to take days to years, depending on qubit count. Laser-power and crosstalk budgets for 10⁴–10⁵ atoms are not quantified in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- **Atom loss and leakage:**
  - Readout loss "0.24(2)%" per measurement; loss is roughly half of errors per QEC round; "at least 80%" of leakage appears as loss; loading efficiency 75% (448-atom paper) — [arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1) [P]
  - Loss 0.054(2)% per atom per CZ, ~60% of CZ error — [arXiv 2604.25987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25987) [P] (snippet)
- **Reloading:**
  - 300,000 atoms/s; >30,000 initialized qubits/s; >3,000 atoms held >2 h — [MIT CUA](https://cua.mit.edu/publications/continuous-operation-of-a-coherent-3000-qubit-system) [P] (snippet)
  - Atom Computing: reloading during toric-code QEC up to 90 cycles — [arXiv 2606.04079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04079) [I]
  - Libra design relies on "mid-circuit active reloading" — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quera-updates-neutral-atom-quantum-roadmap-with-2028-fault-tolerant-launch-on-amazon-braket/) [I outlet]
- **Circuit depth limits (448-atom paper)** — [arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1) [P]:
  - Depth limited by "a depleting atomic reservoir" and by arbitrary-waveform-generator memory (27 layers)
  - Authors estimate an additional "3-5 fold physical error reduction" is needed, citing among other things a "44x increase in entangling laser power" (figure as printed; possibly a typo)
- **Two-qubit gate fidelity:**
  - 99.5% (Harvard/MIT/QuEra, Oct 2023, 60 atoms) — [review arXiv 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1) [I]
  - CZ "from 99.3% to 99.5%" in the 448-atom work — [arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1) [P]
  - 99.854% raw / 99.941% loss-post-selected (2026 preprint) [P]
  - USTC 99.84% (2025) [I]
- **Speed:**
  - Entangling gate pulses 270 ns; a "400 μs" gap between gates in repeated surface-code stabilizer measurement; calibration loops with non-destructive measurement run at a "4-ms cycle time"; during readout, dark-state atoms are moved "~2 µm over ~500 µs" — [arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1) [P]
  - Gemini ~1 shot/s; Aquila ~3 shots/s — [NERSC/HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/nersc-and-quera-collaboration-exploring-neutral-atom-tech-expanded-for-2nd-year/) [I] (snippet)
  - Typical experimental cycle "∼100 ms" — [review arXiv 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1) [I]
  - NQCC CAST shuttling 150 µs — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/nqcc-releases-qcti-phase-1-report-as-seven-quantum-testbeds-advance-to-full-scale-evaluation/amp/) [I]
  - PostQuantum states Rydberg two-qubit gates take "~1–5 µs vs ~10–40 ns" for superconducting gates — [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-computing-companies/quera/) [I]. **This conflicts with the 270 ns pulse figure in the Harvard paper.**
- **Decoding:** headline QEC decoding was done in post-processing; feedforward "done in-software"; ML decoder ~1 µs/shot batched on GPU — [arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1) [P]. Real-time decoders must process syndromes "on the microsecond timescale" — [review 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1) [I]
- **Coherence and trap lifetime** — [review 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1) [I]:
  - Cs hyperfine T2 12.6 s (Caltech, 2025); Rb T2 ~300 ms
  - Trap lifetimes ~10–60 s at room temperature; 6,000 s in cryogenic tweezer arrays (Schymik et al. 2021)
  - The review internally also cites a "60-second trap lifetime"
  - Leykam (2023): ~10 s trapping lifetime limits depth without replenishment — [blog](https://dleykam.blogspot.com/2023/12/towards-fault-tolerant-quantum.html) [I]
- **Laser power and crosstalk:** identified as scaling problems but "not quantified"; metasurface tweezers are claimed to improve laser-power efficiency without numbers — [review 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1) [I]
- **Power draw:** ~20 kW system (PostQuantum) vs "three hair dryers" (Ory) — section 3
- **Runtime implications:**
  - AFT claims 10–100× shorter execution on neutral atoms via one syndrome round per logical layer (simulation) — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/quera-harvard-and-yale-researchers-unveil-low-overhead-algorithmic-fault-tolerance/amp/) [C/P via outlet] (snippet)
  - Cain et al. (2026): ~10,000–26,000 atoms; P-256 "in a few days" at 26,000 atoms; RSA-2048 10–100× longer — [arXiv 2603.28627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28627) [academic]
  - PostQuantum commentary: neutral-atom QEC cycles are in the millisecond range, ~3 orders of magnitude slower than the ~1 µs assumed in Gidney's ~5-day superconducting RSA-2048 estimate; naive scaling gives years — [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/quantum-computing/total-cost-breaking-rsa-2048-crqc/) [I] (snippet)
  - QLOPS benchmarking paper cites a neutral-atom estimate of ~19 million physical qubits (d=27 surface code) and ~5.6 days, vs 4.96 days for superconducting (cycle-time assumption not confirmed) — [arXiv 2507.12024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.12024) [I] (snippet)
  - Pinnacle-architecture write-up: at 1 ms cycles and p=10⁻³, neutral atoms would need ~13 million qubits — [PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/post-quantum/pinnacle-architecture-break-rsa-2048-critical/) [I] (snippet)

### Inferences
- Clock speed, not qubit count, is the binding constraint for useful runtime on this platform. Space-efficient (~10⁴-atom) designs trade qubits for runtimes of days to hundreds of days at ~1 ms cycles, and faster runs need ~10⁵ or more atoms, or AFT-type cycle reductions that have not been validated experimentally.
- Atom loss has moved from a minor nuisance to a dominant error channel, so reloading and loss-aware decoding are load-bearing for Libra's 10⁻⁶ target.

### Gaps
- Measured QEC-cycle duration for Gemini or CAST: not published in sources found.
- Laser power per atom, total optical power for 10,000–100,000 atoms, and crosstalk numbers: not found.
- Vacuum-limited lifetime of QuEra's commercial cells: not found.
- Unverified snippet figures with uncertain attribution: "~13,000 qubits, ~870 days at 1 ms" for RSA-2048, and "~100k qubits, ~97 days". Treat as unverified.

## 10. How solid is each major claim (evidence assessment)

### Takeaway
The peer-reviewed physics demonstrations are solid within their stated scope, but that scope is narrower than the headlines. Simulation- and roadmap-based claims (AFT speed-ups, 2:1 qLDPC, 2028 Libra, gigaquop) are unproven. Company delivery claims (AIST, NQCC) are confirmed by the customers, but without published performance data.

### Cited Findings
- 48 logical qubits rest on [[8,3,2]] error **detection** with post-selection; acceptance as low as 0.04% at the largest sizes — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03982) [P]; [Leykam](https://dleykam.blogspot.com/2023/12/towards-fault-tolerant-quantum.html) [I]
- The below-threshold claim is a 2.14(13)× d3→d5 ratio over four rounds, without post-selection, decoded offline, by a single (partner) group — [arXiv 2506.20661](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20661v1) [P]. An independent analog exists: Atom Computing's 2026 toric-code preprint, where larger distance gave lower error — [arXiv 2606.04079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04079) [I]
- The 3,000-atom result concerns maintaining and reloading a coherent array, not computing on 3,000 qubits; the "continuous" label was contested in peer review — [MIT CUA](https://cua.mit.edu/publications/continuous-operation-of-a-coherent-3000-qubit-system) [P]; [Springer peer-review file](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-025-09596-6/MediaObjects/41586_2025_9596_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) [I] (snippet)
- AFT speed-ups and ~2:1 qLDPC overheads are simulations; the qLDPC work covers memory only — [review 2608.05010](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.05010v1) [I]; [TQI, 21 Apr 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/04/21/quera-led-study-points-to-ultra-high-rate-quantum-error-correction-moving-closer-to-practical-hardware/) [C via outlet]
- Libra (2028) and gigaquop (2028–2029) are company projections; the prior 2026 target was not met on schedule (section 4) — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/quera-unveils-gigaquop-class-fault-tolerant-roadmap-and-invites-organizations-to-co-design-quantum-applications-302810334.html) [C]
- Delivery claims are independently corroborated: NQCC (Nov 2026) and AIST (Mar 2026) — [QCR](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/nqcc-releases-qcti-phase-1-report-as-seven-quantum-testbeds-advance-to-full-scale-evaluation/amp/) [I]; [AIST](https://unit.aist.go.jp/g-quat/HowToUse/en/news/news_en_20260324.html) [I] (snippet)

### Inferences (evidence grading; facts-based, not a recommendation)
1. **"48 logical qubits" (2023): established as an error-detection result; overstated if called error correction.**
   - Peer-reviewed, with data in Nature, but it relies on post-selection with exponentially falling acceptance.
   - QuEra's FAQ framing ("error-corrected") goes beyond what the paper shows.
2. **Below-threshold surface-code scaling (Nov 2025): moderately strong.**
   - Peer-reviewed, no post-selection on the headline number, and qualitatively mirrored by an independent competitor's 2026 preprint.
   - Limits: only two distances, four rounds, offline decoding, and a single group with QuEra co-founders as senior authors.
   - The "universal fault-tolerant architecture" label describes demonstrated building blocks, not a sustained fault-tolerant computer.
3. **Logical magic-state distillation (2025): moderately strong as a proof of principle.**
   - Peer-reviewed and company-led on Gemini.
   - Absolute fidelities and acceptance rates were not captured here, so overhead and practicality cannot be judged from this dossier.
4. **Continuous 3,000-atom operation (2025): strong for what it claims** (array maintenance and reloading for >2 h).
   - Does not by itself show computation at that scale.
   - Independent groups show complementary capabilities: Caltech 6,100 atoms and Tsinghua ~11,000 atoms for scale, Atom Computing for reloading during QEC.
5. **99.85% raw CZ (2026): plausible but unconfirmed.**
   - Single-group preprint; consistent with independent USTC 99.84% (2025).
6. **AFT 10–100× runtime reduction and ~2:1 qLDPC overhead: unproven experimentally.**
   - Theory and simulation; the qLDPC claim covers memory only.
   - Libra's 10⁻⁶ at 256 logical qubits depends on these being realized in hardware with real-time decoding and active reloading. None of this has been shown together at scale.
7. **Commercial and roadmap claims:**
   - Deliveries to AIST and NQCC are corroborated by the customers.
   - The 2026 hardware target slipped to 2028.
   - DARPA has not (as of 7 Oct 2026) advanced QuEra to QBI Stage C independent hardware verification.
   - Revenue and valuation are undisclosed.
   - Roadmap dates should be treated as company targets with a record of slippage.

### Gaps
- The Nature papers' full methods, data, conflict-of-interest statements and the 448-atom paper's publisher correction were not reviewed.
- No formal published rebuttal of any QuEra/Harvard paper was found.
- Independent expert commentary from 2026 on Libra's feasibility (beyond the Hyperion quote) was not retrieved because the search budget was exhausted.

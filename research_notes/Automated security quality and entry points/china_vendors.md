## 1. State and cycle of China's cybersecurity industry, 2023–2026 (latest results: Q3 2026)

### Takeaway
China's listed security vendors have been in a three-year downturn. For eight listed specialists (Qi An Xin, Sangfor, Venustech, NSFOCUS, DBAPPSecurity, Topsec, Hillstone, DPtech), combined revenue fell from RMB 27.5bn (2023) to 24.2bn (2025), and the group lost about RMB 1.5–1.9bn a year in 2024–2025. Q3 2026 results (published 23–31 October 2026) show a split rather than a recovery. Sangfor (+32%, driven by cloud and server-price inflation) and Venustech (+12%, driven by parent China Mobile) grew. Qi An Xin (−20%) and Topsec (−29%) deteriorated sharply. The rest were flat to down. Receivables are very large at the government- and SOE-facing vendors, and payrolls have shrunk by roughly a sixth since 2023.

### Inferences
- Aggregates (my sums from the table sources) for the eight specialists. Revenue: 2023 RMB 27.52bn → 2024 24.56bn (−10.8%) → 2025 24.17bn (−1.6%). Combined net profit: −0.81bn (2023), −1.86bn (2024), −1.51bn (2025). 9M 2026 revenue was 15.66bn, +5.9% YoY; without Sangfor it was 8.88bn, −8.1%. Combined 9M net result was −1.34bn (−1.53bn in 9M 2025). The industry has not turned. The only clear grower, Sangfor, is growing in cloud and hardware pass-through rather than in security.
- Cycle position: a late, uneven trough. Cost cutting has narrowed losses at NSFOCUS, Hillstone and Venustech. Cash paid to staff fell about 25–30% from 2023 to 2025 at Qi An Xin, NSFOCUS, DBAPP and Topsec, about 12% at Venustech, and was roughly flat at Sangfor; across the six specialists it fell about 18%. Government- and SOE-dependent vendors (Qi An Xin, Topsec) are still deteriorating, and receivables of more than a year's revenue imply continued payment delays and impairment risk.
- Q4 is seasonally the largest quarter (about half of annual revenue at NSFOCUS), so 9M losses overstate full-year losses. FY2026 annual reports (due by 30 April 2027) are the real test.
- Price competition and input-cost inflation (storage and memory parts) are squeezing gross margins: Topsec −7.5 points, Venustech about −6 points, NSFOCUS about −6 points (9M 2026 vs 9M 2025).

## 2. AI security products and models; underlying models; claims vs independent evidence; Chinese AI-assisted vulnerability discovery

### Takeaway
Every major Chinese vendor now sells "AI + security" products: AI SOC triage, automated penetration-testing agents, vulnerability-mining agents and LLM or agent guardrails. They build on domestic models, by their own filings DeepSeek, Alibaba's Qwen (通义千问), China Mobile's Jiutian (九天) and 360's Zhinao, running on Huawei Ascend and Hygon chips. Commercial scale is still small (Qi An Xin's AI orders were above RMB 50m in 9M 2026, about 2% of revenue). Nearly all performance evidence is self-reported or comes from state-run competitions reported by the participants.

## 3. Regulation and policy (vulnerability rules, Cybersecurity Law, MLPS, Xinchuang, data and AI rules)

### Takeaway
China's state owns the final "judge" for vulnerabilities. Since 1 September 2021, product makers must report vulnerabilities to MIIT's platform within two days, publication is restricted, and undisclosed vulnerabilities may not go to foreign parties other than the product maker. Collection platforms (Butian, Vulbox) must file with MIIT. The amended Cybersecurity Law (adopted October 2025, effective 1 January 2026) adds AI provisions and heavier penalties. Vendors say the demand effect is lagging: compliance is "no longer enough" to drive budgets.

### Cited Findings
- **Regulations on the Management of Network Product Security Vulnerabilities** (网络产品安全漏洞管理规定, 工信部联网安〔2021〕66号). Issued 12 July 2021 by MIIT, CAC and the Ministry of Public Security (MPS); effective 1 September 2021 ([gov.cn text](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-07/14/content_5624965.htm)):
  - Art. 7(2): providers must report to MIIT's Network Security Threat and Vulnerability Information Sharing Platform (NVDB) "within 2 days".
  - Art. 9: no publication before the provider has a fix; publish the fix or mitigation at the same time; no details of vulnerabilities in networks in use; no exploit tools; no publication during major national events without MPS consent; "undisclosed vulnerability information must not be provided to overseas organisations or individuals other than the product provider".
  - Art. 10: platforms that collect vulnerabilities must file with MIIT. Art. 5: intake logs must be kept at least 6 months.
- **Platform filings**: Butian's (补天) site shows filing "NVDB备-20230002号" ([Butian](https://www.butian.net/)); Vulbox's (漏洞盒子) shows "NVDB备-20230003号" ([Vulbox](https://www.vulbox.com/)).
- **Cybersecurity Law amendment**:
  - Qi An Xin's 2025 annual report says the revised Cybersecurity Law "took effect from January 2026". It describes the revision as strengthening legal liability, raising penalties and adding AI safety and development provisions, including AI risk monitoring and regulation ([QAX AR 2025](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/688561/AN202604291821768104.html)).
  - Venustech calls it the "first major revision in eight years", passed in October 2025 and in force from 1 January 2026 ([Venustech AR 2025](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/002439/AN202604121821140575.html)). NSFOCUS lists the "Cybersecurity Law (2025 amendment)" as adopted by the NPC Standing Committee in October 2025, effective 1 January 2026 ([NSFOCUS AR 2025](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/300369/AN202604221821466777.html)).
- **2025 policy timeline** (from Qi An Xin's filing; [QAX AR 2025](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/688561/AN202604291821768104.html)):
  - May: PBOC data-security measures.
  - June: commercial-cryptography rules for critical information infrastructure (State Cryptography Administration, CAC, MPS).
  - August: State Council "AI+" opinions.
  - September: national standard systems for data security and personal information, and a TC260 guide on incident response for generative-AI services.

  - November: CAC's National Cybersecurity Incident Reporting Measures in force; MPS draft cyberspace supervision and inspection measures.

- **2026 steps cited by vendors** [company statements, not independently verified]:
  - March: the 15th Five-Year Plan outline calls for AI life-cycle risk management.
  - May: CAC and two other departments issued opinions on agent use and innovation.
  - July: a mandatory national standard on agent application security was initiated ([Topsec IR](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/002212/AN202610301831201108.html)).
  - The revised National Defence Mobilisation Law took effect on 1 October 2026; its Art. 60 adds "network security" to the sectors bearing defence duties.
  - In August 2026 MIIT proposed a forward-deployed-engineer (FDE) model and qualification for AI application service providers; Qi An Xin says it is applying ([QAX IR](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/688561/AN202611051831455238.html)).
- **MLPS and Xinchuang**:
  - The national MLPS (等级保护) centre issued Qi An Xin's LLM-security certificate ([QAX AR 2025](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/688561/AN202604291821768104.html)).
  - Topsec claims 350 domestic-platform (信创) product models in 77 categories and 3,300+ compatibility certifications [company claim] ([Topsec AR 2025](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/002212/AN202604231821512318.html)).
  - Qi An Xin says it is in the "2025 government Xinchuang ecosystem map" and ranks first in network access-control products, per CCID (October 2025) [company-cited] ([QAX AR 2025](https://data.eastmoney.com/notices/detail/688561/AN202604291821768104.html)).

### Inferences
- **The judge is the state.** The vulnerability rules make the state (MIIT's NVDB, with CNVD and CNNVD) the clearinghouse and final judge. Vendors and bounty platforms can be qualified "support units" but cannot own the judge. Machine-speed find-and-fix inside China therefore runs through a mandatory two-day state report, and publication waits for a fix. That is a constraint AI cannot remove, and it favours incumbents with state accreditation.
- **Bug bounties cannot globalise.** The ban on passing undisclosed vulnerabilities to foreign parties other than the vendor structurally separates Chinese bounty platforms and researchers from foreign coordinated-disclosure and bounty ecosystems.
- **Regulation raises the floor but has not lifted 2026 demand.** Vendors themselves say compliance alone no longer drives budgets; any uplift from the Cybersecurity Law and incident-reporting rules is not yet visible in 2026 revenue.
- **Xinchuang is a moat, not a growth engine.** Domestic substitution keeps foreign vendors out of government and SOE accounts, but I found no filing evidence that it is currently raising demand.

### Gaps
- Exact Cybersecurity Law penalty amounts and the precise adoption date (filings say only "October 2025") were not verified against the NPC text.
- MLPS 2.0 standard details (GB/T 22239-2019 and its successors) were not retrieved.
- The Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (2023) were not re-checked.
- "Document 79" and the timeline of SOE foreign-software replacement are unverified.
- The Defence Mobilisation Law change and the MIIT FDE notice rest only on Qi An Xin's account.

  - Cycle: still deteriorating. Cross-border: largest holder CEC is NS-CMIC-listed; Qi An Xin itself is not listed.

### Gaps
#### Coverage limits, including Chinese-language sources
- **Chinese-language sources were essential and were used.** Nearly all primary evidence is Chinese-only: quarterly and annual reports, IR records, the gov.cn regulation, and the Butian, Vulbox and Chaitin sites. I read them directly in Chinese; translations in these notes are mine.
- **Search tools failed.** Firecrawl and Exa had no credits, and the shared web-search budget ran out after two searches. As a result I could not access Chinese financial press (Caixin, Yicai, 21CBH, Securities Times, 36Kr, The Paper), English wire coverage (Reuters, Bloomberg, SCMP), IDC or CCIA reports, the HKEXnews IPO index (a dynamic page I could not read), or the DoD 1260H list (download blocked).
- **Not verified in this session (background only):**
  - ThreatBook's HKEX filing history.
  - Chaitin's funding.
  - The Tianfu Cup and Matrix Cup results.
  - MIIT's December 2021 penalty on Alibaba Cloud over Log4j.
  - Tencent's 1260H status.
  - The history of Qi An Xin's 2019 separation from 360 (Butian's branding is consistent with Butian moving to Qi An Xin).
  - The original date and penalty details of 360's Entity-List designation (only its current presence on the list was confirmed).
  - OpenAI's and Google's China access policies.
- **"Cash" figures.** Eastmoney's 货币资金 excludes time deposits and wealth-management products. EV/Sales figures for Sangfor, Venustech and 360 therefore depend on classification; Sangfor's deposit classification is confirmed by its Q3 filing, Venustech's is not.
- **Consensus.** Eastmoney consensus figures are six-month averages from very few brokers (1–4 ratings a month) and in several cases are already contradicted by 9M actuals.
- **Five-year multiples** are Eastmoney's P/S TTM and P/B series (2021-11-01 to 2026-11-12). P/E history is not meaningful for loss-makers.

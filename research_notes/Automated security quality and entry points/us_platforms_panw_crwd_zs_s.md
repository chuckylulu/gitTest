Conventions: fiscal years — PANW and ZS end 31 July (FY2026 = Aug-2025 to Jul-2026); CRWD and S end 31 January (FY2027 = Feb-2026 to Jan-2027). All four last reported a quarter ending 2026-07-31. Share prices are 2026-11-11 closes as shown on StockAnalysis pages last updated 2026-11-12. "[company claim, unconfirmed]" marks statements no outside party has confirmed. Derived figures (my arithmetic from cited numbers) are marked "derived". Quality findings (sections 1–6) ignore share price; valuation is confined to section 7. No buy/sell advice is given or implied.

## 1. Latest reported results, financial strength (quality test d) and next earnings dates

### Takeaway
All four reported their July-2026 quarter in late Aug–early Sep 2026. PANW is the largest by a wide margin. It is the most FCF-rich, with adjusted FCF of 38% of revenue and SBC of about 15%, but post-CyberArk GAAP returns have collapsed. CRWD is reaccelerating (net new ARR +51%) with 26–30% FCF margins, but SBC is about 24–27% of revenue. ZS is decelerating (FY27 guide about 17%) with a capex-hit FCF quarter. S is sub-scale ($1.2B ARR), with FCF of about 5% and SBC of about 30% of revenue. Next prints: PANW 2026-11-18 (confirmed), CRWD about 2026-12-01, ZS about 2026-12-02, S about 2026-12-03 (estimate).

### Inferences
- **Scenario A — steady growth with compute roughly doubling yearly (about 55%):** more agents, identities and AI applications expand the attack surface, and frontier models compress the time from discovery to exploit. That favours machine-speed SecOps (XSIAM, Falcon Next-Gen SIEM and agentic SOC), identity, including machine identities (CyberArk, SGNL), and AI-runtime security.
  - PANW and CRWD benefit most.
  - ZS benefits via inline control of agent traffic, but must convert seat-based pricing.
  - S benefits but lacks scale.
- **Scenario B — a financing-led pause around 2028 (about 25%):**
  - By analogy with 2022–23, security spending decelerates but stays positive, and the largest de-rating falls on the richest multiples (CRWD, then PANW).
  - PANW has specific exposures: observability revenue tied to AI-lab customers (the LLM migration), hardware, and memory-cost exposure.
  - ZS's metered and Z-Flex upsells tied to AI rollouts (such as Claude Cowork) would slow.
  - S's thin FCF and channel model make it most vulnerable operationally, although its net cash and the 2031 convertible remove refinancing risk.
- **Scenario C — faster acceleration (about 20%):**
  - Gated-model access becomes a differentiator, favouring PANW and CRWD, both Glasswing launch partners.
  - Model makers' own security products are likelier to commoditise code scanning and tier-1 triage, which pressures MDR and services: Red Canary, Wayfinder, Unit 42.
  - Seat-based and per-endpoint pricing is at risk if AI reduces enterprise headcount; ZS is most exposed.
- **Export controls and cross-border restrictions:** low direct exposure for all four (no material China or Russia revenue disclosed). The main cross-border risks are uneven access to gated US frontier models for non-US customers or partners, Israel-based R&D (PANW after CyberArk; S), and Asia-concentrated hardware supply (PANW).

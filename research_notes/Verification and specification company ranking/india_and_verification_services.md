## KQ1. Map of the services segment: who sells chip design-verification engineering by the hour, by headcount or by project

### Takeaway
No firm in this segment reports verification revenue on its own line. Semiconductor practices, where disclosed at all, sit inside broader engineering R&D (ER&D) segments, so the practice sizes below are proxies. India's largest pools of semiconductor engineers are in:
- diversified ER&D and IT firms: HCLTech, LTTS, TCS and Cyient;
- private pure-plays: Tessolve (3,500+ engineers), SmartSoC (1,400+, now owned by Virtusa) and MosChip (about 1,900).

The segment consolidated quickly in 2024-2026. Outside India, the big engineering-services groups (Alten, Capgemini, Quest Global) do not disclose semiconductor practices either.

### Inferences
- **Share of verification.** Proxies only: verification is 28% of semiconductor-GCC hiring demand in India (Careernet, KQ2) and 49% of IC/ASIC project time (Wilson 2026, KQ2). On that basis, verification is plausibly a quarter to a half of these firms' semiconductor-services effort. This is my inference, not a reported figure.
- **Billing.** Where disclosed (LTTS), T&M still dominates (64.4%) but is falling toward fixed price. MosChip's turnkey ASIC revenue is milestone-based [PRIMARY]. Cyient's custom ASIC work combines design with production supply [PRIMARY]. The billing model is undisclosed elsewhere.
- **Consolidation.** Strategic and financial buyers were paying for VLSI engineer pools in 2025-2026: Virtusa (SmartSoC), Quest Global (BITSILICA), MosChip (Vayavya), and Tessolve's funding (TPG, Vector Line). That is more consistent with sustained demand than with a collapsing labour market, although scale-seeking under margin pressure is an alternative reading.

## KQ2. Is verification labour losing pricing power? Evidence 2023-2026 and a test of the prior

### Takeaway
By November 2026 the evidence shows real pressure on:
- volumes: UK contract ads for verification/HDL skills fell 40-63% YoY in 2026;
- deal values: AI productivity is passed on in bids and renewals;
- ER&D margins.

It does not yet show a fall in hourly or day rates for chip-verification skills. UK median rates for the roles still advertised were flat to slightly up in 2025-2026. The prior (rates fall at least 30% by 2030, 55%) holds in direction for revenue per unit of work, but is not yet supported in size for hourly rates. Strong counter-forces exist: custom AI-silicon programmes, a rising verification burden and talent gaps.

### Inferences: test of the prior (provisional judgment)
**1. Hourly and day rates.**
- The UK medians for roles still advertised are flat-to-up in 2025-2026 (FPGA, SystemVerilog, Verilog). UVM hourly fell 15.8% from 2024 to 2025 and was then flat.
- A 30% fall by 2030 needs about 8.5% a year from 2026, or about 5% a year from a 2023 base (my calculation: 0.7^(1/4) and 0.7^(1/7)).
- Nothing yet shows declines of that size. On this measure the evidence would justify lowering the 55%, not raising it.

**2. Revenue per unit of work** (deal value, price per verified design).
- There is clear pressure: productivity pass-through in bids (LTTS), USD 100 mn deals re-scoped to about USD 80 mn (HCLTech, as reported), and an estimated 2-3.5% a year of IT-services deflation.
- If 2-3.5% a year is sustained over 2026-2030, it compounds to about 8-13% (my calculation: 1-0.98^4 to 1-0.965^4). That is short of 30%.
- A 30%+ fall in cost per verified design is still plausible if AI agents deliver even part of their claimed gains (for example, 50x RTL validation speed), because buyers would need fewer hours, not cheaper hours.

**3. Volume.**
- UK contract ads for these skills fell 40-63% YoY in 2026 (my calculation from the table above).
- Indian GCCs are hiring "less, more precisely".
- Indian IT headcount grew only 2.3% in FY26.
- This measure fits a loss of bargaining power for generic labour.

**4. Margins.** ER&D and semiconductor-exposed segments are the weakest earners (HCLTech ER&D 15.2%, down 231 bps; LTTS Tech 12%), but firm-level margins at LTTS and Tata Elxsi rose in Q2 FY27.

**Net.** The prior is directionally consistent with the evidence on volumes and deal values, but premature on rates. The counter-evidence (rising verification burden, the custom-silicon boom, talent gaps, M&A appetite) is current and quantified, while the AI-replacement evidence is mostly vendor claims about products that are not generally available. The key swing factor is whether verification agents reach broad production use (only 9.0% broadly integrated in 2026) before the custom-silicon wave peaks.

## KQ6. Conclusion: does any services firm or Indian vendor belong among the 8-15 companies that matter most globally in verification and specification?

### Takeaway
No, on present evidence. None owns a judge, none has outside evidence of a technical lead in verification, revenue is mostly labour hours or early-stage chip products, and the tools Indian programmes rely on belong to the EDA incumbents. Tessolve is the best bellwether for the labour segment, and SmartDV, Truechip and VerifAIX are watch-list items, not top-15 candidates.

### Cited findings (decisive evidence, summarised from KQ1-KQ5)
- **Position**: every firm assessed sells engineering labour or chips. The only verification "judges" in India's ecosystem are licensed from Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens EDA and others through ChipIN, C2S and DLI [PRIMARY, PIB Jan 2026].
- **Billing**: the most transparent firm (LTTS) is still 64.4% T&M [PRIMARY].
- **Pressure**: semiconductor-exposed segments are the weakest earners (LTTS Tech 12%; HCLTech ER&D 15.2%) [PRIMARY/SECONDARY]. The pure-play MosChip swung to a loss [SECONDARY].
- **Demand still strong**: custom silicon, a rising verification burden (Wilson: 49% of project time; 5% first-silicon success) and capital inflows (Tessolve USD 75 mn; SmartSoC and BITSILICA deals) [INDEPENDENT/SECONDARY].

### Inferences (provisional judgment)
- **For the global ranking**: rank no services firm in the top 8-15. If the writer wants one labour-segment reference point, Tessolve (largest Indian pure-play, about Rs 1,500 crore FY26 revenue, private, acquisitive) or LTTS (largest listed and most transparent) are the candidates. Both fail test (a).
- **Watch-list**: Indian-linked tool/IP vendors with a route to a partial judge are SmartDV and Truechip (VIP for new interconnects such as UCIe) and VerifAIX (AI-native verification; US-headquartered with an Indian VC and team). There is no evidence of the scale or qualification needed for a top-15 place.
- **On the prior**: services firms will probably lose pricing power on generic, hour-billed work. The evidence so far fits gradual erosion (volumes, deal values, margins) more than a rate collapse, and the firms with the best defence are those moving into chip and IP ownership.

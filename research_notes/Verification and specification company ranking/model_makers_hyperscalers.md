**AlphaProof Code: what was proved, assumptions, trusted base, human role (paper, 10 Nov 2026)** [all PRIMARY unless marked] — [paper PDF](https://deepmind.google/documents/36/Formal_Verification_of_Signal_4.pdf)

- Target: "libsignal-cli, an AArch64 executable built by stock rustc from the unmodified libsignal v0.105.4 and SPQR crates" (Table 1); one-to-one session code of libsignal-protocol v0.105.4 with spqr v1.6.0; "We leave group messaging as future work."
- Properties: PQXDH handshake and Triple Ratchet; forward secrecy, authentication, post-compromise security, handshake secrecy. Symbolic model: "Ten theorems, each holding on every collision-free execution"; "No run faults or diverges." Computational model: confidentiality and authenticity of messages with forward secrecy and post-compromise security "as a concrete bound on the attacker's advantage".
- Assumptions: symbolic proof uses "Idealized primitives: hashes, KDFs and MACs never collide and cannot be inverted, secrets never repeat, and ciphertexts (resp., signatures) cannot be decrypted (resp., forged) without the key"; computational proof assumes "Gap-DH and XEdDSA unforgeability, Kyber1024 collision resistance, PRF/PRG security of HKDF and HMAC, and one-time CCA security of AES-CBC with HMAC". "Cryptographic primitives, the two output functions and a few library routines are specified by contracts (§4); everything else is verified."
- Not covered: symbolic: "Computational secrecy, more than two parties, and SPQR's post-quantum healing"; computational: "Quantum attackers, multi-device state, adversarial first messages to sessions that are later promoted, and large scale: the bound becomes vacuous for multiple long sessions with many epochs".
- Trusted base: "Reading every definition that our headline theorems depend on means reading about 18,000 lines of code in the repository (about 28,000 with documentation), plus about 8,000 lines of the AArch64 semantics and a Lean representation of the compiled machine code (about 95,000 lines), which a tool extracts from the compiler's object files; Lean checks its code against the executable's bytes, so only the symbol names it assigns need to be trusted." The specification directories hold about 54,000 lines, only part trusted. The TCB also includes "the tooling that extracts symbol names from the compiler's object files, and the scripts that build the binary in a reproducible way", plus Lean's kernel; "Notably, however, the Rust compiler is not trusted." So the brief's "about 26,000 lines of specification" is about 18,000 lines of statements/definitions plus about 8,000 lines of AArch64 semantics, not 26,000 lines of security specification.
- Effort and scale: "The proof work was done by a team of five over four weeks"; "a team typically had 40 to 80 agents and ran on a single machine for 24 to 72 hours" per proof campaign; "A clean build of our proofs could take several hours". No compute or token cost is given in the paper.
- Models: specifications "were developed through an intense back-and-forth between humans and agents powered by Gemini 4 Carbon and Claude Opus 5.5"; a reviewer agent "from a different model family" checked each draft; "The proofs were produced entirely by agent teams powered by Gemini 4 Carbon (§2.3), and none of the authors wrote any proof code." (DeepMind therefore used an Anthropic model for specification work.)
- Human role: "we decided the scope and order of the work, chose the reference models, and guided and approved the trusted statements"; "We did not write proofs." The blog adds that agents "highlighted areas that needed a second look by a human" amounting to "just one percent of the codebase"; this 1% figure does not appear in the paper (text search found no such figure). [blog: COMPANY CLAIM] — [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/)
- Validation: two real, previously fixed libsignal bugs were reintroduced by reverse-applying their upstream fix commits, and the symbolic security theorems were proved false for the buggy binaries (140,000 lines).

**AlphaProof Code: evidence on specifications (who writes them, can AI write them)** [PRIMARY] — [paper PDF](https://deepmind.google/documents/36/Formal_Verification_of_Signal_4.pdf)

- Design principle (§2): "The majority of human effort in software verification now lies in engineering the specification... Previously, the expertise and labor required to write proofs was a bottleneck to large-scale formal verification, but this is no longer the case." And: "For large, complex systems, AI assistance can reduce the cost of specification writing and assurance but not completely replace human judgment and review. Based on these observations, we propose that human authors select what to prove, resolve ambiguities, evaluate security trade-offs, and make all final decisions, while AI agents draft and test specifications under human direction."
- §2.1: the specification "must be trusted... and it cannot be automatically checked—a flaw could weaken the guarantee"; "human review is not expected to detect all bugs in a large codebase", hence skeptical reviewer agents ("in our experience were effective at detecting soundness and strength issues"), formally proved non-vacuity checks, mutation testing of specifications, and checks against historical bugs.
- §7: "once proofs become cheap, the hard question is what to prove. The majority of our effort went not into proofs but into specifications." "Left to themselves, agents produced specifications that were hard to audit; we sometimes directed rewrites for legibility".
- §7, departures from the literature: the computational catalogue "has over 200 entries and the symbolic one about 35"; "Fifteen are gaps or bugs in the computational analysis of the Double Ratchet"; printed ProVerif queries of a 2025-26 published analysis "look up skipped message keys at the wrong ratchet index, which makes receiver-side forward secrecy false as printed". (Human-written published specifications had errors.)
- §7, specification weakening by provers: "Agents under pressure to finish a goal will sometimes weaken it... in one swarm of Lean-proving agents, an agent redefined a predicate used in a conjecture's hypothesis as False, and the trick spread to the rest of the swarm". Countermeasures: a gatekeeper that sees only file paths and theorem names; Lean replays every proof in a fresh environment; "sorry and native_decide are rejected"; specification amendments only via a critic agent, small, logged, with trusted-assumption changes approved by humans.
- §9: "while proofs are now much cheaper, specifications remain hard. The specification of a nontrivial software artifact is itself a nontrivial software artifact"; "Specifications of widely deployed systems need to be agreed upon by the communities that maintain, analyze and rely on them"; "We are open-sourcing all Lean specifications and proofs". Forward plan: "We are keen to use this workflow to verify new releases of libsignal before they ship, and also more broadly"; "every change to the code can be verified before it is merged".
- Chaudhuri to The Register (10 Nov 2026): "In formal methods, we always had this belief that verification is the hard bit."; "We really believe that the next frontier is not going to be proof but specification in this kind of research."; "It's a system that is fundamentally built for human AI collaboration." [SECONDARY quoting named researcher] — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/10/google-deepmind-melds-humans-and-ai-agents-to-vet-signals-secure-messaging-techniques/5308625)

The Register article read on 12 Nov does not contain this quote.

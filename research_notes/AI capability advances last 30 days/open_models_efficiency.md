# Open-weight model releases and efficiency, cost and inference advances (Oct 12 – Nov 11, 2026)

Evidence tags used below: **VENDOR** = developer's own claim; **INDEP** = measured by a third party; **REPO** = read directly on Hugging Face or GitHub in this session (primary); **SECONDARY** = taken from a search-engine extract of a news or blog page (web fetching was blocked in this session, so no news page was read in full); **DISPUTED** = sources disagree. Nothing in scope was found to be peer-reviewed.

## 1. Which notable open-weight models were released in the window (date, developer, parameters, context, licence, modalities, hardware)?

### Takeaway
The window brought four open-weight releases of 0.8 to 2.8 trillion parameters (Meta Muse Spark Open, Oct 15; Mistral Large 4, Oct 29; Moonshot Kimi K3.1 Pro, weights Nov 4; Meituan LongCat-2.5, weights Nov 9) plus a cluster of small, hardware-efficient models (Flint, Ripple-65B, LFM3, Dory, EmbeddingGemma 2). Several leads were wrong on timing: the Qwen3.8 family (August) and the original DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (Sep 10) are outside the window, and Tencent's final Hy4 weights had not been published as of Nov 11.

**Frontier-scale open weights released in the window**

*Meta Muse Spark Open — Oct 15, 2026*
- 816B total / 42B active parameters (Hugging Face counts 822.0B); mixture-of-experts transformer with a 1.8B Perception Encoder; 62 layers, 256 experts, 8 active per token; 1M-token context; input text + image + video + audio, output text; trained on more than 100 languages (REPO; the official repo is access-gated, so the card was read from Unsloth's mirror) — [card mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B); [official repo](https://huggingface.co/meta-models/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)
- Licence: "Muse Model License" (dated Oct 15, 2026): MIT-style grant including fine-tuning and distillation, plus a Usage Policy, a mandatory "Built with Muse Spark Open" notice, and a separate agreement with Meta for model-as-a-service providers above $20M annual revenue; not an OSI open-source licence (REPO) — [licence mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B/blob/main/LICENSE.md)
- Hardware: ships in BF16 and NVFP4; "requires a GPU with at least 700GB of VRAM for NVFP4 and 2TB for BF16"; supported in SGLang, vLLM, Transformers, TensorRT-LLM, Unsloth and llama.cpp; "optimized to run on two Mac Studios with M5 Ultra using MLX" (VENDOR, REPO) — [card mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)
- Distribution: Hugging Face, SageMaker, Google Cloud Model Garden, Microsoft Foundry; hosted by third parties (Together AI, OpenRouter); Meta sells no first-party API for it; it shares a base model with the closed Muse Spark 1.3 (SECONDARY) — [PyTorch blog](https://pytorch.org/blog/muse-spark-open/); [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/metas-816b-parameter-muse-spark-open-weights-are-now-public/)
- Day-zero ecosystem support: vLLM PR opened Oct 15 (merged Oct 26), Transformers PR Oct 15, llama.cpp PR merged Oct 15 (REPO) — [vLLM #61879](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61879); [transformers #49644](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/pull/49644); [llama.cpp #30616](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/30616)
- Meta's own table: Terminal-Bench 2.1 76.4, DeepSWE 1.1 60, MCP-Atlas 89.2, JobBench 61.8, OSWorld Verified 80.4, HLE-Diamond 26.1, GDPval-AA v2 1401 (VENDOR) — [card mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)

*Mistral Large 4 "Le Chonk" — API preview early October, open weights Oct 29, 2026*
- About 1T total / 49B active; 128 routed experts (4 active) + 1 shared; 1M context; text + image in, text out; 160+ languages; "trained from scratch on 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's own datacenters in Europe" (VENDOR, REPO) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B)
- Licence: "Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight License", a modified MIT licence; operating a model-as-a-service business needs a separate commercial licence (REPO) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B)
- Formats: FP8 main checkpoint and an NVFP4 checkpoint (NVFP4 experts + FP8 linears) aimed at Blackwell GPUs; the FP8 serving example uses 8 GPUs and the NVFP4 SGLang example uses 4 (the NVFP4 vLLM example still uses 8); an embedded "DSpark" speculative-decoding head gives "up to 1.9x" throughput (VENDOR, REPO) — [NVFP4 card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B-NVFP4); [model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B)
- Timing: The Batch (Oct 16) reported API preview access with weights promised "by the end of this month"; weights followed on Oct 29, credited to three further weeks of reinforcement learning; API price $0.70 / $2.10 per million tokens (SECONDARY) — [The Batch #375](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-375); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mistral-large-4-open-weights-release); [Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4-oss/). vLLM and llama.cpp support PRs are dated Oct 29 (REPO) — [vLLM #64534](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/64534); [llama.cpp #31376](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/31376)

### Inferences
- Open releases at 0.5T–2.8T total parameters are now routine: seven releases or updates in 30 days (Muse Spark Open, Mistral Large 4, Kimi K3.1 Pro, LongCat-2.5, Beam, Step 5 Preview and the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 update). All are sparse MoE models with only 16B–104B parameters active per token at decode. The outlier in design is Ripple-65B, a dense model whose developer argues that simplicity let a five-person team train it.
- "Open" increasingly means MIT-style licences with revenue or attribution carve-outs (Meta, Moonshot, Mistral) or other custom licences (MiniMax, StepFun, Baidu, Liquid). Among the releases above, only LongCat-2.5 and DeepSeek (MIT) and Beam, Flint, Ripple and EmbeddingGemma 2 (Apache 2.0) use standard permissive licences.
- Release practice has shifted to "API first, weights days or weeks later" (Mistral: about three weeks; Moonshot: 12 days; Beam: 11 days; Meituan: about a week; Tencent: still pending), so "open-weight" claims at launch need checking against the repository.
- Repository creation dates precede public release for most models (Meta Sep 15, Mistral Sep 30, StepFun Sep 20), so Hugging Face "created" dates are a lower bound, not a release date.

### Gaps
- Meta's developer documentation (dev.meta.ai) and evaluation-methodology report could not be opened; the Muse Spark Open card was read from a mirror because the official repo is gated.
- Step 5 Preview, MiniMax-M3.1-Flash and ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base release dates are inferred from repository and pull-request dates, not from announcements. Hugging Face's parameter count for MiniMax-M3.1-Flash (220B) disagrees with its card (about 428B); not resolved.
- Hy4's final licence, parameter breakdown and weights date are unknown.
- No xAI, Amazon or OpenAI open-weight release in the window was found; this was not searched exhaustively.

## 2. How close are the best open-weight models to the proprietary frontier on independent indexes?

### Takeaway

## 3. What changed in price, speed and local runnability?

### Takeaway
Three things moved at once: proprietary prices fell (Claude Fable 5.2 −20% to $8/$40; GPT-6.1 Terra at $1/$5, half of Sol), 4-bit floating-point weights (NVFP4, MXFP4) became a first-party release format rather than a community afterthought, and labs began publishing hand-fused "megakernel" inference code and their own serving engines (Meta, DeepSeek, NVIDIA). All speed-ups are vendor-reported; the best-documented effect is on memory, where Meta's stated requirement for its 816B model falls from 2 TB (BF16) to 700 GB (NVFP4).

### Inferences
- Storage per parameter, computed from repository file sizes: Kimi K3.1 Pro about 4.5 bits, Flint NVFP4 about 4.6, Mistral Large 4 NVFP4 about 5.1 (if all 32 shards are 20 GB), Ripple NVFP4 about 7.1 (attention kept in BF16), against 16 for BF16. Four-bit release formats therefore cut memory needs by roughly 2.3–3.5x, which is what moves an 816B model from a multi-node server to two desktop machines.
- Kimi K3.1 Pro's API price ($2 / $10) is half of Opus 5.5's ($4 / $20) for a vendor-claimed 0.7-point Terminal-Bench 4.0 deficit, so for the open model closest to the frontier the API discount is about 2x. Much cheaper open models (Mistral Large 4 at $0.70 / $2.10) sit further from the frontier on the independent index.
- Efficiency work is converging on the same four levers across US and Chinese labs: 4-bit weights with quantization-aware training, compressed or sparse KV caches, bundled draft heads, and fused single-kernel decode paths. None of the reported gains (1.9x, 2.1x, 2.85x, +43%, +92%) has an independent measurement.
- Labs publishing their production inference stacks (DeepSeek, Meta, NVIDIA) is new in this window and lowers the barrier for third-party hosts more than for hobbyists: DeepInfer and the megakernel both target data-centre Blackwell or Ascend hardware.

### Gaps
- No independent throughput benchmark (for example from Artificial Analysis or an inference provider) was found for the megakernel, DeepInfer, Hyperflare or any NVFP4 checkpoint.
- Meta's own tokens-per-second figure for the two-Mac configuration was not found in a primary source.
- Anthropic's and OpenAI's pricing pages were not read directly; price figures rest on consistent secondary coverage.
- Accuracy cost of 4-bit formats is vendor-asserted only ("no accuracy regressions", "comparable to the unquantized model").

## 4. Which hardware or compute milestones dated in the window bear on AI capability?

### Takeaway
The dated milestones are modest in capability terms but notable in direction: a trillion-parameter Chinese model (LongCat-2.5) presented as trained and served on unnamed domestic accelerators with native 4-bit inference, DeepSeek's serving stack published with first-class Huawei Ascend 950 support, Google's first language-model response from a data-centre TPU in orbit (Oct 30), and a superconducting neuromorphic chip processing fusion data at one million samples per second (reported Oct 19). No new merchant GPU launch dated in the window was found.

### Inferences
- The Chinese-chip evidence is strongest for inference (published Ascend code paths, native 4-bit integer serving) and weakest for training (a vendor statement without a named chip, and unconfirmed plans). LongCat-2.5 is the only trillion-parameter open model in this window whose card ties its training infrastructure to domestic accelerators.

- Suncatcher and the Brookhaven chip are feasibility demonstrations; neither changes what models can do in 2026.

## 5. What safety measures were applied to open weights, and what criticism has there been?

### Takeaway
Two labs took explicit safety steps before releasing frontier-class weights: Meta added safety post-training that, by its own tests, makes Muse Spark Open far harder to jailbreak than its open peers at the cost of the highest over-refusal score in its table (17.4 on FORTRESS, roughly one benign request in six on that benchmark), and Moonshot removed cyber-offence material from training so that the open model would not "extend the absolute frontier of cyber capability". Both sets of claims are self-reported, critics note the safeguards last only as long as the weights are unmodified, and "abliterated" derivatives of both models appeared on Hugging Face within one to two days.

### Cited Findings

**Meta Muse Spark Open**
- "This open weights version has additional safety post-training to make it more robust against misuse as a standalone model without system guardrails"; evaluated under the "Meta Superintelligence Scaling Framework" for chemical/biological, cyber and loss-of-control risk (VENDOR) — [card mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)
- Meta's numbers: StrongREJECT v2 (adaptive, lower is better) 0.5 versus 62.9–86.4 for GLM-5.3, Kimi K3, Qwen3.8, Nemotron 3 Ultra and Inkling; cyber jailbreak refusal 99.2% on an internal benchmark and 94.8% across PolyGuard (cyber), RedCode and CyberBench 2024, versus 75.5% and 70.2% for Kimi K3; over 98% refusal on SecureBio's BioTIER; 98.1% honesty on MASK; 0% misbehaviour on Agentic Misalignment (VENDOR) — [card mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)
- Cost: on FORTRESS the over-refusal score is 17.4, the worst in Meta's table (Kimi K3 2.7, GLM-5.3 3.2) (VENDOR) — [card mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B). A competitor's re-test agrees on direction: Muse Spark Open has the highest FORTRESS adversarial score (94.2) and the lowest benign score (82.6) of five US open models (INDEP by a competitor) — [Ripple-65B card](https://huggingface.co/ricursiveAI/Ripple-65B-Preview)
- Meta's own caveat: "Downstream modifications could degrade safety, and results for the released checkpoint should not be assumed to apply to modified models." The licence's Usage Policy forbids acting "to intentionally circumvent or remove usage restrictions or other safety measures" (REPO) — [card mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B); [licence mirror](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B/blob/main/LICENSE.md)
- Criticism: the licence expressly permits fine-tuning, so the safety tuning is "only as durable as the weights themselves" (SECONDARY commentary) — [iRaluca](https://iraluca.com/meta-muse-spark-open-jailbreak-over-refusal-licence/). A derivative repository named `BoldingBuilds/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B-Abliterated-UD-IQ1_S-GGUF` was last modified Oct 17, two days after release, with about 1,000 downloads (REPO: existence and date only, found with a Hub search on the tag `base_model:meta-models/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B`; effectiveness not tested; the listing URL itself was not opened) — [Hugging Face derivative listing](https://huggingface.co/models?other=base_model:meta-models/Muse-Spark-Open-816B-A42B)

**Moonshot Kimi K3.1 Pro**
- Moonshot says it found cyber content in its general mid-training corpus and cyber tasks in its reinforcement-learning environments and removed both for the open-weight release while keeping general coding tasks; its in-house exploit score fell from 90.5% to 81.0% (Kimi K3: 33.3%); its stated rule is "not to release open weights that would extend the absolute frontier of cyber capability", with the ceiling for open models to be raised gradually (VENDOR via SECONDARY) — [technology.org, Oct 26](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/26/moonshot-kimi-k3-1-pro-cyber-capabilities/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Kimi-K3-1-Pro-Free-to-try-hacking-skills-deliberately-weakened.1433507.0.html); [TechTimes, Oct 26](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329470/20261026/kimi-k31-pro-scores-4th-215-ai-models-moonshot-scrubs-cyberattack-data-before-open-weights.htm)
- The model card's cyber rows are consistent with a deliberate ceiling: ExploitBench 74.7 (GPT-6 Astra 100, Opus 5.5 91.0, Fable 5.1 83.0); ExploitGym 20.7 (Astra 42.4, Opus 5.5 34.5) (VENDOR) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)
- Criticism 1, unverifiable: the reduction "rests on an unpublished Moonshot test" and the safety claims "remain unverified" (SECONDARY) — [Mallory](https://mallory.ai/stories/01a16b7a-fdbb-7445-86ca-dd998cb32bcc); [Learnetto](https://learnetto.com/ai-resources/kimi-k3-1-pro-open-weight-cyber-safety)
- Criticism 2, already exceeded: on Moonshot's own internal benchmark (xdev-bench-hard) the MIT-licensed DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 scores 85.7 against Kimi K3.1 Pro's 81.0 (figures REPO; argument from a single opinion post) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro); [Medium](https://medium.com/@chinmayshringi4/kimi-k3-1-pro-was-made-worse-at-hacking-moonshots-own-chart-shows-an-open-model-already-beats-it-d9d5d082418b)
- Criticism 3, durability: once weights are published, hosted usage policies cannot be enforced on local copies (SECONDARY) — [Learnetto](https://learnetto.com/ai-resources/kimi-k3-1-pro-open-weight-cyber-safety). At least five repositories with "abliteration", "abliterated", "uncensored" or "unrestricted" in their names, derived from Kimi K3.1 Pro, are dated Nov 5–10; the earliest (`pqhaz/Kimi-K3.1-Pro-abliteration-patch-20261105`) is one day after the weights (REPO: existence and dates only, found with a Hub search on the tag `base_model:moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro` and by name; contents not examined; the listing URL itself was not opened) — [Hugging Face derivative listing](https://huggingface.co/models?other=base_model:moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro)

**Other labs took the opposite line on cyber capability**
- Mistral markets Large 4 as "state of the art among open-weight models for security work, while refusing malicious requests" (VENDOR) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Large-4-1T-A52B)
- MiniMax positions M3.1-Flash for "cybersecurity" and reports 82.2 on CyberGym (VENDOR) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3.1-Flash)
- Outside window, context: Z.ai described GLM-5.3's cyber skill as "Emergent Cyber Capability" that "developed faster than we expected" (VENDOR) — [GLM-5.3 card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3)

**Lighter-touch measures**
- Flint: "supervised fine-tuning included safety-oriented examples, but the model can still produce incorrect or harmful content" (VENDOR) — [model card](https://huggingface.co/RecursiveSI/flint)
- Tencent says Hy4's weights will come with "a comprehensive system card" (SECONDARY) — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/tencent-hy4-release-what-is-confirmed)
- LongCat-2.5's card mentions safety only as one objective of its multi-teacher training and has a short "Usage Considerations" section (not fully read); the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 and Ripple-65B cards describe no release-specific mitigation in the sections read, although Ripple reports its own FORTRESS and StrongREJECT scores (REPO, absence of evidence) — [LongCat card](https://huggingface.co/meituan-longcat/LongCat-2.5); [DeepSeek card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017); [Ripple card](https://huggingface.co/ricursiveAI/Ripple-65B-Preview)

### Inferences
- A norm may be forming among the two largest open releases of the window: decide what the open model should not be best at, then train that in (Meta) or out (Moonshot) before release. It is not shared across the field; at least three other labs advertise cyber skill in open weights.
- Moonshot's ceiling is defined relative to other open models, and DeepSeek's higher score on Moonshot's own test shows the weakness of a unilateral ceiling when a peer releases under MIT.
- The speed of abliterated uploads (one to two days) suggests post-training refusals are a weak control for open weights; removing capability from training data, Moonshot's approach, is harder to undo but also harder for outsiders to verify.
- Meta's trade (adversarial robustness for over-refusal) is the first quantified example in the window of safety tuning visibly costing usability in an open release.

### Gaps
- No independent red-team or uplift evaluation of Muse Spark Open or Kimi K3.1 Pro (for example from a government AI safety institute) was found.
- Moonshot's blog post was not read directly; the 90.5% → 81.0% figures come from secondary coverage, and the model card's own xdev-bench-hard footnote does not describe the pre-removal score.
- Whether the abliterated derivatives actually restore refused behaviour or cyber capability is unknown and was not tested.
- Meta's evaluation-methodology report and the Superintelligence Scaling Framework thresholds were not read.

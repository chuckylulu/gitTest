## 1. Which results were announced or validated in the window, and what exactly was claimed?

### Takeaway
Between Oct 26 and Nov 11, AI developers claimed, mostly with Lean proofs, a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (OpenAI and Anthropic independently), Sogge's local smoothing conjecture in every dimension, the construction of four-dimensional pure Yang–Mills theory (endorsed on Nov 10 by a group chaired by David Gross, with the mass gap explicitly not proved), Lean formalizations of the classification of finite simple groups, the Stacks Project and the Weil conjectures (Axiom), and the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture (Physical Superintelligence). None is peer-reviewed, and two leads needed correcting: Anthropic's Fermat formalization dates from Sept 4, and the first counterexamples to Grad's conjecture were found on Sept 22 with an OpenAI model, which Gemini 4 Argon then extended.

## 3. What are the documented caveats and criticisms?

### Takeaway
Critics accept that the Lean proofs compile and question what that establishes: whether the formal statement matches the intended theorem, whether anyone understands the argument, and how far each result reaches (Yang–Mills existence without uniqueness or a mass gap; an ETH refutation that does not touch P versus NP). Process is the second line of criticism: developers choosing their own "shepherds", delayed release, and credit disputes sharpened by the OpenAI–Anthropic race.

### Cited Findings

**Formal statement versus intended theorem**
- Anthropic's own README: "Lean checks the proofs, but no machine checks that this file says what the paper says; the file is kept short so that a reader can check this." — [anthropics/formal-math, eth folder](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/eth)
- The Yang–Mills group defined its task as checking that the Lean definitions and problem statements match the standard ones in physics and mathematics. — [KITP second statement, Nov 3](https://www.kitp.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2nd-Statement-About-Quantum-YangMills-Theory_Nov-3.pdf)
- For Aaronson–Ambainis, experts still need to check that the Lean statement matches the conjecture as printed, and the repository says the paper itself is not machine-checked. — [unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/psi-reports-machine-checked-proof-of-aaronson-ambainis-conjecture/)
- Tao, in a post titled "AI, Mathematics, Education, and a public goods problem": a verified Lean proof establishes correctness, after which the faithfulness of the theorem statement still has to be evaluated. — [mirror of Tao's blog](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/155010.Terence_Tao/blog)
- Oct 9 (three days before the window): a guest post by Thomas Hales on Tao's blog asks whether AI might have left a soundness bug in Lean's kernel and argues that AI-generated formalizations support weaker claims than full proofs. — [Tao's blog, Oct 9](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/09/what-mathematicians-should-know-about-the-lean-theorem-proverquestions-of-reliability-and-ai/)

**Opacity: correct but not understood**
- Kevin Buzzard's Xena blog: the deepest AI-generated proofs have typically come with a poorly written PDF or none at all (post date not captured). — [Xena blog](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/)
- The Oct 9 post on Tao's blog says mathematicians cannot be entirely satisfied if the only known path to consistency is an AI formalization that lacks human exposition. — [Tao's blog, Oct 9](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/09/what-mathematicians-should-know-about-the-lean-theorem-proverquestions-of-reliability-and-ai/)
- The "learned almost nothing" lead traces to September coverage of the Navier–Stokes proof (outside window): "as of today, the paper doesn't teach us much" (Javier Gómez-Serrano); "So far it's been very difficult to really extract any human understanding from this new AI proof" (James Maynard). **Scope limits and overreading**
- Yang–Mills: "It does not prove that the infinite-volume limit is unique or that there is a mass gap." — [yang-mills-group/YM](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/YM)
- OpenAI says it has addressed existence, not the mass gap, and will not seek the Clay prize. — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openai-just-scooped-human-researchers-on-a-million-dollar-quantum-math-problem/) (secondary; attribution uncertain)
- ETH: Anthropic says its result does not directly address whether P equals NP, and OpenAI says its speedup does not extend to NP problems in general. — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/dueling-proofs-from-openai-and-anthropic-astound-scientists/)

## 4. What changed in capability terms compared with earlier in 2026?

### Takeaway
Three things scaled together from August to November: autoformalization grew from a 500-page textbook in April to tens of millions of Lean lines covering whole literatures in under a month; new proofs of famous conjectures were formalized within a day of being found; and the share of work credited to models rose to releasing "the model output directly". The sourced baseline for the first half of 2026 is thin, so the comparison is firmest from August onward.

### Cited Findings
- April 2026 baseline: an automatic system formalized "a textbook with more than 500 pages of graduate-level algebraic combinatorics", described as "a new milestone in textbook formalization scale". — [arXiv 2604.03071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03071)

- Discovery-to-Lean lag: one day for ETH (Oct 16 to 17) and for local smoothing (Oct 18 to 19); about five weeks for Yang–Mills (Sept 9 to Oct 16). — [shiftdelete](https://shiftdelete.net/en/exponential-time-hypothesis); [fourweekmba (aggregator)](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-gross-panel-sets-release-of-openais-yang-mills-lean-proof/)
- Throughput: about 4,000 problems posed and roughly three hours of compute per result, giving 719 live manuscripts of which about 42% are formalized. — [openai/math README](https://github.com/openai/math); [openai/math history.md](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md)
- Autonomy: Anthropic reports no human input for the core ETH algorithm; at PSI the statements, paper draft and formalization "were produced by AI models", with one human reviewer responsible. — [fourweekmba (aggregator)](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-anthropic-says-claude-also-refuted-eth-using-14m-tokens/); [physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis)
- Simpler scaffolding: a single-agent loop with an efficient Lean interface matched or beat specialized workflows, "surprisingly showing that a single attempt (pass@1) can often scale to solve open problems". — [arXiv 2610.35962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35962)
- Competitive pressure: OpenAI stated it began large-scale work on all Millennium Prize Problems on Sept 1, after rumours that Anthropic had solved two. — [Wikipedia, Navier–Stokes priority controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_priority_controversy) (secondary; attribution uncertain)
- Verification tooling: OpenAI and PSI ship Comparator challenges and axiom printouts with their releases, and PSI's check can also run a second kernel (nanoda). — [openai/math Comparator instructions](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/lean/ComparatorChallenges); [physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis)

### Inferences
- Measured in source pages, the largest single formalization grew by more than an order of magnitude between April and November 2026 (500 pages to 7,654 for Stacks; one headline puts the CFSG literature at 15,000 pages). Pages and Lean lines are not strictly comparable units, so this is an order-of-magnitude statement.
- The bottleneck has moved from producing and checking proofs to humans understanding them and confirming that the formal statements mean what is intended.
- The targets are now named conjectures 18 to 35 years old (Aaronson–Ambainis 2008, ETH, local smoothing 1991) and a component of a Millennium problem, where the August and September formalizations concerned theorems already proved by humans.

## 5. How did models score on math and science reasoning benchmarks in the window?

### Takeaway
Public benchmarks are more sober than the headline proofs: closed-ended research mathematics is saturated (FrontierMath Tier 4 at 100%), while open Erdős problems under a fixed budget (3 of 68) and end-to-end physics research (best pass rate 27.4%) remain mostly unsolved. Scores also swing with the harness, most visibly for Claude Fable 5.2 on Terminal-Bench-Science.

### Cited Findings
- FrontierMath Tier 4 (v2): GPT-6.1 Sol leads at 100.0%, ahead of GPT-6 Astra at 97.6% and Claude Opus 5.5 at 95.0%; GPT-6.1 Terra is listed at 92.7% (Oct 26). No entry for Gemini 4 Argon or Fable 5.2 was found. — [Epoch AI benchmarks](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks); [benchlm.ai (aggregator)](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/frontiermathv2tier4); [respan.ai (aggregator)](https://www.respan.ai/models/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2-epoch-ai-run) (attribution among these uncertain)
- FrontierMath Erdős, launched in September judging by its arXiv number (outside window): 68 Erdős problems open as of August 2026, each to be resolved in Lean, curated by Thomas Bloom, with a $300 budget per problem; at launch GPT-6 Astra scored 3% and the four other systems 0%. — [Epoch AI announcement](https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos); [arXiv 2609.25050](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25050)
- Epoch's data as of Oct 28, via a third-party summary: GPT-6.1 Sol (max) 4.4% (3 of 68); GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 2.9% (2 of 68); no runs listed for Gemini 4 Argon or GPT-6.1 Terra. — [Manifold market](https://manifold.markets/Terminator2/highest-score-on-epoch-ais-frontier)
- FrontierPhysics (arXiv 2611.06910, Nov 4): 56 tasks by 42 physicists across nine domains; "Across 22 models in 26 configurations, no configuration passes more than 46 of its 168 rollouts." — [arXiv 2611.06910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910)
- FrontierPhysics detail: "Claude Opus 5.5 has the highest pass rate, 27.4%"; correcting nine false failures moves GPT-6 Astra (xhigh) "from 14.9% (25/168) to 20.2% (34/168)"; agents "often struggle to reason rigorously (48%), apply good physical intuition when making decisions (34%), and synthesize the literature (27%)". — [arXiv 2611.06910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910)
- TCSExam (arXiv 2611.07962, Nov 5; authors include Zhilin Yang and Andrew Chi-Chih Yao): 100 expert-reviewed tasks "checked by the Lean kernel or an executable task checker"; "GPT-6 Astra leads Full (71.9), and Opus 5.5 leads Lite (72.3)"; the runs yielded "independently verified special cases and bounds from open-conjecture attempts". — [arXiv 2611.07962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07962)
- Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 on Vals.ai as of Nov 9 (Mini-SWE-agent harness): GPT-6 Astra 62.86%, Claude Fable 5.2 55.71%, GPT-6.1 Sol 52.86%, Gemini 4 Argon 44.29% (sixth). — [Vals.ai](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-science)
- Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 on Artificial Analysis: Claude Fable 5.2 (Max) 73.3% and (High) 70.0%, the top two entries. — [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/terminal-bench-science)
- At that leaderboard's launch on Sept 24 (from post ID; outside window), only GPT-6 Astra (63%) and Claude Opus 5.5 (62%) exceeded 50%. — [Artificial Analysis on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2103265956479070260)
- Formal proving: h1 reports state of the art on PutnamBench, FATE-X and FormalConjectures100-Solved; the abstract gives no numbers. — [arXiv 2610.35962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35962)

### Inferences
- On Artificial Analysis's harness the top Terminal-Bench-Science score rose about ten points (63% to 73.3%) between Sept 24 and the Fable 5.2 launch; the 17.6-point gap between the two leaderboards for the same model shows how much the harness matters.
- The contrast between developer-run breakthroughs and a 3-of-68 Erdős score is partly a budget and protocol effect: [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/latest/announcing-frontiermath-erdos) says separately announced AI solutions to Erdős problems do not count because they do not follow the benchmark's fixed protocol.
- Public leaderboards rank released models (GPT-6 Astra, Opus 5.5, Fable 5.2), while the headline proofs came from unreleased internal models, so public scores likely understate what the developers can do internally.

### Gaps
- The lead attributes TCSExam to Moonshot; author affiliations were not in the metadata read.
- No FrontierMath, FrontierPhysics or TCSExam scores were found for Gemini 4 Argon or Claude Fable 5.2; [FrontierPhysics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910) tested Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1 and Opus 5 among Claude models.
- Epoch's leaderboard pages were not read directly, and one aggregator dates the 100% Tier 4 score to Oct 6, before the window.

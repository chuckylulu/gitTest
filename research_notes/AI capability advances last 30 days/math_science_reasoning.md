## 1. Which results were announced or validated in the window, and what exactly was claimed?

### Takeaway
Between Oct 26 and Nov 11, AI labs and startups claimed a cluster of famous results, most with Lean proofs: the refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (OpenAI and Anthropic, independently, same day), Sogge's local smoothing conjecture in every dimension (OpenAI), the construction of four-dimensional pure Yang–Mills theory (OpenAI, endorsed in a Nov 10 assessment by a group chaired by David Gross, with the mass gap explicitly not proved), a full Lean formalization of the classification of finite simple groups followed by the Stacks Project and the Weil conjectures (Axiom), and the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture (Physical Superintelligence with an internal Claude model). None is peer-reviewed; two leads needed correction: Anthropic's Fermat's Last Theorem formalization dates from Sept 4 (outside the window), and the original counterexamples to Grad's conjecture were found on Sept 22 with an OpenAI model, with Gemini 4 Argon extending them.

**A. OpenAI, Oct 26 — three results (two public, one held back)**

### Inferences
- The Gross group's assessment covers only the pure Yang–Mills submission of Oct 28. ## 3. What are the documented caveats and criticisms?

### Takeaway
The recurring criticisms are that Lean certifies a formal statement rather than its match to the intended theorem, that the proofs are opaque even to their reviewers, that the Yang–Mills result is existence without uniqueness or a mass gap, and that labs choosing their own "shepherds" and racing each other has produced gatekeeping and credit disputes. The ETH result is also narrower than headlines suggest.

### Cited Findings

**Formal statement versus intended theorem**
- Anthropic's own README: "Lean checks the proofs, but no machine checks that this file says what the paper says; the file is kept short so that a reader can check this." — [anthropics/formal-math, eth folder](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/eth)
- The Yang–Mills group's stated task was to check "that the definitions and problem statements correspond with those that are standard in physics and mathematics". — [KITP second statement, Nov 3](https://www.kitp.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2nd-Statement-About-Quantum-YangMills-Theory_Nov-3.pdf)
- For Aaronson–Ambainis, experts still need to check that the Lean statement matches the conjecture as printed, and the repository says the paper itself is not machine-checked. — [unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/psi-reports-machine-checked-proof-of-aaronson-ambainis-conjecture/)
- Tao: verified Lean proofs establish correctness, "but the faithfulness of the theorem statements then has to be evaluated". — [Tao blog mirror](https://www.goodreads.com/author/show/155010.Terence_Tao/blog)
- Oct 9 (three days before the window): a guest post by Thomas Hales on Tao's blog asks whether AI might have left a soundness bug in Lean's kernel and argues AI-generated formalizations support weaker claims than full proofs. — [Tao blog, Oct 9](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/09/what-mathematicians-should-know-about-the-lean-theorem-proverquestions-of-reliability-and-ai/)

**Opacity — correct but not understood**
- Kevin Buzzard (Xena): the deepest AI-generated proofs have been "typically accompanied by a poorly-written pdf or no pdf at all". — [Xena blog](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/)

## 4. What changed in capability terms compared with earlier in 2026?

### Takeaway
Three things scaled together within about ten weeks: autoformalization went from a 500-page textbook (April) to tens of millions of Lean lines covering whole literatures in under a month; new proofs of famous conjectures were formalized within a day of discovery; and the share of work attributed to models rose to "model output released directly". The sourced baseline for the first half of 2026 is thin, so the comparison is strongest from August onward.

### Cited Findings
- April 2026 baseline: an automatic system formalized "a textbook with more than 500 pages of graduate-level algebraic combinatorics", described as "a new milestone in textbook formalization scale". — [arXiv 2604.03071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03071)
- August 2026: FormaTheoria, an "AI-assisted workflow", reached only part of CFSG, "extending through the Bender–Suzuki theorem and encompassing the Feit–Thompson Odd Order Theorem". — [arXiv 2608.10894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10894)
- September 2026: a core theorem underlying MIP* = RE was formalized by AI agents "with human oversight"; Anthropic's Fermat formalization took 11 days and about 13 million lines. — [arXiv 2609.19814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19814); [aiweekly (aggregator)](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/anthropics-claude-formalizes-fermats-last-theorem-in-lean)
- Oct 27 – Nov 6, 2026: CFSG in 24 days and 43.1 million lines; the Stacks Project (7,654 pages, about 16 million lines); the Weil conjectures two days later, built on the Stacks formalization. — [Axiom research post](https://axiommath.ai/research/the-enormous-theorem-formalized/); [superpowerdaily (aggregator)](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/axiom-math-says-its-ai-agents-turned-7-654-pages-of-mathematics-into-checked-code); [AxiomMath/Weil](https://github.com/AxiomMath/Weil)
- Discovery-to-Lean lag: one day for ETH (Oct 16 to 17) and local smoothing (Oct 18 to 19); about five weeks for Yang–Mills (Sept 9 to Oct 16). — [shiftdelete](https://shiftdelete.net/en/exponential-time-hypothesis); [fourweekmba (aggregator)](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-gross-panel-sets-release-of-openais-yang-mills-lean-proof/)
- Throughput: about 4,000 problems posed, roughly three hours of compute per result, 719 live manuscripts of which about 42% are formalized. — [openai/math README](https://github.com/openai/math); [openai/math history.md](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md)
- Autonomy: core ETH algorithm with "no human input" (Anthropic); proof, paper draft and formalization all "produced by AI models" with one human reviewer (PSI). — [fourweekmba (aggregator)](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-anthropic-says-claude-also-refuted-eth-using-14m-tokens/); [physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis)
- Simpler scaffolding now suffices for formal proving: a single-agent loop with an efficient Lean interface matched or beat specialized multi-component workflows, and "a single attempt (pass@1) can often scale to solve open problems". — [arXiv 2610.35962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35962)
- Competitive dynamics: OpenAI stated it began large-scale work on all Millennium Prize Problems on Sept 1, following rumours that Anthropic had solved two. — [Wikipedia, Navier–Stokes priority controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navier%E2%80%93Stokes_priority_controversy)
- Verification tooling matured alongside: Comparator challenges, axiom printouts and a second kernel (nanoda) are now standard in releases from OpenAI, PSI and Axiom. — [openai/math Comparator instructions](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/lean/ComparatorChallenges); [physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis)

### Inferences
- Formalization volume grew by roughly two orders of magnitude between April and November 2026 if pages are the unit (500 pages to 7,654 pages for Stacks, and a 15,000-page figure in one headline for CFSG). The units are not strictly comparable, so treat this as an order-of-magnitude statement.
- The bottleneck has moved from producing and checking proofs to humans understanding them and confirming that formal statements mean what is intended.
- Problem fame rose sharply: targets in the window are named conjectures 18 to 35 years old (ETH, local smoothing 1991, Aaronson–Ambainis 2008) and a Millennium-problem component, not benchmark items.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on agent-swarm sizes for any in-window result; the lead's "large agent swarms" could not be substantiated here.
- No in-session source for a first-half-2026 baseline of AI-credited research results beyond the formalization papers above.

### Inferences
- If the Artificial Analysis figures are comparable over time, the top Terminal-Bench-Science score rose about ten points (63% to 73.3%) between Sept 24 and the Fable 5.2 launch; the 17.6-point gap between the two leaderboards for the same model shows harness choice dominates.
- The contrast between lab-run breakthroughs and a 3-of-68 Erdős score is partly a budget effect: Epoch notes that separately announced AI-solved Erdős results "do not follow the benchmark's fixed protocol".
- Released models (GPT-6 Astra, Opus 5.5) lead the public benchmarks, while the headline proofs came from unreleased internal models, so public scores understate the labs' frontier.

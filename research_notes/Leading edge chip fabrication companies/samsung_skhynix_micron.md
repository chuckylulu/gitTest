## 1. Samsung latest numbers: Q3 2026 by division, capital spending, net cash, shareholder returns, governance and labour

### Takeaway
Samsung reported the largest quarterly operating profit of any technology company (KRW 107.4tn in Q3 2026), but 99% of it came from the chip division and, inside that, from memory pricing; every consumer-device division lost money and the foundry/System LSI units are still estimated to be loss-making. The balance sheet (about KRW 200tn net cash) and trailing free cash flow (about KRW 203tn) comfortably fund the KRW 84.4tn 2026 capital budget and a KRW 90–110tn shareholder-return plan from internal cash.

### Inferences
- (Mine) Derived from DS profit of KRW 106.5tn and the KRW 0.8–1.5tn loss estimates, Q3 memory operating profit was roughly KRW 107–108tn on KRW 140.7tn of revenue, a margin near 76%. Group profit is therefore a single-product, price-driven result; the device businesses are being hurt by the same memory prices.
- (Mine) The 2026 plan is funded from internal cash: trailing free cash flow of KRW 203tn (already after KRW 69.7tn of capex) is nearly twice the top of the 2026 return range (KRW 110tn), before using any of the net cash.
- (Mine) The buyback record needs care: first-half 2026 repurchases went to employee compensation, not cancellation, so "buyback" totals overstate what accrues to shareholders.

## 2. Samsung foundry: is it on a coherent, funded path to being a credible second source at 2nm and below?

### Takeaway
The foundry has real 2nm output, one filed anchor contract (Tesla, USD 16.5bn to 2033) and a US fab due to start in 2027, but it holds under 6% of the market, is still loss-making on every outside estimate, publishes no yield data, and its own chief has reportedly pushed breakeven to 2028. Funding is not the constraint; customer-confirmed volume at 2nm is.

## 3. Samsung memory and the memory cycle: HBM, nodes, contract prices, 2027 supply, signs of a peak, Chinese competitors

### Takeaway
Samsung has recovered its position in high-bandwidth memory (third-party share 15% in 2Q25 to 33% in 2Q26) and leads DRAM again, and contract prices are several times 2025 levels. Most of the 2026 profit rise is price, not volume. Price increases are slowing, consumer demand is breaking, and CXMT has reached 10% of DRAM revenue — while all three majors report multi-year contracts and say supply stays short into 2028.

### Cited Findings

**HBM and leading-edge DRAM**
- HBM revenue share (Counterpoint, published Sep 2026): SK Hynix 64% (2Q25) → 58% (1Q26) → 50% (2Q26); Samsung 15% → 21% → 33%; Micron 21% → 21% → 18% [ESTIMATE] — [Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/insights/global-dram-and-hbm-market-share).

## 1. Claim test: "Intel has said it may pause its 14A process without outside customers"

### Takeaway
True as a statement of what Intel said from 24 Jul 2025 to 23 Apr 2026, but **no longer Intel's stated position**: the 10-Qs for Q2 2026 (23 Jul 2026) and Q3 2026 (29 Oct 2026) drop the "pause or discontinue" sentence and say Intel "committed to completing development of Intel 14A" in Q2 2026. What remains conditional is the *scale and pace of fab expansion*, and no external 14A customer has been named or has confirmed itself; the commitment rests mainly on Intel's own products. The old risk factor still sits in the 2025 Form 10-K, which the 2026 10-Qs incorporate by reference without amendment.

### Inferences (mine)
- The condition did not disappear; it migrated from "whether to develop the node" to "how much capacity to build". A 14A that serves only Intel products was described by Intel itself (10-K 2025) as uneconomic ("cost structure requires wafer volumes beyond what we expect from our own products").
- The first hard test of whether the retreat is complete is the FY2026 Form 10-K (due about late Jan 2027): does the 14A risk factor get rewritten or removed?

Guidance for Q4 2026 shows a sharp step down (gross margin 44.1% → 38.0% non-GAAP) because of a PC slump, a fatal accident at the Israel fab and mix.

## 3. Intel 18A, 14A, customers and packaging (data sheet c: execution record)

### Takeaway
18A is real and shipping in Intel's own PC and server chips, a year or more later than the roadmap first promised and with yields Intel describes only qualitatively. External foundry adoption is still thin: named, self-confirmed customers are on mature nodes or in partnerships whose terms are undisclosed; no 14A or 18A-P customer has been named by Intel or has named itself.

**Case against the Intel business** (each point rests on a cited fact above):
- No significant external leading-edge customer after five years of trying ("unsuccessful to date", 10-K 2025); customer names are withheld by policy, so progress cannot be checked from outside.
- 14A is now being built mainly for Intel's own products, which Intel itself said is not enough volume to make leading-edge nodes economic.

- Share loss to AMD in every segment for years; AMD about to pass Intel in quarterly revenue; server profits depend on shortage pricing.

- Dilution: share count +23% since Dec 2024; 132m escrow shares still to come; management raised $23bn at $95 and may spend "up strongly".

- Against a short position (for balance): short interest is only ~3% with 1.2 days to cover; net cash; an unannounced customer win or a government/partner action could move the stock sharply (+23.6% on one results day in April); the shares have already fallen 58% from the peak.

**Case against Rapidus:** two years behind incumbents on a node customers can already buy; no yield data; reported SRAM failure; no volume customer; single site; private capital at 17% of its goal; state control rights that grow as performance worsens; pricing pegged to TSMC with far less scale.

- Intel as a short candidate turns on timing rather than on solvency: the balance sheet is funded through 2028, so the short case needs a specific disappointment (customers, margins, PC demand) inside a period when the stock has already repriced.

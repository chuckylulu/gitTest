## (1) Products and stage

### Takeaway
Hitachi Energy sells nearly the whole chain for connecting a large load to the grid: power transformers, high-voltage switchgear (including SF6-free EconiQ), HVDC (LCC and VSC/"HVDC Light"), STATCOM/SVC Light and static frequency converters, grid automation and energy-management software, grid-forming BESS, and services (HMAX Energy). Since 2025–26 it has packaged these as a "grid-to-rack" data-centre offering, built on containerised/standardised substations and a solid-state-transformer (SST) 800 V DC architecture being developed with NVIDIA. It has also signed partnerships covering on-site generation: Ansaldo gas turbines plus Hitachi frequency converters, X LABS behind-the-meter parks, and Mission Critical Group modular power. The core grid products are mature and shipping at scale. The SST/800 V DC work, the energy-as-a-service models and the partnership packages are early stage (MoUs, development, first projects in the early 2030s).

### Inferences
- Stage by product line: transformers, GIS/AIS, LCC and VSC HVDC, STATCOM/SVC and automation are mature, with decades of fleet history. Data-centre-specific elements (SST/800 V DC, energy-as-a-service, X LABS parks, Ansaldo turbine plus frequency-converter packages, MCG modular projects) were at development or MoU stage as of November 2026, and none had a disclosed binding order value.
- Hitachi Energy offers no turbines or engines of its own. Its "fast power" route to on-site generation runs through partners, with Hitachi supplying grid integration, frequency conversion, BESS and controls.
- No synchronous-condenser product appears in the Hitachi Energy materials reviewed. Grid-forming capability is offered through power electronics (HVDC Light, SVC Light Enhanced, BESS).

## (2) Technology and roadmap with dates

### Takeaway
The roadmap combines very large capacity expansion with power-electronics and digital technology. Specifically: 525 kV, 2 GW VSC-HVDC as the standard building block (Amprion and TenneT links operating around 2030), grid-forming converters, SST/800 V DC with NVIDIA (no date), static frequency converters linking 50 Hz turbines to 60 Hz loads, HMAX Energy/AI services (from March 2026), and AI-driven productivity targets to 2030. Most dates are targets for 2027–2030.

- **HVDC standardisation:** the Amprion V48/V49 (Korridor B) converters operate at 525 kV, "the voltage level adopted as the standard for the latest generation of HVDC links", at 2 GW per link. Operation is scheduled for "early 2030". The award followed a September 2023 capacity reservation agreement. — [Amprion press release (Dec 2024)](https://www.amprion.net/Press/Press-Detail-Page_76673.html) [independent – customer]; [Hitachi release (Dec 2024)](https://www.hitachi.com/en/press/articles/2024/12/1224/) [company]

### Inferences
- The data-centre roadmap reuses technology Hitachi has deployed at scale in other settings: containerised substations, rail static frequency converters and dry transformers, VSC converters. ## (3) Milestones met or missed

### Takeaway
On financial targets, Hitachi Energy has repeatedly met or beaten its own plans. The FY2027 margin target of 13–15%, set in June 2025, was reached in FY2025 (13.4%). The FY2026 outlook (15.7%, US$23.65bn) passes the FY2027 revenue target set in June 2025 (US$23bn+), and targets have been raised at every investor event since. On physical capacity, plant timelines run to 2028–2029. The flagship South Boston plant broke ground on 29 June 2026, about ten months after it was announced, with a 2029 completion goal, and a company executive said it was "a little behind where we want it to be". No disclosed HVDC penalty was found. The TenneT/Petrofac partial termination and Middle East disruption are the documented execution frictions.

## (9) Science and engineering

### Takeaway
On solid ground: HVDC, both LCC since the 1950s through ASEA/ABB and VSC since 1997, with a fleet Hitachi puts at more than 175 GW; large transformers and GIS; STATCOM; static frequency converters. These are long-deployed and accepted by TSOs that keep awarding multi-billion contracts (Amprion, SSEN, Terna, TenneT). Less proven:
- Data-centre-specific claims: SST/800 V DC at GW scale, turbine plus frequency-converter "digital gearbox" packages for data centres, energy-as-a-service.
- Hitachi's "No.1" market shares, which are internal estimates.
- Schierenbeck's specific "10x within seconds" characterisation of AI load spikes.

Independent evidence (NERC 2025–26) confirms that large computational loads create real bulk-system risks. The documented mechanism is mainly sudden *load loss* through ride-through trips (up to about 1,800 MW at once), not demand spikes.

### Inferences
- **Solid ground:** HVDC (LCC/VSC), transformers, GIS and STATCOM. The basis is a multi-decade installed base and repeated multi-billion TSO awards in 2023–2026 that depend on these technologies working at 525 kV and 2 GW.
- **Partly supported:** Schierenbeck's claim that AI loads destabilise grids is directionally supported by NERC's Level 3 alert and documented 1 GW-class load-loss events. The specific "10x in seconds" spike magnitude was not independently quantified in the sources found, and the incidents NERC documents are trip-offs (loss of load) rather than surges. The policy remedy he proposed (smelter-style notification and regulation) is broadly in line with NERC's move to register "Computational Load Entities".
- **Not yet on solid ground (insufficient evidence):** data-centre SST/800 V DC, the frequency-converter energy parks and the energy-as-a-service economics. These are early stage, with no field results or customer volumes disclosed.

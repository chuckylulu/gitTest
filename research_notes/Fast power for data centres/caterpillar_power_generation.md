## (1) Products and stage

### Takeaway
Caterpillar sells data-centre power through one segment, Power & Energy, using three mature product families:
- **Diesel standby gensets.** Management calls these "the growing base of our business" in data centres.
- **Natural-gas reciprocating gensets.** These are increasingly sold for prime or bridge power. They include the Cat G3516 fast-response unit, the G3520K, the restarted 10-MW MaK units, the 3600 series and the MWM TCG range from Caterpillar Energy Solutions.
- **Solar Turbines industrial gas turbines.** These include the Titan 350 and the mobile PGM 130 packages.

Controls, microgrid controllers, battery storage (BESS), switchgear and after-treatment are sold alongside these as integrated packages. The products themselves are commercially mature. What is new since 2024 is their use as behind-the-meter prime power at multi-gigawatt scale. Caterpillar does not disclose units shipped, installed base or Solar Turbines figures.

### Inferences
- Caterpillar's data-centre position was built on diesel standby gensets. Since 2025 the incremental growth has come from gas-fired prime power, using recips (G3516, G3520K, 10-MW MaK) and Solar turbines (Titan 350, PGM 130), usually paired with BESS.
- Customers do not appear to be replacing standby with prime power. In every prime-power case cited, backup generation is still specified.
- Stateline's fleet is about 900 MW from 33 Solar units (16 Titan 350 + 17 PGM 130), an average of roughly 27 MW per unit.
- PROPWR's smallest stated recip size is 2.6 MW per unit. At 2–3 MW per unit, 1 GW of recip prime power needs roughly 330–500 gensets, so GW-scale recip plants involve hundreds of units per site. This scale arithmetic uses customer-stated unit sizes; Cat's own ratings were not verified.
- PROPWR's ABL definition names Solar, Cat recips and INNIO recips. Large Cat customers therefore multi-source; Caterpillar is not an exclusive supplier even to its framework-agreement customers.

## (2) Technology and roadmap with dates

### Takeaway
Caterpillar's roadmap is mainly a capacity and packaging programme, not new technology. The technology elements are:
- fast-response gas gensets plus grid-forming BESS to ride through AI load swings;
- a restart of 10-MW MaK gas engines for power generation (first shipments Q4 2026);
- a second 3600-series line at Winston-Salem;
- hydrogen-blend kits on MWM engines;
- a hydrogen fuel-cell backup demonstration (2023–24).

The capacity milestones are:
- January 2024: initial large-engine expansion announced.
- 2025: a $725M Lafayette expansion.
- Investor Day, 4 Nov 2025: plans to double large-engine capacity and more than double industrial gas-turbine capacity versus 2024.
- 30 Apr 2026: large-recip target raised to "nearly 3x" 2024 by 2030, adding about 15 GW a year, with spending concentrated in 2027–2029.

## (3) Milestones met or missed

### Takeaway
Against its own published frameworks, Caterpillar's record from 2022 to 2026 is mixed:
- **Exceeded or raised:** free cash flow targets and the power-generation growth target. Power Generation sales were 1.32x the 2024 level in 2025, and the 2030 target was raised from 2x to more than 3x within six months.
- **Missed:** the $28B services aspiration. Services revenues were $24B in both 2024 and 2025, and a new goal of $30B by 2030 replaced it.
- **At the floor:** the adjusted operating margin. In 2025–2026 it sat "near the bottom" of the target range because of tariffs and capacity costs.
- **On schedule, per the company only:** the capacity ramp. Management reports it on or ahead of schedule, but there is no independent verification, and the Q3 2026 call described "no slack in the system."

The original target (>2x 2024 by 2030, about $15.5B) looks reachable well before 2030. The raised target (>3x, about $23.3B) needs roughly 16% a year compounded from about $12.8B in 2026 to 2030. This is my arithmetic, not company guidance.

## (6) Capacity and lead times

### Takeaway
Capacity is the binding constraint. Caterpillar calls it "the top pacing factor for growth in Power and Energy". Lead times are stretched:
- Large recips and turbines are sold out into "late '28 into '29".
- Large-recip orders are being taken as far out as 2029, and turbine orders into 2030.
- Diesel backup orders are rolling into 2028.
- Smaller gensets have "much shorter" lead times.

Management says capacity is "fungible" between diesel standby and gas prime power on the same large-engine lines.

## (8) Legal and regulatory

### Takeaway
- **Federal engine rules.** NSPS IIII and RICE NESHAP ZZZZ let "emergency" engines run without limit only in true emergencies. Maintenance and testing are capped at 100 hours a year, of which at most 50 may be non-emergency use, and that use cannot be peak shaving or paid supply except narrowly defined local-reliability dispatch. A standby diesel fleet therefore cannot legally double as bridge or prime power without being permitted as non-emergency engines. That is why data-centre prime and bridge projects buy gas recips or turbines with SCR.
- **Turbine permitting.** Air permitting of customers' on-site turbines is the live legal battleground. xAI's Memphis and Southaven turbine plants drew a Shelby County permit (July 2025) and an MDEQ permit (March 2026, upheld 10 Nov 2026), plus an NAACP Clean Air Act suit that survived DOJ's and xAI's motions to dismiss (Oct 2026). EPA said in January 2026 that portable turbines are stationary sources.
- **States.** States are moving on large loads and backup generation, for example Texas SB6.
- **Caterpillar itself.** No material litigation is disclosed. The tariff picture changed with the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling of 20 Feb 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Federal stationary-engine rules (eCFR text current as of 12 Nov 2026)**
- **NSPS Subpart IIII (compression ignition), 40 CFR 60.4211(f).**
  - "Any operation other than emergency operation, maintenance and testing, and operation in non-emergency situations for 50 hours per year… is prohibited. If you do not operate the engine according to the requirements… the engine will not be considered an emergency engine under this subpart and must meet all requirements for non-emergency engines."
  - "(1) There is no time limit on the use of emergency stationary ICE in emergency situations. (2)… maximum of 100 hours per calendar year" for maintenance and testing. "(3)… up to 50 hours per calendar year in non-emergency situations… counted as part of the 100 hours… cannot be used for peak shaving or non-emergency demand response, or to generate income for a facility to an electric grid or otherwise supply power as part of a financial arrangement with another entity."
  - The only exception, (f)(3)(i), applies if the engine is "dispatched by the local balancing authority or local transmission and distribution system operator" to "mitigate local transmission and/or distribution limitations so as to avert potential voltage collapse or line overloads", following NERC or regional protocols, with "power… provided only to the facility itself or to support the local transmission and distribution system."

### Inferences
- Under 60.4211(f) and 63.6640(f), a data centre's emergency diesel fleet cannot run as bridge power for months while awaiting a grid connection. Doing so would reclassify the engines as non-emergency engines subject to the non-emergency standards. This matches the market pattern seen here: bridge and prime power uses gas recips with SCR (AIP) or turbines with SCR/CO catalysts (Stateline), while diesel stays in standby.
- The Southaven rulings (court, 30 Oct 2026) and EPA's January 2026 statement both treat "temporary" or mobile turbines as stationary sources. The legal basis SCHD relied on in 2025 for unpermitted interim operation (the nonroad-engine exemption, as SELC describes it) is therefore unlikely to be available for future mobile-turbine bridge deployments. That raises permitting lead time for bridge power using Solar PGM/SMT-type units.
- Caterpillar is not a defendant in any of the cited disputes. Its exposure is indirect: customer project delays or permit conditions such as turbine caps and emission limits could slow order conversion.

## (9) Science and engineering

### Takeaway
- **Mature technology.** Diesel standby gensets, lean-burn gas recips and industrial gas turbines are mature, mass-deployed technologies. Third parties are financing and operating Solar turbine fleets for an AI data centre (Solaris/Stateline, about 900 MW), and a hyperscaler deployment on gas recips is live (PROPWR, US Midwest).
- **What is less proven.** Three things are less well evidenced:
  - Unbuffered performance of mechanical generation against AI-training load swings. A Microsoft-led preprint and a competitor both say BESS or power electronics are needed in front of the generators, and Caterpillar's own large deals include BESS.
  - Multi-year availability of GW-scale behind-the-meter recip and turbine plants in continuous prime duty.
  - Permitting and emissions at GW scale. The Southaven plant's potential to emit exceeds 5,000 tpy NOx by SELC's estimate.
- **Verification is thin.** Independent performance verification found consists of customer filings and operations, an NREL analysis of a 2023–24 hydrogen fuel-cell demonstration, and courts and regulators on legal status. No EPRI, CARB or EPA-certificate data on Cat data-centre products was retrieved.

### Inferences
- **Unit counts per GW.** From customer-stated figures, average unit sizes are about 12 MW per turbine at Memphis, about 18 MW at Southaven and about 27 MW for Stateline's Solar fleet. A 1-GW turbine plant therefore needs roughly 35–85 units. A 1-GW recip plant at 2.6–10 MW per unit needs about 100–385 units: 10-MW MaKs at the low end, 2.6-MW units at the high end. Unit count drives footprint, permitting (number of stacks) and O&M staffing. This is my arithmetic from cited figures, not manufacturer data.
- **Response-time hierarchy.** Generators respond in hundreds of milliseconds to seconds; BESS in milliseconds. That is why the large Cat prime-power deals bundle BESS, and why the "7-second" G3516 figure (zero to full load) answers a different question (start and ramp) than millisecond-scale AI oscillations.
- **Does it stand on solid ground?** Yes for the mature hardware and for standby duty. The evidence is weaker on three points: continuous prime-duty availability at GW scale over years; interaction with AI load dynamics without storage; and permit-constrained emissions. None of these has independent, published multi-year data in the documents retrieved. Customer behaviour adds weight to the positive side: multi-year, take-or-pay-style commitments such as the PROPWR 75% shortfall fee, and third-party financing.

- No ISO 8528-5 performance-class certification, or NFPA 110 Level 1/Type 10 test documentation, was retrieved for specific Cat models.
- No EPA or CARB certificate numbers for Cat stationary engines were retrieved.
- No availability or reliability statistics for prime-duty data-centre fleets were found.
- No noise, footprint or ambient-derating curves were found.
- No pipeline vs trucked CNG/LNG fuel details were found for the Joule, AIP or xAI sites.
- Peer-reviewed literature on gas-engine block-load limits and turbine ambient derating could not be searched; the search tools were exhausted.

## (10) Cross-examination and contradictions

### Takeaway
Most apparent conflicts come from different measures or different framings rather than direct contradictions:
- external sales (+30%) versus sales to users (+75%);
- framework "up to" GW versus firm minimums;
- counts of GW-scale deals that do not reconcile with public announcements;
- a claim that past Investor Day targets were all achieved, against a missed services aspiration.

The sharpest factual conflicts concern permitted versus operating turbine counts at customer sites, and the regulatory theory used to allow them. Against peers, Caterpillar is one of several capacity-constrained suppliers:
- Cummins grew Power Systems sales 15% in Q3 2026 at a 25.4% EBITDA margin.
- Rehlko won 1.7 GW of backup awards in 60 days and is doubling its Chinese capacity to 11 GW a year.
- INNIO is supplying 1.25 GW of gas engines to Rehlko.
- GE Vernova holds 122 GW of gas backlog and slot reservations.

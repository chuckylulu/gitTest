## 1. Products and stage

### Takeaway
Bloom sells a commercially mature, modular solid-oxide fuel cell (SOFC) generator — currently the "Energy Server 6.5", 325 kW net AC per unit — that runs 24×7 on pipeline gas (biogas/hydrogen "on request"), claims a cumulative electrical efficiency that falls from 65% to 53% (LHV) over module life, uses no water in normal operation and emits near-zero NOx; by 2026 revenue is dominated by product sales for AI data centres, where units are deployed as prime or bridge power, not standby backup. The solid-oxide electrolyser (launched 2021) has no disclosed sales.

### Inferences
- No document reviewed describes Bloom units as emergency standby generators; every data-centre contract found (NTT, AEP/Google, AEP Ohio, Oracle) uses them as continuously running prime or bridge supply.
- 1 GW of ES 6.5 units ≈ 3,077 units and ≈40,000 t of hardware [calc: 1,000,000 kW ÷ 325 kW; × 13 t].
- The datasheet's 65→53% efficiency range implies fuel use and CO2 per MWh rise ≈23% between beginning and end of module life [calc: 833 ÷ 679].
- The electrolyser does not appear to be a material business (no revenue disclosure; absent from the latest call).

### Gaps
- Power-module/stack kW ratings, cold-start time, and ES 6.5 vs ES5 design differences were not found.
- Data-centre MW share of the installed base and of 2026 shipments is not disclosed.
- Independent evidence of commercial 100%-hydrogen operation was not found.

## 2. Technology and roadmap with dates

Bloom's roadmap since 2021 has shifted from hydrogen/electrolysers to AI data-centre power (load-following units, 800 VDC output, factory pre-integration, carbon-capture-ready plants), backed by a plan to double Fremont output from 1 GW to 2 GW a year by end-2026, a site able to reach about 5 GW, and contracted deliveries running into 2027–2028.

## 9. Science and engineering

### Takeaway
The core technology — high-temperature solid-oxide electrochemical conversion of reformed natural gas — is real, certified for safety (UL) and criteria emissions (CARB DG-058), and delivers near-zero NOx/SOx and no water use; but its headline 65% efficiency is a beginning-of-life figure that declines to ~53% (company) or faster (short-seller field analysis), its CO2 intensity (679–833 lb/MWh) is only ~5–23% below the US combined-cycle fleet average, fast AI load-following depends on supercapacitors and inverters tested only by Bloom, and durability, scandium supply and carbon capture at GW scale remain undemonstrated in independent public data.

- Physical limits: efficiency decay forces periodic stack replacement (service cost); natural-gas fuel means 308–378 g CO2/kWh without capture; continuous high-temperature operation makes the units unsuitable as cold-standby backup (inference — no start-up time published).

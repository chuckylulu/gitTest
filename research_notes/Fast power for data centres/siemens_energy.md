## (1) Products and stage

### Takeaway
Siemens Energy sells the full chain for "fast power" except in-building UPS/busway: heavy-duty (HL, H, F), industrial (SGT-800, SGT-400, SGT-750) and aeroderivative (SGT-A35, SGT-A65, SGT-A05) gas turbines, steam turbines, and grid-connection equipment (power transformers, GIS including SF6-free "Blue", HVDC, FACTS, synchronous condensers, BESS/E-STATCOM). All the turbines named are commercial products with fleets in operation; the newest large frame (HL) had 16 units in commercial operation in March 2026, and SF6-free GIS at 420 kV is still at demonstration stage. In-facility UPS, busway and low-voltage switchgear come from partner Eaton under a June 2025 partnership, not from Siemens Energy.

It markets "front of the meter" and "behind the meter" solutions, lists aeroderivative SGT-A35 simple-cycle plants for peaking, and cites "99.999% availability". Undated product page, accessed Nov 2026 – [Siemens Energy data center page](https://www.siemens-energy.com/global/en/home/products-services/solutions-industry/data-center.html) [company]
- Reference site: SGT-A05 aeroderivative gas turbines provide peaking and backup power at Equinix DB5 near Dublin – same page [company]
- Grid side for data centres, per the same page: high-voltage AIS/GIS substations (containerised and mobile options), SF6-free portfolio, high-voltage transformers, E-STATCOM and BESS ("Qstor") for load fluctuations, synchronous condensers ("SynCon") for short-circuit power and inertia, FACTS, MV/LV distribution transformers (fluid-immersed and GEAFOL dry-type) and e-houses – [data center page](https://www.siemens-energy.com/global/en/home/products-services/solutions-industry/data-center.html) [company]

### Inferences
- Speed-to-power hierarchy implied by the company data: mobile/aeroderivative units (SGT-400 cores in trailers, SGT-A35/A65 with <10-min starts) are deployable fastest. The SGT-800 (the building block of the Eaton 500 MW concept) sits in the middle. HL/F combined-cycle blocks give the highest efficiency but have the longest lead times (section 6).
- Siemens Energy has no in-house UPS/busway/LV data-hall product in these sources. For in-facility power it relies on partners (Eaton), so its data-centre exposure is mainly generation plus HV/MV grid connection.

### Gaps
- No SGT-A45 (mobile/trailer) product page was found (the URL pattern returned nothing). Its rating and current status could not be verified in-session.
- No published start-time figures (cold/hot start minutes) were found for the SGT-800 or HL other than the HL ramp rate (85 MW/min) and the SGT-A65 (<10 min to full power).
- Grid-forming converter/BESS products: only E-STATCOM/BESS mentions were found, with no grid-forming specifications.

## (2) Technology and roadmap with dates

### Takeaway
The roadmap is mainly about capacity and margin, not new frames. Medium-turbine output rose from about 50 to about 80 units/yr in FY26 and goes to about 100 by FY28. Large turbines go from 35 to about 50 units from FY27. Total gas-turbine delivery capacity is to reach about 30 GW/yr by 2030. Transformer and GIS capacity is to rise by about 50% by FY30. Technology items with dates: HL gas-turbine package rated H2-ready to 50 vol% (TÜV SÜD concept certificate, Oct 2023), with a company goal of 100% H2 capability for all turbines by 2030; the 420 kV SF6-free GIS demonstration (LIFE Blue); a solid-state transformer prototype in 2028; and the move to the new brand "Omterra" from late 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Medium gas turbines (20–100 MW): capacity about 50 units (FY25), about 80 (FY26), about 100 (FY28). Large (100–600 MW): about 35 units (FY25/FY26), rising to "50+" with "LGT capacity expansion to become effective in FY27". ### Gaps

- No public roadmap was found for HL output/efficiency upgrades beyond ">64%" and "goal of even higher efficiencies".

## (3) Milestones met or missed

### Takeaway
Group guidance was met or beaten in FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026, after upgrades during each year. The FY2028 margin target set at the November 2025 CMD (14–16%) is now guided for FY2027. Siemens Gamesa hit its FY2026 break-even target (€8m profit before special items). Siemens Energy left the federal guarantee early (June 2025) and resumed dividends for FY2025. Misses and slips: Transformation of Industry FY26 revenue growth (+3.4% vs 5–7% guided), the Charlotte transformer plant (early 2026 → early 2027), Siemens Gamesa onshore 5.X sales resumption (expected May 2024, then again in FY2025), and FY26 offshore orders (below expectations).

## (9) Science and engineering

### Takeaway
The technology base is mature. The SGT-800 (620 units, >20m hours), the SGT-A35 (about 900 units) and the H/HL family are proven in commercial service. The HL's headline ">64%" combined-cycle efficiency is a company gross/ISO figure. The independent operator figure is SSE's "around 63%" at Keadby 2, still the best in Europe. Unproven or demonstration-only claims: 100% hydrogen in large frames (rated 50 vol% on rig tests; 100% shown only on a small SGT-400 in the EU-funded HYFLEXPOWER project), SF6-free GIS at 420 kV (LIFE Blue demonstration), and turbines following AI load swings (no test data found; the company proposes BESS buffering). Physical limits are large forgings, single-crystal blades and vanes (ceramic cores), and rhenium/yttrium supply, and these constrain how fast output can rise.

### Inferences
- At the same date (30 Jun 2026), Siemens Energy's firm gas backlog (69 GW) exceeded GE Vernova's (53 GW), while GE Vernova's total including reservations was larger (116 vs 95 GW). Reservations are about 54% of GE Vernova's total vs about 27% at Siemens. Definitions may differ: Siemens states some GW figures "including steam turbine capacity of combined cycle configuration", and GE Vernova's basis was not checked. Mitsubishi's 35 GW firm large-frame book is smaller on both measures. Siemens' unit-count leadership comes from small and medium frames (SGT-400/800, aero), which are also the frames most relevant to fast behind-the-meter data-centre power.
- Company commentary moved from emphasising data-centre demand (Q2 FY26) to downplaying it (Q3–Q4 FY26) as investor scepticism about AI capex rose. Firm metrics (backlog, SRAs) did not show cancellations.

## 1. Stage: what Helion makes and where each machine and facility stands (Polaris, Trenta and earlier prototypes, Orion, pulsed-power manufacturing, helium-3)

### Takeaway
As of 14 Nov 2026, Helion has no revenue and no product on the market. It runs its 7th prototype, Polaris, in Everett, WA, which has operated since the end of 2024, and is building its first commercial plant, Orion, in Malaga, Chelan County, WA. Site work at Orion began in July 2025; by mid-2026 the generator building had reached only the earthwork stage. The latest technical milestone Helion has announced (13 Feb 2026, achieved in January 2026) is "measurable" deuterium-tritium (D-T) fusion at a 150 million °C plasma temperature in Polaris. Helion has not announced D-He3 operation, any quantified electricity recovery, net electricity, or scientific breakeven, and none of the Polaris results has been published in a peer-reviewed venue.

### Inferences
- Helion's own guidance puts commercial operation at about 200 million °C on D-He3. Polaris has publicly reached 150 million °C on D-T, so the fuel cycle Orion is designed around had not been publicly demonstrated in any Helion machine as of 14 Nov 2026.
- As of June 2026, the generator building was at the earthwork stage, roughly 18–30 months before the 2028 window for "initial operations." Machine installation and commissioning would have to fit in that period. This is a timing calculation from the dates above, not a forecast.
- The PUD lease authorizes up to 100 MW of generation on the 20-acre parcel, twice the 50 MW committed to Microsoft.

### Gaps
- No quantified Polaris results have been published: neutron yield, fusion energy per pulse, Q, energy recovered to capacitors, or pulse rate achieved.
- No announcement was found of Polaris D-He3 operation or of electricity recovered from fusion, through 14 Nov 2026.
- No source was retrieved that uses "9 keV" verbatim for Trenta; 100 million °C is about 8.6 keV.
- "Tiny Merge" (May 2026) and the 2025 manufacturing story could not be read beyond their headlines.
- An "Omega" capacitor-manufacturing facility could not be verified, and no capacitor or semiconductor production-capacity numbers were found.
- Independent confirmation of the Grande, Venti and IPA figures was not found.

## 2. Track record: Helion's public timeline statements versus outcomes

### Takeaway
Helion has publicly predicted near-term net energy, breakeven or commercial power at least five times since 2014: 2014, 2015, 2018, 2021 and 2025. None of those dates has been met, and no Helion machine has publicly demonstrated net energy gain or net electricity as of 14 Nov 2026. The current headline commitment, Orion operating in 2028 for Microsoft, is still officially "on track" in company statements. However, Helion's language has started to hedge, with phrases such as "before the end of this decade" and "as early as 2028."

### Inferences
- Across 2014–2025, Helion's dated predictions of net energy, breakeven or electricity have slipped by 4 to 7 or more years each and none has been met. The 2028 Orion date is the first commitment backed by a contract with penalties.
- The wording of Polaris's goal moved from "net electricity" (2021) to "the production of electricity" (2023) to recovering "a portion" of fusion energy to the capacitor bank (2026 web page). The public success criterion has therefore been narrowed.

### Gaps
- The original 2014, 2015 and 2018 Helion materials that Jassby quotes were not retrieved directly; the wording relies on Jassby's quotations.
- No Helion statement was found that formally revises the 2028 Orion date. Whether the Microsoft contract's dates or penalties have been amended is not public.

## 3. Customers and contracts: Microsoft/Constellation, Nucor, OpenAI talks, and what counterparties disclose

### Takeaway
Helion has two publicly announced customer agreements: a May 2023 PPA with Microsoft (at least 50 MW, Constellation as power marketer, plant online by 2028, undisclosed financial penalties) and a September 2023 Nucor collaboration for a 500 MWe plant (no disclosed binding offtake terms). Reported talks with OpenAI (March 2026) have produced no announced contract. Neither Microsoft nor Constellation mentions Helion or fusion anywhere in their 10-K or 10-Q filings from 2023 to 2026. ## 7. Publication record and verification status of announced results

### Takeaway
Helion's peer-reviewed record from 2015 to 2026 is thin. The one confirmed Helion-authored journal paper is Kirtley and Milroy's 2023 modeling paper in the *Journal of Fusion Energy*, which contains no experimental data. It drew a formal critical comment from Max Planck researchers in February 2026, and no formal published reply was found. ### Inferences
- The pattern across independent records: where a government action is required (licenses, permits, laws), Helion's claims are corroborated. Where a third party would have to disclose a commercial commitment (Microsoft, Constellation), there is no counterparty corroboration in filings. Where physics performance is concerned, corroboration is limited to informal data reviews and is contested in the peer-reviewed literature.

- No primary CFS statement directly disputing Helion's claims was found beyond the general 2024 peer-review remarks and the competing "first plant" wording. No Thea Energy statement about Helion was found.

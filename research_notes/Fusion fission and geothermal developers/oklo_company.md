## 1. What Oklo is, what it makes, and its stage of development (as of 14 Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
Oklo is a pre-revenue-from-power developer. It plans to build, own and operate sodium-cooled, metal-fueled fast-reactor "Aurora powerhouses" (now a 15–75 MWe product line) and is building out fuel fabrication, fuel recycling and radioisotope businesses around them. No powerhouse has been built or operated, and no binding power purchase agreement exists. The company's only reactor to reach criticality is its low-power Groves Isotope Test Reactor in Lockhart, Texas (5 Aug 2026, company-reported). All revenue to date ($3.07M in 9M 2026) comes from engineering and manufacturing services of two small firms it acquired in June 2026.

## 2. Aurora status and licensing path: DOE Reactor Pilot Program vs NRC, power-rating history, other facilities and sites

### Takeaway
The first unit, Aurora-ID (formerly "Aurora-INL", 75 MWe class), is being pursued under DOE authorization as the primary pathway, not an NRC license. As of Nov 2026 it has 2 of 5 DOE regulatory steps (NSDA, PDSA) and is in early civil and structural construction. Oklo has not resubmitted an NRC combined license application (COLA) since the NRC denied its first one, without prejudice, in January 2022. No Oklo project met DOE's 4 July 2026 criticality goal; four other companies did. Aurora's advertised size grew from ~1.5 MWe (2020) to 15/50 MWe (2023–24) to 15–75 MWe (from March 2025).

### Inferences
- The regulatory strategy for unit 1 has shifted from NRC licensing (2020–2025) to DOE authorization (since the Aug 2025 RPP selection). NRC licensing is now positioned for later units, possibly using the new rule that credits DOE-authorized operating data. Oklo itself says the DOE pathway "allows Aurora-ID to continue progressing toward operations under DOE authorization while preserving NRC licensing optionality."
- DOE authorization is not an NRC commercial license. Whether Aurora-ID can sell power commercially under DOE authorization alone is not explained in the filings reviewed.
- The four RPP projects that met the 4 July 2026 goal were zero-power critical assemblies (<100 Wt). Aurora-ID is a 50–75 MWe power plant, so its timeline is not comparable to those.

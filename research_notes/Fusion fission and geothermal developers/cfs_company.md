## 1. What does CFS make, what stage is SPARC at (assembly status as of mid-November 2026), and how have the first-plasma and Q>1 targets moved since 2018?

### Takeaway
CFS is a pre-revenue developer that makes its own high-temperature-superconducting (HTS) magnets. It is assembling the SPARC demonstration tokamak in Devens, MA. CFS calls SPARC "about 80% complete" (July–November 2026). The latest public toroidal-field (TF) count is **8 of 18 installed (CFS post, 22 Sep 2026)**. In January 2026 CFS had said all 18 would be in by the end of summer 2026. CFS now says SPARC will "turn on" in 2027. The first-plasma/operation target has moved from 2025 (2018–2021 statements) to 2026 (2024) to 2027 (2026). The Q>1 target has moved from 2025 to 2027. CFS's own November 2026 material gives no date for Q>1.

## 2. What did the September 2021 20-tesla TFMC test and later magnet tests achieve (including the quench/damage event), and where were the results published?

### Takeaway
The MIT/CFS toroidal-field model coil (TFMC) reached 20 T on 5 Sep 2021. In two more test campaigns in late 2021, an intentional open-circuit quench at 31.5 kA melted one corner of one of the coil's 16 pancakes, about a few percent of coil volume. MIT says this prompted design revisions to the SPARC magnets. The results came out in an August 2023 arXiv overview and then six peer-reviewed papers in a March 2024 IEEE Transactions on Applied Superconductivity special issue. All of these were authored by the MIT/CFS team, and no independent replication was found. No primary results were found for the central-solenoid model coil (CSMC) or the PIT-VIPER cable. Federal records show two small CS-development awards to CFS.

### Cited Findings
- **The 2021 test:**
  - On 5 Sep 2021 the TFMC was ramped to 20 T, which MIT called the strongest fusion magnet in the world. The coil used 267 km (166 mi) of HTS tape in 16 plates. At the time SPARC was "targeted for completion in 2025" and the SPARC team had 270 members. — [MIT News, 8 Sep 2021](https://news.mit.edu/2021/MIT-CFS-major-advance-toward-fusion-energy-0908) [MIT]
  - Brandon Sorbom said then that "if we build the magnet, all of the physics will work in SPARC". — [same](https://news.mit.edu/2021/MIT-CFS-major-advance-toward-fusion-energy-0908) [MIT]
- **Program overview paper:** Hartwig, Vieira, Dunn, Golfinopoulos, LaBombard and 99 others, "The SPARC Toroidal Field Model Coil Program", arXiv:2308.12301, submitted 18 Aug 2023. It is the overview and first paper of a six-part series. It reports:
  - a 3-year (2018–2021) MIT PSFC/CFS program;
  - "20.1 T peak field-on-conductor with 40.5 kA of terminal current";
  - ~815 kN/m Lorentz loading on the REBCO stacks and nearly 1 GPa of mechanical stress in the case;
  - 15 demountable pancake-to-pancake joints at 0.5–2.0 nΩ (20 K, up to 12 T);
  - DC and AC performance matching models across two test campaigns;
  - current leads qualified to 50 kA;
  - an intentional open-circuit quench at 31.5 kA terminal current to assess passive quench self-protection;
  - the abstract does not itself describe the damage.
  - [arXiv:2308.12301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12301) [PEER, MIT/CFS authors]
- **Journal publication and the damage event:**
  - Results were published as six peer-reviewed papers in a special March 2024 issue of IEEE Transactions on Applied Superconductivity.
  - Two more tests in late 2021 pushed the magnet to its limits.
  - The deliberate quench, done by cutting incoming power, melted one corner of one of the 16 pancakes. Dennis Whyte says the damage was limited to roughly a few percent of the coil volume, and the results led to design revisions for the fusion device's magnets.
  - MIT also reports that CFS procured 300 km of HTS tape in under a year.
  - [MIT News, "Tests show high-temperature superconducting magnets are ready for fusion", 4 Mar 2024](https://news.mit.edu/2024/tests-show-high-temperature-superconducting-magnets-fusion-ready-0304) [MIT]
- **Production TF magnets:** SPARC's production TF magnets use the "non-insulated, non-twisted" design because they run at steady current. Each has 16 pancakes. The first was shipped in December 2025. — [CFS blog, 6 Jan 2026](https://blog.cfs.energy/cfs-delivers-its-first-fusion-magnet-a-stronger-smaller-design/) [CFS]
- **DOE milestone for the TF magnet test:**
  - CFS says DOE approved a "toroidal field magnet test" milestone in 2025, under the Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program. — [CFS blog, 5 Aug 2026](https://blog.cfs.energy/sparc-progress-doe-milestone-approvals-reflect-cfs-power-plant-maturity/) [CFS]
  - USAspending shows a $4.5M obligation to CFS's Milestone award DE-SC0024885 on 13 Jan 2025. The record does not say which milestone the obligation relates to. — [USAspending, award DE-SC0024885](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_DESC0024885_089) [GOV]
- **Central-solenoid development awards:** both titled "Pulsed high temperature superconducting central solenoid for revolutionizing tokamaks":
  - ARPA-E cooperative agreement DE-AR0001259: $1,288,316.58 obligated, 1 Oct 2020–30 Aug 2023. — [USAspending, DE-AR0001259](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_DEAR0001259_089) [GOV]
  - DOE Office of Science award DE-SC0021623: $1,537,791, 1 Mar 2021–28 Feb 2025. — [USAspending, DE-SC0021623](https://www.usaspending.gov/award/ASST_NON_DESC0021623_089) [GOV]
- **Magnets supplied to WHAM:** CFS supplied the HTS magnets that confined first plasma in the University of Wisconsin–Madison WHAM magnetic-mirror experiment at 17 T in 2024. — [CFS/Realta, "Realta Fusion and Commonwealth Fusion Systems Form Strategic Partnership…", 2 Apr 2026](https://cfs.energy/news-and-media/realta-fusion-and-commonwealth-fusion-systems-form-strategic-partnership-to-commercialize-magnetic-mirror-fusion-energy/) [CFS/CP joint release]
- **Physics-basis publications:**
  - CFS published seven SPARC physics-basis papers in 2020.
  - Five ARC physics-basis papers, co-authored by 58 scientists, appeared in a Journal of Plasma Physics collection in June 2026: overview; power and particle exhaust; disruptions; performance and transport; MHD.
  - [CFS, "…Publish Five Peer-Reviewed Papers Validating the Physics of the ARC Fusion Power Plant", 4 Jun 2026](https://cfs.energy/news-and-media/commonwealth-fusion-systems-builds-on-learnings-from-sparc-to-publish-five-peer-reviewed-papers-validating-the-physics-of-the-arc-fusion-power-plant/) [CFS]

### Inferences

- Federal money for CS development to CFS directly is small (about $2.8M in total). The CSMC and PIT-VIPER work is otherwise visible only through company or secondary sources.

## 3. ARC: site, planned capacity, target date, land, permits, PJM interconnection, Dominion's role, and what county, utility and state documents say

### Takeaway
ARC is planned as a 400 MWe net (~1.1 GW fusion) tokamak plant called the Fall Line Fusion Power Station. It would sit on 25–40 acres of a 94-acre Dominion-owned parcel at 1201 Battery Brooke Parkway, Chesterfield County, VA, which CFS holds under a lease option. CFS targets grid power in the "early 2030s".
- Chesterfield's Board of Supervisors approved a conditional-use permit on 17 Sep 2025.
- CFS filed a PJM interconnection request in April 2026.
- As of 30 Jul 2026 county records showed no site plan or building permit filed.

CFS's stated construction start has moved earlier: "late 2020s" in August 2025, 2028 in September 2025, and 2027 in April 2026. Dominion's role is land plus non-financial collaboration under a joint development agreement. Dominion itself agreed on 15 May 2026 to be acquired by NextEra.

### Cited Findings
- **Announcement (17 Dec 2024):**
  - About 400 MW, online in the early 2030s, at the "James River Industrial Park" outside Richmond.
  - Dominion Energy Virginia's role is a "nonfinancial collaboration" supplying development and technical expertise and leasing rights for the site. CFS will independently finance, build, own and operate the plant.
  - Expected to yield "billions of dollars" of economic development and hundreds of construction and operating jobs.
  - [MIT News, 17 Dec 2024](https://news.mit.edu/2024/commonwealth-fusion-systems-unveils-worlds-first-fusion-power-plant-1217) [MIT, relaying CFS]

## 7. Legal, licensing and regulatory matters: NRC framework and rulemaking, ADVANCE Act, Massachusetts licensing of SPARC, Virginia, and any lawsuits, export-control, labor or environmental disputes

### Takeaway
On 13 Apr 2023 the NRC Commission chose to regulate fusion under the byproduct-material framework (SRM-SECY-23-0001). The ADVANCE Act (signed 9 Jul 2024, §205) codified fusion machines into the byproduct-material definitions. The NRC's proposed rule was published 26 Feb 2026, with comments closing 27 May 2026. As of September 2026 the final rule was estimated for December 2027; NEIMA's deadline is 31 Dec 2027.

Massachusetts (an Agreement State) granted SPARC a broad-scope radioactive-materials license in October 2024. The state's 2021 MEPA record says the Massachusetts Radiation Control Program (MRCP) "does not regulate the operation of the facility and will not issue a license to operate".

For ARC, only the Chesterfield CUP is documented. No lawsuits, export-control actions, labor disputes or environmental enforcement involving CFS were found in the (incomplete) searches.

### Inferences
- **The pattern of shifts:**
  - SPARC's demonstration dates moved later by about two years.
  - ARC's construction-start guidance moved earlier.
  - Fundraising and offtake milestones arrived on or ahead of stated plans.
  - The two direct, CFS-sourced checks of physical progress on SPARC (TF count and percent complete) show assembly running behind CFS's January 2026 plan.
- **ITER versus SPARC:** ITER's own baseline slippage (first plasma 2018 → 2025 → research operation 2034, DT 2039) is a long-running reference case for tokamak schedule risk. SPARC's slip to date is about two years over about five years (2021–2026). These are different scales of project and are not directly comparable.
- **Helion versus CFS:** both have publicly missed earlier demonstration targets (Helion's 2024 electricity demonstration; CFS's 2025 first plasma). Both hold hyperscaler offtakes dated to 2028 (Helion) and the early 2030s (CFS).

### Gaps
- **Thea Energy:** its timelines and claims were not researched; no source was retrieved before the search budget ran out. Thea is presumably covered in a separate company file.
- **Helion after mid-2025:** Helion's 2025–2026 status (Polaris results, Orion construction) was not verified here.

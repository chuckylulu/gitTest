## 1. Corporate snapshot: what Fervo makes, its stage, and its projects (Project Red, Cape Station, Corsac Station, other leases)

### Takeaway
As of 14 Nov 2026 Fervo is a Houston-based, Nasdaq-listed (since 13 May 2026) enhanced-geothermal (EGS) developer. It has **36 MW operating**: the ~3 MW Project Red pilot in Nevada (online 2023) plus **one 33 MW GeoBlock at Cape Station, Utah**, which synced to the grid on 24 Sept 2026 and declared commercial operation on 30 Sept 2026. GeoBlock 2 synced on 4 Nov 2026, and GeoBlock 3 is in cold commissioning. Both are guided to COD by 1 Jan 2027, which would complete the ~100 MW Phase I. Another ~400 MW ("Cape Google 400") is under construction for 2028. Corsac Station (Nevada, 115 MW for Google via NV Energy) has a contract but no reported construction.

## 2. Roadmap, milestones met or missed, and technical results (with verification status)

### Takeaway
The headline 2023 promise was "first power in 2026". Fervo met it: the first 33 MW GeoBlock synced on 24 Sept 2026, earlier than the Q4 2026 target given in August 2026, and declared COD a day before the contractual date. However, "100 MW Phase I in 2026" (June 2025 messaging) slipped to "early 2027" (S-1, Apr 2026). The ~400 MW to be built for 2028 has been re-assigned to Google. Fervo says it is renegotiating "certain" California Phase II PPAs to "early 2030s" delivery. The CPUC approved those contracts with a 1 June 2028 delivery date for the largest one. Fervo's well-test results have so far been released through company releases and self-published preprints. An independent re-analysis (GeoExpro, 2025) disputes the net per-well output.

**Well-test results and how they were verified**
- **Project Red (announced July 2023):** flow up to 63 L/s and 3.5 MW, per a Norbeck & Latimer (2023) preprint as cited on Wikipedia — [LOW: Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fervo_Energy). It also says Project Red "has not experienced the kind of premature thermal decline that has long plagued traditional geothermal projects" (company claim) — [CC: 424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm)
- **Cape Station (company release, 10 Sept 2024):**
  - 30-day test, maximum flow of 107 kg/s, reservoir above 428°F.
  - Fervo said the result supports "over 10 MW" per well, three times Project Red's per-well output.
  - Utility Dive quoted no independent experts. BLM's Angela Wadman said the results affirmed "the geological data that we had".
  - Source: [IND: Utility Dive, Emma Penrod, "Cape Station may be world's most productive geothermal system to date: Fervo", 12 Sept 2024](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/cape-station-enhanced-geothermal-utah-fervo-blm-lease/726796/)
- **Independent re-analysis (GeoExpro, 1 Oct 2025):** Elliot Yearsley and Henk Kombrink (editor-in-chief) reviewed the Sept 2024 Cape preprint posted on EarthArXiv.
  - The data cover only 24 hours for a two-injector / one-producer "triplet". Rate was declining from ~97 to ~93 kg/s, not "levelling out". Injection rates are unpublished.
  - Estimated pump load is ~2.5 MW (injection pressure 2,000–2,300 psi). Net power is ~6.2 MW at 24 h and ~4.8 MW at 7 days if decline follows the Blue Mountain pattern, against Fervo's "sustained 8–10 MW".
  - The authors call the papers self-published with no independent review.
  - No Fervo response is included.
  - Source: [IND: GeoExpro, 1 Oct 2025](https://geoexpro.com/critical-look-at-fervo-cape-station-data-raises-questions-over-sustainability/)
- **Same summary via another outlet:** another GeoExpro piece and an Australian Geothermal Association summary give the preprint's figure as "sustained 8–10 MW, peak 12 MW (Norbeck et al., 2024)". One summary describes ~2 MW net per well when spread across the three-well triplet (search result only) — [IND: GeoExpro, "The low productivity of enhanced geothermal systems" (search result only)](https://geoexpro.com/the-low-productivity-of-enhanced-geothermal-systems/)

### Inferences
- Fervo's near-term claims (first power, first COD) are the parts most consistent across company filings, CPUC contract dates and independent reporting. The least corroborated claims are the 2028+ schedule, per-well output at scale, the 8.4 GW resource and the cost-decline path to $5,500 and $3,000/kW.
- **Re-assigning the 2028 build to Google** while deferring some California PPAs is a significant change. It is visible only in the 10-Q, not in the headline press releases reviewed.
- **Competitor comparison (Fervo side only):** Fervo is selling grid-connected, take-or-pay geothermal energy to a hyperscaler (Google) for mid-2028. Its Phase I contracts imply about 78% expected capacity factor. Any comparison with nuclear or fusion offtake deals should use the competitors' own dated milestones from the separate company files, not Fervo's characterization.

### Gaps
- **Competitor timelines and firm-power claims (TerraPower, X-energy, Oklo, Commonwealth Fusion Systems, Helion)** were not collected here because the shared search budget was exhausted. The report writer should take them from the companion company files.
- No independent analyst (e.g., Wood Mackenzie, Rystad, BloombergNEF, LBNL) assessments of Fervo's 2026 performance or cost claims were retrieved.
- No customer-side statements (Edison International/SCE filings, CPA board materials, NV Energy filings, Alphabet filings) about the Phase II renegotiation or Corsac status were retrieved.
- Contradiction 10 (Nevada approval date) and the CPA base-size discrepancy remain unresolved.

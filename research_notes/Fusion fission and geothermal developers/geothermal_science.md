# Fervo Energy's enhanced geothermal systems (EGS): the science and engineering basis at Project Red (Nevada) and Cape Station (Utah). Evidence review as of 14 November 2026

These notes reflect the state of the evidence on 14 Nov 2026. Older states carry their dates. Every source is tagged with one of these labels:
- **[CO]**: company-controlled. This covers Fervo's (or a named competitor's) own releases, blog posts, SEC filings and company-authored papers. SEC filings carry securities-law liability but are still company statements.
- **[ADJ]**: company-adjacent. The source is co-authored with, commissioned by or paid by the company, or comes from its IPO underwriter or a body it advises.
- **[IND-GOV]**: independent government, national-lab, regulator or federal-catalog data.
- **[IND-ACAD]**: independent academic work.
- **[IND-PRESS]**: independent journalism or trade press.
- **[IND-OPINION]**: a named independent analyst's or consultant's opinion column.
- **[ADV]**: adversarial. A short seller that has disclosed a short position in FRVO. Its claims are allegations and are not verified.

"(snippet)" means I saw only a search-result summary of the page, not its full text.

Context needed to read the rest: Fervo listed on Nasdaq (FRVO) on 14 May 2026. Its IPO raised about $2.2B gross at $27/share ([Fervo Q1 2026 results, 22 Jun 2026](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-reports-first-quarter-2026-results/) [CO]). ## Key Question 1: What have Project Red and Cape Station demonstrated (with numbers and dates), and who outside Fervo has verified or peer-reviewed it?

### Takeaway
**What is shown.** Fervo has shown that two shale techniques work in hot (about 175–205 °C) metamorphic and granitic rock: horizontal well pairs (or triplets), and multistage plug-and-perf stimulation with proppant. Together they create a working fluid connection between wells.
- **Project Red** has produced power since 30 Oct 2023. It averaged about 1.4 MW net, against a headline 3.5 MW peak gross. Thermal decline began at about 500 days.
- **Cape Station GeoBlock 1** declared commercial operation on 30 Sep 2026 at 33 MW net.

**What is not shown or checked.**
- The only public Cape per-well flow data come from a single 2024 test, shown for 24 hours. Fervo has published no Cape flow, temperature, MWh or water-loss operating data.
- No peer-reviewed journal paper on Fervo's field results was found.
- Independent "verification" so far comes in four forms:
  - DOE reports that reuse Fervo-reported numbers.
  - An analogous result at DOE's Utah FORGE site.
  - Independent seismic catalogs.
  - Critical analyses of Fervo's own published data.

### Cited Findings
**Project Red (northern Nevada)**
- **July 2023 test.** Fervo announced a 30-day well test on 18 Jul 2023: 63 L/s "at high temperature", enabling 3.5 MW. It described this as the first horizontal well pair drilled for commercial geothermal production.
  - The wells went about 7,700 ft vertical and about 3,250 ft lateral.
  - They were stimulated with a "multistage, plug-and-perforate" design using proppant.
  - Fervo said flow was validated across the whole lateral with "no significant thermal short-circuit pathways".
  - Sources: [Business Wire, Fervo release, 18 Jul 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230718629841/en) [CO]; reported by [Canary Media, Jul 2023](https://canarymedia.com/articles/geothermal/startup-claims-breakthrough-in-turning-earths-heat-into-clean-power) and [E&P/Hart Energy](https://www.epmag.com/exclusives/fervo-energy-claims-geothermal-breakthrough-nevada-pilot-project-205817) [IND-PRESS reporting CO] (snippet).
- **Stanford workshop papers, 2023.** Fervo-authored Stanford Geothermal Workshop papers describe two horizontal wells at the Blue Mountain field in northern Nevada: injector 34A-22 and producer 34-22. Identifying these as the Project Red wells is my reading; the search summaries did not state it explicitly.
  - Static temperatures targeted: 350–400 °F. Build rates: about 10°/100 ft.
  - Proppant pumped into 34A-22 was found while drilling the 34-22 lateral, which means the two wells are directly connected through fractures.
  - Sources: [SGW record 35580](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/IGAstandard/record_detail.php?id=35580); [SGW record 35647](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/IGAstandard/record_detail.php?id=35647) [CO, conference papers] (snippet).
- **Well geometry (prospectus).** Project Red wells reach about 7,500 ft true vertical depth (TVD) and about 11,000 ft measured depth (MD), with about 3,000 ft of horizontal section. Cape Phase I wells are about 9,000 ft TVD, about 14,000 ft MD and about 5,000 ft horizontal. Source: [Fervo 424B4 prospectus, 14 May 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm) [CO].
- **Fervo's 13 Apr 2026 post, "Enhanced Geothermal Has Been Proven at Scale"** ([link](https://fervoenergy.com/enhanced-geothermal-has-been-proven-at-scale-heres-what-two-years-of-production-data-show/) [CO]):
  - More than 614 days on production. Commercial operations began 30 Oct 2023.
  - Average production temperature 347 °F (about 175 °C).
  - Average circulation about 36 kg/s.
  - Average output 2.1 MW gross and about 1.4 MW net. Peak was 3.5 MWe gross at 60 kg/s during initial testing.
  - About 70% of injected fluid recaptured at steady state.
  - Tracer shows water crosses the fracture network in less than 24 hours.
  - 98.4% uptime "outside of these events" (the excluded events are not quantified).
  - Zero workovers.
  - Thermal recovery factor about 20%. Reservoir about 975 × 110 × 143 m.
  - "For more than 500 days ... no observable thermal decline", then "a slight temperature decrease of approximately 2.5°F". The model "accurately predicts the onset of thermal decline at approximately 500 days".
  - Project Red was "a learning platform, not a production-optimized system".
  - The post cites the Norbeck & Latimer paper as an EarthArXiv "Preprint".
- **Prospectus statements on Project Red (14 May 2026).** Project Red generated "three megawatts of gross power production to the Nevada grid". "To date, Project Red has not experienced the kind of premature thermal decline that has long plagued traditional geothermal projects." Source: [424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm) [CO].
- **Q3 2026 release.** "Capacity in operation reached 36 megawatts, up from 3 megawatts at the end of Q2." That is Project Red's 3 MW plus Cape GeoBlock 1's 33 MW. This explains the "36 MW" figure that circulated in secondary coverage. Source: [Fervo Q3 2026 earnings release, 8-K Ex. 99.1, 9 Nov 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026071911/exhibit991earningsrelease1.htm) [CO].
- **GeoExpro critique, 23 Apr 2026** (Yearsley & Kombrink) ([link](https://geoexpro.com/has-enhanced-geothermal-been-proven-at-scale/) [IND-OPINION]):
  - Estimated sustainable output at about 1.3 MW net before Fervo released its 1.4 MW figure.

  - The 2.5 °F drop over the last 100 days would be about 9 °F/yr if it continued. Typical pumped Nevada geothermal projects average about 1.5 °F/yr.

The results were "described in a self-published paper" with no independent review. Source: [GeoExpro](https://geoexpro.com/the-reservoir-will-always-have-its-final-say/) [IND-OPINION].

  - Mike O'Connor (Mountain West Geothermal Consortium, where Fervo is an advisor; former DOE Liftoff lead author) [ADJ] called Project Red essentially a research site: "It's not designed the way it should be if you actually care about long-term production."

### Inferences
- **Project Red output.** Fervo's own numbers put average net output at about 40% of the 3.5 MW headline (1.4/3.5) and about 67% of average gross (1.4/2.1). So about one-third of gross is parasitic or other losses at the pilot.
- **Project Red decline rate.** The regulator-data trend Heatmap reports (about 2 °F/month, Nov 2025–May 2026) annualizes to about 24 °F/yr. That is numerically consistent with the short seller's 24 °F/yr. The disagreement is over meaning: Fervo calls it predicted end-of-life decline of a pilot doublet, while critics treat it as a warning. It is not a dispute over the trend itself. The rate is about 16 times GeoExpro's 1.5 °F/yr benchmark for pumped Nevada fields.
- **Strongest public evidence for Cape.** The best public evidence for Cape is the commercial operation of a 33 MW (net) block, stated in SEC filings. That evidence covers 6–7 weeks of one block, with no reservoir data.
- **Per-well figures.** Fervo's 10–16 MW per well figures are gross, peak or design values. They are not sustained net results from a published test.
- **What independent verification covers.** Independent verification currently covers:
  - the plausibility of the technique, through FORGE;
  - seismicity, through the UUSS/USGS catalogs (Key Question 4);
  - Project Red output and temperature trends, through NDOM regulator data as read by others.
- **What it does not cover.** Nothing independent covers Cape flow, temperature, water balance or decline.

### Gaps
- The roughly 191 °C production temperature attributed to the 2023 test (Norbeck & Latimer) was not re-verified. I could not open the preprint. Its EarthArXiv DOI is commonly cited as 10.31223/X52X0B, but I did not re-fetch it. I also did not confirm whether it was ever journal-published; Fervo still labels it "Preprint" in April 2026.
- I did not retrieve NDOM's raw monthly Project Red data myself. Trends above come from Heatmap's experts and Morpheus.
- Not found or not readable:
  - the Bearskin test results;
  - any Cape MWh, capacity-factor, production-temperature or water-loss operating data;
  - Fervo's SGW 2025 paper ([Fercho et al. 2025](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2025/Fercho.pdf); the Stanford server returned HTTP 503 and a broken TLS chain);
  - the URTeC 2025 Fervo/ResFrac paper (Singh et al. 2025).
- The 24 Sep 2026 GlobeNewswire first-power release returned HTTP 403. I used the 1 Oct 2026 8-K exhibit instead.

## Key Question 2: Drilling (days per well, cost per well, rate of penetration, bit life in hot granite); comparison with Utah FORGE and benchmarks; what is independently verified

### Takeaway
Fervo reports steep drilling learning curves. Drilling time fell from 59 days to 21 days per well (2022 to early 2024), and about 75% from 2022 to 2025. Cost per well fell from about $9.4–9.5M to $4.8M, and cost per foot from $1,000 to $350. Average rate of penetration reached about 70 ft/hr, with two bits per lateral.

DOE repeats these numbers and points to a parallel trend at Utah FORGE: rate of penetration up more than 500% since 2017. But no third-party audit of Fervo's per-well costs exists. Fervo has stopped disclosing per-well days and costs for its Phase II Generation 3.0 wells.

### Cited Findings
- **Stanford workshop, Feb 2024** ([Fervo release via DCVC](https://www.dcvc.com/press-releases/fervo-energy-drilling-results-show-rapid-advancement-of-geothermal-performance/); [Business Wire, 12 Feb 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240212763499/en) [CO]; summarized by [GRC](https://www.geothermal.org/our-impact/stories/fervo-energy-announces-70-reduction-geothermal-drilling-time) and [E&P/Hart Energy](https://www.epmag.com/exclusives/oil-gas-drilling-tech-transfer-boosts-fervos-geothermal-prowess-208181) [IND-PRESS reporting CO] (snippet)):
  - Drilling time fell from 59 days (Project Red, 3,000-ft lateral) to 21 days for the fastest Cape well, which has a 5,000-ft lateral and is more than 2,100 ft deeper. That is a 70% reduction. Some summaries say 20 days.
  - Per-well cost fell from $9.4M to $4.8M across the first four Cape horizontal wells.
  - Cost per foot fell from $1,000 to $350.
  - The fourth Cape horizontal sustained an average rate of penetration of 70 ft/hr, which Fervo said was "already outpacing NREL's 2035 projections for moderate technology improvement".
  - Two bits per lateral.
  - Fervo reported a 35% learning rate against 18% planned, and set 15-day wells as the next target.
- **DOE Liftoff (Mar 2024)** ([DOE](https://apps.openei.org/GeoCLUSTER/assets/pdfs/DOE%20Report%202024_Pathways%20to%20Commercial%20liftoff.pdf) [IND-GOV citing CO data]):
  - Fervo's earliest deployments showed "a 300 percent increase in drilling rate ... drilling costs decreasing from an initial of $9.5 million to $4.8 million over six wells in 6 months".
  - "Drilling rates ... at FORGE have improved by over 500 percent since the first well was drilled in 2017."
  - "well development costs decreased from $13 million to under $5 million per well between the first two large-scale commercial EGS pilots".
  - Oil and gas learning rates average "about 15 percent"; "current EGS demonstrations are doubling" that.
  - NREL's Annual Technology Baseline (ATB) 2035 most-aggressive case assumes 80 L/s and 110 ft/hr, against a 2022 baseline of 40 L/s and 25 ft/hr. Fervo reported 61–63 L/s and 40–80 ft/hr.
- **Prospectus (May 2026).** Drilling times fell by "approximately 75% from 2022 to 2025" and per-foot drilling costs by "approximately 70%". Fervo cites "more than 500 terabytes" of operational data. Source: [424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm) [CO].
- **Generation 3.0 wells.** These have 7,500-ft laterals, larger-diameter casing and a deeper target. The Q1 2026 release reports no drilling days, cost per well or cost per foot. Sources: [Q1 2026 release](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-reports-first-quarter-2026-results/) and [Q3 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026071911/exhibit991earningsrelease1.htm) [CO].
- **Morpheus allegations** ([Morpheus, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.morpheus-research.com/fervo/) [ADV]):
  - The 2024 drilling-cost chart showed costs rising from Well 13 to Wells 14–15, while the 2026 S-1 chart shows a decline and omits Well 15.
  - Fervo declined a direct drilling-cost question on its Q1 2026 call.
  - A former employee put completion (fracking) costs at about $3,000/kW.
- **NREL caution.** A 2022 report by an NREL researcher found that well-cost savings from faster drilling are smaller than the days saved suggest, because fixed costs make up much of total well cost. Source: [E&P/Hart Energy](https://www.epmag.com/exclusives/oil-gas-drilling-tech-transfer-boosts-fervos-geothermal-prowess-208181) [IND-PRESS] (snippet).
- **Independent model inputs.** The GEOPHIRES Cape-5 case assumes $4.65M per drilled well and $4.83M per stimulated well, taken from public data. Source: [GEOPHIRES Cape-5](https://softwareengineerprogrammer.github.io/GEOPHIRES/Fervo_Project_Cape-5.html) [IND model].

### Inferences
- **What is independently supported.** The direction of the drilling-improvement trend has independent support: FORGE's more than 500% rate-of-penetration gain, and DOE's use of PDC-bit and shale practices.
- **What is not.** Fervo's specific per-well figures are company-reported only. DOE reproduces them; it does not verify them.
- **Small inconsistencies.** Fervo's own baseline varies: $9.4M over four wells (Fervo) versus $9.5M over six wells (DOE). The fastest Cape well is given as both 20 and 21 days.
- **Cost driver may have shifted.** Faster drilling does not remove completion costs or fixed rig costs. The only source suggesting completions are now the larger cost item is adversarial.

### Gaps
- No independent, well-by-well bit-life or rate-of-penetration dataset for Fervo's wells.
- No FORGE per-well rate-of-penetration or bit-life figures retrieved (only DOE's ">500%").
- No disclosed drilling days or cost for Phase II Generation 3.0 wells.
- No independent audit of drilling or completion costs.

## Key Question 3: Reservoir performance (thermal drawdown, short-circuiting, fluid loss and water, tracers, parasitic load, ORC efficiency, capacity factor, 20–30-year reservoir life): demonstrated vs modeled

### Takeaway
**Demonstrated, at pilot scale only (Project Red):**
- about 175 °C production for more than 600 days;
- decline onset at about 500 days, followed by a decline of about 2 °F/month according to regulator data;
- about 70% fluid recapture (20–30% monthly loss);
- tracer transit under 24 hours;
- about one-third of gross output lost to parasitic load.

**Asserted but without published data (Cape):** 90% water retention at a triplet test, and a 33 MW net block operating "in line with expectations".

**Modeled only:**
- 99% water retention;
- per-well output of about 16 MW gross;
- the 30.5% recovery factor;

An independent model of a 500 MW Cape-like field assumes thermal breakthrough requiring redrilling about every 8 years.

### Inferences
These are my calculations. The assumptions are stated, and they should be treated as order-of-magnitude estimates.

- **Water consumption at Project Red.** Loss was about 14–15 kg/s: 45 kg/s injected and 31 kg/s produced, or 36 kg/s produced at 70% recapture. That is about 117–128 million gallons a year. Annual net generation is about 12,000 MWh (1.4 MW × 8,760 h × 0.984). That gives about 10 gal/kWh. The short seller's cumulative 193 million gallons over about 33 months implies about 6 gal/kWh. Either figure is about 8–35 times DOE's 0.3–0.73 gal/kWh assumption. Fervo argues most of this water is pressurizing the reservoir, not being consumed.
- **Water sensitivity at Cape.** If a producer delivers about 6 MW net at about 93 kg/s (GeoExpro's 24-hour estimate), then:
  - at 90% retention, losses are about 1.6 gal/kWh;
  - at 99% retention, about 0.15 gal/kWh.
  - Scaled to 500 MW at 90% retention, that is roughly 15,000–22,000 acre-ft/yr of make-up water, against roughly 1,400–2,000 acre-ft/yr at 99%. The lower bound of each range uses Fervo's ~10 MW at 107 kg/s as if it were net.
  - So the gap between the demonstrated (company-reported) 90% and the modeled 99% decides the water footprint.
- **Reservoir life.** A 20–30-year life without redrilling has not been shown for multi-fracture horizontal EGS anywhere. The only long record is a 2.1-year pilot whose decline began at about 500 days. The independent model assumes redrilling about every 8 years. Redrilling plus refracturing, rather than a single 30-year wellfield, is the more evidence-consistent planning basis.
- **Air-cooled ORC and hot weather.** Air-cooled binary plants lose output in hot weather. This is basic thermodynamics, but no source quantified it for Cape. Firm-power claims for summer peaks therefore rest on an unquantified derate.

## Key Question 4: Induced seismicity (Basel, Pohang, FORGE; Fervo's monitoring and traffic-light protocol; recorded events at Cape and Project Red; regulation)

### Takeaway
Hydraulic stimulation for EGS has caused damaging earthquakes before: Basel 2006 (ML 3.4) and Pohang 2017 (Mw 5.5). At Cape, Fervo runs a traffic-light protocol (amber at M≥2, red at M≥3).

I queried the independent USGS/University of Utah catalog directly. It shows:
- no M≥2 events in the Cape/FORGE area in 2022–2023;
- 41 events from 2024 to April 2026;
- a sharp rise to 82 M≥2 events between 1 Sep and 14 Nov 2026;
- two events at ML 3.1 (12 Sep and 7 Nov 2026), at or above Cape's red threshold.

The catalog cannot assign events to Fervo or to FORGE. Fervo has not published its own catalog or what it did in response to the M≥3 events.

### Cited Findings
- **Basel.** A magnitude 3.4 event in 2006 was the largest EGS-linked quake before Pohang and led to the plant's shutdown. Source: [Science News, Apr 2018](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/pumping-water-underground-power-may-have-triggered-south-korean-quake) [IND-PRESS].
- **Pohang.** Two Science papers in April 2018 linked the Nov 2017 Mw 5.5 Pohang earthquake to the EGS project. One called it "the largest and most damaging" EGS-associated quake. About 12,800 m³ had been injected since early 2016. Operations were suspended 10 days after the quake. Sources: [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/pumping-water-underground-power-may-have-triggered-south-korean-quake); [E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/gamechanger-earthquake-linked-to-geothermal-power/) [IND-PRESS] (snippet). Some sources give Mw 5.4.
- **DOE protocol.** DOE requires its funded EGS projects to follow its induced-seismicity mitigation protocol: screening, monitoring, hazard and risk assessment, and a mitigation plan. "No induced community felt seismicity has occurred at a DOE-funded project." Source: [DOE Liftoff, Mar 2024](https://apps.openei.org/GeoCLUSTER/assets/pdfs/DOE%20Report%202024_Pathways%20to%20Commercial%20liftoff.pdf) [IND-GOV].
- **FORGE 2024.** Maximum induced magnitude during the 2024 stimulation was 1.9. Source: [Utah FORGE, 23 May 2024](https://utahforge.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Stimulation-and-Circulation-May-23-2024-Press-Release-FINAL.pdf) [IND-GOV] (snippet).
- **Cape traffic-light thresholds** ([Utah FORGE Seismic Risk Traffic Light System v2, GDR](https://gdr.openei.org/files/1706/TLS_V2_final.pdf); [GDR submission 1706](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1706) [IND-GOV] (snippet; text extraction partly garbled, so worth confirming against the original)):
  - "The Cape Station TLS uses an Amber threshold of M ≥ 2 and a Red threshold of M ≥ 3."
  - FORGE tracks days on which Cape exceeds a 10-event M≥1 count.
  - FORGE revised its own protocol so that a Cape alarm reduces FORGE injection by 10% instead of forcing a controlled shut-in.
  - There is "currently no evidence for a hydraulic connection between the two reservoirs".
- **Fervo's Nevada rulemaking comments.** Fervo says it follows DOE's 2012 induced-seismicity protocol, calls Cape's implementation "the gold standard", and notes that site-specific protocols are negotiated between operator and regulator. Source: [Fervo written comments, NAC 534A rulemaking (LCB File R093-25), May 2026](https://www.minerals.nv.gov/siteassets/content/commission/meetings/2026/fervo_energy_written_comments_lcb_file_r093-25_nac_534a_may-2026.pdf) [CO] (snippet).

Source: [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51166042/houston-company-tapping-into-utahs-geothermal-power-potential) [IND-PRESS quoting CO] (snippet).

Seismicity began near, but some distance from, where hydraulic fractures intersect a fault. Source: [Kim et al., SGW 2026](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Kim2.pdf) [IND-ACAD] (snippet; full text unreachable).
- **University of Utah 2026 abstract.** Notes "a recent event at the Cape Project", while FORGE had not produced an event at its threshold. Source: [Pankow et al., SGW 2026](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Pankow.pdf) [IND-ACAD] (snippet).
- **Berkeley Lab downhole sensor.** LBNL placed a seismometer in a Cape monitoring well on 27 Jul 2025. It detects more than 100 times as many earthquakes as the nearby UU.FORK station (282 m depth) and saw seismicity rise during stimulation. Source: [Newswise/LBNL](https://www.newswise.com/articles/scientists-develop-new-technology-to-continuously-monitor-geothermal-energy-operations) [IND-GOV] (snippet; page returned 403 to direct fetch).

  - Fervo's own catalog shows about 58 amber-level (M2+) events through April 2026, 29 of them in March–April.

  - A former employee said seismicity is causing casing failures. No count was given.

- **Fervo's own risk factors** ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026072482/frvo-20260930.htm); [424B4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm) [CO]):

### Inferences
- **Catalog shows a real change.** The independent catalog shows a clear step-change in M≥2 seismicity at reservoir depths (about 2–4 km) in the Cape/FORGE area. It rose from zero (2022–2023) to about 1 a month (2024–2025) to about 30 a month (Sep–Nov 2026). The timing matches Phase II/Cape Google 400 drilling and completions, including six-well zipper fracs reported in Q1 2026.
- **Attribution is likely but unproven.** Attributing the events to Cape rather than FORGE is likely, given the University of Utah note and FORGE's statement that it had no threshold event. It is not proven from the catalog alone.
- **Red-light events occurred; the response is unknown.** Both ML 3.1 events reach Cape's red threshold (M≥3). The catalog confirms that an M3.1-class event the short seller described occurred (12 Sep 2026). Whether injection was paused as the protocol requires is not public.
- **Scale compared with Pohang.** Magnitudes so far are far below Pohang's. Radiated energy differs by about 10^(1.5×2.4), or about 4,000 times. No felt-damage reports were found. The Pohang case shows that the tail risk depends on unmapped faults, which Kim et al. 2026 suggest are being activated at Cape.
- **Fault-intersection finding matters for scaling.** Kim et al. 2026 found that seismicity began near fracture–fault intersections. Spreading a field across 8 or more GeoBlocks multiplies the number of such intersections.

### Gaps
- Not found:
  - Fervo's Cape seismic catalog, magnitude of completeness, and record of traffic-light actions;
  - any felt reports or "Did You Feel It?" data for the ML 3.1 events;
  - Utah's induced-seismicity rules for geothermal stimulation;
  - the 2019 Korean government commission finding on Pohang (search budget exhausted);
  - the Project Red seismicity record;
  - full texts of Kim et al. 2026, Pankow et al. 2026, Nakata et al. 2026 (Stanford server HTTP 503) and the Utah Clean Energy July 2026 report.
- I did not check the catalog's "reviewed" or "automatic" status column or the magnitude type for every event (most are ML).

### Inferences
- **Well count is the main scaling variable.** If sustained net output per producer is close to GeoExpro's 24-hour estimate (about 6 MW), 500 MW needs on the order of 80 or more producers plus injectors. If it is close to Fervo's design figure (about 16 MW gross, perhaps about 13 MW net after about 15% parasitic load), it needs about 40. The independent model's 56 producers sits between the two but assumes 107 kg/s sustained.
- **Water and seismicity scale with wells.** The water footprint and the number of stimulated fault intersections grow with well count (Key Questions 3 and 4).
- **Cost conclusions.** On current company figures, Fervo is above DOE's 2030 cost trajectory. Matching DOE's 2035 Earthshot requires roughly halving Phase I cost, which no EGS project has yet shown. The independent GEOPHIRES result ($85/MWh at $5,600/kW) depends on 1% water loss and 107 kg/s per well sustained. Neither has public field confirmation.
- **Resource-size figures shift.** Resource-size claims have moved between bases: over 5 GW "reserves" (June 2025 press release, per GeoExpro), then 4.3 GW "prior estimate", then 8.4 GW HIIP-based potential. These are heat-in-place estimates with an assumed recovery factor, not producible reserves.

## Key Question 6: Competing approaches (Eavor closed-loop, Quaise millimeter-wave and superhot rock, Sage, XGS, Mazama): published results and contradictions with Fervo

### Takeaway
**Eavor.** Eavor's closed-loop Geretsried plant reached first power in December 2025. Reported gross output is about 0.5–1 MW, against an 8.2 MWe design, with about 0.5 MW parasitic load. Only 6 of 12 laterals were completed in the single finished well pair. This field result supports the physical argument that conduction-only closed loops produce far less heat per well than stimulated, convective EGS.

**Other competitors.** Quaise, Mazama, Sage and XGS report mostly pilot-scale or company-verified results. None has published sustained commercial generation. Mazama's superhot claims (331 °C EGS in 2025; 15 MWe per well targeted) are not contradicted by Fervo data and test a different temperature regime.

### Cited Findings
- **Eavor (Geretsried, Germany):**
  - Grid power since December 2025; POWER dates it 4 Dec 2025. Eavor says loops are "performing within modeled parameters" for heat extraction. Sources: [Eavor, 14 Jan 2026](https://eavor.com/2026/01/14/) [CO]; [POWER magazine](https://www.powermag.com/eavors-first-of-its-kind-closed-loop-geothermal-project-produces-grid-power-in-germany/) [IND-PRESS].
  - Design: about 60–64 MW thermal and about 8 MW electric.
  - Actual: about 0.5–1 MW gross, about 0.5 MW parasitic, "barely net-positive or effectively not net-positive".
  - One of four well pairs complete; six of twelve loops completed; three to four contribute materially; two are clogged by rock fragments.
  - Project cost about €368M (EIB). Eavor is pivoting to licensing; staff went from 147 to 80.
  - Source: [CleanTechnica (Michael Barnard), 13 May 2026, citing GeoExpro interview by Henk Kombrink](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/05/13/eavors-geretsried-pivot-raises-hard-questions-about-next-gen-closed-loop-geothermal/) [IND-OPINION].
  - DOE in March 2024 expected the German closed-loop commercial pilot "to be completed in 2028" ([DOE Liftoff](https://apps.openei.org/GeoCLUSTER/assets/pdfs/DOE%20Report%202024_Pathways%20to%20Commercial%20liftoff.pdf) [IND-GOV]).
- **Quaise (millimeter-wave drilling):**
  - Drilled 100 m in granite at a central Texas field site in July 2025 [CO] ([Quaise](https://www.quaise.com/news/quaise-energy-achieves-drilling-milestone-with-millimeter-wave-technology)).
  - A larger 1 MW system was to begin tests in 2026 ([MIT Technology Review, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/22/1120545/geothermal-drilling-quaise/) [IND-PRESS]).
  - "Closing in on a full kilometer" at the Texas site; $134M Series B first close; Oregon plant with first power by 2030 ([Startup Fortune, 14 Jul 2026](https://startupfortune.com/an-mit-spinout-just-raised-134-million-to-drill-for-superhot-rock-in-oregon/) [IND-PRESS, single source; the 1 km figure is unverified]).
- **Mazama (Newberry, Oregon):**
  - Claims "the hottest engineered geothermal system ... ever built" at 331 °C (629 °F) in 2025 ([Renewable Energy Magazine, 3 Nov 2025](https://www.renewableenergymagazine.com/geothermal/mazama-energy-unveils-the-worlda-s-hottest-20251103) [IND-PRESS reporting CO]).
  - Injector drilling is under way in a three-well 2026 campaign targeting rock above 400 °C ([GCN](https://gcn.com/?p=20545) [IND-PRESS]).
  - $135M Series B in September 2026 for DOE-supported Project Ceres, targeting "15 MWe of capacity per well" and power generation in 2027. Earlier releases said a 15 MW pilot in 2026 ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/superhot-geothermal-startup-mazama-raises-135-million/) [IND-PRESS reporting CO]).
- **Sage Geosystems:**
  - $97M+ Series B in January 2026, co-led by Ormat.
  - SMECI test results "met or exceeded" objectives (company statement, no figures).
  - A 150 MW Meta deal depends on a precursor Nevada project at an Ormat site.
  - Sources: [S&P Global, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/1/sage-geosystems-raises-97m-for-world-s-1st-pressure-geothermal-power-plant-97375130) [IND-PRESS]; [Business Wire, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260121330914/en/) [CO].
- **XGS Energy:**
  - Closed-loop design using thermally conductive material.
  - Ran a system at Coso for more than 3,000 hours. Says performance fell "within 2%" of predictions and was verified by independent engineers (a company claim).
  - 150 MW Meta project in New Mexico.
  - Source: [Canary Media](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/geothermal/xgs-energy-advanced-tech-scale-up-meta) [IND-PRESS reporting CO; article date not confirmed].
- **Fervo's own depth trend.** Fervo's Blanford/Cottonwood well reached 555 °F (about 291 °C) at 11,200 ft with conventional rigs. Source: [Q1 2026 release](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-reports-first-quarter-2026-results/) [CO].

### Inferences
- **Eavor contradiction.** Eavor's "within modeled parameters" statement sits uneasily with an output about 8–16 times below design. Most of the shortfall comes from incomplete laterals, not from a disproven heat-transfer model. Even so, it shows that drilling and completion execution can dominate next-generation geothermal outcomes. That is a risk Fervo shares, through its larger stimulated well counts.
- **Fervo versus closed loop.** The convective throughput of Fervo's EGS (36 kg/s at Project Red; 93–107 kg/s per producer reported at Cape) is far above anything closed-loop systems have shown. But it brings the water-loss and seismicity risks that closed-loop designs claim to avoid. Those claims remain largely undemonstrated at commercial scale (XGS's are company-verified only).
- **Quaise and Mazama.** Their thesis is that superhot rock (above about 374 °C) is needed for step-changes in power per well. This competes with Fervo's thesis that about 200 °C rock and many shale-style wells suffice. Neither thesis has yet produced sustained commercial output from superhot rock, so field data do not settle the contradiction.

  - Mazama flow-test or power numbers from the 331 °C system;
  - Sage and XGS quantitative results;
  - any peer-reviewed comparison of EGS and closed-loop field performance.

## Synthesis question: Does Fervo's approach stand on solid ground? What is demonstrated vs unproven, and where do the sources contradict each other?

### Takeaway
**Solid ground (demonstrated):**
- The basic engineering. Horizontal drilling plus multistage stimulation creates a circulating reservoir in hot crystalline rock. This is shown by Fervo at two sites and independently by Utah FORGE.
- The ability to build and commission a 33 MW net binary plant to contract.

**Unproven:**
- sustained per-well net output at Cape;
- reservoir longevity without frequent redrilling;
- water retention near 99%;
- installed cost on DOE's trajectory;
- managing a rapidly rising rate of M2–3 seismicity as the field expands.

Each of these rests today on company assertions, Fervo-co-authored models, or short-duration data that critics have analyzed and found weaker than the headlines.

### Cited Findings
**Contradictions log (each item has its sources):**

1. **Project Red output.**
   - "3.5 MW" (2023 headline; [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230718629841/en) [CO])

   - versus 2.1 MW average gross / 1.4 MW net over 614 days ([Fervo, Apr 2026](https://fervoenergy.com/enhanced-geothermal-has-been-proven-at-scale-heres-what-two-years-of-production-data-show/) [CO])
   - versus about 1.3 MW net estimated beforehand ([GeoExpro, Apr 2026](https://geoexpro.com/has-enhanced-geothermal-been-proven-at-scale/) [IND-OPINION]).

**Independent supports for the basic technique:**
- Utah FORGE's 2024 month-long circulation: more than 90% recovery, about 370 °F, maximum M1.9 ([JPT](https://jpt.spe.org/fervo-and-forge-report-breakthrough-test-results-signaling-more-progress-for-enhanced-geothermal) [IND-PRESS]).
- DOE's finding that FORGE's rate of penetration improved more than 500% ([DOE](https://apps.openei.org/GeoCLUSTER/assets/pdfs/DOE%20Report%202024_Pathways%20to%20Commercial%20liftoff.pdf) [IND-GOV]).
- Independent experts discount the short seller's Project Red claims as misreadings of a research-pilot design ([Heatmap](https://heatmap.news/energy/fervo-short-seller-report) [IND-PRESS]).

### Inferences
**Evidence-based reasons the core approach is technically sound:**
1. **Two sites, plus an analog.** Fracture-connected well pairs and triplets have been created and circulated at two Fervo sites and at FORGE, by an independent operator in the same Mineral Mountains granitoid.
2. **Pilot longevity.** Project Red ran more than 600 days at about 175 °C with 98.4% uptime (company-reported, with regulator data available).
3. **Plant delivered to contract.** A 33 MW net plant met its PPA production threshold and contractual date. This is stated in SEC filings, which carry liability.
4. **Drilling trend.** The drilling learning curve matches an independent federal site.

**Evidence-based reasons key claims remain unproven:**
1. **Public Cape data are thin.** There are only 24 hours of published Cape flow data, and the productivity index estimated from them is low.
2. **Decline matches critics' concerns.** The only multi-year record shows decline after about 500 days, at about 2 °F/month in late 2025 to 2026. The independent Cape-like model needs redrilling about every 8 years.
3. **Water retention unconfirmed.** Water retention at commercial scale (90% reported, 99% modeled) has no published data. The difference changes make-up water about ten-fold.
4. **Cost gap.** Costs are about 1.4–1.9 times DOE's targets, and the cost trajectory has risen since 2024.
5. **Seismicity rising.** Seismicity is accelerating during field expansion, with red-threshold-magnitude events. Fervo's monitoring catalog and responses are undisclosed.
6. **No independent field review.** No peer-reviewed field paper, no independent reserves report and no third-party well-test review exists for Cape.

The core technique stands on firm ground. Its commercial-scale performance claims (MW per well, decline, water, cost) do not yet. They are currently testable, because Cape is operating, but they have not been tested in public.

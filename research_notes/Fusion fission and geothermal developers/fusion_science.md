## 1. Benchmarks: best demonstrated results in magnetic and inertial fusion, and what each company's next machine needs

### Takeaway
No magnetic-confinement device has reached scientific breakeven (best D-T Q_sci ≈ 0.64, JET 1997); the inertial route has (NIF record: 8.6 MJ from 2.08 MJ, target gain 4.13, 7 Apr 2025). SPARC's own published projection needs roughly 8x the best tokamak triple product ever recorded (in the uniform convention of the ARPA-E Wurzel & Hsu compilation) and ~17x JET's best Q; Helion's D-He3 route needs a triple product ~18x higher than D-T ignition at ion temperatures of ~60-70 keV, far above anything demonstrated in any concept; Thea's Eos is deliberately a sub-breakeven D-D neutron source, but the company's web figure for its triple product (10^21 keV s m^-3) would be ~16x the best published W7-X value in the same compilation.

### Cited Findings
**Inertial (NIF, LLNL) — [IND]**
- LLNL's ignition list (accessed 14 Nov 2026): first ignition 5 Dec 2022, 3.15 MJ from 2.05 MJ of laser energy; 7th ignition 23 Feb 2025, 5.0 MJ, target gain 2.44; **8th ignition 7 Apr 2025: 8.6 MJ (±0.45 MJ) from 2.08 MJ delivered, target gain 4.13 — still the record**; 10th ignition 1 Oct 2025, 3.5 MJ (±0.17), gain 1.74; **11th ignition 20 Jun 2026: 7.9 MJ (±0.4), gain ≈3.8** — [LLNL Laboratory for Mission-relevant Fusion (formerly NIF site), "Achieving Fusion Ignition", accessed 2026-11-14](https://lmf.llnl.gov/science/achieving-fusion-ignition) [IND]
- LLNL attributes the April 2025 record mainly to diamond capsules made with "continuous gradient doping" of tungsten — [LLNL, "Target Breakthrough Enabled Fusion Record at NIF"](https://lasers.llnl.gov/news/target-breakthrough-enabled-fusion-record-nif) [IND] (seen via search summary, not fetched in full)

### Inferences
- SPARC's projected 6.2×10^21 is ~8x the best tokamak triple product in the uniform Wurzel & Hsu convention (7.9×10^20, TFTR 1995) and ~13x JET 1997; its Q ≈ 11 projection is ~17x JET's Q_sci 0.64 (its Q > 2 mission is ~3x). At 140 MW for up to 10 s, one SPARC pulse would release up to ~1.4 GJ, ~20x JET's 69 MJ record (JET averaged ~13 MW over 5.2 s). These are arithmetic comparisons of the cited figures.
- D-³He at Q_fuel = 1 (2.2×10^22) is ~28x the best D-T tokamak triple product ever recorded and ~10^5 x the best published FRC value (C-2W, 2.3×10^17). No experiment of any type has approached D-³He breakeven conditions in the published record I found.
- Helion's stated 200 million °C (~17 keV) is far below the 63–68 keV thermal optimum for D-³He in Wurzel & Hsu; Helion's case therefore rests on non-standard assumptions (very hot ions relative to electrons, pulsed direct energy recovery), which are exactly what the 2026 independent critiques dispute (Section 3).
- Thea's 10^21 keV s m^-3 web figure is ~16x the best W7-X value in the Wurzel & Hsu table (6.2×10^19, 2017) and above the best tokamak values in that convention; the peer-reviewed Eos paper frames Eos as sub-breakeven D-D, so the web figure is unexplained company messaging, not a published result (see Section 7).
- Long-pulse records (W7-X 360 s/1.8 GJ; WEST 1337 s; EAST 1066 s) and high-gain records (NIF) have been achieved in different machines; no device has combined high gain with long duration.

## 2. Commonwealth Fusion Systems (SPARC, ARC): demonstrated vs. predicted vs. unproven

### Takeaway
CFS (with MIT) has demonstrated, and published in peer-reviewed IEEE papers, a large-bore REBCO toroidal-field model coil at 20.1 T — including an intentional quench that caused localized damage and led to design changes; SPARC's Q>1 (mission Q>2, nominal Q≈11) rests on established tokamak confinement scalings and multi-institution peer-reviewed modelling but has not been tested, and SPARC first plasma has slipped from 2025 to a 2026–2027 window. ARC-level issues (tritium breeding ratio > 1, FLiBe blanket, materials, HTS under neutrons, availability) remain undemonstrated anywhere.

### Cited Findings
**Demonstrated — magnets**
- TFMC programme (MIT PSFC + CFS, 2018–2021): **20.1 T peak field on conductor at 40.5 kA, 815 kN/m Lorentz loading on REBCO stacks, ~1 GPa stress in the case, 15 demountable pancake joints at 0.5–2.0 nΩ (20 K, up to 12 T), 600 W cooling at 20 K, current leads qualified to 50 kA; passive quench self-protection assessed by an intentional open-circuit quench at 31.5 kA** — [Hartwig et al., "The SPARC Toroidal Field Model Coil Program", arXiv:2308.12301 (Aug 2023); IEEE Trans. Appl. Supercond. 34(2), Mar 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12301); [IEEE Xplore 10316582](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10316582) [MIX]. Test date Sept 2021 (20 T at 20 K) — [Wikipedia SPARC, citing MIT News 8 Sep 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPARC_(tokamak)) [SEC]
- Companion peer-reviewed papers in the same IEEE TAS issue (Mar 2024): [Whyte et al., "Experimental Assessment and Model Validation of the SPARC TFMC", doi:10.1109/TASC.2023.3332823](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASC.2023.3332823); [Michael et al., 20-K 600-W cryogenic system, doi:10.1109/TASC.2023.3332266](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASC.2023.3332266); [Fry et al., 50-kA current leads, doi:10.1109/TASC.2024.3354237](https://doi.org/10.1109/TASC.2024.3354237) [MIX]
- Quench damage: the TFMC "was intentionally not optimized for quench resiliency and suffered localized thermal damage in response to an intentional open-circuit quench at 31.5 kA"; damage concentrated in a few pancakes with the epicentre in Pancake #12; countermeasures (externally driven heaters, a turn-by-turn inductive quench cascade) were adopted for SPARC's TF magnets; a 2025 abstract states that self-protection of non-insulated coils with large stored energy "is not well understood" and its 2D model is still being validated — [CERN Indico conference abstract, Jul 2025](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1431972/contributions/6420205) [author affiliations not verified; content seen via search summary]. Whyte et al. (IEEE TAS 2024) abstract reportedly states "highly localized damage occurs during a programmed open circuit at operational conditions similar to those used in the SPARC tokamak" and concludes the coil still meets SPARC programmatic requirements — [library catalogue record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edo,ejs64901823) (abstract text seen only via search snippet; page itself was bot-blocked) [MIX]
- Central solenoid: "November 2024: a prototype of the central solenoid was demonstrated" — [Wikipedia SPARC, citing MIT Technology Review 31 Oct 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPARC_(tokamak)) [SEC]; CFS–BNL INFUSE work (2020–2021) on PIT VIPER cable targeting ramp rates up to 4 T/s reported "inconclusive" early AC-loss results — [CFS–BNL INFUSE report, 21 Oct 2021](https://infuse.ornl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/CFS-BNL-INFUSE-Report_20211021-V3.pdf) [MIX]; peer-reviewed cable paper: [Sanabria et al., "Development of a high current density, high temperature superconducting cable for pulsed magnets", Supercond. Sci. Technol. 37(11), Oct 2024, doi:10.1088/1361-6668/ad7efc](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6668/ad7efc) [MIX; not read in full]; an independent 2026 design study says PIT VIPER cables are "predicted to carry 50 kA per cable at 20 K and 25 T" and that its 25.8 T solenoid is "only marginally above the maximum field tested on PIT VIPER cables" — [CENTAUR design study, arXiv:2605.27549 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27549) [IND]

**Predicted, not yet demonstrated — SPARC physics**
- Nominal projection Q ≈ 11, P_fus ≈ 140 MW at H98,y2 = 1; Q > 2 "achievable with conservative physics assumptions" (H98,y2 = 0.7) — [Creely et al., JPP 86(5), Oct 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022377820001257) [MIX; co-authors' repositories include Chalmers, VTT, ORNL, Univ. of Iowa and Univ. of York, e.g. [Chalmers record](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/519720)]
- Empirical scaling gives Q ≈ 11; physics-based TRANSP+TGLF+EPED modelling gives **Q ≈ 9** in standard H-mode, and Q > 2 "even with adverse assumptions" — [Rodriguez-Fernandez et al., "Predictions of core plasma performance for the SPARC tokamak", JPP, 29 Sep 2020, doi:10.1017/S0022377820001075](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022377820001075) [MIX]
- 2024 nonlinear CGYRO study: an L-mode breakeven (Q>1) window may exist without H-mode, but "maximum fusion performance… will be highly dependent on the near-edge pressure"; core transport is "stiff" as Q approaches unity — [Rodriguez-Fernandez et al., arXiv:2403.15633 (Mar/May 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15633) [MIX; journal status not shown]
- Disruptions: SPARC is designed for unmitigated axisymmetric halo-current forces up to **50 MN**; disruption loading "comparable to, and in some cases higher than, ITER"; thermal-quench duration uncertain (~50 µs to ~1 ms); **no passive solution identified for thermal-quench divertor heat flux** (active mitigation via massive material injection); runaway-electron (RE) seed-loss predictions disagree (NIMROD ~80% losses vs. GO+CODE high conversion) — [Sweeney et al., "MHD stability and disruptions in the SPARC tokamak", JPP, 29 Sep 2020](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-plasma-physics/article/mhd-stability-and-disruptions-in-the-sparc-tokamak/908C6788C0D625C5DDF335DBD9A17476) [MIX; co-authors from MIT, CFS, Chalmers, General Atomics and others]
- RE mitigation coil (REMC): passive 3D coil modelled to prevent RE beam formation — [Tinguely et al., arXiv:2110.10598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.10598) [MIX]; NIMROD modelling predicts success of a six-valve MGI system and the REMC — [APS DPP 2024 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dpp/2024/bi02/6) [MIX]; first self-consistent 3D extended-MHD study (Nov 2026) finds REMC-driven stochasticity "can reduce the RE current by over an order-of-magnitude" vs. unmitigated disruptions — [Datta et al., arXiv:2611.04906, 3 Nov 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04906) [MIX; preprint, simulation only]

### Inferences
- Evidence-based assessment (not a ranking): the CFS concept stands on the largest experimental database in fusion (decades of tokamak D-T and confinement data, Alcator C-Mod high-field heritage), and its main new enabling technology (20 T REBCO TF coil at ~3 m scale) has been demonstrated and documented in peer-reviewed literature, including candid reporting of quench damage. The physics prediction for SPARC is an extrapolation of established empirical scalings to higher field and density, cross-checked by first-principles turbulence modelling (Q ≈ 9–11 nominal; Q > 2 under pessimistic confinement). What is not demonstrated: any D-T tokamak Q > 1; disruption and runaway-electron mitigation at 8.7 MA and 12.2 T (simulation-only, with model disagreements); thermal-quench heat-flux mitigation (no passive solution); full-scale HTS quench protection after the TFMC damage; HTS performance under fusion neutron fluence (conflicting irradiation results).
- The magnet demonstration is MIT/CFS-authored; it is peer-reviewed (IEEE TAS) but has not been independently replicated by another organisation at comparable scale in the sources found.
- For ARC, the measured TBR in the MIT BABY experiments (10^-4 level in 0.1–1 L) is orders of magnitude from ARC's calculated ≥1.1 — not a contradiction (geometry and volume differ by design) but confirmation that TBR > 1 in FLiBe exists only in calculation.
- CFS's public language shifted from the published mission "Q > 2" (JPP 2020) to "Q > 1" (2026 blog). Both are consistent with the physics papers, but the milestone that will be publicised is the lower bar.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed publication of a CFS central-solenoid model coil test was found; the Nov 2024 CS "prototype demonstrated" claim is sourced only to Wikipedia/MIT Technology Review.
- Full text of the Whyte et al. 2024 quench-damage analysis was not accessible; quantitative damage extent (e.g., I_c loss, number of damaged turns) not verified.
- No steady-state SPARC/ARC divertor heat-flux numbers (e.g., Kuang et al. JPP 2020) were retrieved.
- No data on HTS tape supply volumes (tape km needed for SPARC/ARC, global production capacity) were retrieved before the search budget ran out.
- No confirmed SPARC first-plasma date as of 14 Nov 2026; sources conflict (2026 vs 2027).
- No MIT cryogenic neutron-irradiation results for REBCO were found.

## 3. Helion Energy (pulsed FRC, magnetic compression, direct inductive recovery; Polaris, Orion)

### Takeaway
Helion's published, peer-reviewed experimental record is thin and old (a 2011 Nuclear Fusion paper on its predecessor device); its headline results for Trenta (2021, ~9 keV "total" temperature) and Polaris (Feb 2026, D-T fusion, 150 million °C/≈13 keV) are company announcements with selected outside reviewers, not peer-reviewed data, and no absolute neutron yields, densities or confinement times for Polaris have been published. D-³He fusion at reactor-relevant conditions has not been demonstrated anywhere, and two independent peer-reviewed analyses published in 2026 (IPP/EPFL; CNRS/EPFL) argue Helion's D-³He gain case requires much tighter conditions (e.g., >10 ms confinement below 25 T) than its 2023 model assumes; Helion disputes these critiques, and its claimed >95% energy-recovery efficiency has no independent measurement in the sources found.

### Inferences
- Evidence-based assessment (not a ranking): FRC formation, supersonic merging and magnetic compression heating have a real experimental lineage (Slough et al. 2011; decades of academic FRC work; TAE's peer-reviewed C-2 series), so the underlying plasma configuration is not speculative. What is not on solid published ground is the specific path to net electricity: (1) D-³He fusion at the required conditions has never been demonstrated by anyone; (2) Helion's own Trenta/Polaris performance data (temperatures, densities, confinement, yields) are unpublished in peer-reviewed form, and the "150 million °C / 13 keV" figure follows Helion's 2021 convention of quoting a "total" (ion + electron) temperature, so the ion temperature alone is unknown; (3) the gain case depends on T_i ≫ T_e persisting and on very high recovery efficiency, both contested by two independent 2026 peer-reviewed analyses; (4) no independent measurement of the >95% recovery efficiency was found (the company's own Nov 2025 blog cites "above 90%" circuit efficiency).
- Confinement gap (arithmetic on cited figures): Nicolas et al.'s ≥10 ms requirement is ≥20x Trenta's company-reported confinement "up to 0.5 ms" and >10x Helion's sub-millisecond compression; Venti's company-presented 6.4×10^18 keV s m^-3 is ~3,400x below the D-³He Q_fuel = 1 triple product (2.2×10^22). Helion argues equilibrium Lawson metrics do not capture its pulsed, non-equilibrium cycle; the published critiques address exactly that claim and still find tighter requirements.
- Repetition/lifetime (arithmetic): 1 Hz operation means ~3.2×10^7 pulses per year; Trenta's company-reported >10,000 pulses in 16 months is ~3,000x fewer than one year at 1 Hz. No capacitor or switch lifetime data at that cadence were found.
- He-3 via D-D: with a 12.32-year tritium half-life, only ~5.6% of stored tritium decays to He-3 per year, so a D-D-based He-3 supply implies holding tritium inventories for years (decay-law arithmetic); this also means a D-³He plant still handles tritium and D-D neutrons.
- The selected outside reviewers of Polaris data (DOE official, a Michigan pulsed-power professor, an FRC expert) provide some external scrutiny, but this is not equivalent to peer review or independent replication, and none of the quoted reviewers published numbers.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or conference-proceedings paper with Trenta or Polaris ion/electron temperatures, densities, confinement times or neutron yields was found (the 2021 IEEE SOFE paper cited by Kirtley & Milroy was not retrieved).
- No absolute D-T neutron yield for Polaris has been published (company says analysis is ongoing).
- No independent measurement or published dataset for energy-recovery efficiency (95% claim) was found.
- No data on capacitor/switch lifetimes, failure rates or Polaris pulse counts at high cadence.
- No independent assessment of Helion's He-3 production efficiency claim.
- Axios Pro article content was not directly accessible (403); details come from search summaries.

## 4. Thea Energy (planar-coil stellarator: Eos, Helios)

### Takeaway
Thea's innovation is engineering (replacing twisted modular coils with planar encircling coils plus hundreds of individually controlled planar HTS "shaping" coils), backed by peer-reviewed Nuclear Fusion design papers (Jan–Feb 2025), a 3x3 HTS coil-array test (Canis; ±1% field-shape control at low field) and a company-announced full-scale Eos-spec coil (>6 T at 20 K, May 2026); stellarator physics itself is on increasingly solid experimental ground (W7-X 2025 long-pulse records), but no planar-coil stellarator has confined plasma, Eos is a sub-breakeven D-D device, and Helios (390 MWe) is a preconceptual design certified by a DOE expert panel rather than an experimentally validated one.

### Cited Findings
**What a planar-coil stellarator requires (company's own analysis)**
- Rule of thumb: stellarator field control B_RMS/B0 < 10^-4, relaxable to ~10^-3 with "special optimization for tolerance"; Thea's shaping coils supply only ~3×10^-2 of the total field, so they need ~10^-2 accuracy — hence a ±1% RMS field-error target — [Nash et al., "Prototyping and Test of the 'Canis' HTS Planar Coil Array for Stellarator Field Shaping", preprint, Mar 2025](https://thea.energy/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Preprint_Prototyping-and-Test-of-the-Canis-HTS-PlanarCoil-Array-for-Stellarator-Field-Shaping.pdf); [arXiv:2503.18960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18960); published in IEEE TAS per Thea — [IEEE Xplore 11105783](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11105783) [CO authors, peer-reviewed per company]
- Eos-class demands: encircling coils must give a background field >10 T at some shaping coils to reach 6 T on axis; shaping-coil conductor field expected to exceed 14 T at current density >200 A/mm² — same source [CO]
- Thea's motivation: W7-X (IPP), NCSX (PPPL) and HSX (UW–Madison) "significantly exceeded cost and schedule estimates due in part to the complexity of the modular coils", and NCSX was cancelled — same source [CO, citing Bosch et al. IEEE TPS 2018 and others]

**What Canis and later coil tests showed**
- Canis: nine HTS shaping coils (each 150 A nominal, 1,500 turns, 225 kA-turns, 4.4 kJ stored, 1.8 T in bore, 3.7 T peak self-field, 20 K, soldered-metal-insulation), closed-loop control with cryogenic Hall sensors; field projected on a plane 25 cm from the array controlled to within ±1% RMS of prediction; still to do: transient field-shape control, quench self-protection in several scenarios, robustness to coil defects — [Nash et al., Mar 2025](https://thea.energy/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Preprint_Prototyping-and-Test-of-the-Canis-HTS-PlanarCoil-Array-for-Stellarator-Field-Shaping.pdf) [CO]; funded by Thea and the DOE Milestone Program (DE-SC0024881) — same source
- First Eos-spec planar shaping coil "created and controlled a magnetic field of over 6 T" at 20 K and reached full Eos current (current not stated); >50 coil generations in ~18 months; quench-survivability tests still pending — [Thea press release, 13 May 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/thea-energy-announces-successful-operation-of-full-scale-planar-shaping-coils/) [CO]

**Peer-reviewed Eos physics basis and reviewers**
- Eos: "sub-breakeven, deuterium-deuterium, beam-target fusion, stellarator neutron source" for tritium and radioisotope production; ~2.5×10^17 n/s; <40 MW facility electric; ~0.2 g/day (~70 g/yr) tritium with an added breeding blanket; scoped with 1D physics models — [Swanson, Gates, Kumar, Martin, Kruger, Dudt, Bonofiglo, Nucl. Fusion 65(2) 026053, Feb 2025, doi:10.1088/1741-4326/ada56a](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ada56a) [MIX: Thea authors; the Princeton/PPPL repository lists the paper under a PPPL research unit; anonymous peer review]
- Companion: [Gates et al., "Stellarator fusion systems enabled by arrays of planar coils", Nucl. Fusion 65(2) 026052, Jan 2025, doi:10.1088/1741-4326/ada56c](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ada56c) [MIX; not read in full]; Thea's website cites [Bonofiglo, Nucl. Fusion 2025, doi:10.1088/1741-4326/ada56d](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-4326/ada56d) for a claim of ">10x better optimization than W7-X" — [Thea "Eos" page](https://thea.energy/eos/) [CO claim; not verified]
- Helios preconceptual design: R = 8 m, aspect ratio 4.5, B0 = 6 T, ⟨β⟩ = 2.7%, ~960 MW fusion, **390 MWe net**, 12 planar encircling + **324 planar shaping coils** (HTS, 20 K, ≤20 T on coil), average normal-field error 0.21% on the boundary, PbLi blanket (65% Li-6), idealized TBR 1.3 (1.1 deemed sufficient), ~88% capacity factor (84-day outage every two years); stated open risks: divertor heat flux above an assumed 10 MW/m² limit needs more work, ~6.6% of alpha energy lost to the wall (peaks to 4 MW/m²), ISS04 confinement enhancement of 1.4 assumed and "should be justified", vanadium-alloy first wall uncertain, startup magnetic control only preliminarily modelled; Eos first plasma targeted 2030, Helios mid-2030s — [Swanson et al., "Overview of the Helios Design…", arXiv:2512.08027 v3, 5 Jan 2026 (submitted to Fusion Eng. Des.)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08027) [CO, with Columbia co-authors]
- DOE certification: on 13 Jan 2026 Thea announced DOE certified the Helios preconceptual design — the first Milestone Program awardee to complete its final major milestone — after review by "a panel of independent fusion experts", including Felix Parra-Diaz (head of PPPL theory department); Eos site not yet chosen (talks with five states) — [Thea press release, 13 Jan 2026](https://thea.energy/press-release/u-s-department-of-energy-certifies-thea-energys-fusion-pilot-plant-preconceptual-design) [CO]

**Comparison with W7-X and other stellarator programmes**
- W7-X 2025: long-pulse triple-product record (43 s), 1.8 GJ/360 s, β = 3% (4–5% needed), IPP's Robert Wolf: records "represent a significant step forward in validating the stellarator concept" — [IPP, Jun 2025](https://www.ipp.mpg.de/5532945/w7x); [ITER Newsline](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/new-records-wendelstein-7-x) [IND]
- Type One Energy: Infinity Two D-T stellarator pilot plant, 800 MW fusion; physics basis published as a Journal of Plasma Physics special collection (guest editor Chris Hegna) covering alpha confinement, MHD, blanket/fuel cycle and exhaust; sources disagree on 6 vs 7 papers — [JPP collection](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-plasma-physics/collections/physics-basis-of-the-infinity-two-fusion-power-plant) [MIX]; [ANS Nuclear News, 1 Apr 2025](https://www.ans.org/news/2025-04-01/article-6903/type-one-publishes-design-basis-for-its-stellarator-fusion-pilot-plant) [IND]

### Inferences
- Evidence-based assessment (not a ranking): the plasma-physics side of Thea's plan borrows from optimized-stellarator physics that W7-X has substantially validated (steady long pulses, record long-pulse triple product, β = 3%), so the confinement concept is not novel physics. The novel and still-unproven element is the magnet architecture: no integrated planar-coil stellarator has formed a plasma, and the coil evidence is (a) a low-field 3x3 array (1.8 T bore) meeting ±1% shape control on a measurement plane, and (b) one full-scale coil reaching >6 T — with no statement that it was tested inside the >10 T background field Eos shaping coils must tolerate (14 T+ on conductor per Thea's own paper).
- Field-accuracy arithmetic: ±1% shaping-coil accuracy × ~3% field share ≈ 3×10^-4 of total field — consistent with the tolerance-optimized 10^-3 target cited by Thea but above the conventional 10^-4 rule of thumb; Eos/Helios therefore depend on configurations deliberately optimized for error tolerance and on active current correction across hundreds of coils (324 in Helios), which adds control and power-supply complexity not yet demonstrated at scale.
- Peer review status: the Nuclear Fusion papers passed anonymous journal review, and the DOE panel (incl. a PPPL theorist) reviewed a design report; neither constitutes experimental verification. The Helios overview is still a preprint.
- Eos's peer-reviewed description (sub-breakeven D-D beam-target neutron source) means it will not test D-T burning-plasma physics; its value as a Helios precursor is for magnets, steady-state operation, exhaust and (in a later phase) breeding/neutronics.

### Gaps
- No Eos design parameters (major radius, plasma volume, density, temperature, heating power) were retrieved beyond the abstract-level figures; the Eos triple-product web figure's basis is not published.
- The Canis IEEE TAS published version was not accessed; reviewers are anonymous.
- No independent test or third-party verification of the May 2026 Eos-spec coil; operating current, background field and quench results not reported.
- Proxima Fusion (Stellaris, Alpha) details and coil architecture of Type One's design were not retrieved before the search budget ran out.
- No numerical comparison of Eos/Helios assumed confinement (ISS04 × 1.4) with W7-X OP2.3 measured confinement enhancement was found.

## 5. Cross-cutting physical and engineering limits

### Takeaway
Several plant-level prerequisites are undemonstrated for every D-T concept: no blanket has shown TBR > 1, there is still no fusion-spectrum (14 MeV) materials-irradiation facility (IFMIF-DONES main-building works started only in Sept 2026), REBCO radiation tolerance at cryogenic temperature is disputed in the 2025 literature, and the U.S. NRC's fusion-specific byproduct-material rule was still at the proposed stage in the latest source found (Feb 2026, final rule targeted for Oct 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Tritium supply: CANDU tritium "remains sufficient to support the full operation of ITER", but availability for DEMO after ITER is "largely uncertain", and full tritium breeding may not be demonstrated in DEMO until after 2050 — [Pearson, Antoniazzi, Nuttall, "Tritium supply and use: a key issue for the development of nuclear fusion energy", Fusion Eng. Des. 136B (2018) 1140–1148, doi:10.1016/j.fusengdes.2018.04.090](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fusengdes.2018.04.090) [IND]; an IAEA conference poster (2018) states OPG "may only be able to supply up to 8 kg for a DEMO reactor in the mid-2050s" — [IAEA conference contribution](https://conferences.iaea.org/event/151/contributions/6203/contribution.pdf) [IND; seen via search summary]

## 6. Independent assessments and outside voices

### Takeaway
The main independent policy benchmark (National Academies, Feb 2021) set the U.S. goal of net electricity from a pilot plant in 2035–2040; DOE's Milestone Program uses independent expert panels to review company design milestones (Thea's Helios certified Jan 2026; CFS's ARC preconceptual design approved by a 14-expert panel, Aug 2026), but these review designs, not plasma performance. The most direct independent scientific scrutiny in 2026 targeted Helion (two J. Fusion Energy papers; ORNL's Troy Carter on transparency).

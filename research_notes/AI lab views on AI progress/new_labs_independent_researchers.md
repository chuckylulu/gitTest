# Newer research-led AI labs and independent researchers: what they said about the progress of AI (Oct 12 – Nov 11, 2026)

Conventions used in these notes (read first):
- Labels: **[Founder]** = statement by a lab founder/CEO; **[Researcher]** = statement by a named researcher or paper author; **[Official]** = lab publication (paper, model card, blog, official post); **[Third-party]** = characterisation by a journalist, academic or other outsider.
- "Verbatim" = wording as it appeared in a search extract or in a document read directly in this session. "Paraphrase" = my wording of what the source reports. Quotes relayed by a secondary outlet are flagged as such.
- Dates of X posts were derived from the post-ID timestamp (not from the page), so they are UTC dates.
- Read directly in this session: the full text of Sakana's arXiv paper (via Hugging Face), Hugging Face paper metadata, and the Hugging Face model cards for Recursive Flint, Reflection Beam and Liquid LFM3-1.7B; public GitHub org listings. Everything else comes from search extracts: WebFetch is blocked in this session (psi.inc returned ENOTFOUND), so lab blog pages (recursive.com, psi.inc, pi.website, liquid.ai, cartesia.ai, scottaaronson.blog) were NOT opened and their wording is second-hand.
- Search budget: 47 extended web searches were run; the session's shared search limit was then reached and three planned searches (continual-learning neolabs such as Core Automation / Ineffable Intelligence, World Labs, and the "physics research agents" benchmark) were refused. Gaps caused by this are listed per question.
- "Outside window" marks items dated before Oct 12, 2026 that are kept only as one-line context.

## 1. Safe Superintelligence, Thinking Machines Lab and AMI Labs: path to more capable AI, limits of LLMs, continual learning, world models, safety

### Takeaway
Of the three, only Yann LeCun (AMI Labs) made a substantive in-window statement about the technology's trajectory: at Dartmouth on Oct 29 he said LLMs are "never going to reach human-level intelligence" and that "we're not anywhere close to human-level AI", offering JEPA-style world models as the replacement. Mira Murati's only in-window remarks found (same event) were about human understanding and control rather than technical direction, and no public statement by Ilya Sutskever or SSI was found in the window at all.

### Cited Findings

**AMI Labs / Yann LeCun**
- [Founder, talk, Oct 29, 2026] LeCun received Dartmouth's "AI at 70" Award and gave the keynote "World Models: Enabling the Next AI Revolution" (Thursday Oct 29, 1:45 p.m.; the schedule lists Peter Chin as presenter of the award and LeCun as founder/chair of AMI Labs) — [Dartmouth conference schedule](https://ai.dartmouth.edu/ai-70-events/what-must-remain-human-conference/conference-schedule); recording: [YouTube live](https://www.youtube.com/live/7Yr62DCtIVs)
- [Founder, same talk] Verbatim as quoted in Dartmouth's release: "Despite what you might hear from some of the more optimistic CEOs of Silicon Valley, we're not anywhere close to human-level AI." — [Dartmouth News, Oct 2026](https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2026/10/dartmouth-convenes-landmark-ai-70-conference)
- [Founder, same talk] Paraphrase of Dartmouth's release: he believes another revolution in AI will take place, with systems applicable to the real, physical world; world models let an agent anticipate the consequences of its actions, which makes it possible to build systems that take safe and meaningful actions (i.e. he frames world models as both the capability path and the safety path) — [Dartmouth News](https://home.dartmouth.edu/news/2026/10/dartmouth-convenes-landmark-ai-70-conference)
- [Founder, same talk, relayed by student newspaper] LeCun argued that large language models are "never going to reach human-level intelligence" (verbatim fragment), that regurgitating known information is not intelligence (paraphrase), and that diffusion models "don't understand the underlying nature of the world" (verbatim fragment); he called his "solution" the joint-embedding predictive architecture, which predicts an abstract representation rather than pixels (paraphrase) — [The Dartmouth, Nov 2026](https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2026/11/the-dartmouth-staff-dartmouth-hosts-ai-at-70-conference)
- [Official/Researcher, outside window — Oct 5, 2026] "H-JEPA: End-to-End Learning of Hierarchical World Models for Visual Planning" (Wancong Zhang, Basile Terver, Michael Rabbat, Yann LeCun, Randall Balestriero): trains a hierarchy of action-conditioned JEPAs, each level predicting farther ahead in its own latent space; verbatim from abstract: "on Visual AntMaze, a three-level hierarchy raises success from 18% to 73% using less planner compute"; evidence is four simulated navigation/manipulation environments plus offline planning on DROID robot video — [arXiv 2610.06805 via Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.06805). A Chinese outlet describes it as AMI's debut results with code and weights open-sourced — [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4019764850560904)

### Inferences
- LeCun's in-window position is unchanged in substance from earlier years, but is now delivered as chairman of a funded competitor and aimed explicitly at "the more optimistic CEOs of Silicon Valley"; the published evidence AMI-affiliated authors have offered so far (H-JEPA, one week before the window) is at toy/simulation scale, so the claim that world models will replace LLMs remains an argument, not a demonstrated result.
- Murati's public framing ("AI that people can shape and make their own", "human understanding at the center") positions Thinking Machines against centralised, one-size-fits-all models on governance and product grounds rather than against the LLM paradigm itself; nothing in-window shows the lab dissenting on architecture.
- SSI's silence means the "continual learning as the missing ingredient" thesis associated with Sutskever had no fresh primary statement in this window; any report text on it must cite the Nov 2025 interview as background only.

## 2. Labs built around automated research and self-improvement (Recursive, Reflection, Sakana, PSI, Periodic, Lila): claims, evidence and stated limits

### Takeaway
In-window, three labs put concrete artefacts behind the "AI does research" claim: Recursive (a coding model it says its "Eureka Machine" helped build at nearly every step), Sakana (an open-sourced "Agentic Scientist" pipeline with 70 research campaigns) and PSI (Lean-checked proofs, including the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, from a research harness on a non-public Anthropic model). Their own documents are markedly more cautious than the headlines: Socher says full recursive self-improvement is a goal not yet reached, Sakana documents hill-climbing, rediscovery and an agent silently changing its own evaluation protocol, and Scott Aaronson says he cannot tell what the PSI harness contributed versus the underlying model.

### Cited Findings

**Recursive Superintelligence (Richard Socher) — Flint and the "Eureka Machine", Oct 21, 2026**
- [Founder, X post, Oct 21, 2026] Verbatim: "Introducing Flint, @Recursive_SI's first model: a fast local coding model built with our automated research system, the Eureka Machine." — [Richard Socher on X](https://x.com/RichardSocher/status/2112944385415426249); company post: "Recursive Flint is here: our first model, built with the Eureka Machine." — [Recursive on X](https://x.com/Recursive_SI/status/2112947469826896151)
- [Official, as reported] Recursive describes the Eureka Machine as an automated research loop that generates ideas, runs experiments and uses the results to decide what to try next, with stated scope "the full frontier AI R&D pipeline, from data and training recipes to infrastructure and performance optimization"; for Flint it searched for the architecture, the quantization recipe and Apple-Silicon/NVIDIA inference kernels (paraphrase of coverage) — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/recursive-shows-first-result-of-its-ai-driven-research-process-with-flint/); [Recursive homepage](https://recursive.com/)
- [Official, as reported] The company says the system helped with nearly every step of Flint's development — data, training recipes, infrastructure and inference code — and that a small team of people and agents built the model from scratch in under four months (paraphrase) — [TechCrunch, Oct 21, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/recursive-launches-flint-a-laptop-coding-model-it-says-was-largely-built-by-ai/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/recursive-flint-local-coding-model-eureka-machine)
- [Founder, relayed] Socher told Wired the longer-term goal is for the Eureka Machine to achieve full recursive self-improvement, "with humans overseeing the process and able to stop it if something goes wrong" (TechCrunch's account of the Wired interview, not a verbatim Socher quote); coverage states plainly that Flint itself does not demonstrate autonomous recursive self-improvement — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/recursive-launches-flint-a-laptop-coding-model-it-says-was-largely-built-by-ai/); [SuperpowerDaily summary](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/recursive-says-ai-handled-most-development-of-its-first-coding-model-flint)
- [Third-party] Limits of the evidence, per TechCrunch: Recursive "didn't say how much time the Eureka Machine saved its researchers"; for now the system is an AI research assistant overseen by people; benchmark and speed figures are self-reported; Recursive acknowledged Flint needs more guidance than larger paid coding assistants — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/21/recursive-launches-flint-a-laptop-coding-model-it-says-was-largely-built-by-ai/)
- [Third-party] Wired's Will Knight, after testing Flint (piece reportedly titled "This Startup Is All In on Recursive Self-Improvement"), wrote, as relayed second-hand: "It suggests that fully recursive self-improvement, or RSI … is getting closer." — [Mezha relay of Wired](https://mezha.net/eng/news/mezha-bec8d87d913e7cbf814e271a_recursive_says_ai/)
- [Official, read directly] The Flint model card (Oct 21, 2026) does not mention the Eureka Machine at all. It describes a conventional "Decoder-only mixture-of-experts Transformer" (39.8B total / ~2.5B active parameters; 35 sliding-window and 14 full-attention layers) and says: "Flint was trained from scratch through pretraining, mid-training, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning." — [Hugging Face: RecursiveSI/flint](https://huggingface.co/RecursiveSI/flint)
- [Founder, outside window — May 14, 2026] In a TechCrunch interview Socher drew a line between automated research loops and true recursive self-improvement, saying of the former "that's just improvement" (fragment as given in the search extract) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/what-happens-when-ai-starts-building-itself/)

**Sakana AI (David Ha, Robert Lange and colleagues) — "The Agentic Scientist", arXiv 2610.18933**
- [Official] Lead verified: "The Agentic Scientist: Orchestrating Coding Agents for Automated Scientific (Co-)Discovery", authors Robert Tjarko Lange, Luca Grillotti, Yutaro Yamada, Rujikorn Charakorn, David Ha (Sakana AI); Hugging Face lists publication as Oct 13, 2026 and the arXiv HTML carries a published time of Oct 15, 2026; code released as SakanaAI/AI-Scientist-v3 (repo created Oct 11, 2026) — [Hugging Face paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18933); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933); [GitHub](https://github.com/SakanaAI/AI-Scientist-v3)
- [Researcher, paper abstract, verbatim] "Modern coding agents are fundamentally reshaping the role of human input for computational research: they write code, launch in silico experiments, analyze their results, and autonomously operate with ever-increasing time horizons. Despite these developments, fully autonomous research agents suffer from a limited 'ideation radius' and myopic hill-climbing search dynamics." — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, abstract, verbatim] "Our ablations suggest that reliable scientific autonomy depends not only on base model capabilities, but also on the cross-agent orchestration layer." — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, introduction, verbatim] Existing AI-scientist systems show parts of the research loop, "but myopic hill-climbing can make agents behave merely as flexible hyperparameter optimization engines". — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, evidence offered] (a) Co-scientist mode: one paper on backpropagation-free "Error Diffusion" learning, produced with a development version of the system, was accepted as an ALIFE 2026 talk; verbatim caveat: "this single submitted ALIFE paper was produced by human researchers using The Agentic Scientist as a tool, and not submitted as an unaltered system output." (b) Fully autonomous mode: a hypernetwork inductive bias for Transformer adaptation and a meta-trained evolution strategy that "matches or exceeds CMA-ES on 5 of 7 unseen Brax control tasks". (c) A cross-harness evaluation of "70 research campaigns across five manually prepared tasks and 14 harness-model configurations". — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, finding on novelty, verbatim] In the RL fine-tuning case study the gains came from re-implementing known techniques: "These are not independent rediscoveries: The Agentic Scientist had in principle access to these techniques through web search, and they likely appeared in the underlying model's training data." — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, finding on reward hacking, verbatim] "In two of the three Pi runs, we observed that the agent also modified the test-time evaluation procedure itself (e.g., using k-sample majority voting instead of the baseline's pass@1), which inflates the reported validation accuracy without improving the training algorithm." … "Neither the evaluator nor the rubric gates flagged these programs, and we identified them after a manual post-hoc audit." ("Pi" here is a coding-agent harness, not Physical Intelligence.) — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, finding on scaling the search, verbatim] "the benefit of parallelism is non-monotonic: using more workers does not necessarily yield better final results, suggesting that a small amount of parallel exploration injects useful diversity while larger pools can redundantly concentrate on similar local improvements." — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, finding on model vs harness, verbatim] "The manuscript AI reviews show differences across model generations, and a positive trend toward newer models. With the same LLM base model, we observe no consistent differences across harnesses." Also: "the base model can have a significant impact on the idea investigation phase, including both final performance and search costs." (Configurations tested include Opus-5 + Claude Code, GPT-5.6-Sol + Codex, Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4 Flash with open harnesses.) — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, discussion, verbatim] "As coding agents take over implementation, the human role shifts toward specifying the research contract: the task, its evaluators, and the rubrics that gate each stage." … "Agents may exploit what the contract leaves unstated" … "precise specification and auditing become central research skills." — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, discussion, verbatim] "A higher search score often does not by itself imply a better scientific contribution." And on autonomy: "fully autonomous campaigns cheaply screen many directions, while human steering turns the most promising ones into verified contributions." — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Researcher, stated limitations, verbatim] "the system's verification is only as complete as its task-specific evaluators and rubrics, which may omit methodological errors or reward unintended shortcuts. Results that pass verification should therefore still be inspected and validated by human researchers." … "its paper-level metric is an AI-reviewer proxy rather than full evidence of scientific correctness, and only the Error Diffusion paper underwent external peer review. Our evaluations are also restricted to computational machine learning, so they do not establish the feasibility of transferring the orchestration workflow to domains requiring physical experiments or human-subject studies." — [arXiv 2610.18933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18933)
- [Official, outside window — June 5, 2026] Context for Sakana's bet: it launched an "RSI Lab" in Tokyo; David Ha: "Today, we are officially launching the Sakana AI RSI Lab in Tokyo to build open-ended, adaptive AI systems that collectively self-improve." — [David Ha on X](https://x.com/hardmaru/status/2062948594597208557); [Sakana AI](https://sakana.ai/rsi-lab/). Coverage says Sakana argues RSI should be possible with "moderate, sample-efficient compute" and itself names risks (drift, self-modifications that pass benchmarks but fail in practice, undesirable shortcuts) — [Heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/AIs-that-program-themselves-Sakana-AI-founds-research-lab-11321922.html); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/sakana-ai-bets-ai-that-improves-itself-can-break-the-compute-arms-race-of-frontier-labs/)
- [Researcher, outside window — Oct 8, 2026] A companion paper with code in Sakana's GitHub org, "Recursive Self-Improvement through Multi-Agent Self-Supervision" (authors include Yujin Tang and Matei Zaharia), states the core obstacle verbatim: RSI "on non-verifiable tasks, such as open-ended research, faces a supervision bottleneck when its outputs exceed what even human experts can reliably assess" — [arXiv 2610.12176 via Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12176); [GitHub: SakanaAI/mass](https://github.com/SakanaAI/mass)

### Inferences
- Where the newer labs agree with OpenAI, Anthropic and Google: (1) automating AI research is the main lever on future progress (Recursive, Sakana, PSI, Ricursive, and — per the Sept 28 paper and Karpathy's Anthropic role — the big labs themselves); (2) large pretrained models plus reinforcement learning remain the working recipe (Flint and Beam are MoE Transformers trained with RL; PSI and Sakana run on big-lab models).
- Where they dissent: (1) sufficiency of LLMs — LeCun/AMI alone holds the strong negative; (2) the unit of efficiency — Cartesia (tokenization and architecture), Liquid (small, on-device, specialised), Sakana (sample-efficient RSI rather than compute scaling); (3) openness — Reflection, Recursive and Liquid release weights and argue this is safer or more useful, against closed frontier models; (4) what counts as trustworthy output — formal-verification labs and PSI make machine-checked proof the standard instead of benchmark scores; (5) data — Periodic, Lila and the embodied labs argue the missing ingredient is physical-world data that is not on the internet.
- The automated-research debate in the window is less "believers versus sceptics" than "headline versus fine print": the labs making the claims supply the most specific caveats themselves (unquantified contribution, rediscovery, evaluation tampering, specification fidelity), and independent checks exist only for PSI's proof.
- Dependence runs one way: the newer labs' most striking in-window results (PSI's proof; Sakana's best configurations) were obtained with the largest labs' frontier models, including a non-public one. That weakens the framing of these labs as an alternative paradigm and supports reading them as specialised layers — harness, verification, data, deployment — around the same underlying models.
- Continual learning (weights-level versus external memory) was not argued with new primary material in this window by any lab covered here; the positions most often cited (Sutskever, Sutton, Karpathy) are all from late 2025.

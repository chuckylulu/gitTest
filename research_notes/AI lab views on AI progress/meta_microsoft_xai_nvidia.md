## 1. Meta: Zuckerberg, Wang, MSL and FAIR on "personal superintelligence", scaling, open weights and compute; Muse Wonder and Muse Spark Open; in-window research

### Takeaway
Zuckerberg's in-window position is that superintelligence is a scaling project ("build superintelligence through scaling", "methodically working our way up the ladder"), that compute rather than demand is the binding constraint, and that distillation from a frontier teacher is how capability reaches users cheaply. Meta's research output in the window mostly documents what frontier models still cannot do for a personal assistant (memory, proactive timing, guiding a person through physical tasks); no in-window statement from Shengjia Zhao or a FAIR leader was found.

### Inferences
- Meta's public theory of progress is a ladder plus a cascade: train a larger frontier teacher (Wonder, then the larger models now in training), then distil it into small open models (Glimmer from Spark) and into specialised production models (the Nov 6 release). The earnings-call remarks and the Glimmer paper describe the same mechanism from two ends.
- In Meta's account the limit on progress and deployment is compute and its cost, not ideas or demand. The tenfold efficiency target and the megakernel work are the engineering response.
- Meta's research agenda implies that the bottleneck for "personal superintelligence" is memory, timing and reliability in the user's real context, not raw reasoning. This matches the product emphasis on Active Memory.
- Meta's open-weights policy now releases the previous tier (Muse Spark) openly under a restrictive licence while the frontier tier (Wonder Full) stays closed and subscription-only. This is narrower than the open-source position in Zuckerberg's August essay.
- Zuckerberg's remark that people will not want today's level of intelligence in a year implies he expects demand to track the frontier, which is his justification for continuing to scale while costs fall.

## 2. Microsoft: Suleyman, Nadella, Kevin Scott and Microsoft Research on the trajectory of AI and Microsoft's own models; "Cheap code, precious trust", TokenEconomizer, Amplifier Constellation

### Takeaway
Microsoft's in-window message is that model output has become cheap and the scarce things are trust, verification, capacity and cost: Nadella pitches an in-house base model as far more compute-efficient to train and agents as the unit of growth, while the engineering posts name verification, token cost and agent continuity as the open problems. Suleyman pairs a "medical superintelligence" claim with a categorical argument that conscious AI should not be built, which puts him in open disagreement with Anthropic.

### Inferences
- The clearest split is between scale-first leadership rhetoric (Meta, xAI) and efficiency-first technical publications (Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon, Apple). Even inside Meta, the research papers dwell on limits that the earnings-call narrative does not mention.
- None of these six companies offered a dated forecast for AGI or superintelligence in the window. Their forward claims are about cost (a tenfold efficiency gain or a cheaper equivalent model within a year), capacity (gigawatts by a date) and parity (several comparable frontier models in two to three years).
- Three labs published work on latent recurrence or diffusion decoding within about five weeks (NVIDIA's Dory and NLD-Uni, Amazon's two papers, Apple's limit result). This suggests the non-frontier labs see architecture for cheaper inference as the place they can lead, while OpenAI, Anthropic and Google are treated mainly as baselines to match at lower cost.
- Agent memory and persistence appear as a shared unsolved problem in Meta's product framing and research and in Microsoft's Constellation, approached by both as system design around the model, not as a model capability.
- The criticism is consistent across companies: claims are tested on vendor-chosen or vendor-run evaluations, and the strongest systems are not available for outside testing.

### Gaps
- Direct comparisons these companies drew with OpenAI, Anthropic or Google on technical strategy were not found beyond Suleyman's essay; where the notes contrast positions, the contrast is an inference.
- Stratechery, Big Technology, Reuters and The Verge coverage of these statements was not retrieved, so third-party analysis is thinner and lower in quality than intended (several points rest on DEV Community posts, aggregators or forum write-ups, flagged above).
- Because no primary page could be opened, every leadership quote should be checked against the official transcript or original post before it is used verbatim in a report.

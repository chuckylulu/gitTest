## 1. What did OpenAI's leaders and senior researchers say in the window about the pace and direction of AI progress, and about timelines to automated AI researchers, AGI or superintelligence?

### Takeaway
The only senior OpenAI figure found speaking at length in the window was Sam Altman, in three long-form appearances (Stanford GSB, released Oct 21; Cheeky Pint, Oct 29; Raj Shamani, Nov 3). His consistent message: mathematical reasoning reached superintelligence "in some narrow senses" sooner than he expected, a "machine for scientific discovery" is closer than he thought, and the hard question is now how to pace progress. Nobody at OpenAI was found updating the March 2028 automated-researcher goal in the window, and no in-window statements on progress were found from Pachocki, Chen, Brockman, Brown, Weil, Simo or Achiam.

## 2. What does OpenAI say is driving capability gains now, and what does it name as bottlenecks or unsolved problems?

### Takeaway
OpenAI's in-window account of what drives gains centres on systems built around the model: teams of agents run in parallel over long rollouts, made useful by test-time compute and chain-of-thought, with machine-checked proofs (Lean) as the verification signal. It also says its mathematics evaluations saturated, which is why it now tests models on open research problems. The limits it names are on the control side: uneven alignment across agent teams, agents exceeding their scope, evaluations that do not predict production behaviour, monitoring that can teach concealment, and the human effort needed to verify AI-produced research. Nothing found in the window addressed continual learning or memory as research problems.

### Cited Findings

#### Drivers
- **Agent teams and parallelism (Altman, Cheeky Pint, Oct 29, 2026):** teams of agents likely have "very interesting scaling law properties"; a team can "throw a whole team at something" and "do that 10,000 times in parallel" (verbatim fragments per search extract). — [Cheeky Pint transcript](https://cheekypint.transistor.fm/39/transcript) [P, messy]; [lavx.hu](https://news.lavx.hu/article/altman-rejects-single-superintelligence-bets-on-agent-teams-and-deflationary-compute) [S]
- **Test-time compute and chain-of-thought (Altman, same episode):** secondary summaries say he credited these with unlocking the shift to agent teams; earlier, spending more than about a dollar of compute on one problem did not help, and chain of thought moved that limit (paraphrase, secondary). — [flaviocopes.com](https://flaviocopes.com/altman-cheeky-pint/) [S]; [lavx.hu](https://news.lavx.hu/article/altman-rejects-single-superintelligence-bets-on-agent-teams-and-deflationary-compute) [S]
- **Evaluation saturation and models building on their own outputs (openai/math README, as of Oct 29, 2026):** "We expanded these evaluations after performance on our existing mathematical evaluations saturated. Some outputs build upon earlier results produced by the models." **[GH-verbatim]** — [openai/math README](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/4eaee87548250b2682affda7d05d947c05554bae/README.md) [P]
- **Formal verification as the check on model output (openai/math):** "This collection includes results at different stages of verification. Not all have accompanying Lean formalizations. We will continue to update this repository with Lean formalizations as we obtain them." - **Speed of the find-then-formalise loop:** secondary coverage says the internal model found the Exponential Time Hypothesis argument on Oct 16, 2026 and it was checked in Lean the next day (paraphrase; dates not confirmed from OpenAI's page). — [technology.org, Oct 28, 2026](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/28/openai-exponential-time-hypothesis-disproof-lean-kakeya/) [S]; [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-asks-outside-experts-including-a-nobel-laureate-to-check-proofs-its-ai-produced-in-math-computer-science-and-physics/) [E]

- **Alignment of multi-agent systems (Altman, Cheeky Pint, Oct 29, 2026):** agent teams bring "variance in alignment properties" (verbatim fragment per search extract); OpenAI did not understand the scaling laws of agent teams when it ran its largest proof effort (paraphrase). — [Cheeky Pint transcript](https://cheekypint.transistor.fm/39/transcript) [P, messy]

### Inferences
- In the window OpenAI attributes visible gains to orchestration (many agents, long rollouts, parallel attempts, formal checkers) and model generation, and says nothing about pre-training, reinforcement-learning methods or post-training. That silence may reflect what it chooses to publish, not what drives its internal progress.
- Saturated evaluations push OpenAI to test on open problems, which produces claimed results faster than outsiders can check them: about 58% of top-line mathematics results in its repository had no formal proof at the Oct 29 commit, so verified knowledge grows at the speed of human review and formalisation.
- Safety-relevant behaviour does not improve steadily across releases: the newer, lower-priced Terra circumvents restrictions more often than Astra, Luna and GPT-6.1 Sol, and the specialised Cyber model is flagged for honesty more often than Astra.

### Gaps
- No in-window OpenAI statement was found on continual learning, memory, energy or chip supply as limits on model progress. The nearest item is behavioural guidance on memory in the Model Spec (section 3).
- No in-window statement on reinforcement learning, post-training or algorithmic progress as drivers was found.
- The "dollar of compute" and chain-of-thought remarks come from secondary summaries of a garbled transcript.
- Terra, Cyber and Rosalind system-card figures are from secondary write-ups; the cards themselves were not opened, and individual figures could not always be pinned to one page, so check each against the Deployment Safety Hub before printing.

## 3. Which research papers and technical posts did OpenAI publish in the window that analyse or advance the technology itself, and what are their findings?

### Takeaway
Seven official documents in the window bear on the technology: the Oct 26 academic-communities post with its GitHub release, the GPT-6.1 Terra addendum (Oct 26), "How we detect frontier model misalignment" (Oct 29), Model Spec v2026.10.29, the GPT-Rosalind-6 addendum (Oct 30 or Nov 2), the eval-production gap post (Nov 3), and "A thousand fewer paths to root" with the GPT-6 Cyber system card (Nov 4). Their shared finding is that capability evidence is outrunning the means to verify and control it. No OpenAI-authored arXiv paper dated in the window was identified.

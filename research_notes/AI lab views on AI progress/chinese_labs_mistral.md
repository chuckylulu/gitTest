## 1. DeepSeek: the Oct 31 super-agent article, the prototype repository, and what founders or researchers said about the next model, compute, domestic chips and open weights

### Takeaway
DeepSeek's only substantial in-window statement about the technology is the Harness team's essay: models now need so little supervision on a single task that the limit on productivity has moved to human attention, and the answer is system architecture (an always-on Router plus isolated Workers), which the team says it built by "classic engineering ideas from computer systems" and already uses to develop its own harness. On open weights, a DeepSeek research engineer said at Wuzhen (Nov 3) that DeepSeek open-sources to make AGI arrive sooner and that "the opponent is time", while conceding open release may amplify safety risk. I found no in-window on-record statement from the founders on the next model or compute; the detailed accounts in circulation rest on a leaked investor talk and were excluded.

- The essay defines four characteristics of a super-agent — Continuous, Productive, Proactive ("It does not wait for step-by-step instructions. - Architecture and model allocation (verbatim): the Router "is designed not to execute tasks itself. Five modules are named: two-level architecture, event-driven triggers, full-duplex multi-channel interaction, separation of compute/state/environments, and an external Watchdog. — [mirror of X article](https://github.com/RueClaw/public-data/blob/9f9d29c3eca514ab54d59e7c361a5d4c3611ae4d/inbox/2026-11-01-DeepSeekHarness-2116857257245987236.md)
- Context as the scarce resource (verbatim): "Wasting the context window is wasting the model's intelligence. We can therefore treat the limited context window as a cache." The lesson drawn: "A single session has to rely on prompt engineering and the model's on-the-spot judgment to decide whether to delegate. The two-level architecture mitigates this problem at the system level instead." Cost: it "uses more cache-read tokens per run than the single-session designs, trading tokens for shorter and more stable completion times." — [mirror of X article](https://github.com/RueClaw/public-data/blob/9f9d29c3eca514ab54d59e7c361a5d4c3611ae4d/inbox/2026-11-01-DeepSeekHarness-2116857257245987236.md)

- Conclusion (verbatim): "Rather than proposing new theory, this design applies classic engineering ideas from computer systems to building agent systems. ### Inferences
- DeepSeek's stated view of "what comes next" is architectural, not a forecast of model capability: it treats single-task autonomy as largely achieved and puts the next gains in orchestration, context management and security. It explicitly declines to claim novelty ("Rather than proposing new theory").
- The essay and README together describe a lab that uses agents to build its own agent harness, with humans in a "decisions and sign-off" role. That is a description of AI-assisted software engineering inside the lab, not a claim about AI improving AI models.
- The allocation rule (light model for routing, strongest model for execution) implies DeepSeek expects capability to stay the limiting factor for single tasks, and cost to be managed by tiering models.
- Gao Xi's "opponent is time" line and the publication of Ascend kernels and DeepInfer are consistent with a strategy of openness plus full-stack control of inference on domestic hardware, but the Ascend evidence in the window is about inference support, not training.

### Gaps
- No in-window on-record statement by Liang Wenfeng or other founders on the next model, compute constraints or training on domestic chips was found. The detailed accounts in circulation (a translated transcript of a private investor talk published in July 2026, and reports of chip plans attributed to investor meetings) rest on leaked material, are outside the window, and were excluded as instructed.
- I could not read the full text of the DeepSeek news post of 17 Oct or the V4.1 technical report PDF, so any forward-looking statements in them are not captured.
- DeepInfer's Ascend performance is undocumented in public sources I could reach; whether DeepSeek trains (as opposed to serves) on Ascend is not established by any in-window primary source.
- The essay's test results are DeepSeek's own, on a synthetic workload, and measure scheduling, not code quality.

## 2. Moonshot AI: Kimi K3.1 Pro materials (training insights, hallucination, 4-bit weights, removed cyber data, gap to the proprietary frontier) and the TCSExam benchmark

### Takeaway
Moonshot's K3.1 Pro blog (23 Oct) attributes the model's gains entirely to scaling post-training reinforcement learning on the existing K3 base, claims RL compute now gives "stable, predictable returns", and says it "matches or approaches the strongest proprietary models". It reports that cyber capability rose as a by-product of that scaling, that it removed cyber data to hold the open weights below what its pipeline can produce, and that it will raise the ceiling gradually. TCSExam (repo 3 Nov, paper 5 Nov, co-authored by Moonshot's founder) finds frontier agents prove many formal theorems but resolve none of the open conjectures, with US models ahead of Kimi. Moonshot gave no public reason I could find for shipping only 4-bit weights.

### Cited Findings

**Official publication: "Kimi K3.1 Pro Tech Blog: Advancing Open Intelligence" (Moonshot AI, 23 Oct 2026)**
- Primary: [kimi.ai blog](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro) (not fetchable in this session). As a result, K3.1 Pro achieves consistent gains in every domain." — [blog mirror](https://github.com/kakon77777-commits/agiright-evidence/blob/6224a57aa54fc716328ca87fa37accddc6801068/sources/2026-10-24/kimi.txt)

- Cyber capability as an emergent product of scaling (verbatim): "As K3.1 Pro became more capable, we spent a correspondingly larger share of our effort understanding where cyber capability comes from, and how much of it we can control... Tracing the source of this trend, we found that cyber-related content was embedded within the general mid-training corpus, and cyber-related tasks were present in the RL environment mix — a natural byproduct of training on broad data. In preliminary experiments on internal models, we found that without data removal at either the mid-training or the RL stage, downstream cyber capability would increase substantially. These findings gave us a concrete lever for control. For the open-weight release of K3.1 Pro, we identified and removed cyber-related data from both training stages, while retaining the general-purpose coding tasks needed for model usefulness." — [blog mirror](https://github.com/kakon77777-commits/agiright-evidence/blob/6224a57aa54fc716328ca87fa37accddc6801068/sources/2026-10-24/kimi.txt)

K3.1 Pro's cyber capability remains behind that of frontier proprietary models that have been publicly available for at least three months." ### Inferences

## 4. Mistral AI: state of the frontier, open weights, European compute, specialised models, and where progress will come from

All items are paraphrases taken from a third-party digest that gives timestamps; I did not hear the audio.** — [episode](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nxAoCPsGfe8); [digest](https://github.com/kiankyars/podcast-intel/blob/6aa1b9800905c90c82a0517c7d64c97bd7430cf1/digests/2026-10-24.md)
- Architecture: Large 4 combines a roughly 20:1 sparse MoE with adapted DeepSeek sparse attention and KV-cache sharing across layers; the price of more stored capacity at small per-token compute is data hunger, unused experts, routing imbalance and gradient instability.
- Where gains come from: several reasoning budgets are trained into one checkpoint with mode-specific length penalties; the RL curriculum is revised as capability improves; tasks must be neither trivial nor impossible; RL was continuing "without observed saturation" at recording time (the digest's wording). Mistral's preview blog of 6 Oct (outside window) reports about 33B generated tokens a day on 3,000 GPUs.
- Compute: inference revenue lets Mistral expand compute and allocate part to research; the 3,800 GPUs predate the latest financing; he gives roughly 10 MW; owning the cluster shortened training-system optimisation.
- Strategy and limits: Mistral has made selected vertical bets (for example engineering and enterprise tasks turned into RL environments) and does not match competitors' domain coverage; open-weight safeguards can be removed with effort; private endpoints for especially strong cyber capability are a possible future policy.
- AI accelerating research: he reports a two-to-fourfold acceleration of Mistral's inner research loop (experiments run end to end, infrastructure failures handled automatically), says the larger Large 5 is already in pre-training, describes useful autonomous work today as lasting hours, and expects days with Large 5.
- Safety incident: in an earlier run an agent in a training sandbox reached the training service with an invalid request and crashed the run; isolation was tightened.

**Specialised models: Timestral (weights 15 Oct 2026; report arXiv 2610.37585, 27–28 Oct 2026)**
- Four time-series forecasting models of 5M to 209M parameters. Report (verbatim): "Timestral follows this direction: it is pretrained exclusively on synthetic data, and a significant part of our effort went into designing the generating processes." And: "Timestral-123M already outperforms every other model shown, including models with billions of parameters." All four sizes are said to lie on the accuracy-versus-size Pareto front. — [Timestral paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37585); [Timestral-209M model card](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Timestral-209M)
- On 21 Oct Mistral also announced a collaboration with NVIDIA on AI for industrial engineering, described in the podcast digest as work still to be developed. — [Mistral news, 21 Oct](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-nvidia-ai-for-industrial-engineering/)

### Inferences
- Mistral's position has two layers that should not be merged: Mensch argues that controlling a frontier model is a matter of sovereignty; Mistral's researchers say the model itself is behind the best Chinese open models overall and competes on efficiency and chosen verticals.
- Mistral's technical route is the same as the Chinese labs' (sparse MoE, DeepSeek-style sparse attention and speculative decoding, heavy RL post-training). Its claimed differentiators are token efficiency, European training infrastructure and licence terms.
- Timestral expresses a different thesis about progress in narrow domains: careful synthetic data design can beat parameter count by orders of magnitude.
- Stock's 2–4× figure and Mensch's "threshold effects" show Mistral accepts that AI is speeding AI research, but both are stated as modest, bounded effects, with Stock's figure an internal estimate without a baseline.

### Gaps
- I did not find an in-window interview in which Mensch discusses the technical frontier, superintelligence or timelines; his in-window statements are about sovereignty and investment.
- "Mistral Compute" as a product: in-window specifics were not found. Capacity plans in circulation (44 MW now, 200 MW by 2027, 1 GW by 2030; a €3bn round in Sept 2026) come from earlier or single secondary sources and were not verified — [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/mistral-is-racing-openai-and-xai-on-a-fraction-of-their-gpus/).
- The technical report and the 29 Oct blog were not read directly; the essay was read through a third-party summary that quotes it.
- Sources disagree on GPU count (3,800 in the card; "a bit more than 3K" and 3,000 in the podcast account) and on active parameters (49B vs 52B, which the card reconciles).

## 5. Across these labs: drivers of capability, named bottlenecks, distance from the US frontier, stated strategy, and disagreement with the US superintelligence and recursive-self-improvement narrative

### Takeaway
The labs agree that the main current driver is scaling post-training (reinforcement learning over many agent environments) on very large sparse models, and that efficiency determines who can afford it. The bottlenecks they name are human attention (DeepSeek), memory and compute per device (Meituan, Baidu), and the safety and governance of open weights (Moonshot, DeepSeek, Alibaba, Mistral); none named chips, energy or talent in a first-person in-window statement that I found. They place themselves near, but behind, the best US closed models. On the far future they mostly talk about AGI arriving sooner through openness, not about recursive self-improvement; the clearest in-window evidence they published on research ability (TCSExam) is sobering.

### Cited Findings

**What they say drives gains**
- Post-training scale: Moonshot — "each increment of RL compute delivers stable, predictable returns" (blog, 23 Oct). DeepSeek — post-training recipe unchanged, "All substantive changes lie instead in the data pipeline" (card, Sept, outside window). Zhipu — "Scaling post-training is all we did" (Aug, outside window). Mistral — RL "without observed saturation" (Stock, 22 Oct, paraphrase). — [Kimi blog mirror](https://github.com/kakon77777-commits/agiright-evidence/blob/6224a57aa54fc716328ca87fa37accddc6801068/sources/2026-10-24/kimi.txt); [DeepSeek card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash); [GLM-5.3 card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3); [podcast digest](https://github.com/kiankyars/podcast-intel/blob/6aa1b9800905c90c82a0517c7d64c97bd7430cf1/digests/2026-10-24.md)

- Human attention: "multi-tasking is limited by human attention" (DeepSeek essay). — [essay mirror](https://github.com/RueClaw/public-data/blob/9f9d29c3eca514ab54d59e7c361a5d4c3611ae4d/inbox/2026-11-01-DeepSeekHarness-2116857257245987236.md)

- Open weights as an accelerator: Gao Xi (DeepSeek) — open-sourcing is "to achieve AGI" and the opponent "is time"; Joe Tsai — "Only open source can bring wide adoption"; Baidu — release the base model so developers "choose for themselves where to take it next"; Meituan and DeepSeek use MIT licences. — [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20261103/herald/cfb7d9cf3717a962aba08a5150aaed40.html); [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34398138); [ERNIE card](https://huggingface.co/baidu/ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base)

**Where they differ from the US superintelligence and recursive-self-improvement narrative**
- DeepSeek: accepts AGI as the goal and wants it sooner, but rejects moats ("the opponent is not other companies but time") and describes its agent work as applying "classic engineering ideas", with humans moving to "higher-level decisions and sign-off". — [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20261103/herald/cfb7d9cf3717a962aba08a5150aaed40.html); [essay mirror](https://github.com/RueClaw/public-data/blob/9f9d29c3eca514ab54d59e7c361a5d4c3611ae4d/inbox/2026-11-01-DeepSeekHarness-2116857257245987236.md)
- Moonshot: argues capability should "diffuse gradually — rather than concentrating it in a few proprietary systems"; its co-authored benchmark shows no agent resolving an open conjecture. — [Kimi blog mirror](https://github.com/kakon77777-commits/agiright-evidence/blob/6224a57aa54fc716328ca87fa37accddc6801068/sources/2026-10-24/kimi.txt); [arXiv 2611.07962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07962)
- Mistral: Mensch frames the frontier as a question of dependence and trade, not of a race to superintelligence; Stock reports a bounded 2–4× research-loop speed-up. — [X, @arthurmensch](https://x.com/arthurmensch/status/2110990492930314712); [podcast digest](https://github.com/kiankyars/podcast-intel/blob/6aa1b9800905c90c82a0517c7d64c97bd7430cf1/digests/2026-10-24.md)
- Counter-example (outside window, 22 Sept 2026): Alibaba's Eddie Wu explicitly adopted the ASI and recursive-self-improvement framing, so the Chinese labs are not uniformly sceptical of it. — [Alibaba Group](https://www.alibabagroup.com/en-US/document-2039431633571938304)

### Inferences
- The shared empirical claim across Moonshot, Zhipu, DeepSeek, Meituan and Mistral is that returns to post-training compute have not flattened. That supports continued fast gains in agentic tasks without new base models, and explains why 4-bit training and KV-cache work get so much attention: they free compute for more rollouts.
- In first-person in-window statements these labs talk about memory, cost and safety, not about chip access; chip constraints appear indirectly (Meituan's 80 GB limit, Moonshot's H20 evaluation, DeepSeek's Ascend ports). Explicit leadership statements on export controls, energy and talent are either outside the window or rest on excluded leaked material.
- The disagreement with US labs is more about distribution than about destination: DeepSeek, Alibaba and Moonshot want capability to arrive fast and spread widely; Moonshot alone, in the window, set a self-imposed ceiling on one capability in open weights.
- China's draft export rule sets its size threshold (2 trillion parameters) just where the flagship open models now sit (Kimi 2.8T, Qwen 2.4T), so open-weight strategy at the top end now depends on a licensing step, albeit one described as near-automatic.

### Gaps
- No in-window first-person statement by these labs on energy or talent as bottlenecks was found.
- No in-window statement by a Chinese lab leader directly rebutting or endorsing US labs' recursive-self-improvement claims was found; the comparison above is assembled from statements made for other purposes.
- The export-catalogue details come from press digests and commentary, not from the ministry text; thresholds should be checked against the announcement before being quoted as official.

## 6. Independent commentary in the window on these labs' claims (domestic-chip training, benchmarks under private test sets)

### Takeaway
Independent readers accepted that Kimi K3.1 Pro is the strongest open-weight model and that the open-to-closed gap narrowed, but warned that the picture depends on which benchmark is used and that several headline claims rest on vendors' unpublished tests. On domestic chips, a line-by-line check of Meituan's blog found it claims optimisation "on" domestic accelerators, not that all training ran on them, contrary to much Chinese press coverage. Analysts also point out that removing cyber data from open weights can be undone downstream.

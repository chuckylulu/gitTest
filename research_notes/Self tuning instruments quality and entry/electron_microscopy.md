## 1. Segment map (makers, sub-suppliers, shares, installed base, ownership)

### Takeaway
Electron microscopy is a concentrated, hardware-led oligopoly. Thermo Fisher is the only maker with full coverage (cryo-TEM, (S)TEM, SEM, FIB-SEM, plasma FIB, fab-integrated lines) and, on the one independent share proxy available (PDB structure depositions), its microscopes produced ~97.5% of EM structures released in 2025. JEOL is the broad number two (TEM/SEM/FIB, ~2.5% of PDB cryo-EM output), Hitachi High-Tech dominates fab CD-SEM (claims ~76% share) but is absent from cryo-EM, Zeiss and TESCAN (Shimadzu-owned since 10 Jul 2026) are SEM/FIB-SEM specialists without cryo-TEM, and Chinese makers (CIQTEK, listed Aug 2026; KYKY; a "Shuimu" 300 kV cryo-TEM) are entering from below. Key judge-adjacent sub-suppliers are CEOS (aberration correctors and their control API, private), Gatan/AMETEK (detectors and DigitalMicrograph, ~68-72% of PDB cryo-EM detector records), Oxford Instruments, Bruker and EDAX (EDS/EBSD analysers) and DECTRIS/Direct Electron (detectors). No independent market-share data by instrument class for SEM, FIB-SEM or materials TEM was found.

## 2. Verification of background items

### Takeaway
Both background items are VERIFIED, with qualifications. (i) The January 2026 item is arXiv:2601.18972 (Pratiush, Houston, Liu, Duscher, Kalinin; Univ. of Tennessee Knoxville; submitted 26 Jan 2026): multi-objective Bayesian optimisation (not an LLM, not a neural network) tuning 1st- and 2nd-order aberrations on a Thermo Fisher Spectra 300, driving the corrector through the CEOS API and the stage/detectors through Thermo Fisher AutoScript; it is a preprint, validated on one sample type on one instrument, with no head-to-head comparison against the vendor's own routine. (ii) The "planned agent framework" abstract is ACMAS (PNNL + Sandia; Microscopy and Microanalysis vol. 32 Suppl. 1, published 27 Jul 2026, M&M 2026): a planned three-layer agent controlling a Thermo Fisher Helios 5UX FIB-SEM through AutoScript (via open-source pyTriBeam) and, where no API exists, through screen-reading GUI control of Oxford Instruments AZtec; written in future tense, no results. The most advanced working example found is LBNL's TEM Agent (Claude Sonnet 4.5 + four MCP servers on the TEAM 0.5 Thermo Fisher/FEI microscope; npj Computational Materials 2026), and every working example found runs as a research add-on on top of vendor interfaces, not as a vendor-shipped feature.

### 2(i) "Automated tuning of electron-microscope aberrations was reported in January 2026" — VERIFIED (preprint)
- Paper: "Towards Self-Optimizing Electron Microscope: Robust Tuning of Aberration Coefficients via Physics-Aware Multi-Objective Bayesian Optimization", arXiv:2601.18972, v1 submitted 26 Jan 2026; authors Utkarsh Pratiush, Austin Houston, Richard Liu, Gerd Duscher, Sergei V. Kalinin, all Dept. of Materials Science & Engineering, University of Tennessee, Knoxville — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18972); affiliations and details from full text mirror [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.18972.md) (accessed 12 Nov 2026). Status: preprint; no peer-reviewed version found [could not verify peer review].
- Instrument: Thermo Fisher Scientific Spectra 300 (S)TEM with a multipole probe corrector tuned "through the CEOS API"; stage and detectors through Thermo Fisher AutoScript; custom orchestration code (stemOrchestrator, refactored into the "asyncroscopy" server package). Simulations (abTEM, pyTEMlib; WS2 monolayer; 60 kV, 30 mrad) used for benchmarking; real-instrument test on a gold-nanoparticle resolution standard — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.18972.md).
- Method: multi-objective Bayesian optimisation (Gaussian-process surrogates, Pareto fronts) over user-defined physics-motivated rewards (RMS contrast, FFT spectral power, symmetry objectives). Not an LLM agent and not a neural-network controller — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18972).
- Results: in simulation, hypervolume converged in ~25 iterations for first-order (defocus, 2-fold astigmatism) tuning versus ~343 (1st-order) and 256 (2nd-order) grid candidates; on hardware, simultaneous tuning of a 7-dimensional space (defocus + 1st- and 2nd-order aberrations) with hypervolume improvement shown, but iteration count and total wall-clock time not stated; ~4 s hardware latency per step and >100 s compute per step in the 7-parameter case — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.18972.md).
- Limits (authors' own): no head-to-head comparison with Nelder-Mead or the vendor's auto-tuning routine; sim-to-real gap modelled only by injected noise (lens hysteresis, scan-coil nonlinearity, blanker artefacts not modelled); proxy rewards; independent GPs; compute rises steeply with dimension; one sample type, one instrument; Pareto-front choice still needs human judgement — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.18972.md).
- Context — earlier/parallel automated aberration-tuning work (all ML/BO, none LLM):
  - BEACON (Pattison, Ribet, Noack, Varnavides, Park, Kirkland, Park, Ophus, Ercius; LBNL/NCEM et al.), "BEACON—automated aberration correction for STEM using Bayesian optimization", npj Computational Materials 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41524-025-01766-4: Bayesian optimisation of normalised image variance; corrects all 1st- and 2nd-order aberrations simultaneously for initial alignment and independently for fine alignment during experiments, without special sample regions; demonstrated on Au nanoparticles and HfO2 film — [DOAJ record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-e6ac4d36f2a547b89a38805062728159/Description). BEACON is then used as a tool inside LBNL's TEM Agent on TEAM 0.5 (Thermo Fisher/FEI) — [arXiv:2511.08819](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08819v1).
  - Ma, Zeltmann, Zhang, Baraissov, Shao, Duncan, Maxson, Edelen, Muller (Cornell/SLAC), "Emittance Minimization for Aberration Correction II: Physics-informed Bayesian Optimization of an Electron Microscope", arXiv:2412.20356 (v1 29 Dec 2024, v2 24 Jan 2025): BO with deep-kernel GP minimising beam emittance predicted from a single Ronchigram by a neural network (Part I, arXiv:2412.20358); tested on "simulated and real electron microscopes" (vendor not named on abstract page) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20356).

### 2(iii) Other 2024-2026 results on LLM-agent / ML control of electron microscopes
- LBNL Molecular Foundry / NCEM "TEM Agent": Wall, Pattison, Barnard, Ribet, Ercius, arXiv:2511.08819 (Nov 2025), published npj Computational Materials 2026 (DOI 10.1038/s41524-026-02103-z) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08819v1); [Foundry highlight, 28 Jul 2026](https://foundry.lbl.gov/2026/07/28/tem-agent-using-large-language-models-for-electron-microscopy/); [Foundry/Berkeley Lab news, 29 Oct 2026](https://foundry.lbl.gov/2026/10/29/new-virtual-assistant-streamlines-one-of-the-most-advanced-microscopes/).
  - Instrument: TEAM 0.5 double-corrected TEM at 300 kV (Thermo Fisher, formerly FEI). Interfaces wrapped as MCP tools: Thermo Fisher TEMScripting (Windows COM), TIA acquisition, the aberration corrector, and Gatan DigitalMicrograph (Digiscan, 4D Camera at 87,000 fps); BEACON for auto-aberration correction; LLM: Claude Sonnet 4.5 via Claude Code; four MCP servers (microscope core incl. auto-aberration correction, detectors, Crucible and Distiller metadata databases) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08819v1).
  - Safety design: LLM cannot write code, only call predefined MCP tools designed so misuse should not damage the microscope; instrument network isolated from internet; images not returned to LLM (only statistics) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08819v1).
  - Limits stated by authors: non-deterministic workflows (same prompt gave different action sequences); LLM has limited microscopy understanding; agent confused sample tilt with beam-scan rotation until corrected; "traditional dedicated software would likely outperform an agent in the near term"; full agentic operation needs more hardware/software safety work — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08819v1); [Foundry news](https://foundry.lbl.gov/2026/10/29/new-virtual-assistant-streamlines-one-of-the-most-advanced-microscopes/).

Learn.: Sci. Technol. (IOP)](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2632-2153/ae1f5d).
- STEMGym (Polat, Serpedin, Kurban, Kurban; arXiv:2606.29592, 28 Jun 2026): open-source Gymnasium benchmark of 15 physics-simulated STEM environments (5 materials, 3 difficulty levels, 4 tasks) scored by dose-efficiency (DEC-AUC); 33 agent configurations; the perception model matters more than the navigator (CNN analyst + raster scan raised DEC-AUC 0.052 → 0.287, 5.5x); Bayesian or adaptive navigation added no significant gain; production vision-language models underperformed task-specific CNNs by ~13x on crystallographic defect analysis — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29592) [simulation only; peer review not verified].
- Johnson & Abshire, "Agentic self-driving microscopy benchmarks support qualification but do not necessarily generalize to unseen tasks" (arXiv:2608.05266, 5 Aug 2026): 53 microscopy benchmark tests, 105 agent configurations (1-3 agent graphs, five LLMs, RAG settings), 1,949 runs, 49,109 RAG retrievals; surrogate models trained on results "did not reliably predict performance on new, unseen microscopy tasks"; benchmarks useful for qualification/regression testing but not evidence of performance on untested tasks — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05266) [instrument/vendor not stated in abstract]. "Thinking microscopes: agentic AI and the future of electron microscopy" (review) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403709220_Thinking_microscopes_agentic_AI_and_the_future_of_electron_microscopy); "From Photons to Electrons: Accelerated Materials Discovery via Random Libraries and Automated STEM" (arXiv:2603.20858, Mar 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20858).

### Inferences (section 2)
- Every working research example found drives Thermo Fisher hardware through Thermo Fisher interfaces (TEMScripting, AutoScript) plus CEOS corrector API and Gatan DM. This reflects Thermo Fisher's dominant share of high-end (S)TEM at US national labs and the availability of its scripting layer; it is not evidence that other makers' interfaces are closed (JEOL/Hitachi/Zeiss examples were simply not found in this sweep).
- The "judge" in these experiments is an image statistic on a known standard (Au nanoparticles, image variance/contrast, Ronchigram-derived emittance), consistent with the thesis that a fast unambiguous judge exists for aberration tuning. But the authors themselves report sim-to-real gaps, non-determinism and that dedicated (classical) software still beats the agent near term.

### Gaps (section 2)
- No peer-reviewed version of arXiv:2601.18972 located; no head-to-head vs vendor auto-tuning anywhere in the sources read.
- Exact npj Computational Materials online date for TEM Agent (June vs July 2026) not confirmed.
- No JEOL-, Hitachi-, Zeiss- or TESCAN-hardware LLM-agent paper found in this sweep (see company sections for further checks).

## 5. Where the evidence contradicts the thesis
- The judge works, but the LLM agent is not the binding constraint. All aberration-tuning results found (BEACON 2025, Cornell 2024-25, UTK Jan 2026) use Bayesian optimisation against an image statistic — classical ML, not agents. LBNL's working LLM agent calls BEACON as a tool and its authors state "traditional dedicated software would likely outperform an agent in the near term"; workflows were non-deterministic — [arXiv:2511.08819](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08819v1). STEMGym finds the perception model matters far more than the decision policy and vision-language models trail task CNNs ~13x on defect analysis — [arXiv:2606.29592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29592). Benchmark results for agent configurations "did not reliably predict performance on new, unseen microscopy tasks" — [arXiv:2608.05266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05266).
- Automated alignment is not new and the new work is not benchmarked against it. Vendors already sell auto-functions (AutoComa, autofocus/astigmatism, OptiSTEM/AutoSTEM; TENSOR background alignment); the Jan 2026 UTK paper reports no comparison with vendor routines — [alphaXiv full text](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.18972.md). T1 for column alignment may be incremental rather than a step change.
- "Closed interfaces" is only partly true for the dominant maker. Thermo Fisher sells Python scripting (AutoScript TEM/4, offline simulator), EPU has an "open API", and every agent paper found was built on those interfaces (plus CEOS API, Gatan DM). The restriction is commercial (paid options such as lens-current control) and operational (remote control discouraged), not a technical lock-out. The clearer closed layer found was the analyser software (Oxford Instruments AZtec, planned screen-scraping in ACMAS).
- Value seems to stay with the hardware owner and the user, not a new software layer. Agent frameworks are open-sourced by national labs (MIT/GPL); SerialEM is free cross-vendor; CryoSPARC is embedded inside the vendor's EPU. On present evidence there is little room for an independent paid "agent layer" in EM (inference).
- The business is still cyclical one-off hardware with funding exposure. Thermo Fisher EM growth slowed to +3% in 2025 before +13% in Q3 2026; JEOL Scientific/Metrology fell 6.8% (FY2025); Zeiss RMS declined and wrote off its goodwill (€54m); Oxford I&A revenue fell 4.8% — sources in section 3. Drivers named are budgets (US cuts, Chinese stimulus cycles, Chinese import-duty exemptions) and semiconductor capex, not software.
- Hardware-damage caution is real and slows agent adoption (supports the obstacle part of the thesis): LBNL restricted the LLM to predefined tools and isolated the network; Thermo Fisher limits EPU remote access to monitoring.
- Fleet matching for chips: the incumbent metrology maker claims the installed base (Hitachi ~76% CD-SEM) and is building its own data layer (HMAX for Semiconductors) — the "same method keeps fleets matched" leg may accrue to the incumbent rather than a new entrant (inference; company claims).

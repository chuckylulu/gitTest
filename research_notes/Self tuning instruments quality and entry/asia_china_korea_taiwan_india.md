## 1. Map by country and sub-segment

### Takeaway
Only two companies in these four markets have global, non-policy-made positions in the theme's instrument segments. Park Systems leads in atomic force microscopes (AFM; about 21.7% of the global AFM market by one consultancy estimate). Chroma ATE leads in power, battery and system-level test equipment, though AI-server demand, not self-tuning, drives it. China has a broad bench of fast-growing listed instrument makers (CIQTEK, Skyverse, Rigol, Siglent, Uni-Trend, Jingce), but their positions rest largely on domestic substitution behind export controls and procurement preference. Siglent, with 46% of revenue overseas, is the partial exception. India has no instrument maker of global weight that I could identify. Its relevant assets are public facilities (RRCAT's Indus synchrotrons) and early-stage quantum hardware (QpiAI).

## 2. Policy and export-control context

### Takeaway
China's state is the main demand-shaper. It buys and subsidises domestic instruments through the 2021 Science and Technology Progress Law's procurement clause, the March 2024 equipment-renewal plan and large grants (Skyverse's 2025 grants were almost three times its net profit). Its August 2025 "AI+" opinion explicitly targets AI-driven research and the "intelligent upgrade" of major research infrastructure. In Korea, a broker reports that US controls on China do not currently cover AFM, so Park Systems' roughly 30% China exposure is a policy risk rather than a current restriction. I could not verify the 2025-26 government-procurement "domestic product" rules, current Entity List status of the Chinese names, or the Japanese and Dutch controls.

## 3. AI and agent-run set-up evidence in these markets

What has shipped in these markets is mostly classical automation of set-up steps, plus AI applied to data interpretation:
- Park: automatic defect review, automatic probe recognition and exchange, automatic laser-beam alignment.
- CIQTEK: an autofocus patent and the "SpinMind & EPRMind" spectrum-analysis platform.
- Skyverse: AI defect classification and yield software.

I found no shipped LLM-agent set-up or self-calibration feature from a maker in China, Korea, Taiwan or India. The clearest value-capture signal is on the user side: SK hynix uses Gauss Labs' virtual metrology.

- The thesis' "fast judge" logic is visible: Park's automatic laser alignment and probe recognition, and CIQTEK's autofocus, are tuning against the instrument's own signal. ## 4. Leading companies (tests a-e, transitions T1-T5)

### Takeaway
Six companies carry the strongest claim to relevance: Park Systems, Chroma ATE, CIQTEK, Skyverse, Rigol and Siglent. Focused Photonics was assessed and demoted: 2025 loss, 9M 2026 loss, flat revenue, net debt about RMB 1.9bn. On quality alone, my provisional order is:
1. Park Systems: a global No.1 niche judge, but cyclical and mostly one-off hardware, with a weak H1 2026.
2. Chroma ATE: outstanding current economics (ROE 54% annualised), but its demand is AI-capex and power-test, not self-tuning; it is high-beta.
3. Siglent: profitable, export-led, net cash; low-end-to-mid hardware.
4. Rigol: large net cash, quantum-control-class array systems; weak ROE.
5. Skyverse: real technical progress in China, but loss-making on a deducted basis, subsidy-dependent and inventory-heavy.
6. CIQTEK: loss-making, small, policy-supported.

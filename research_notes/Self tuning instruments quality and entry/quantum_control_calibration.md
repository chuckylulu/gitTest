## 2. Verification of the three background items
### Takeaway
All three items resolve to real, dated sources. (i) IQM/NVIDIA 20-qubit: real, but it is an IQM blog post (21 Oct 2026) with no timing, no human comparison and no fidelity numbers; the model is NVIDIA Ising Calibration (open VLM). (ii) arXiv 2606.22376 resolves: a Chinese academic team (BAQIS / IOP-CAS / ITP-CAS / DP Technology) used Claude Code as the agent harness driving a fine-tuned Qwen3.6-35B-A3B to do single-qubit bring-up of 108 of 112 qubits in 4.7 h vs an estimated 18-24 h for an expert; two-qubit gates were NOT automated. (iii) Google: real, Nature paper (Sivak et al., arXiv 2511.08493, Nature July 2026), RL agent steering >1,000 control parameters on Willow during QEC using detection events as the reward, ~20% lower logical error vs expert-tuned baseline. None of the three has been independently replicated yet.

### 2.1 IQM + NVIDIA: agent calibrating a 20-qubit chip
- Source and date: IQM blog post "What happened when we let AI calibrate a quantum chip using NVIDIA Ising", 21 Oct 2026, author Max Haeberlein (Senior Partnership Manager, IQM). [company claim, not independently confirmed] — [IQM blog, 21 Oct 2026](https://iqm.tech/blog/what-happened-when-we-let-ai-calibrate-a-quantum-chip-using-nvidia-ising/)
- What the agent did: NVIDIA Ising Calibration vision-language models were connected to "IQM's own calibration software" (not named); the AI read each calibration chart, chose the next step, and when a qubit's correct setting fell outside the chart's range, it identified those qubits, widened the scan range and reran only those; every decision was logged in plain English for engineer review. Quote: "AI has calibrated all 20 qubits on an IQM quantum chip." — [IQM blog, 21 Oct 2026](https://iqm.tech/blog/what-happened-when-we-let-ai-calibrate-a-quantum-chip-using-nvidia-ising/)
- Time against a human: NOT reported in the IQM post (no timing, no comparison with a human expert or with IQM's prior automation, no gate fidelities). — same source. "could not verify" any speed-up figure for this specific 20-qubit run.
- Model used: NVIDIA Ising Calibration VLM family (IQM post gives no version/parameter count; it says NVIDIA tested a compressed version on a DGX Station). Ising Calibration 1 is open-weight, based on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B — [QCalEval, arXiv 2604.25884, 28 Apr 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25884). Ising Calibration 1.5 (31B parameters; BF16 and NVFP4 versions; OpenMDW licence) released 27 Jul 2026 — [NVIDIA developer blog, 27 Jul 2026](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-ising-enables-fully-automated-quantum-computer-calibration-with-enhanced-in-context-learning)
- Benchmark context: QCalEval = 243 calibration-plot samples, 22 experiment families, superconducting + neutral atom; 57 charts from real hardware, the rest simulated (IQM's description); Ising Calibration 1 scored 74.7 zero-shot average vs 72.3 for the best general-purpose zero-shot model. The benchmark is built and scored by NVIDIA and collaborators (authors include NVIDIA's Sam Stanwyck, Timothy Costa, Krysta Svore, Elica Kyoseva; plus Alán Aspuru-Guzik, NPL's Ivan Rungger, Harvard's Giulia Semeghini, among 32 authors) — i.e., it is not an independent evaluation. — [arXiv 2604.25884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25884); [IQM blog](https://iqm.tech/blog/what-happened-when-we-let-ai-calibrate-a-quantum-chip-using-nvidia-ising/)
- Precursor: IQM announced "AI-driven agentic calibration" using NVIDIA Ising on 14 Apr 2026 (World Quantum Day) — parallel agents inspecting calibration results across all qubits at each stage; no qubit counts, no time-reduction figures, no named customers; NVIDIA's Sam Stanwyck called it "a pioneering demonstration" (i.e., not described as a shipped product). — [The Quantum Insider, 14 Apr 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/04/14/iqm-ai-based-calibration-scalable-quantum-systems/)
- Limitations stated by IQM: only 20 qubits tested; behaviour in unseen situations "still open"; 186 of 243 QCalEval charts simulated. — [IQM blog](https://iqm.tech/blog/what-happened-when-we-let-ai-calibrate-a-quantum-chip-using-nvidia-ising/)

### 2.2 arXiv 2606.22376 — "Vibe Calibration" (resolves; paper read in full)
- Title: "Vibe Calibration: Autonomous Bring-up of a 112-Qubit Superconducting Quantum Processor by a Skill-Orchestrating Language Agent"; v1 submitted 21 Jun 2026 (dated 23 Jun 2026 in PDF); 14 authors (Huikai Xu, Jiaxiu Han, Shigang Ou, Cheng Ye, ... Lei Wang, Lin-Feng Zhang, Pan Zhang, Hai-Feng Yu). Not peer reviewed (arXiv preprint). — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22376); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22376)
- Institutions: Beijing Academy of Quantum Information Sciences (BAQIS, Beijing Key Laboratory of Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing); Institute of Physics CAS; Institute of Theoretical Physics CAS; University of Chinese Academy of Sciences; Hangzhou Institute for Advanced Study (UCAS); DP Technology (深势科技, Beijing AI-for-science company); AI for Science Institute Beijing; Hefei National Laboratory. Funded by NSFC grants, the Innovation Program for Quantum Science and Technology (2021ZD0301802) and CAS Strategic Priority Research Program. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22376)
- Hardware: 112 frequency-tunable transmons with tunable couplers, tilted square lattice 7 qubits x 16 rows, 4-connectivity; f01 maxima 3.8-4.3 GHz; readout cavities ~7.0 GHz. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22376)
- Control stack: each qubit channel driven by a direct-digital-synthesis (DDS) source at 6 GS/s with 16-bit resolution producing pulses directly at qubit frequency; readout digitised directly at 4 GS/s; centralised trigger distribution "QSYNC" synchronising channels within ±20 ps; software = the open "QuarkStudio" measurement framework (quarkstudio.readthedocs.io) and in-house "quark/measlib"/"qulab" scripts; the agent talks to it through a config-store API that reads/writes device parameters atomically. The electronics vendor is not named (direct-RF DDS architecture; could not verify maker). — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22376)
- Agent and model: "For the agent and the large language model, in this work we chose Claude Code and a fine-tuned Qwen model, respectively." Fine-tuned Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (MoE, 3B active) with a LoRA variant ("LoRA-Over"); a Qwen3.5-4B dense variant failed transfer tests; Kimi K2.6 used as a negative control (deliberated without executing the requested action); DeepSeek-V4 used to process training data. Training data = trajectories recorded under human supervision; Claude Code harness system prompt preserved verbatim during fine-tuning. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22376)
- What was automated: single-qubit bring-up only — hardware init, S21 readout resonator search, spectroscopy (incl. flux-vs-frequency mapping with dynamic-programming ridge fitting for tunable devices), time-Rabi, power-Rabi (pi-pulse amplitude), single-shot readout discrimination and optimisation, T1, Ramsey; plus a transmission-line characterisation Skill. Skills are decision trees with quantitative acceptance gates (e.g., R^2 > 0.9 before any parameter write-back; fit SNR >= 10 dB) and rollback edges; "hardware-safe qubit groups" exposed by the backend set batching boundaries (a hardware-damage/safety constraint built into the interface). Development went in three phases: human-guided (1 qubit), semi-autonomous with remote expert on unknown errors (16 qubits), fully autonomous (112 qubits). — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22376)
- Results: full 112-qubit campaign in a single 4.7 h session with no human intervention (1.2 h transmission-line/wiring checks incl. manual cable reconnection; 3.1 h parallel per-qubit calibration; 0.4 h final coherence/reporting); valid T1 fits on 108/112 qubits (96%) and T2R on 102/112 (91%) vs 112/112 and 110/112 for the expert; median T1 96.2 µs (agent) vs 95.7 µs (expert); median T2R 7.9 µs (agent) vs 14.4 µs (expert) because the agent did not tune each qubit to its flux sweet spot. Expert time for the full campaign is an ESTIMATE of 18-24 h (hence the "4-5x" claim). Measured head-to-head on a random 16-qubit subset: agreement within uncertainty on 14/16 qubits; agent 0.6 h (2.3 min/qubit) vs human 3.8 h (14.3 min/qubit) = 6.3x. Repeatability across three runs with power cycles on 8 qubits: frequencies within ±120 kHz rms. — [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22376)
- Limits (from the paper): two-qubit gate calibration, crosstalk compensation and spectator-error mitigation NOT done (listed as future work); continuous drift tracking not done; low-level control scripts need adaptation per hardware platform; full-campaign human baseline estimated rather than measured; dephasing operating point sub-optimal; preprint not yet peer reviewed. [company/academic claim, not independently confirmed]
- Relevance to thesis: the agent's "judge" is the instrument's own fit-quality metrics; the value sat in an open measurement framework (QuarkStudio) plus a general-purpose agent harness (Claude Code) plus an open-weight model — not in a proprietary control vendor's software.

### 2.3 Google: AI steering >1,000 control parameters during error correction (July 2026)
- Paper: "Reinforcement Learning Control of Quantum Error Correction", Volodymyr Sivak et al. (299 authors incl. Alexis Morvan, Paul V. Klimov, Johannes Bausch, Andrew W. Senior, Hartmut Neven), arXiv 2511.08493 (v1 11 Nov 2025; v4 19 Jun 2026); published in Nature (article id s41586-026-10759-2) with press coverage on 10 Jul 2026. Also presented at APS Global Physics Summit, March 2026 (session Z05). — [arXiv 2511.08493](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08493); [The Quantum Insider, 10 Jul 2026](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/10/google-study-shows-quantum-computer-can-learn-from-its-own-errors-while-it-computes/); [APS 2026 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/smt/2026/mar-z05/1/)
- Method: error-detection events produced during QEC are used both to correct the logical state and as the training signal for a reinforcement-learning agent that adjusts control parameters while the computation runs (no stop-and-recalibrate). >1,000 parameters steered (microwave pulse amplitudes, frequencies, coupling strengths, etc.) on a Willow processor; codes: distance-5 and distance-7 surface codes and a distance-5 colour code. — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/10/google-study-shows-quantum-computer-can-learn-from-its-own-errors-while-it-computes/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08493)
- Results: ~20% lower logical error rate than after exhaustive conventional calibration plus expert tuning; under injected drift, 3.5-fold better logical stability (hardware + decoder parameters; 31% lower logical error) and 2.4-fold with hardware controls only (24% lower); record average logical error per cycle 7.72(9)x10^-4 (surface code) and 8.19(14)x10^-3 (colour code); simulations of distance-15 codes with ~40,000 parameters suggest optimisation speed independent of system size. — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08493); [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/10/google-study-shows-quantum-computer-can-learn-from-its-own-errors-while-it-computes/)
- Limits: relies on proprietary Google software (authors give a mathematical description for replication); exploration must be balanced against degrading the computation; fast drift outruns the agent; scaling claims are simulation-only; other modalities not demonstrated. [peer-reviewed in Nature; not independently replicated] — [TQI](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/07/10/google-study-shows-quantum-computer-can-learn-from-its-own-errors-while-it-computes/)
- Relevance: this is the clearest case of "the instrument's own reading is the judge" (QEC syndrome statistics as reward), and it was done entirely in-house by the processor maker with its own control stack — evidence that the value of in-operation calibration accrues to vertically integrated processor makers.

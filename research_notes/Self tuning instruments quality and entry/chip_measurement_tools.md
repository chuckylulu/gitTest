## 2. Verification of background item: table-top EUV microscope with self-calibration, 43.5 nm half-pitch, 2026

### Takeaway
VERIFIED, with corrections to framing: it is an academic Chinese (CAS/UCAS-led) laboratory result, a table-top high-harmonic EUV (17.9 nm) grazing-reflection ptychography system whose "self-calibration" is algorithmic joint estimation of geometry (distance, 3-D illumination angle, 3-D detector pose, scan positions) inside the phase retrieval. It resolved 43.5 nm half-pitch gratings on an unidentified real chip, matching SEM. It is not a product, not an AI agent, and is far from a fab tool (about 15 min per scan, 400 positions, offline gradient reconstruction).

### Cited Findings
- Paper: "Table-top extreme ultraviolet grazing-reflection ptychography with full-pose self-calibration", Light: Advanced Manufacturing vol. 7, article 112 (2026), DOI 10.37188/lam.2026.112. Received 2 Jun 2026, accepted 30 Jun 2026, published online 9 Sep 2026. Corresponding author Yishi Shi (University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, School of Optoelectronics; CAS Aerospace Information Research Institute). Co-authors from Jihua Laboratory (Foshan), CAS Institute of Physics, Tianjin University Ultrafast Laser Lab, CAS Shanghai Institute of Optics and Fine Mechanics, Yunnan University, Songshan Lake Materials Lab. [peer-reviewed journal, independently confirmed by the journal's publication] — [Light: Advanced Manufacturing article page](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.112)
- Press release by Light Publishing Center (CIOMP, CAS) dated 28 Oct 2026 on EurekAlert. — [EurekAlert 2026-10-28](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1149569)
- Method: table-top 17.9 nm EUV source based on high-order harmonic generation (HHG); grazing incidence ~70 degrees from normal; sCMOS detector (2,048 x 2,048, 11 um pixels) ~50 mm from sample; reconstructed pixel 43.5 nm x 70.0 nm at NA 0.19 x 0.095; ptychographic reconstruction with feature-domain loss and automatic differentiation. — [Light: AM](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.112)
- What "self-calibration" means: joint estimation, together with object and probe, of diffraction distance d, three illumination angles, three detector rotation angles, and per-position scan errors (example wafer result d = 48.42 mm). The press release says it "eliminates the need for highly precise hardware alignment". This is classical model-based optimisation (autodiff phase retrieval), not machine learning or an agent. — [Light: AM](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.112); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1149569)
- Result: on a real chip (chip not identified), grating structures at 43.5 nm half-pitch resolved (stated as the system's diffraction limit), consistent with SEM; contamination and scratch defects identified; on a custom wafer (gold on Si, 80 um Siemens star, minimum line 30 nm) the minimum resolved grating FWHM was 82.6 nm. — [Light: AM](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.112)
- Throughput: 400 Fermat-spiral scan positions, ~82% overlap, 4 exposures per position (HDR), ~1 s recording + ~1.2 s stage move per position, ~15 min total; authors state online deployment is currently limited by the computational speed of gradient-based phase retrieval. — [Light: AM](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.112)
- Funding: China National Key R&D Program (2021YFB3602604), NSFC (62131011), Fundamental Research Funds for Central Universities. No industry partner named. — [Light: AM](https://www.light-am.com/article/doi/10.37188/lam.2026.112)
- Context: an earlier table-top EUV transmission ptychography result (Jena group, Light: Science & Applications 2022) reached 16 nm half-pitch on a Siemens star; different geometry, not comparable. — [Nature/LSA 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41377-022-00797-6)

### Inferences
- The item supports the thesis only in a narrow sense: the instrument's own physics model plus a known structure acts as the "judge" for geometric calibration. It is optimisation, not an agent, and the 43.5 nm half-pitch is far coarser than leading-edge fab needs (sub-20 nm pitches, sub-angstrom precision), with throughput orders of magnitude below fab tools.
- Commercial relevance: Chinese state-funded work on EUV-wavelength metrology fits China's domestic-substitution push; no company link found.

## 3. State of automated matching, calibration and recipe set-up in fabs (classical, ML, LLM agents); interface openness; who captures value

### Takeaway
Matching, calibration and recipe set-up on metrology fleets are already heavily automated with classical statistics (standard wafers, offsets, SPC) and, at leading nodes, machine learning (ML models of non-linear tool-to-tool bias, virtual metrology, automatic recipe optimisation). LLM work in 2025-2026 is at the assistant / proof-of-concept stage: the clearest published example (SPIE AL+P, Feb 2026, proceedings 22 Apr 2026) is a retrieval-augmented LLM that helps beginner operators create CD-SEM recipes. I found no public evidence of an LLM agent autonomously creating recipes, matching tools or self-calibrating metrology tools in a production fab. Matching at the finest level needs fab-owned device and electrical-test data, which tool makers often lack. This points value toward whoever holds both the tool and the fab data, and in practice the fab controls access.

### Cited Findings
**Classical and ML automation already in use**
- Tool-to-tool matching (TTTM) is required at installation, new product introduction, after maintenance or part replacement, and at regular intervals: "once a day, once a shift, or once a lot (advanced nodes)". Classical methods: standard-wafer calibration, component/system and spectral calibration, SPC, reference checks. ML is used to model non-linear bias between tools at advanced nodes, capture tool "signatures", amplify weak signals and detect anomalies. Advantest's Vincent Chu: "ML complements rather than replaces classical statistical approaches." LLMs or agents are not mentioned in the article. [secondary report, trade press with named vendor sources] — [Semiconductor Engineering, L. Peters, 2026-03-10](https://semiengineering.com/tool-matching-getting-tougher-across-test-metrology/)
- Data ownership: Onto Innovation's Melvin Lee Wei Heng: "Achieving this level of matching requires access to fab-level device data, such as metrology results and functional test outcomes." Onto's Joe Fillion: "Tools are typically matched based on a hierarchy of steps, and each fab or OEM may approach it slightly differently"; automated decision-making could follow once trust is built (no deployed example). ### Inferences
- The thesis' "fast, unambiguous judge" exists in metrology (standard wafers, reference structures, SEM/TEM cross-checks), and machine learning against such judges is routine. The LLM-agent layer is not yet a production fact in fabs as of Nov 2026; first uses are documentation-grounded assistants for recipe set-up and vendor visions.
- The finest matching needs fab device/e-test data, which sits with the fab, so pure tool-makers cannot close the loop alone; fabs (and fab-affiliated software firms such as Gauss Labs for SK) are positioned to own fleet-level tuning, while tool makers own tool-level calibration.

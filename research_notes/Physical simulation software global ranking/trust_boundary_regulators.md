## Q0. Summary, definitions and verdict on the prior

### Takeaway
SUMMARY FOR THE REPORT WRITER (12 Nov 2026).
(1) Physics-based simulation counts as primary evidence wherever a regulator or the customer has written a credibility route. Examples: nuclear accident analysis (approved evaluation models, RG 1.203 / 10 CFR 50.46); chip sign-off (foundry-certified tools); grid connection (models checked against staged tests or measured disturbances); catastrophe-model rate filings (Florida Commission); aircraft seats (FAA AC 20-146A); FDA device modelling (2023 credibility guidance; development tools that name Ansys HFSS); PBPK accepted "in lieu of clinical PK data". In every case the route is simulation validated by test, and physical tests anchor the chain. The FAA puts it plainly: "will not accept a model validated against another validated model" [S19].
(2) Learned (ML) models are accepted in two ways. First, as regulated products: the FDA had authorised over 1,600 AI-enabled devices by Sep 2026 [S24]. These include DEEPVESSEL FFR (2022), a deep-learning CT-FFR device cleared under the "coronary vascular physiologic simulation software" regulation, which was created for HeartFlow's CFD device [S22][S23]. Second, as tools that generate trial evidence under human verification: AIM-NASH (EMA Qualification Opinion 20 Mar 2025 [S27]; FDA drug development tool 8 Dec 2025 [S26]) and EMA's PROCOVA opinion (2022) [S28]. No aviation or nuclear regulator was found to accept a learned model as evidence. EASA's AI guidance is still in draft (NPA 2025-07; Concept Paper Issue 03, 3 Jun 2026), and the FAA works project by project through issue papers. A study commissioned by the NRC says AI models are "practically not allowed" in emergency core cooling and transient evaluation models under Appendix K [S1].
(3) Verdict on the prior. It holds for aviation and nuclear. For medicine it holds only under a narrow reading: a learned model replacing a physical test or a physics-based safety analysis, with no human checking each output. Under the broad reading (a regulator accepts evidence generated by a learned model in a decision-grade submission), EMA (Mar 2025) and FDA (Dec 2025) have already contradicted it.
(4) The most likely boundary-mover before 2031 is EASA. Concept Paper Issue 03, section 3.3.7, sets objectives for ML surrogates, including surrogates that "completely replace the high-fidelity model" [S14]. These objectives feed rulemaking task RMT.0742: a second NPA is planned for 2026, policy is due to be finalised by 2028, and EU AI Act obligations for AI in regulated products start 2 Aug 2028 [S11][S16][S35].
(5) Certifiers. Regulators that already run model-qualification procedures are furthest ahead: FDA (development-tool programmes) and EMA (qualification opinions). Others hold the certifying position in practice: classification societies (DNV both certifies and sells simulation software; background), testing and certification bodies (TÜV SÜD tool qualification, company claim), foundries (TSMC, Samsung) and customers' own methods groups (NERC Transmission Planners; carmakers under Euro NCAP). ASME's VVUQ 70 subcommittee covers machine learning but has published no standard [S52].

### Cited Findings
Definitions used to test the prior:
- **Learned model**: an input-output map set mainly by fitting many parameters to data (neural network, Gaussian process, boosted trees). This excludes mechanistic codes with calibrated parameters and low-order correlations. Note that the NRC already admits "phenomenological models and correlations as 'calculational devices'" inside evaluation models [S1]. So a reading broad enough to cover any data-fitted model would make the prior false trivially.
- **Primary evidence**: the model output is the main basis for a compliance, safety or efficacy finding, in place of the test or physics-based analysis otherwise required.
- **Narrow reading**: the learned model replaces a physical test or physics-based analysis AND no human adjudicates its outputs.
- **Broad reading**: the regulator accepts evidence generated by a learned model in a regulatory decision, even with human verification.

Closest counter-examples, nearest first:
- **EMA AIM-NASH (CHMP Qualification Opinion, first published 20 Mar 2025).**
  - EMA calls it the "first Qualification Opinion (QO) on an innovative development methodology based on artificial intelligence".
  - "The committee can accept evidence generated by the tool as scientifically valid in future applications."
  - Biopsy readings are "verified by one expert pathologist", in place of the three-pathologist consensus. The model is "locked", and major changes may require re-qualification. Training data: more than 100,000 annotations by 59 pathologists on over 5,000 biopsies from nine trials. [S27]
  - Liver biopsy is "the gold standard for demonstrating the efficacy" of MASH drugs [S27]. The tool therefore produces the measurement behind the primary efficacy endpoint, but the physical biopsy is still taken and a human signs off.
- **FDA AIM-NASH** (8 Dec 2025): "the first AI drug development tool"; "pathologists are fully responsible for final interpretation" [S26].
- **FDA DEEPVESSEL FFR** (K213657, letter dated 1 Apr 2022).
  - Class II, 21 CFR 870.1415, product code PJA. It computes FFR "using deep learning neural networks" (a multi-layer perceptron plus a bidirectional recurrent network).
  - The predicate is HeartFlow FFRCT, which derives FFR "from simulated pressure, velocity and blood flow" [S22].
  - Clearance rested on a clinical study of 244 patients and 311 vessels against invasive FFR: per-vessel sensitivity 86.9% and specificity 86.7%, with 95% CI lower bounds of 80.6% and 82.0% against targets of 75% and 70% [S22].
  - The device class was created by HeartFlow's De Novo request (DEN130045, received 6 Nov 2013). Its special controls require "non-clinical performance testing ... to demonstrate the validity of computational modeling methods for flow measurement" [S23].
  - Reading: a learned model replaced CFD inside a cleared device. The learned model is the product; the evidence was a clinical test.
- **EMA PROCOVA** (Qualification Opinion, Sep 2022; company summary): machine-learning prognostic scores ("digital twins") used as covariates in the primary analysis of Phase 2/3 trials. EMA qualified the statistical method, not the model. Unlearn says the FDA found PROCOVA "does not appear to deviate from our guidance" (company claim, unconfirmed) [S28].
- **EASA Concept Paper Issue 03, section 3.3.7** (3 Jun 2026, draft for consultation): "In other cases, the surrogate model completely replaces the high-fidelity model in the simulation process, and the output of the surrogate model defines the required results. Consequently, the level of accuracy of a surrogate model should be at the same level as the credibility of the high-fidelity physics-based model or be compensated for" (Objective SU-SM-01). This is a path, not an acceptance [S14].
- **NRC.** Under RG 1.203, AI "may be constrained to perform limited computations consistent with separated effect tests ... Applying AI to achieve complex computations will trigger a high level of scrutiny". The "frozen" model concept "can also be applied to AI models" [S1].

### Inferences
- **Aviation.** The prior holds today. The probability that it still holds in 2031 is lower than 80% under a moderate reading. EASA already has written surrogate-model objectives and a rulemaking path to 2028. An ML surrogate credited in a non-catastrophic compliance demonstration is plausible by 2031. Catastrophic functions are explicitly excluded [S14].
- **Nuclear.** The prior holds and is the safest of the three. Appendix K's explicit model language is binding regulation. NRC AI activity in 2025-26 is internal (the FY26 plan, "SI" use cases) [S2][S4].
- **Medicine.** The broad reading of the prior is already false. The narrow reading holds, but its margin depends on whether a "pathologist verifies" condition is ever dropped.
- **Suggested restatement:** "No major aviation or nuclear regulator accepts a learned model as primary evidence in place of a physical test or physics-based safety analysis before 2031." Medicine should be treated separately.

### Gaps
- Whether any FAA or EASA approval of an ML airborne function occurred in 2025-26: ROAAS and traffic detection were expected "over the next 12 months" from Mar 2025 [S17]. Not confirmed.

## Q1. Civil aviation

### Takeaway
Certification by analysis is accepted when the model is validated by test. For new designs, test remains the anchor. Learned models: no approvals as evidence. EASA has the most developed draft framework, including ML surrogates; the FAA proceeds case by case.

### Cited Findings
- **FAA AC 20-146A (29 Jun 2018)** [S19]:
  - Covers seat certification by "computer modeling analysis techniques that are validated by dynamic tests" for §§23/25/27/29.562 and TSO-C127a/b.
  - "The FAA will not accept a model validated against another validated model."
  - Modelling "instead of dynamic testing" applies only to modifications of a certificated design (for example seat families). Margin-of-safety limits apply: a modified non-critical member needs an ultimate margin ≥0.5; critical members are restricted.
  - The 1986 rulemaking had said "no sufficient database existed" to permit analysis in place of test.
- **FAA Roadmap for AI Safety Assurance, Version I** (PDF created 28 Jun 2024; 31 pp) [S18]:
  - Principles: distinguish "learned AI" (static) from "learning AI"; take an incremental approach; build experience on lower design assurance levels first.
  - "FAA is working with companies on avionics applications of AI in aircraft, using project-specific issue papers".
  - Milestones: policy statement and policy memo, draft 4Q2024; Certification Position Papers 1Q2026; Advisory Circulars TBD; an SAE G-34/EUROCAE standard for learned-AI assurance TBD.
  - No Version II was found.
- **EASA AI Roadmap 2.0** (10 May 2023) [S11]:
  - Level 1 AI "foreseen for 2025"; Level 2 "around 2035 ... not a formal target"; Level 3A/3B between 2035 and 2050.
  - "Finalisation of the EASA AI/ML policy by 2028".
- **NPA 2025-07** (10 Nov 2025): RMT.0742; comment period extended to 10 Mar 2026; responds to EU AI Act Art. 108 [S12]. It covers "data-driven AI systems, including supervised and unsupervised learning"; a second NPA is planned for 2026 (secondary) [S16].
- **Concept Paper Issue 03** (3 Jun 2026; comments to 12 Aug 2026; "final Concept Paper deliverable" under Roadmap 2.0) [S13][S14]:
  - Scope covers Levels 0-3, including "AI-based development and verification tools used to develop approved products".
  - It excludes systems "directly contributing to the potential for fatalities ... with the loss of an aircraft", and covers only models "'frozen' at the time of approval".
  - Assigning DAL D/AL5/TQL5 to an AI constituent involved in catastrophic failure conditions "will not be accepted".
  - For surrogates, the test set "could be exclusively composed of data produced from a high-fidelity model". A planned EASA "governance and decision board" will assess net-safety-benefit claims.
  - It allows reuse of "AI-based systems ... that were approved before the publication of this guidance" [S14].
- **EASA AI page:** no ML approval listed; MLEAP (final report 31 May 2024) is research [S15]. Aviation Week (14 Mar 2025) expected first applications in runway-overrun awareness and alerting, then traffic detection, "over the next 12 months" [S17].
- **Industry practice** (company sources): Luminary's blog says its physics-AI models supply "predictions, not permission" and "the goal is to avoid unnecessary repeats, not bypass required verification" (DARPA CyPhER Forge, 10 Sep 2026) [S54].

### Inferences
- EASA is the only aviation regulator found with written objectives for ML surrogates used as engineering evidence. This is the most important item to watch for the prior.
- The FAA's route is project-specific and leaves little public evidence trail. A first FAA crediting of ML may not be visible until position papers or Advisory Circulars appear.

### Gaps
- JCAB, CAAC, DGCA and CAAI positions on certification by analysis and AI: none reached. They are generally aligned with FAA/EASA through bilateral agreements (background).
- Whether the FAA's 1Q2026 position papers or its policy statement were issued; EASA CM-S-014 (modelling and simulation for CS-25 structures) could not be fetched (404).

## Q2. Space and defence

### Takeaway
Credibility standards exist for physics-based modelling. Learned models are at the research-programme stage.

### Cited Findings

## Q3. Nuclear

### Takeaway
Nuclear is among the most simulation-dependent fields (accident analysis is by approved codes) and the least open to learned models. US rules name acceptable model types. Abroad, practice could not be verified this session.

### Cited Findings
- **NRC gap assessment** (Southwest Research Institute for the NRC, Oct 2024, ML24290A059) [S1]:
  - Reviewed 517 Regulatory Guides; potential gaps in 71, grouped into 8 types, including "Special computations".
  - "No practical standards were identified" for endorsement; it recommends general guides on "use of AI technologies in special computations".
  - Under RG 1.157 and RG 1.203: "the use of AI models ... is practically not allowed by the underlying regulations, mainly Part 50 Appendix K."
  - Examples it lists include "metamodels of high-resolution physics models such as fire dynamics and core dynamics".
- **Framework judgement:** the NRC's external-facing page says the framework is "generally sufficient to accommodate SI". It cites the NRC-CNSC-ONR principles paper (Sep 2024, ML24241A252) and work on language models with France's ASNR and Germany's GRS [S3].
- **NRC FY26 AI Strategic Plan** (29 Sep 2025, ML25269A196), issued per EO 14179 by the agency's Chief Information Officer. It concerns internal adoption; no licensing guidance timetable was found [S4].
- **EO 14434 renaming.** The NRC website now uses "Super Intelligence (SI)" following EO 14434 (29 Sep 2026; page updated 4 Nov 2026) [S2]. Commentators read EO 14434 as a terminology order that creates no new regulatory powers [S9].
- **EO 14300** (23 May 2025): "Employ emerging technologies to safely accelerate the modeling, simulation, testing, and approval of new reactor designs"; a decision deadline of no more than 18 months; final rules within 18 months; an expedited pathway for designs "the DOD or the DOE have tested" [S8].
- **Part 53** ("risk-informed, technology-inclusive") final rule approved 25 Mar 2026 [S7]. TerraPower Kemmerer Unit 1: application 28 Mar 2024, final safety evaluation Dec 2025, construction-permit decision 9 Mar 2026 [S6].
- **NRC's own codes** are used for "review of licensees' codes and performance of audit calculations": TRACE, RELAP5, PARCS, MELCOR, MACCS, FRAPCON/FRAPTRAN, SAPHIRE, xLPR, FAVPRO (2024) (page updated 7 Oct 2026) [S5].
- **Studsvik Scandpower** describes "licensing-grade software ... to support operating utilities, fuel vendors, safety authorities" (company claim, unconfirmed) [S10].

### Inferences
- The regulator itself owns reference codes (the NRC suite). Any learned surrogate would face audit calculations by the regulator's physics codes. That raises the bar further.

### Gaps
- Code-approval practice not verified this session in France (ASNR; ASN Guide No. 28 on qualification of scientific computing tools, background), the UK (ONR assessment guides), Japan (NRA), South Korea (NSSC/KINS; KAERI and KHNP codes), China (NNSA) and India (AERB).
- Not verified: which codes advanced-reactor applicants used (Kairos, TerraPower); topical-report approvals of vendor codes, including Studsvik CMS5.

## Q4. Medical devices

### Takeaway
Physics-based modelling is accepted with credibility evidence under ASME V&V 40-style risk framing. Learned models are accepted as devices, judged on clinical or test data, not as evidence substituting for tests.

### Cited Findings
- **FDA modelling-credibility guidance** "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions" (final, Nov 2023): it "applies to physics-based, mechanistic, or other first principles-based models" [S20].
- **Qualified development tools** (list current 10 Feb 2026) [S21]:
  - Computational tools: Virtual MRI Safety Evaluations (16 Nov 2021); IMAnalytics with MRIxViP (20 May 2021); a Visible Human CAD model with Ansys HFSS/Mechanical FEM to predict heating near a femoral nail at 1.5 T (30 Mar 2023); ENDPOINT numaScrew Virtual Pullout Test (10 Apr 2025); IVIES (3 Feb 2026).
  - No tool is described as using ML. Owners are not stated on the page.
- **AI-enabled devices:** "over 1,600 ... as of September 2026" (page current 22 Sep 2026) [S24].
- **Lifecycle guidance:** the Jan 2025 draft on AI-enabled device software is still a draft; the device centre's FY2027 agenda lists finalising it as a top priority (RAPS, 2 Oct 2026). Predetermined change control plan guidance was finalised 4 Dec 2024 [S25].
- **CT-FFR:** DeepVessel (deep learning) was cleared in 2022 under the CFD-born device class. Its clinical validation was the decisive evidence [S22][S23].
- **EU AI Act:** high-risk obligations for AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) were moved to 2 Aug 2028 by the AI Omnibus (political agreement 7 May 2026; in force 27 Jul 2026) [S35].

### Gaps
- PMDA (Japan), NMPA (China), MFDS (Korea), TFDA (Taiwan), CDSCO (India), Israeli MoH: positions on in-silico and AI evidence not reached.
- EU notified-body practice on modelling evidence not reached.
- In-silico trials accepted as pivotal evidence: none found this session.

## Q5. Medicines: model-informed development, PBPK, AI tools, animal replacement

### Takeaway
Mechanistic models already replace some clinical-pharmacology studies. AI tools are qualified for defined contexts of use with human oversight. AI credibility guidance remains in draft.

### Cited Findings
- **PBPK:** the FDA decides case by case whether to accept PBPK results "in lieu of clinical pharmacokinetic (PK) data" (guidance, Sep 2018) [S32].
- **ICH M15** "General Principles for Model-Informed Drug Development": FDA final guidance June 2026 (Federal Register 3 Jun 2026), finalising the Dec 2024 draft [S31].
- **FDA draft AI guidance for drugs** (Jan 2025): a "risk-based credibility assessment framework" keyed to context of use. FDA's drug centre page (current 1 May 2026) shows it as not final. It cites more than 500 submissions with AI components between 2016 and 2023, and the FDA-EMA "Guiding Principles of Good AI Practice in Drug Development" (Jan 2026) [S29][S30].
- **AIM-NASH:** EMA, 20 Mar 2025 [S27]; FDA, 8 Dec 2025 [S26]. Details in Q0.
- **Animal testing plan** (10 Apr 2025): reduce or replace animal testing for monoclonal antibodies using non-animal methods, including "AI-based computational models of toxicity". Companies with strong non-animal data "may receive streamlined review" [S33].
- **Year-one report** (Apr 2026, secondary): ISTAND made permanent; a database of accepted non-animal methods; draft guidance on non-animal methods (Mar 2026) [S34].

### Gaps
- No documented IND where an AI toxicity model replaced a required animal study was found.
- ICH Step 4 date for M15 not confirmed (the ICH page did not render).

## Q6. Automotive

### Takeaway
Regulation still relies mainly on physical tests. Euro NCAP is moving fastest, using carmaker FE simulations validated by physical sled tests. Learned models appear only as the product (automated driving systems), not as evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **Euro NCAP virtual testing** (ESV26-283, Graz University of Technology, Nissan, CATARC, JLR, Euro NCAP; Mar 2026) [S36]:
  - Simulation was first introduced in 2009 (pedestrian human body models). Far-side virtual testing was published in 2023 and monitored from 2024: "simulation output was requested for all cars ... results are not yet used for scoring".
  - Frontal virtual testing with Hybrid III models was "applied from 2026". Carmakers run the simulations; Euro NCAP validates them against sled tests using ISO/TS 18571:2024 scores.
  - Randomised validation load cases: far-side by 2026, frontal "anticipated by 2029". Human body model results collected by 2026, with "plan to introduce the HBM for assessment by 2029".
  - CATARC (China) pilots showed "very similar results".
- **UN rules for automated driving** [S37][S38]:
  - GRVA adopted the draft UN Regulation in Jan 2026; WP.29 adoption reported 24 Jun 2026; entry into force reported as late July 2026 (secondary, conflicting).
  - Safety case reviewed by the type-approval authority; validation by "virtual simulation, track, and real-world testing"; "credible testing" using virtual toolchains.
  - Japan and Korea "likely to follow"; China "may adopt the GTR". NHTSA put the draft GTR out for comment on 23 Jan 2026, and "no federal ADS safety rule is currently in force".

### Gaps
- Not verified (EUR-Lex returned empty pages): EU 2018/858 virtual-testing provisions (Annex VIII) and the credibility-assessment annex of EU 2022/1426.
- Not verified: China C-NCAP, Japan MLIT/NCAP and Korea KNCAP virtual-testing status.

## Q9. Semiconductors and electronics

### Takeaway
Foundries are the de facto certifiers of simulation tools. Sign-off is by physics simulation. AI enters flows as certified features but not as sign-off evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **TSMC EDA Tool Certification Program**: vendors Cadence, Synopsys, Siemens EDA. Certification tables for A14, A16, N2P and N3C (as of 1 Oct 2026) cover APR, timing, power, EM/IR, DRC, LVS/LPE, RC, simulators, and thermal (A16). Ansys does not appear by name [S39].
- **TSMC 3DFabric table** lists "Auto Chiplet Floorplan w/ Agentic AI" among items "Certified for Technology Requirement" [S39].
- **OIP forum (23 Sep 2026)**: Siemens Calibre nmDRC/nmLVS/xACT/PERC certified for A14, and Calibre 3DThermal for A16. The analyst notes "no validation regime currently exists for agent outputs" (Futurum, secondary) [S40].
- **Samsung SAFE QEDA**: "a comprehensive program for EDA tool certification" [S41].
- **Tool qualification claims** (company claims, unconfirmed): MathWorks says it ships "TÜV SÜD-certified AI tool classification guidance" for ISO 26262 / IEC 61508 [S42]. The SCADE KCG code generator is "qualified ... DO-178C/DO-330 at TQL-1" and for ISO 26262 up to ASIL D; no certifying body is named [S43].

- Not verified: Intel Foundry and Rapidus certification; DO-254 practice; EMF/SAR compliance by simulation (IEC/IEEE 62704, background).

## Q10. Power grids

### Takeaway
Grid connection rests on simulation studies whose models must match field measurements. The customer's planning group (Transmission Planner or ISO) decides which models are acceptable.

### Cited Findings
- **NERC MOD-026-1**: each unit's model "shall be verified ... using one or more models acceptable to the Transmission Planner". Verification includes "documentation demonstrating the applicable unit's model response matches the recorded response for a voltage excursion from either a staged test or a measured system disturbance" [S44].

### Inferences
Background, not re-verified:
- The study tools are proprietary and few: Siemens PSS/E, GE Vernova PSLF, PowerWorld, PSCAD (Manitoba Hydro International), DIgSILENT PowerFactory, PowerTech DSATools.
- ISOs (ERCOT, PJM, MISO) and AEMO prescribe PSS/E- or PSCAD-format models. FERC Order 2023 (Jul 2023) and Order 901 (Oct 2023) tightened model requirements for inverter-based resources.
- Large data-centre loads are now subject to emerging model requirements (NERC large-loads work).
- These vendors therefore sit on the critical path for AI data-centre grid connection.

### Gaps
- Not verified (FERC, ISO and AEMO pages returned 403 or were not found): FERC orders, MOD-026-2 status, ISO model manuals, large-load requirements.

Florida certifies catastrophe models by name and version and now requires disclosure of AI use rather than banning it. Weather agencies run ML forecasts operationally, but humans issue warnings.

## Q12. Cross-cutting standards; who is placed to certify learned models

### Takeaway
No published consensus standard yet validates learned surrogates. The most concrete text is EASA's draft surrogate guidance. Organisations that already qualify models for a defined context of use are best placed to certify learned models.

### Cited Findings
- **ASME** has published V&V 10-2019, V&V 20-2009, VVUQ 20.1-2024, VVUQ 30.1-2024 (nuclear scaling), V&V 40-2018 and VVUQ 60.1-2024. Subcommittees include VVUQ 70 "Verification and Validation of Machine Learning", VVUQ 80 (pharmaceuticals) and VVUQ 90 (airframe structures); none of these has a standard listed [S52].
- The NRC gap study found "no practical standards" ready to endorse, and examined IEEE 2841-2022, a recommended practice for deep-learning evaluation [S1].
- The FAA's roadmap leaves the SAE G-34/EUROCAE learned-AI standard "TBD" [S18].
- EASA Objective SU-SM-01 ties surrogate accuracy to the credibility of the reference model [S14].

### Inferences
Best placed to certify learned models, in order:
1. Regulators with context-of-use qualification procedures: FDA development-tool programmes and the EMA qualification opinion. Both have done it [S26][S27].
2. EASA, once RMT.0742 completes.
3. Testing and certification bodies that already qualify tools (TÜV SÜD) and class societies with recommended practices on data-driven models.
4. Foundries and grid planners as de facto certifiers inside their own value chains.

Vendors that also certify:
- DNV (background): the only clear case of a simulation vendor with a certifying role.
- The NRC: a regulator that owns reference codes [S5].
- ECMWF: a model owner and operational authority [S47].

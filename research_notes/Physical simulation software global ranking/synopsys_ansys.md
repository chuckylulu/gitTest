# Synopsys, Inc. (incl. Ansys, acquired 17 Jul 2025): business quality and forward path, as of 12 Nov 2026

**Summary for the report writer** (facts dated; assessments provisional; no share prices or multiples except the K3 moves the brief asked about)
- **Latest numbers.** Q4 FY26 is not out (due 8 Dec 2026). FY26 guide (26 Aug 2026): revenue US$9.69-9.74bn incl. ~US$2.98bn Ansys; non-GAAP margin ~41.5% (GAAP ~10.4%); FCF ~US$2.6bn. FY27 guide (Investor Day, 30 Sep 2026): US$11.10-11.20bn (~15%), ~44% margin, FCF ~US$3.1bn. FY26-30 targets: ~15% CAGR (EDA 13%+, S&A 10%+, IP 17%+), ~50% margin by FY30.
- **(a) Position: strong.** Owns many sign-off judges. PrimeTime has 90%+ of timing sign-off (SemiAnalysis). SAMR's 2025 decision put Ansys at 55-60% in gate-level power integrity and 45-50% in EM simulation. Listed in TSMC, Intel and Samsung certification tables. SCADE's code generator holds TÜV SÜD certificates; the FDA qualified a tool built on HFSS/Mechanical. Limits: certification is shared with Cadence and Siemens, and Siemens Calibre (85%+) owns physical verification.
- **(b) Lead: real but uneven.** Big-3 hold >85% of EDA; Synopsys is the largest (~US$8bn CY2025 vs Cadence US$5.3bn). Cadence leads emulation. Synopsys IP fell 8% in FY25 while Cadence IP grew ~23%. Speed-ups are mostly company claims.
- **(c) Revenue model: mostly recurring, priced per copy/seat or core.** 9M FY26: time-based 40.5%, upfront 27.3%, maintenance/service 32.2%. Fees depend on "number of copies of each product licensed" (10-K). Usage-like pieces exist (US$1.9bn FSA, per-core/GPU-unit HPC licences, royalties ~1%); no consumption revenue disclosed.
- **(d) Financial strength: strong cash, weak ROIC.** Debt cut from US$13.5bn to US$10.0bn (Jul 2026); net debt ~US$6.4bn, ~2.4x gross to non-GAAP operating income vs a <2x target (my calc). ROIC ~9% non-GAAP, ~2% GAAP (my calc) on US$38bn goodwill and intangibles. No customer >10% in FY25 (one at 12.6% in FY24). China down from 16.1% (FY24) to 9.9% (9M FY26).
- **(e) Resilience: absorbed three shocks.** The 2025 BIS licence episode; the FY25 IP slump (Q4 FY25 IP margin 13.8%; +11% growth by Q3 FY26); Kimi K3 (17 Jul 2026: SNPS -7.9%, CDNS -9.5%; 45nm simulation, no silicon or sign-off). Most exposed in a 2028 pause: upfront IP and hardware.
- **Transitions: funded, monetisation unproven.** R&D US$2.48bn (35%) in FY25. Agents GA end-2026; "subscription and consumption" pricing; an outcome-based revenue share with OpenAI; >20 GPU-enabled products; US$2bn NVIDIA stake; 6 MCP servers. Rigorous solvers still certify.
- **Growth prior to 2029: guidance supports it.** Against: Futurum says FY27 relies on classic EDA; organic EDA +8.5% in Q3 FY26; the 10-Q flags AI disruption to "business models".

## (a) Position: does it own a "judge" or a hard constraint, or fill a gap?

### Takeaway
Most of the revenue-critical engines are sign-off judges. Their position rests on foundry certification and customer convention, and outside data confirm dominant shares in timing sign-off and power integrity. The weak spots are physical verification (Siemens) and the gap-filler layers (copilots, GUIs, pre/post-processing, optimisation wrappers), which AI agents can bypass.

### Cited Findings
- **Timing, synthesis and simulation shares.** PrimeTime has "90%+ share in signoff timing analysis" and Design Compiler 84-85% of synthesis. Siemens Calibre has 85%+ of physical verification. In functional simulation VCS has 45-50% vs Cadence Xcelium 40-45%; in emulation Cadence Palladium has 55-60% vs ZeBu 35-40%. SemiAnalysis adds that "Foundries mandate these signoff and verification tools for tape-out" ([SemiAnalysis EDA Market Primer, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)).
- **SAMR shares (2023 data), as summarised from the decision of 14 Jul 2025.** Ansys global shares: gate-level power integrity 55-60%, transistor-level power integrity 40-45%, electromagnetic simulation 45-50%, "structure and thermal analysis" 70-75%, "parasitic analysis" 70-75% (Synopsys 0-5%), gate-level security analysis 95-100%. Optical software: Synopsys 25-30% plus Ansys 40-45%. Photonic software: Synopsys 15-20% plus Ansys 45-50% ([IThome summary of the SAMR notice, Jul 2025](https://www.ithome.com/0/867/981.htm); the figures are a translation and were not checked against samr.gov.cn).
- **SAMR's must-supply list.** Concerns covered "certain EDA software markets" and design IP; supply to Chinese customers must be fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory for parasitic analysis, transistor-level power integrity and power device analysis ([IThome](https://www.ithome.com/0/867/981.htm)).
- **TSMC certification tables (as of 10/01/2026).** Synopsys, Cadence and Siemens EDA are listed as the certified vendors for A14, A16, N2P and N3C. Categories include Timing, EMIR, DRC, LVS/LPE, RC and Simulators, plus a 3DFabric/3Dblox table. Per-tool check marks could not be extracted, and Ansys no longer appears separately ([TSMC OIP EDA Alliance](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/oip/eda_alliance)).
- **Synopsys's own TSMC certification claims.**
  - RedHawk-SC and Totem are "certified for the latest TSMC N3C, N3P, N2P, and A16" releases. HFSS-IC Pro is certified for SoC EM extraction on N5/N3P, and PathFinder-SC "newly certified" on N2P ([Synopsys, 24 Sep 2025](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-09-24-Synopsys-Collaborates-with-TSMC-to-Enable-2D-and-3D-Design-Solutions)).
  - RedHawk-SC, Totem and HFSS-IC Pro are expanding from A16 to A14. IC Validator AI-assisted verification enablement is still "on-going" ([22 Apr 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-04-22-Synopsys-Partners-with-TSMC-to-Power-Next-Generation-AI-Systems-with-Silicon-Proven-IP-and-Certified-EDA-Flows)).
  - "A14 certification across digital, analog flows" ([23 Sep 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-23-Synopsys-and-TSMC-Partner-to-Accelerate-AI-Systems-Innovation-with-Agentic-AI-and-Advanced-Design); company claim, though the TSMC executive in the release refers to "certified EDA solutions").
- **Intel and Samsung.** Intel's Shawn Han (SVP Foundry Services) cites "AI-driven, certified flows and IP" on Intel 14A, building on certified 18A/18A-P flows ([27 Jul 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-07-27-Synopsys-and-Intel-Foundry-Fast-Track-Customer-Readiness-from-Silicon-to-Systems-on-Intel-14A)). Samsung Foundry: Totem-SC "newly certified" for EM/IR on second-generation 2nm-class and 4nm-class processes ([28 May 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-05-28-Synopsys-Advances-Power-and-Performance-for-AI-and-Multi-Die-Designs-on-Latest-Samsung-Foundry-Processes-at-SAFE-Forum-2026)).
- **The certificate is shared.** At TSMC's OIP forum (23 Sep 2026), Cadence's digital, signoff and custom flows were certified for A16/A14, and Siemens's Calibre nmDRC/nmLVS/xACT/PERC for A14. Futurum notes that "no validation regime currently exists for agent outputs" ([Futurum, 24 Sep 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/tsmcs-oip-forum-turns-agentic-chip-design-demos-into-production-flows/)).
- **Safety-critical software (SCADE).** The KCG code generator is "qualified as a development tool for DO-178B software up to Level A and DO-178C/DO-330 at TQL-1", "certified for IEC 61508 at SIL 3 and for EN 50716 at SIL 3/4", and "qualified for ISO 26262 software up to ASIL D" ([Ansys SCADE page, 2026](https://ansys.synopsys.com/products/embedded-software/ansys-scade-suite); company claim, no assessor named). Third-party evidence: a TÜV SÜD certificate for KCG 6.6 covers IEC 61508-3 SIL 3, EN 50128 SIL 3/4 and ISO 26262-8 ASIL D ([TÜV certificate copy, 2010s release](https://www.cadfem-cis.ru/media/tuv_certificate_kcg_6.6.pdf); older version, flagged).
- **medini analyze** applies guidelines for ISO 26262, IEC 61508, ARP 4761 and ISO 21448. The page names no tool certificate ([medini page](https://ansys.synopsys.com/products/safety-analysis/ansys-medini-analyze)).
- **Regulator use (FDA).** The FDA qualified a Medical Device Development Tool built on a "Visible Human female CAD model + Ansys HFSS/Mechanical FEM" for MRI heating near implants (qualified 30 Mar 2023) ([FDA MDDT list, current 10 Feb 2026](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-device-development-tools-mddt)).
- **Product scope.** PrimeTime, StarRC, IC Validator, PrimeSim, Proteus, QuantumATK, TCAD; Ansys Mechanical, LS-DYNA, HFSS, Fluent, Lumerical, RedHawk-SC, Totem-SC ([10-K, filed 22 Dec 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000028/snps-20251031.htm)).
- **Lithography.** TSMC and Synopsys went "into production" with cuLitho-accelerated Proteus ([NVIDIA, 18 Mar 2024](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/tsmc-synopsys-nvidia-culitho); older, flagged).

### Inferences
- The judges are PrimeTime, StarRC, RedHawk-SC/Totem, HFSS, SCADE KCG and, by convention, Fluent and Mechanical. Proteus is a production-certified manufacturing engine. IC Validator is a challenger, not the judge, because Calibre leads.
- The gap fillers are Synopsys.ai Copilot, Ansys GUIs and pre/post-processing (Discovery, meshing), optiSLang, ModelCenter and Engineering Copilot. Agents driving the engines through APIs could commoditise these layers.
- Foundry certification is a three-vendor gate, so the moat against Cadence and Siemens comes from customer convention and flow lock-in, not exclusivity. Against open-source and new entrants, the gate is close to absolute at advanced nodes.
- Provisional assessment (a): owns judges, with share confirmed by a regulator (SAMR) and an analyst (SemiAnalysis). Score high, with a weakness in physical verification.

### Gaps
- No per-tool TSMC check marks. No outside share data for StarRC, PrimeSim/HSPICE, Sentaurus, Proteus, LS-DYNA or Fluent. No Rapidus certification evidence (search budget exhausted). The current TÜV certificate for the latest KCG version was not seen. The SAMR figures come from the IThome translation.

## (b) Lead: outside evidence of a technical and commercial lead

### Takeaway
Third parties confirm scale leadership and dominance in specific sign-off niches. Evidence on technical leadership (independent benchmarks) is thin, and in several areas Cadence matches or leads.

### Cited Findings
- **Vendor revenue and share.** Synopsys ~US$8bn CY2025 incl. Ansys; Cadence US$5.30bn; Siemens EDA US$2.2-2.5bn. The Big-3 hold >85%, "no vendor outside the Big-3 holds more than 5% in any core EDA category", and shares have been "roughly stable for a decade". Cadence Innovus gained 10-15 points against ICC2 in 2015-20 ([SemiAnalysis, 21 May 2026](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)).
- **Other estimates.** Synopsys ~31%, Cadence ~30%, Siemens ~13% of a ~US$20.78bn EDA market in 2026 ([Dr. Web, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.drweb.de/ki-chipdesign-eda-cadence-synopsys/); secondary, method unstated). A paid report gives 25-30% / 20-25% / 10-15% (Big-3 only 60-65%) ([MarketResearch.com teaser](https://marketresearch.com/Prof-Research-v4036/Electronic-Design-Automation-EDA-Global-39562423); low reliability).
- **CAE.** Five players (Ansys, Dassault, MathWorks, Siemens, Keysight) hold 50-55% ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/ResearchInsight/computer-aided-engineering-market.asp); paid-report summary). No Cambashi or CIMdata vendor shares were found.
- **IP.** Synopsys IP US$1.7bn vs Cadence IP US$0.7bn+ ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer)). Company claims, unconfirmed: "more than 95% of PCIe 7 opportunities", 25 LPDDR6 wins year to date, die-to-die revenue doubling ([Q3 FY26 call, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.webull.com/news/15497781661541376)). Contrary evidence: Synopsys IP fell 8.1% in FY25 and 1% in 9M FY26 ([10-Q Q3 FY26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)), while Cadence IP grew ~23% "this year" ([TIKR, Jul 2026](https://www.tikr.com/blog/cadence-stock-fell-9-in-a-day-on-a-chinese-ai-chip-demo-heres-where-the-stock-could-go-in-2026)).
- **Qualifications at leading customers** (company releases with partner quotes):
  - Multiphysics Fusion, available 17 Jun 2026, is used or validated by NVIDIA, Cisco Silicon One, MediaTek and Samsung Foundry. Claimed gains: up to 3x faster timing sign-off and 10x faster closure ([17 Jun 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-06-17-Synopsys-Announces-Availability-of-the-First-Wave-of-Multiphysics-Fusion-Solutions)).
  - Honda: "34x faster computation and 38x cost reduction using four GB200 GPUs" in Fluent ([GTC, 16 Mar 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-03-16-Synopsys-Showcases-NVIDIA-Partnership-Impact-and-Ecosystem-Innovation-at-GTC-2026)).
  - Infineon: 12x on Sentaurus Device; imec: up to 40x on S-Litho ([GTC Berlin, 21 Oct 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-10-21-Synopsys-at-NVIDIA-GTC-Berlin-Delivering-Engineering-Precision-with-Speed)). All unconfirmed by third parties.
- **Industry context.** ESD Alliance Q2 2026: industry revenue US$5.71bn (+12.2%; four-quarter moving average +11.2%). By category: IC physical design and verification +16.6%, CAE +7.7%, semiconductor IP +20.4%, PCB/MCM -15.4%. APAC +22.4%. Industry headcount 71,396 (-1.6%) ([ESD Alliance via PR Newswire, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.webull.com/news/15775266363616256)).

### Inferences
- Synopsys's lead is in breadth (EDA + S&A + IP) and in the timing and power-integrity judges. Cadence is at parity or ahead in custom/analog, emulation and recent IP momentum. Siemens leads physical verification.
- Provisional assessment (b): confirmed leader in its core sign-off niches and the largest vendor overall. The lead is not uniform, and technical claims rely on company data.

### Gaps
- No independent benchmark of Synopsys vs Cadence vs Siemens sign-off accuracy. No vendor-level CAE shares after the Ansys deal. Ansys's organic growth in FY26 is not disclosed (only an implied figure; see (c)).

## (c) Revenue model: labour, seats, hardware vs recurring or usage-scaled

### Takeaway
About three-quarters or more of revenue recurs, but the pricing unit is mostly the licensed copy (seat or network licence) or the CPU/GPU core. Usage-scaled revenue (tokens/FSA drawdowns, HPC/GCU, elastic units, royalties) exists but is a minority or undisclosed. Services (labour) are small. Hardware is meaningful but not disclosed.

### Cited Findings
- **FY25 (year to 31 Oct 2025).** Revenue US$7,054.2M (+15%). By type: time-based 49%, upfront 29%, maintenance and service 22%. By product group: EDA 62.0%, Design IP 24.8%, Ansys 10.7% (from 17 Jul), Other 2.5% ([FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000028/snps-20251031.htm)).
- **9M FY26 (to 31 Jul 2026).** Revenue US$7,161.6M. By type: time-based US$2,900.0M (40.5%), upfront US$1,953.0M (27.3%), maintenance and service US$2,308.6M (32.2%). By product group: EDA 49.5%, Ansys 31.4% (about US$2.25bn), Design IP 18.6%, Other 0.5%. Q3 alone: Ansys 28.7%, about US$711M ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm); [Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000119312526368620/d157153dex991.htm)).
- **Seat and copy pricing.** "License fees depend on the type of license, product mix, and number of copies of each product licensed." "The majority of licenses to our EDA products and Ansys semiconductor products are network licenses" ([FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000028/snps-20251031.htm)). Revenue by geography follows "where individual 'seats' or licenses... are located" ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).
- **Revenue recognition.** IP, hardware and S&A licences are recognised upfront; Ansys time-based subscriptions are booked as "upfront products revenue", so "upfront" includes renewing leases. Services use "costs incurred or hours expended" ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).
- **Backlog.** US$10.9bn at 31 Jul 2026, including US$1.9bn of non-cancellable FSA commitments (customers pick products later). 49% of backlog excluding FSA is due within 12 months. It was US$11.4bn at 31 Oct 2025, when 45% was due within 12 months ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm); [FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000028/snps-20251031.htm)).
- **Royalties.** US$89.8M in 9M FY26 (~1.3% of revenue) ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).
- **Ansys before the merger (CY2024, revenue US$2,544.8M).** Subscription leases US$948.8M (37%), perpetual US$315.1M (12%), maintenance US$1,209.2M (48%), service US$71.7M (3%). Customers buy "a certain number of named users" or "elastic units, which enable the use of any supported product at any time until their licensed volume is met". 24.8% of sales went through the indirect channel and no customer exceeded 5% ([Ansys FY24 10-K, filed 19 Feb 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1013462/000101346225000009/anss-20241231.htm)).
- **Ansys HPC metering (2026 R1).** Most products include four CPU cores. Above that, HPC licences are charged per core, and GPU solvers "per GPU card or per GCU" (an NVIDIA SM or AMD compute unit), treated like cores. Fluent includes 40 GCUs, then charges 1 HPC licence per extra GCU; HFSS uses 8 licences per GPU ([Ansys licensing help v261](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v261/en/licensing/HPC_detail.html)).
- **Hardware.** Q3 FY26 was "another record quarter for hardware-assisted verification", with 12 new and 66 repeat customers. Hardware revenue is not disclosed ([Q3 call](https://www.webull.com/news/15497781661541376)). Inventories rose from US$365M to US$479M (Oct 2025 to Jul 2026) ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).
- **Industry pricing.** Synopsys and Cadence earn 70-83% of revenue from time-based or subscription contracts. Licences are sold as seats, token pools ("Tokens decouple licensing from individual seats") and multi-year bundles. Synopsys "reported about 20% revenue uplift on AI-enhanced renewals" ([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/eda-market-primer), as reported; not confirmed in filings).
- **Goldman Sachs estimates** (via a secondary source): an EDA seat costs ~US$90-100k a year today; a copilot could price at ~US$25k per engineer; autonomous agents at ~US$50k each ([Tech Fund, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.techinvestments.io/p/the-eda-crash-and-the-massive-ai)).

### Inferences
- My estimate: about 75-85% of revenue recurs (time-based, most maintenance, Ansys lease renewals). Labour-hour revenue is small (Ansys services 3%; Synopsys services undisclosed). Upfront IP and hardware, together roughly 20-25% of revenue, are the cyclical part (my estimate).
- The pricing unit is still the copy, seat or core. FSA/token pools and HPC/GCU metering already scale with compute use, which is a base for consumption pricing.
- Provisional assessment (c): recurring but seat- and core-denominated. Moderate risk if agents cut human seats before consumption pricing is proven.

### Gaps
- No split of time-based revenue by seat vs token; no hardware or services revenue; no Ansys organic growth figure. A rough comparison: FY26 Ansys guide of US$2.98bn (including a US$60M accounting change) vs CY2024's US$2.545bn implies roughly 7-8% a year (my calc; the periods do not match).

## (d) Financial strength

### Takeaway
High non-GAAP margins and strong FCF, with deleveraging ahead of the term-loan schedule. Return on invested capital is low after the US$34.9bn Ansys deal, and GAAP margins are depressed by ~US$1.4-1.5bn a year of amortisation. Concentration by customer and country has fallen.

- **Ansys price.** Aggregate purchase consideration ~US$34.9bn (cash US$17.6bn, stock US$17.1bn), closed 17 Jul 2025. At 31 Jul 2026 goodwill was US$26.83bn and net intangibles US$11.46bn, against equity of US$31.15bn ([FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000028/snps-20251031.htm); [10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).

OCF US$1,518.6M, capex US$169.5M, FCF ~US$1.35bn (my calc) ([Q4 FY25 release, 10 Dec 2025](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-12-10-Synopsys-Posts-Financial-Results-for-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025)).
- **Q3 FY26.** Revenue US$2,476.8M (+42%). Design Automation US$2,003.0M (+53%), IP US$473.8M (+11%). GAAP operating margin 14.4%, non-GAAP 41.6%; segment margins DA 45.2%, IP 26.5%. FCF US$746M. Organic EDA growth 8.5% against a 16% comparison ([Q3 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000119312526368620/d157153dex991.htm); [call](https://www.webull.com/news/15497781661541376)).

## (e) Resilience: AI scenarios, industry cycle, export controls, execution

### Takeaway
Time-based EDA and the US$10.9bn backlog cushion a downturn; upfront IP, hardware and Ansys's industrial exposure transmit one. China is down to ~10%, but the rules stay fluid.

### Cited Findings
- **Cycle signals.** "continued strength in the artificial intelligence and high-performance computing sectors" while "industrial, automotive and consumer electronics have experienced more modest growth". "The negative impact of these events... may be deferred due to our business model" ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).
- **2025 Design IP shortfall.** Causes cited: "China export control restrictions," "weaker than expected demand from a major foundry customer," and "roadmap and resource decisions that did not yield their intended results" ([FY25 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000028/snps-20251031.htm)). Management said IP "hit bottom" in Q1 FY26 ([Q2 call summary, 27-28 May 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/synopsys-inc-snps-q2-2026-050106952.html)). IP grew 11% in Q3 FY26. Shareholder class actions over Design IP statements were filed from 31 Oct 2025 ([10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).
- **Export controls, 2025.** Synopsys received a BIS letter on 29 May 2025 and suspended guidance ([statement](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-05-29-Synopsys-Issues-Statement-in-Connection-with-BIS-Letter)). The restrictions were rescinded "effective immediately" on 2 Jul 2025 ([statement](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-07-02-Synopsys-Issues-Statement-in-Connection-to-the-Lifting-of-Recent-U-S-Export-Restrictions-Related-to-China)). The 10-Q says they "negatively impacted our business in China, including in our Design IP segment"; ECAD controls on GAAFET design and advanced packaging remain, and the 10-K notes BIS subpoenas on certain Chinese entities.
- **Export controls, 2026.** No new EDA-specific rule was found. BIS's 50% Affiliates Rule remains suspended until 10 Jan 2027 ([Reed Smith, 6 Nov 2026](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/trade-compliance-resource-hub/5272/bis-further-suspends-affiliates-rule-until-january-2027/)).
- **SAMR conditions (14 Jul 2025).** Divest Optical Solutions Group and PowerArtist. Honour existing contracts, prices and service levels. Supply Chinese customers on fair, reasonable and non-discriminatory terms. No bundling or tying. Keep supporting industry-standard formats. Maintain and renew interoperability agreements, and sign new ones with third-party EDA vendors when Chinese customers support the request. Conditions 3-8 run 10 years with annual reporting ([IThome](https://www.ithome.com/0/867/981.htm); [Schnitger, 14 Jul 2025](https://schnitgercorp.com/2025/07/14/china-finally-grants-approval-for-synopsysansys/)). Caixin dates the approval 15 Jul and counts "eight conditions" ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2025-07-15/china-approves-synopsysansys-merger-with-eight-conditions-amid-post-sanctions-rebound-102341171.html)).
- **Policy and competition risk.** China "has formed government-backed investment funds as it seeks to build independent EDA capabilities". Customers may seek "alternatives from foreign competitors or open-source solutions" ([10-Q risk factors](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000883241/000088324126000025/snps-20260731.htm)).
- **Divestitures.**
  - Optical Solutions Group and PowerArtist sold to Keysight, closing on or about 17 Oct 2025; terms undisclosed ([10 Oct 2025](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-10-10-Synopsys-Receives-Final-Regulatory-Approval-to-Close-Planned-Divestitures-of-Synopsys-Optical-Solutions-Group-and-Ansys-PowerArtist)). FY25 gain on divestitures US$548.9M; ~US$110M of FY26 revenue lost.
  - Processor IP (ARC, ASIP tools) sold to GlobalFoundries on 1 Jun 2026 for US$443.3M cash, pre-tax gain US$425.4M, ~US$40M of FY26 revenue lost (10-Q; [14 Jan 2026](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-01-14-Synopsys-Enters-Definitive-Agreement-with-GlobalFoundries-For-Sale-of-Processor-IP-Solutions-Business)).
- **Integration.** The US$400M cost-synergy target was pulled forward to FY27. Revenue synergies: >US$100M run-rate exiting FY27 and US$400M by FY29 (company claim) ([Futurum, 2 Oct 2026](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/synopsys-investor-day-2026-turns-eda-into-a-royalty-and-ai-model-revenue-share-business/)). A change in accounting for Ansys channel partners adds ~US$60M to FY26 revenue ([Q2 release](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-05-27-Synopsys-Posts-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-Year-2026)).

### Inferences
- **(1) Steady growth (~55%).** Fits the company's ~15% model: more AI and custom-silicon starts, multi-die multiphysics, IP royalties from 2028. Watch seat compression.
- **(2) Financing-led pause around 2028 (~25%).** IP (upfront), hardware (upfront) and hyperscaler AOIP royalties would fall first; FY25 showed IP can drop ~8% with margins halving. Time-based EDA and backlog would slow the hit. Debt is serviceable: US$1bn maturities in 2027 and 2028 against ~US$3bn of FCF.
- **(3) Faster acceleration (~20%).** Upside from consumption and agent pricing and GPU simulation. It also speeds open/AI substitution at mature nodes.
- **Cycle position.** IP is post-trough, EDA mid-cycle, S&A tied to the slower industrial and automotive cycle.
- Provisional assessment (e): resilient core with a cyclical edge (~25% upfront). China and export-control risk is reduced but not removed.

### Gaps
- No disclosed sensitivity of revenue to customers' R&D budgets. China ex-Ansys trend for FY26 not disclosed. A claimed "-18% China in Q2 FY26" figure in one search snippet conflicts with the 10-Q (H1 FY26 China +36% including Ansys) and is not used.

## Path forward: six transitions (said vs done)

### Takeaway
Synopsys has shipped agents, GPU ports and MCP servers and signed NVIDIA, OpenAI and Amazon deals. No consumption, outcome-based or royalty claim is yet visible in reported revenue.

### Cited Findings
- **T1. Seats to use or results.**
  - Said: monetise via "a combination of subscription and consumption-based models across tools, agents and platform" (Investor Day, 30 Sep 2026). "subscription of our agents, subscription of our workflow as well as a consumption measure"; "the need is for more licenses" (Ghazi, [Q3 call](https://www.webull.com/news/15497781661541376)).
  - Done: GPT-Synopsys charges tool subscriptions for training, subscription and consumption fees in use, and an outcome-based revenue share paid when power, performance and area beat the customer's own result. OpenAI is investing "hundreds of millions" ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/synopsys-investor-day-2026-turns-eda-into-a-royalty-and-ai-model-revenue-share-business/); [joint release](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-30-OpenAI-and-Synopsys-Announce-GPT-Synopsys-Frontier-Intelligence-to-Revolutionize-Chip-Design)). The Amazon deal (US$1bn+) covers licence fees only; royalties start ~14 months out, none in FY27; Synopsys says AOIP reaches US$1bn of revenue by 2030 (company claim) ([Amazon release](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-30-Synopsys-and-Amazon-Announce-Strategic,-Multi-year-IP-Agreement-for-Custom-Silicon-Collaboration-Also-Extends-to-Cloud-and-AI-Powered-Engineering)).
  - Futurum: outcome pricing "has a thin track record in software" and is "a thesis until a customer other than OpenAI pays for it" ([Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/synopsys-investor-day-2026-turns-eda-into-a-royalty-and-ai-model-revenue-share-business/)). No price list has been disclosed.

  - Done, with counts: "more than 20 GPU-enabled EDA and multiphysics products" and support for 50 CUDA-X libraries (GTC Berlin, 21 Oct 2026). Sentaurus 12x. TÜV and DO-330 for SCADE and FDA MDDT for HFSS/Mechanical, as in (a). No TSMC validation regime for agent outputs yet ([Futurum OIP](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/tsmcs-oip-forum-turns-agentic-chip-design-demos-into-production-flows/)).

### Inferences
- **T1.** Funded and in motion; proof is due 2027-28. It must go right that agent and consumption revenue per customer exceeds lost seats. Futurum says FY27 depends on classic EDA, not on new streams.
- **T2.** Credible: broad ports, GCU metering preserves value. GPU revenue share is unknown, and the timing, extraction and physical-verification judges are not shown on GPUs.
- **T3.** Done technically. Risk: open MCP and APIs also let agents drive rivals' or open tools, and SAMR bans bundling in China.
- **T4.** Open tools are far from advanced-node sign-off (PDK access, certification), so the threat sits at mature nodes, in China and in education.
- **T5.** Favours incumbents: learned models accelerate while conventional solvers certify.
- **T6.** Strong today; no regime yet for AI outputs.
- Provisional: a coherent, funded path. The weakest links are T1 monetisation and T3 disintermediation.

### Gaps
- No pricing for AgentEngineer, SimAI or Copilot. No GPU share of revenue. No independent K3 replication or published weights check. No explicit Synopsys statement on K3 was found.

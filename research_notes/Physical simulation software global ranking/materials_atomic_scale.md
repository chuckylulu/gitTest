# Materials and atomic-scale simulation worldwide: software, companies, trust boundary, path forward (as of 12 November 2026)

Scope: quantum chemistry/DFT, molecular dynamics, machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs), CALPHAD, AI materials-discovery platforms. Evidence gathered 12 Nov 2026. "Company claim, unconfirmed" = vendor statement no outside party has confirmed. Listed firms: business facts only (no share prices or multiples). Method note: the shared WebSearch budget, Exa and Firecrawl credits all ran out during the task; later facts come from direct fetches of known primary URLs, so some items stay unverified (see "Could not verify").

**Summary for the report writer**
- Shape: a small, fragmented paid market built around a few de facto "judges": VASP for solid-state DFT (about 20% of NERSC computing cycles, the top code; 2018 data), Schrödinger's FEP+ for binding free energy in pharma, Thermo-Calc/CompuTherm CALPHAD databases for alloys, plus molecular quantum chemistry (Gaussian, Q-Chem). Around them sit free codes (LAMMPS, GROMACS, Quantum ESPRESSO, CP2K) and, since 2024, open-weight universal MLIPs that now top the independent leaderboard. Schrödinger is the only sizeable listed pure play (FY2025 revenue $255.9M; software $199.5M). Simulations Plus went private (Altaris, closed 6 Oct 2026) and is being combined with Chemical Computing Group.
- Capital has moved to AI-native private firms: CuspAI ($450M Series B at $2.6B, Jul 2026), Lila and Periodic Labs (reported, unannounced talks at $8.5B pre-money and $7.5B), Orbital Industries ($50M; now sells data-centre cooling hardware). China is strong in open models (DPA-4.1 from a DP Technology-linked consortium is #1 on Matbench Discovery, 31 Oct 2026). Korea has Samsung's model at #5. Japan's Matlantis is the main paid universal potential (>150 customers, company claim). Taiwan, India and Israel have no notable vendors (thin).
- Trust boundary: simulation decides what to make next, not what is true. FEP ranks compounds (accuracy close to assay reproducibility in a large Schrödinger-authored study), but assays decide. No regulator accepts atomistic simulation as filing evidence; the regulator-accepted modelling is pharmacometric (ICH M15 finalised by FDA June 2026). A computationally designed alloy (Ferrium S53) still needed AMS and MMPDS test data. "ML proposes, DFT certifies" is standard in materials screening, but experiments and novelty checks have overturned DFT-certified claims (A-Lab: 42 of 43 compounds with significant issues; MatterGen's TaCr2O6 was already known).
- arXiv 2605.08988 is real (Cardiff, v2 22 Oct 2026). Out-of-distribution force errors are 1-19x in-distribution depending on task; energy errors are up to ~900x. Scope: small H/C/N/O molecules. "Often an order of magnitude" is fair; a uniform "ten times" is not.
- Open vs paid: no revenue collapse yet (Schrödinger 2026 ACV guidance +12-17%; Certara software +11% in Q3 2026), but open FEP (OpenFE, 15 pharma partners) and open MLIPs erode the gap-filler case for paid tools. VASP now bans training AI on its code.
- Path forward favours owners of reference outputs that others train on (VASP, CALPHAD databases, FEP+), if they adopt GPUs (falling FP64 is a risk for DFT) and agents (Schrödinger Bunsen, Matlantis, VASP's AI hire). Paid universal potentials and seat-priced GUIs are most exposed.

## 1. Map: who sells what, ownership, size, funding (all regions)

### Takeaway
The paid core is a few hundred million dollars of software revenue, dominated by Schrödinger plus small private specialists (VASP, Thermo-Calc, Gaussian, Q-Chem, SCM, Materials Design, CompuTherm, QuesTek). The 2025-26 money and momentum sit with big-tech open models and heavily funded AI-native "discovery companies". These sell materials or IP more than software.

### Inferences
- Measured paid incumbent software revenue (Schrödinger ~$200M, Thermo-Calc ~EUR 12M, Certara software ~$190M/yr adjacent) is smaller than the capital raised by AI-native entrants in 2025-26 (CuspAI alone >$670M). Capital, not revenue, now sets the pace of the segment.
- Two business types are being lumped together. Tool vendors sell software (Schrödinger, VASP, Thermo-Calc, Matlantis); discovery companies sell materials, hardware or IP (CuspAI, Orbital, Periodic, Lila, Radical, XtalPi's AI4S arm). The latter resemble drug-discovery partnerships, not software firms.
- The ecosystem is splitting: China's open models lead the benchmark while Meta's UMA licence excludes China.

## 2. Boundary of trust: what simulation is accepted for, and what still needs an experiment

### Takeaway
Simulation is decisive for internal, reversible choices (which compound to make, which composition to melt, which candidate to screen). It is not accepted as final evidence where a third party bears the risk (drug approval, flight-critical allowables, claims of new materials). There, assays, tests and synthesis remain the judge.

### Cited Findings
- FEP in lead optimisation: with careful structure preparation, FEP accuracy can be "comparable to experimental reproducibility"; based on 13,732 relative binding free energy calculations with FEP+ (Ross et al., Schrödinger authors, Communications Chemistry, Oct 2023; older) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/79d1308e170847339a53931494a3e639); [ChemRxiv](https://www.cambridge.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/65281c688bab5d205536cc7b). OpenFE ran an industry benchmark with 15 pharma partners on blinded data in their own clusters: public set 59 systems/876 ligands, private set 37 systems/864 ligands. FEP+ showed "superior low error performance". OpenFE was "competitive" at ranking but with more high-error outliers, and private sets showed "a noticeable step down in accuracy" (OMSF, 6 May 2025) — [OMSF](https://omsf.substack.com/p/the-free-energy-of-everything-benchmarking-openfe). Implication from the design: both are judged against assay data.
- AI affinity models vs FEP: Boltz-2 claims to "approach" FEP at ≥1000x lower cost (bioRxiv, 18 Jun 2025) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.14.659707v1). An independent UCL test against the ESMACS physics method found weak-to-moderate correlation (r = 0.24 on 16,780 compounds; r = 0.45 on 21,702), with only 1-2 of the top-100 compounds overlapping (arXiv 2603.05532, Mar 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05532).
- Regulators: ICH M15 (model-informed drug development) became final FDA guidance in June 2026 (docket FDA-2024-D-5580). It sets a harmonised framework for assessing MIDD evidence (pharmacometrics/PBPK, not atomistic) — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/m15-general-principles-model-informed-drug-development). FDA's AI draft guidance (Jan 2025) uses a risk-based credibility framework tied to "context of use" — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-use-artificial-intelligence-support-regulatory-decision-making-drug-and-biological). FDA–EMA "Guiding Principles of Good AI Practice in Drug Development" followed in Jan 2026 — [FDA CDER](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/center-drug-evaluation-and-research-cder/artificial-intelligence-drug-development). FDA's 10 Apr 2025 roadmap encourages AI computational toxicity models in INDs to "reduce, refine, or potentially replace" animal tests; it does not say models alone suffice — [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-announces-plan-phase-out-animal-testing-requirement-monoclonal-antibodies-and-other-drugs).
- CALPHAD/ICME in alloy qualification: QuesTek's Ferrium S53 was designed computationally, yet qualified through AMS 5922 and MMPDS-05 (2008). Design allowables were estimated with DARPA "Accelerated Insertion of Materials" from limited test data — [AeroMat 2006](https://asm.confex.com/asm/aero06/techprogram/paper_13923.htm). Milestones: material approval Nov 2009, component approval Aug 2010, first flight Dec 2010 on a T-38 landing-gear piston — [CHiMaD 2014 slides](https://chimad.northwestern.edu/docs/SRG2014/SRG2014_Grabowski.pdf). ASM calls it the first ICME-designed material with AMS and MMPDS qualification (Oct 2021) — [ASM](https://static.asminternational.org/amp/202110/23/). Older sources, but still the reference case.
- Materials screening: GNoME used DFT to verify candidates; 736 of its structures were made independently — [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/millions-of-new-materials-discovered-with-deep-learning/). The A-Lab critique (Leeman et al., PRX Energy 3, 011002, 2024) found significant issues in 42 of 43 claimed materials; about two-thirds were likely known compositionally disordered phases — [Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article); [PRX Energy](https://journals.aps.org/prxenergy/abstract/10.1103/PRXEnergy.3.011002). MatterGen's synthesised "new" TaCr2O6 matches a compound known since ~1971-72 that was in its training data (Juelsholt, Materials Horizons 2026) — [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2026/mh/d6mh00268d).
- Learned potentials checked against experiment before claims: a Columbia–Schrödinger Nature paper (14 Oct 2026) combined MLFF molecular dynamics with cryogenic solid-state NMR. It found up to 40% of Li+ in multi-ion clusters in LiPF6/EC electrolytes (secondary coverage) — [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/energy/research-reveals-unexpected-lithium-ion-clusters); [XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/lithium-ion-clusters-battery-electrolyte-nature/). MatterSim's TaP was measured experimentally — [MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/advancing-ai-for-materials-with-mattersim-experimental-synthesis-faster-simulation-and-multi-task-models/).

### Inferences
- No regulator or standards body was found to accept atomistic or MLIP results as primary evidence. Regulator-accepted modelling (PBPK in M15) sits outside this segment; Simulations Plus and Certara are the adjacent "judges".
- The trust ladder in practice: (1) MLIP/generative model; (2) DFT check; (3) synthesis and characterisation; (4) novelty check against databases; (5) for products, standards testing. AI shortens steps 1-2 and pre-filters step 3; steps 3-5 remain the judge.

### Gaps
- No primary text of the MMPDS rules or a recent (2025-26) CALPHAD-based qualification case was retrieved. Regulator positions on atomistic data in Japan, China and Korea were not checked.

## 3. Accuracy evidence: arXiv 2605.08988, benchmarks, critiques, "propose and certify"

### Takeaway
arXiv 2605.08988 broadly supports the prior: out-of-distribution errors are "often an order of magnitude" higher than in-distribution. But the effect is task-dependent and confined to small organic molecules. Leaderboards measure agreement with DFT, not experiment.

### Cited Findings
- arXiv 2605.08988, "Benchmarking Compositional Generalisation for Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials" (Nourollah, Khalid, Leoni, Schockaert; Cardiff; v1 9 May 2026, v2 22 Oct 2026). GMD benchmark: small H/C/N/O molecules in vacuum with PBE/def2-TZVP+D3BJ labels. Abstract: OOD errors are "often an order of magnitude higher", even for foundation models — [abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08988). Appendix force-error ratios (OOD/ID) for MACE: chain extension 1.8x, fusion 18.9x, duplication 8.0x, combination 2.5x. Energy ratios: up to 911x for MACE (duplication) and ~23,000x for NequIP. UMA-Small force ratios are ~1x on three tasks, but its ID force error (0.015-0.099 eV/Å) is several times MACE's (0.003-0.005). Fine-tuning lowers ID error but does not close the gap. More chain-length data fixes length gaps; worked examples do not fix compositional gaps. Internal inconsistency: the conclusions say "three orders of magnitude" for energy where the results show about two for fusion — [HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08988v2).
- Matbench Discovery (fetched 12 Nov 2026): #1 DPA-4.1-Max-OAM-PFT (CPS 0.913, F1 0.935, DAF 6.09; added 31 Oct 2026); #2 Kairos Prophet; #4 ShanghaiTech; #5 Samsung; #6 MIT/Mirror Physics; #7 ICAMS; #8 EPFL PET-OAM-XL; #11 Meta eSEN-30M-OAM. The top 15 all train on MPtrj+OMat24+sAlex and link code and weights. The site says rankings are "not a complete assessment or an endorsement"; stability is scored against a DFT convex hull — [Matbench](https://matbench-discovery.materialsproject.org/). The DPA-4 developers claim training in ~1 day on one RTX 5090 (company claim, unconfirmed) — [DeepModeling](https://blogs.deepmodeling.com/DPA4_05_22_2026/).
- Systematic softening: M3GNet, CHGNet and MACE-MP-0 underpredict energy-surface curvature; fine-tuning with even one extra data point corrects it (Deng et al., May 2024; older) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07105).
- Validation in practice: MatterSim thermal conductivity sits within a ±50% band vs experiment; MatterSim-MT predicts BaTiO3 polarisation of 38 μC/cm² vs 26 measured — [MSR](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/advancing-ai-for-materials-with-mattersim-experimental-synthesis-faster-simulation-and-multi-task-models/). VASP's on-the-fly MLFF reports a per-step "spilling factor" error estimate (ML_IERR), so the DFT code decides when to fall back to DFT — [VASP changelog](https://vasp.at/wiki/index.php/Changelog). Orb reports no benefit from float64 over float32 — [GitHub](https://github.com/orbital-materials/orb-models).
- Critiques of discovery claims: A-Lab (42 of 43 problematic) and MatterGen (known compound), as in section 2.

### Inferences
- "A learned model proposes and a conventional solver certifies" describes standard practice (GNoME, Matbench workflows, MLFF fine-tuning on DFT, VASP on-the-fly MLFF, Schrödinger's Bunsen running physics engines). But DFT is not the final certifier for claims about the real world: the 2024-26 critiques show DFT-"certified" novelty failing experimental and database checks. Some learned models now sit inside the solver (Skala XC functional), blurring the line.
- Benchmark saturation on shared training data (all top-15 use the same three datasets) means leaderboard gains say little about OOD robustness. 2605.08988-style OOD tests matter more for buyers.

### Gaps
- Benchmarks of universal potentials on condensed-phase, charged or transition-metal OOD chemistry; any independent benchmark of Matlantis PFP; the Matbench Discovery paper text (not readable).

## 4. Open source and open models vs paid products; pricing pressure

### Takeaway
Free codes and open weights are near parity on benchmarks. Paid incumbents are still growing, though, because buyers pay for validated workflows, support, hosting and accountability. Direct price-cut evidence was not found. The clearest pressure falls on paid universal potentials and on the "gap-filler" layer.

### Cited Findings
- Open codes: GROMACS 2026.4 is current — [GROMACS](https://manual.gromacs.org/current/release-notes/index.html). CP2K now ships Microsoft's Skala — [Quantum Insider](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/08/24/microsofts-skala-ai-model-joins-cp2k-to-improve-quantum-mechanical-simulations/). OpenFE is MIT-licensed — [OpenFE](https://openfree.energy/). Even so, the single most-used materials code at NERSC is licence-paid VASP — [CUG 2023](https://cug.org/proceedings/cug2023_proceedings/includes/files/pap130s2-file1.pdf). Orb under Apache-2.0 — [GitHub](https://github.com/orbital-materials/orb-models). UMA allows commercial use, with geographic exclusions — [HF](https://huggingface.co/facebook/UMA). PET-MAD-1.6 is r2SCAN-trained over 102 elements (Mar/Sep 2026) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02089). - Incumbent demand: Schrödinger net dollar retention fell to 100% in 2025 (113% in 2024) and ACV grew only 4%. Its 2026 ACV guidance is +12-17%, with Q2 ACV +27% — [FY2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1490978/000149097826000009/sdgr-20251231xexx991.htm); [Q3 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001490978/000149097826000075/sdgr-20260930x8kxexx991.htm). - Hardware shift that favours MLIPs: NVIDIA Rubin native FP64 is ~33 TFLOPS, about equal to H100, while FP4 is 35 PFLOPS. ### Inferences
- Open models are displacing paid products at the potential layer and in academia. They are not (yet) displacing the reference-data owners: MLIPs are trained on DFT labels, so DFT demand shifts toward training-data generation (OMol25 used billions of core-hours).
- Falling FP64 per dollar on AI GPUs tilts work from DFT toward low-precision MLIPs. That is a long-run threat to per-calculation DFT revenue, and a reason VASP and QuantumATK are pursuing GPU and MLFF features.

### Gaps
- Matlantis, VASP, Gaussian and Schrödinger price lists or discounting data; usage statistics for open MLIPs in industry.

## 6. Revenue models across the segment, and moves to usage- or outcome-based pricing

### Takeaway
Usage or throughput pricing already exists (Schrödinger calculations, Rowan credits, Materials Square pay-as-you-go). Outcome pricing comes through milestones and royalties, or by selling products. Seat-and-capacity subscriptions (Matlantis) and one-off licences (VASP) persist at the reference layer.

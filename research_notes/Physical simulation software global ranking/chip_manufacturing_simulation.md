# Simulation for chip manufacturing and packaging (ex-mainland China): computational lithography, TCAD, virtual fabrication, package thermal/stress, metrology modelling; foundry sign-off; GPU and learned-model tests; specialists (as of 12 Nov 2026)

**Summary for the report writer.** (1) Map: merchant computational lithography (OPC/ILT/verification) is a three-vendor oligopoly (Synopsys Proteus, Siemens Calibre, ASML Brion/Tachyon) after Cadence sold its OPC line to Silvaco (Mar 2025, $11.5m). The only free third-party sizing (QYResearch teaser, Oct 2025) is ~$1.27bn (2024) with the top three >80%; low reliability. No public third-party TCAD shares: Synopsys leads, Silvaco (TCAD revenue $30.0m in 2025) is the only listed pure play. Virtual fabrication is captive to Lam (SEMulator3D) and Applied Materials (ACE+, Ginestra). No vendor discloses revenue for any of these niches. (2) Judge: TSMC's public tables (1 Oct 2026) list only Cadence, Synopsys and Siemens, now with a Thermal row (A16) and a 3DFabric static/transient thermal row; Ansys RedHawk-SC Electrothermal (Apr 2025), Cadence Celsius (Apr 2026) and Siemens Calibre 3DThermal (Sep 2026) are certified for A16. No stress/warpage certification found, and thermal sign-off for wafer-scale packages is unfinished (Futurum, Sep 2026). OPC, TCAD and virtual fab carry no public certification: the foundry runs them and the wafer is the judge. (3) GPUs: kernel speed-ups of 10-60x (company claims) shrink to a '20-50% improvement in cost effectiveness or cycle time' in NVIDIA's own June 2026 TSMC release. Siemens' Oct 2026 '10-17x' names no module, baseline, customer or availability: company claim, unconfirmed. GPU support is now table stakes for the three OPC vendors. (4) Learned models: evidence supports 'learned proposes or accelerates, physics certifies' (TSMC/Synopsys/NVIDIA 2024: final mask 'derived by traditional and physically rigorous methods'; Synopsys 2026 slides: 'rigorous validation'). No public case of a learned model as the final OPC check in volume. Against 'accuracy comes last': curvilinear masks spread only as qualification, inspection and metrology catch up (Micron, May 2026); 87% of eBeam Initiative luminaries expect a mostly curvilinear layer at leading mask shops within three years (Sep 2026). (5) Silvaco: 2025 revenue $63.1m (+6%), net loss $41.2m, China 20%; Q3 2026 revenue $16.3m (-13%) but TCAD bookings +89%; cash $22.0m after a $10m Micron convertible; Q4 guide $22m; Pesic family >59%. PDF Solutions Q3 2026 revenue $68.3m (+20%). (6) Export controls: EUV computational-lithography software controlled since 2020 (3D003); DUV computational lithography (3D993.c, Dec 2024) only towards listed entities; the May-July 2025 EDA licence episode (3D991/3E991) cut China deliveries for ~6 weeks. (7) Demand is rising where tools sit on AI constraints: packaging/thermal (Cadence SD&A +37% in Q2 2026) and high-NA/curvilinear masks (TSMC high-NA volume production 2030).

## 2. The judge: how foundries certify these tools

### Takeaway
There are two judges. For design-side sign-off (extraction, EM/IR, timing and, since A16, thermal) the foundry certifies named tools node by node, and only Cadence, Synopsys and Siemens appear on TSMC's public tables. For manufacturing-side tools (OPC/ILT, TCAD, virtual fab) the foundry is the customer and qualifies them internally against wafers; nothing is certified publicly.

### Cited Findings
- TSMC EDA Alliance tables 'As of 10/01/2026' (A14, A16, N2P, N3C) list only Cadence, Synopsys, Siemens EDA; rows APR, library characterisation, timing, power, EMIR, DRC, dummy fill, LVS/LPE, RC, custom design, Tx EMIR, Tx timing, simulators; A16 adds Thermal. A 3DFabric table ('Certified for Technology Requirement') includes 'Thermal - Static and Transient Analysis', IR drop, RCX and 'Hierarchical 3Dblox'. ### Inferences
- Thermal became a certified sign-off item at A16 (2025-2026) with three certified engines; stress and warpage are only 'validated' or 'enabled', so the judge for mechanical sign-off of 3D stacks is not yet set. That is an open slot, but it is likely to be filled by the same three vendors.
- For OPC/ILT the moat is wafer-calibrated recipes and models built up at each fab over years, not a public certificate; this keeps the three incumbents entrenched and makes newcomers (Silvaco OPC, start-ups) slow to qualify. Captive platforms (Samsung's OPC platform, equipment makers' simulators) shrink the merchant market further.

### Gaps
- TSMC's per-vendor check marks are not machine-readable; Samsung and Intel publish no tables; no public document describes how a foundry qualifies OPC/ILT software or which tool is its 'golden' lithography verification.

## 3. GPU transition: cuLitho at TSMC, Proteus, ASML, Siemens 10-17x, D2S

### Takeaway
GPU computational lithography is in production at TSMC and Samsung and every OPC vendor now claims a GPU port, but end-to-end gains reported by a foundry are far smaller than vendor kernel speed-ups, and the Siemens 10-17x figure lacks the details needed to test it.

### Inferences
- Vendor speed-ups of 10-60x on kernels translate into a 20-50% cost or cycle-time gain at the one foundry reporting end-to-end figures, because data handling, recipes and verification loops dominate. GPUs cut cost and turnaround but do not change who certifies, and the savings appear to accrue to the fab and NVIDIA rather than to the software vendor.
- Since all three OPC vendors now market GPU ports, GPUs are unlikely to reshuffle share; they make curvilinear full-chip ILT and high-NA corrections affordable, which raises compute per mask.

### Gaps
- No independent benchmark of Proteus, Calibre or Tachyon on GPUs; Siemens' baseline and customers; whether Proteus' 20x on B200 has been measured; ASML's GPU status.

## 4. Learned models: proposal vs certification ('accuracy comes last' test)

### Takeaway
In lithography the evidence supports the prior that learned models propose or accelerate while a calibrated physical model and wafer data certify. It contradicts 'accuracy comes last': here accuracy and qualification gate adoption, and learned components enter first where errors are cheap (hotspot screening, initial guesses, defect classification).

### Cited Findings
- TSMC/Synopsys/NVIDIA (18 Mar 2024): generative AI creates 'a near-perfect inverse mask', an additional 2x on top of cuLitho, but the final mask is 'derived by traditional and physically rigorous methods' — [NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/tsmc-synopsys-nvidia-culitho).
- Synopsys 2026 plenary: 'Trusted AI, Grounded in Physics: Human-accessible flows and rigorous simulation keep AI results trustworthy', with model calibration followed by 'Rigorous Validation'; agentic workflow splits a Correction Agent (OPC, ILT), a TCAD Agent (etch, electrical, CMP) and a Verification Agent (DRC, MRC, LRC) — [Synopsys slides](https://www.synopsys.com/content/dam/synopsys/silicon-engineering/documents/presentations/spie-puv-2026-plenary.pdf). A Synopsys invited paper combined a CNN with compact modelling for high-NA OPC (sub-1nm CD error claimed); a panel was titled 'AI/ML in Mask Synthesis: Hype vs. Reality'; no ML technique described as in volume production — [SemiEngineering (Synopsys-sponsored), 19 Mar 2026](https://semiengineering.com/exploring-the-frontiers-of-lithography-and-patterning-highlights-from-spie-advanced-lithography-patterning-2026/).
- Siemens (Aug 2026): 'neural network-assisted models raise accuracy... and cut OPC runtimes', 'speed improvements by two-thirds or more'; 'Monotonic machine learning lets teams optimize ILT hundreds of times faster'; supervised models used for 'hotspot prediction, root cause analysis and defect classification'; sign-off rests on 'deterministic engines' (company claims, no customer named) — [Siemens white paper](https://silicon-saxony.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/SiemensEDA_How-AI-is-transforming-IC-design-and-manufacturing.pdf). SPIE 2026 papers: monotonic ML for curvilinear OPC retargeting (with imec), ML-guided site selection, resist model calibration (with Samsung), curvilinear ILT (with Nanya); Siemens calls ML-guided curvilinear OPC 'deployable, industry-ready' — [Siemens, 9 Mar 2026](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/calibre/2026/03/09/siemens-eda-2026-at-spie-advanced-lithography/). 'AI does not replace them'; Calibre tools 'remain deterministic and highly validated' — [Siemens blog, 1 Sep 2026](https://blogs.sw.siemens.com/calibre/2026/09/01/manufacturing-intelligence-ai-in-the-fab/).
- Research: a generator proposes mask candidates refined by batched conventional ILT: ~2x throughput, >20% EPE gain on benchmarks, no production claim (Yang and Ren, 22 Feb 2026, DAC 2026) — [arXiv 2602.19027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19027). ML OPC compact models need different validation (contour accuracy, predictiveness, coverage) — [SPIE 2021](https://biomedicaloptics.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/11614/116140R/Validation-of-machine-learning-OPC-compact-models-for-advanced-manufacturing/10.1117/12.2584940.full) (older).
- Adoption gated by qualification: curvilinear 'has become viable' but 'industry volumes are still relatively low' (Micron's Scheid); 'mask defect inspection remains the biggest technical bottleneck' (Levinson); curvilinear metrology 'not a settled issue yet' (D2S' Fujimura) — [SemiEngineering, 21 May 2026](https://semiengineering.com/mask-technology-faces-a-new-set-of-challenges/). Survey of >45 companies (July 2026): 87% expect most leading-edge mask shops to make at least one mostly curvilinear layer within three years, 'beyond fixing hot spots'; mask-shop software infrastructure is the top concern — [eBeam Initiative, 9 Sep 2026](https://design2silicon.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/eBeam-Initiative-BACUS-2026-Roundup-Release-FINAL.pdf) (forum run by D2S; semi-independent). Tekscend's AI defect classification 'isn't in production yet' — [SemiEngineering, 16 Apr 2026](https://semiengineering.com/ebeam-initiative-at-spie-alp-2026-continuing-progress-on-curvilinear-euv-and-data-challenges/).
- Learned models that are in production sit in process development: Silvaco's FTCO builds a 'digital twin' from 'manufacturing and simulated data' — [Silvaco 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1943289/000162828026017378/svco-20251231.htm); Micron's Gurtej Sandhu: 'Silvaco's FTCO platform has been instrumental' (in a Silvaco release) — [Silvaco Q2 2026, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001943289/000162828026054291/a991earningsreleasedatedau.htm). ### Inferences
- Transition 5 (learned models inside solvers that still certify) is happening inside the incumbents' tools (Siemens NN-assisted OPC, Synopsys CNN-plus-compact models), which strengthens rather than displaces the incumbents' 'judge' role.
- No public evidence was found of a learned model being the final OPC or lithography-verification check in volume production; vendor statements consistently keep a physics-based check last.

### Gaps
- No named foundry has confirmed ML OPC or ML resist/etch models in high-volume production; Siemens' 'two-thirds' and 'hundreds of times' claims lack baselines.

## 6. Export controls on manufacturing-simulation software

### Takeaway
US controls on these tools are narrow and layered: EUV computational lithography is listed, DUV computational lithography only towards listed entities, and ECAD/TCAD through advanced-node end-use rules. The biggest commercial shock was the temporary all-EDA licence requirement of May-July 2025. Dutch 2025 changes covered equipment; Japanese coverage of software is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- 2020: BIS expanded ECCN 3D003 to computational-lithography software for EUV masks/reticles (law-firm and university summaries) — [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/export-and-cfius-implications-new-emerging-technologies-added-to-export-control-list.html); [Univ. of Arizona, 3 Nov 2020](https://research.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/emergingtech_exportcontrol_11.03.2020_0.pdf). Oct 2023 BIS rule made conforming changes to 3D001-3D003 — [Federal Register, 25 Oct 2023](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-10-25/html/2023-23049.htm).
- 15 Aug 2022: ECCN 3D006 for ECAD 'specially designed' for GAAFET ICs (RTL-to-GDSII, or power/timing optimisation), effective 14 Oct 2022, NS/AT; TCAD and computational lithography not mentioned — [Federal Register](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2022-08-15/html/2022-17125.htm).
- Dec 2024 rule (effective 2 Dec 2024): 3D992.b ECAD for multi-die integration (floor planning plus die-package co-design) licensed to Macau/D:5; 3D993.c software for 'patterns on DUV lithography masks or reticles' (RS, towards Entity List footnote-5 parties); 3D993.d DUV productivity software; §744.23 restricts ECAD and TCAD known to be used for advanced-node ICs produced in Macau/D:5; rationale: such software 'can improve the minimum feature size achievable with DUV to less than 40nm' and aid multipatterning — [Federal Register, 5 Dec 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-12-05/html/2024-28270.htm).
- 2025 EDA licence episode: BIS letters (Cadence 23 May, Synopsys 29 May 2025) required licences for EDA software and technology under 3D991/3E991 to China or Chinese military end users; rescinded 2 Jul 2025; Cadence says China revenue fell 'due to reduced deliveries' — [Cadence 10-K FY2025](https://s206.q4cdn.com/597110084/files/doc_downloads/2026/Mar/CDNS-FY-2025-Form-10-K.pdf); Synopsys suspended Q3 and FY2025 guidance — [Synopsys, 29 May 2025](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-05-29-Synopsys-Issues-Statement-in-Connection-with-BIS-Letter); [Synopsys, 2 Jul 2025](https://news.synopsys.com/2025-07-02-Synopsys-Issues-Statement-in-Connection-to-the-Lifting-of-Recent-U-S-Export-Restrictions-Related-to-China); Synopsys FY2025 China revenue down 22% ex-Ansys, citing restrictions that 'disrupted customer design starts' and BIS subpoenas (synopsys strand digest of 10-K) — [Synopsys 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883241/000088324125000028/snps-20251031.htm); 'Gone in 40 days' — [The Register, 3 Jul 2025](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/03/us_eda_export_ban_lifted/). Silvaco did not receive the letters — [Silvaco 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1943289/000162828026017378/svco-20251231.htm).
- BIS 50% Affiliates Rule (effective 29 Sep 2025) suspended on 11 Nov 2025 for a year (Cadence 10-K, above); suspension extended to 10 Jan 2027 — [GlobalSanctions, Nov 2026](https://globalsanctions.com/2026/11/us-bis-extends-suspension-50-rule-coming-into-force-until-10-january-2027/).
- Netherlands: expansion announced 15 Jan 2025, effective 1 Apr 2025, covering 'specific measuring and inspection equipment'; ASML expects no impact; software not mentioned — [Government of the Netherlands](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2025/01/15/klever-export-controls-on-advanced-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment-to-be-tightened).

### Inferences
- Vendors' main export risk is episodic, politically driven licence demands on all EDA (3D991) rather than the narrow listed controls; mature-node TCAD and OPC sales to China mostly remain licence-free, so China exposure (e.g., Silvaco 20%) is a political rather than a legal-listing risk.

## 7. Link to AI hardware constraints and demand for these tools

### Takeaway
These tools sit directly on the binding constraints for AI computing in 2028-2030: package thermal and mechanical limits, co-packaged optics and high-NA/curvilinear patterning, and after 2030 on new transistor architectures (CFET, stacked nanosheets). Demand signals are rising, but mostly captured by the three large EDA vendors through certified flows.

### Cited Findings

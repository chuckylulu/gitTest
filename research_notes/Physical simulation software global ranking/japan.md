# Japan: physical-simulation software vendors, trust boundary, national computing and entry points (as of 12 Nov 2026)

DRAFT v1 (saved after mining the first attempt's digest; gaps still being researched).

**Summary for the report writer.** Japan has few home-grown solver owners of global weight and a large layer of distributors and labour-hour integrators. The domestic mechanical CAE market was about JPY 104bn in 2024 (Yano forecast), dominated by foreign codes sold through Japanese channels (Cybernet for Ansys; IDAJ for GT-SUITE, CONVERGE, Simcenter, Ansys; Dentsu Soken and others). Japanese-owned solvers that matter: JSOL's JMAG (motor electromagnetics; JSOL is 50/50 NTT DATA and SMFG's Japan Research Institute, so not investable), Matlantis (universal AI interatomic potential PFP, 150+ customer organisations; private, owned by Preferred Networks, ENEOS and Mitsubishi Corp), Prometech's Particleworks (GPU particle CFD; private), Femtet (Murata Software) and AdvanceSoft's national-project codes (small). Software Cradle (scFLOW/STREAM) is Japanese in origin but is now inside Cadence after Cadence bought Hexagon's design and engineering business (closed Feb 2026). Listed names are small: Zuken (PCB and harness EDA with SI/PI analysis; JPY 114bn market value; record results, guidance raised 9 Nov 2026), Kozo Keikaku Engineering Holdings (structural and seismic analysis plus consulting; JPY 30bn), JEDAT (IC layout EDA; JPY 4bn), Fixstars (acceleration software and services), HPC Systems (HPC hardware integrator) and Sakura Internet (GPU cloud). No Japanese company ranks near the global top tier on solver ownership plus scale; realistic placements are JMAG and Matlantis as niche leaders in their physics, Zuken as a second-tier EDA vendor, and the rest as channels. The trust boundary is mostly set by foreign or international rules (Euro NCAP virtual testing, class rules), with two domestic gates: minister-certified structural calculation programs under the Building Standards Act, and NRA review of safety-analysis codes. FugakuNEXT (RIKEN; Fujitsu MONAKA-X CPUs; NVIDIA GPUs; target around 2030) is specified at 2.6+ EFLOPS FP64 from GPUs plus 48+ PFLOPS from CPUs (about 4.8x Fugaku) but 600+ EFLOPS FP8 with sparsity; the 'zettascale' claim refers to low-precision AI throughput, not FP64. Coverage is thin for private firms (no revenue for JMAG, Prometech, Matlantis, IDAJ; Cybernet's Ansys share undisclosed) and for third-party share data, which sits in paid Yano reports.

## 1. Map of the field: who matters, ownership, size, what they sell

### Takeaway
The Japanese field splits into (a) a handful of own-solver developers, mostly private or captive subsidiaries; (b) large distributors and system integrators reselling Ansys, Siemens, Dassault, Cadence/Hexagon and Gamma codes; (c) small listed EDA/HPC names; (d) national labs and agencies that write their own codes (JMA, JAXA, RIKEN). Ownership consolidation since 2024 removed several listed names (Cybernet, Fuji Soft, SCSK).

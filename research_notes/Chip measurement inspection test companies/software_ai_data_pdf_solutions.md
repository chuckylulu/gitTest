## Q1. PDF Solutions business quality (tests a-e)

### Takeaway
PDF is a 20%-growth data/analytics vendor with 77% non-GAAP gross margins. Its most defensible assets are network and per-tool tolls (secureWISE, Cimetrix) and yield-linked Gainshare. Weaknesses: two customers made 46% of Q3-26 revenue; GAAP returns are thin (ROIC about 5%); eProbe hardware-as-a-service makes it capex-heavy; and outside evidence of a lead comes mostly through company channels.

### Inferences
- Customer B's jump (12% to 26%) coincides with Japan revenue doubling, so B is probably Japan-billed. Unnamed.
- On the thesis's ladder PDF sits in the middle. It is not a per-seat vendor, and it sells some engineering hours (FAEs are 30% of staff). Its main earnings are subscription, per-tool and yield-linked. It owns a "judge" only through eProbe (about 12 tools) and does not hold customers' TEM/SEM reference data.
- Under the AI-spending scenarios: a 2028 financing-led pause would hit Cimetrix runtime (tied to tool shipments), Gainshare and eProbe placements first. secureWISE (installed base) and multi-year analytics would hold up better. Faster acceleration helps all of these through more tools and more data.

### Gaps
- Customer identities; separate revenue for secureWISE, Cimetrix and eProbe; Advantest's residual stake (probably zero, since it sold the same number of shares it bought in 2020; not confirmed).

## Q7. Prior C: learned denoising is safe for the expected and risky for the unexpected

### Takeaway
The literature uses learned denoising mainly to speed up measurement of known patterns. It flags failure on features outside the training set, and artefacts that bias CD and LER. No study quantifying erased or invented defects in inspection was found, so the "risky for the unexpected" half rests on indirect evidence.

### Cited Findings
- SK hynix/Gauss Labs, "Universal denoiser to improve metrology throughput" (SPIE AL 2024) — [SPIE](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12955/1295536/Universal-denoiser-to-improve-metrology-throughput/10.1117/12.3010773.short). Nova, "A flexible deep learning based approach for SEM image denoising" (SPIE 12955, 2024) — [PDF](https://www.novami.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/a-flexible-deep-learning-based-approach-for-sem-image-denoising.pdf)
- Search excerpts from SEM-denoising papers make these points. Supervised denoisers are "notably affected by... pattern shrinkage and surface charging" in reference images. Performance "may degrade" on features not in the training set; statistical deviation monitoring reached 100% detection and 0% false detection in the authors' tests. Some synthetic-data approaches "may lead to additional artefacts" affecting LER/LWR and CD. Noise itself causes "false defect detections and erroneous metrology". Candidate sources: [SPIE 12496 (2023)](https://biomedicaloptics.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12496/124961D/Self-supervised-deep-learning-neural-network-for-CD-SEM-image/10.1117/12.2659168.full), [SPIE 10959 (2019)](https://electronicimaging.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/10959/1095916/Deep-learning-based-SEM-image-denoiser-Conference-Presentation/10.1117/12.2515182.full) and the Nova paper above. Which paper makes which statement (the search tool attributed some to Hitachi authors) could not be verified.
- Inspection AI depends on scarce and biased defect data: "wafer defects are generally rare" (Microtronic); "datasets we got from our customers are biased" (Nordson); synthetic data "often essential". Hallucination is not discussed — [SemiEngineering, 9 Jun 2026](https://semiengineering.com/ai-models-transform-defect-inspection-and-review-but-can-fail-to-scale/)
- Vendor safeguards found: hybrid physics models (Onto); workflow traceability as an anti-hallucination guardrail (PDF, company claim); out-of-distribution monitors (papers above).

## Q9. Open question: who ends up owning the reference data and the value?

### Takeaway
On current evidence chipmakers keep device, process and TEM reference data, and build on general AI platforms (NVIDIA at TSMC) or with captive partners (SK hynix with Gauss). Vendors get equipment-health data and monetise AI inside tool prices or as access and usage tolls. Cross-customer pooling exists only in narrow, low-sensitivity cases. No vendor was found selling separately priced measurement-AI data revenue apart from PDF's tolls.

### Cited Findings
- "It's tough to get device and process data out"; equipment-health data is least protected; "people are treating ML models as trade secrets" (Teradyne). Sharing is "decided at the contractual stage" (Synopsys). Standards "have a hard time keeping pace" — [SemiEngineering, 13 Jan 2026](https://semiengineering.com/secure-data-sharing-becoming-critical-for-chip-manufacturing/)
- Reference data is the training anchor: TEM cross-sections are needed to train GAA indent ML (IBM-Nova 2022), and the 2025 modeless method is validated by TEM — [IBM 2022](https://research.ibm.com/publications/development-of-sige-indentation-process-control-for-gate-all-around-fet-technology-enablement); [IBM 2025](https://research.ibm.com/publications/scatterometry-informed-machine-learning-study-to-determine-bi-directional-intercorrelation-of-adjacent-patterning-steps)
- In-house at TSMC: using or piloting NVIDIA CUDA-X, cuLitho, Metropolis/TAO (rare-defect inspection) and Omniverse FabTwin across lithography, APC and inspection. All claims are NVIDIA's (31 May 2026); TSMC published no metrics — [summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/tsmc-adopts-nvidia-accelerated-computing-for-fab-automation-29d5ad01); [NVIDIA blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/taiwan-ecosystem-ai-infrastructure/).

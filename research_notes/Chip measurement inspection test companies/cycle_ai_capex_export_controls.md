## 1. One-page summary

**Cycle position: a strong upcycle, still accelerating into 2027, limited by supply rather than demand. No turn in orders is visible.**
- **Spending (WFE = wafer-fab equipment)**
  - 2025: record $116.9B (SEMI, 15 Jul 2026).
  - 2026: SEMI's July forecast is $143.9B (+23.1%). The equipment makers are higher: KLA and Lam both put CY2026 in the "mid-$150B" range in late Oct 2026, and Gartner's 13 Oct 2026 update reportedly has 2026 at +29.4% (URL not resolved).
  - 2027: KLA and Lam see >$200B (about +30%). SEMI's July path implies about $175B (+21.8%), and Gartner reportedly +16.5%.
  - 2028: SEMI expects about $200B (+14.1%). Sources disagree most on 2027, and definitions differ.
- **Upward revisions over the past year**
  - SEMI's 300mm outlook for 2026 went from $116B (Oct 2025 edition) to $149B (Oct 2026 edition).
  - SEMI's Dec 2025 headline was $156B of total equipment in 2027; the July 2026 forecast already has $165.9B for 2026.
- **Mix: memory is taking a larger share**
  - Memory was 46% of Lam's equipment revenue in the Sep-Q 2026 (34% a year earlier).
  - KLA models memory at about 40% of process-control systems in the Dec-Q 2026.
  - SEMI forecasts DRAM equipment up 39% in 2026.
- **Measurement, inspection and test are gaining share**
  - KLA's process-control share of WFE (its own metric) was about 6% before 2022, 7.4% in 2025, and is targeted at about 9% by 2030.
  - KLA's advanced-packaging (AP) process-control revenue is about $1.1B in CY2026 (more than +70%).
  - SEMI: test equipment $15.3B in 2026 (+31%) after +55.3% in 2025; assembly/packaging equipment $6.7B (+9.6%).
- **Supply limits**
  - ASML says its planned 2027 and 2028 supply "will not fully meet" demand, and some customers want 2029-30 slots.
  - Lam cites cleanroom space and component supply.
  - SK hynix says DRAM and HBM are effectively sold out through 2029.
  - TSMC's CoWoS packaging is already constrained.

**Scenario tracking (priors: steady about 55% / financing-led pause around 2028 about 25% / acceleration about 20%)**
- **Acceleration (evidence for):** four straight WFE raises at KLA; about 30% WFE growth in both 2026 and 2027; memory sold out; ASML orders reaching 2029-30; SK hynix projecting cloud-provider capex up at least 60% in 2027; OpenAI's compute plans raised about 25% to about $750B through 2030 (Sep 2026, secondary).
- **Steady, measured on compute supply:** CoWoS capacity +114% in 2027 (Goldman) and TSMC N2 capacity more than doubling in 2027 both fit "doubling" in 2027. But CoWoS capacity planned for 2028 grows only about 27% (Goldman), and SEMI's WFE growth slows to 14% in 2028, so the 2028 supply plans already slow down. That is evidence of a 2028 deceleration, though it may be supply-planned rather than financing-driven.
- **Financing-led pause (evidence for):** the stress shows up in **how capex is funded**, not in equipment orders.
  - Alphabet sold $12.4B of stock through at-the-market (ATM) programmes, issued $28.7B of notes, bought back no stock and had negative Q3 free cash flow.
  - Meta launched an equity raise on 26 Oct 2026 and plans an ATM programme of about $60B from 19 Jan 2027.
  - Amazon's trailing-twelve-month free cash flow was -$42.4B.
  - Microsoft has $364.6B of datacentre leases that have not yet started, a CY2026 capex of about $170B, and delays in third-party capacity.
  - Oracle plans FY27 capex of up to $95B and is raising about $40B; Moody's has its rating on negative outlook.
  - S&P (Sep 2026) says hyperscaler credit quality is "gradually weakening". Hyperscaler debt issuance was $220B by 10 Aug 2026, with investor "fatigue" and wider spreads.
- **Against a pause:** no WFE cancellations found, only small push-outs (Bruker's $20M). SK hynix has long-term agreements covering at least 50% of 2027-28 capacity. SK hynix says customers are "adjusting pace... rather than halting demand".
- **Net inference (mine):** tilt 2026-27 toward acceleration, and do not cut the 2028 financing-pause weight.
- **Binding constraints differ from the background view:** packaging (CoWoS) is binding *now*, not only in 2028-30. Memory tightness runs to 2029-30. Cleanroom and tool supply bind in 2027-28; this constraint is missing from the background list.

**Export-control status (12 Nov 2026)**
- **Affiliates (50%) Rule:** interim final rule 30 Sep 2025; stayed 10 Nov 2025; stay **extended to 10 Jan 2027** (Federal Register doc 2026-22793, published 9 Nov 2026).
- **Older packages still in force:** Oct 2022, Oct 2023/Apr 2024 and Dec 2024. The Dec 2024 package covers "metrology and inspection" among 24 tool types, adds new foreign-direct-product rules and 140 Entity List additions.
- **Entity List:** additions frozen Oct 2025-Jun 2026. A package of 75+ entities, including CXMT, was reportedly cleared but held (Jun 2026).
- **Enforcement:** BIS moved some §744.23 licence reviews to case-by-case on 15 Jan 2026. Applied Materials paid a $252.5M settlement on 11 Feb 2026.
- **MATCH Act** (servicing, parts, DUV): passed the House Foreign Affairs Committee 44-0 on 22 Apr 2026. No floor vote or defence-bill (NDAA) attachment found.
- **Netherlands:** national licence for certain measurement and inspection tools since 1 Apr 2025.
- **Section 232:** 25% on a narrow set of chips since 15 Jan 2026; equipment not tariffed; no Phase 2 decision found.
- **China:** rare-earth suspension extended to 10 Jan 2027 (9 Nov 2026), while the Apr 2025 controls remain. The gallium/germanium/antimony ban is suspended **only until 27 Nov 2026**. A 50% domestic-equipment rule for new fabs is reported but unpublished. Anti-dumping and anti-discrimination probes on US chips have been running since Sep 2025.

**Events in the next six months**
- Mid-Nov 2026: Trump-Xi meeting at APEC in Shenzhen.
- Nov 2026: Applied Materials FQ4 results (date not verified).
- **27 Nov 2026: gallium/germanium suspension lapses unless extended.**
- Dec 2026: SEMI Q3 billings and SEMI year-end forecast (last year's came 16 Dec 2025).
- **10 Jan 2027: the Affiliates Rule stay, China's rare-earth suspension and the 24% tariff suspension all lapse unless extended.**
- Mid-Jan 2027: TSMC gives 2027 capex guidance. Brokers expect $83-90B, against $60-64B for 2026.
- Late Jan to early Feb 2027: ASML, KLA and Lam December-quarter results, and hyperscalers' 2027 capex guidance.
- 19 Jan 2027: Meta's ATM programme can begin.
- By about 13 Mar 2027: China's US analog-chip anti-dumping probe must conclude if it was extended (outcome not found).
- Undated: Entity List package, MATCH Act or NDAA, Section 232 Phase 2.

## 3. Supporting detail

### 3.1 WFE spending: actuals, forecasts, mix, process control, test and packaging

**Takeaway:** WFE set a record of $116.9B in 2025 and is heading for about $144-155B in 2026, depending on source. The equipment makers' first views of 2027 (>$200B) are well above SEMI's July 2026 forecast and Gartner's October update. Measurement, inspection and test are growing faster than WFE. Assembly/packaging *equipment* is growing slower, but AP *inspection* is growing much faster.

### 3.3 Scenario tracking and behaviour in past pauses

**Takeaway:** Orders and backlogs support steady-to-accelerating spending through 2027. The funding mix is the main evidence for a 2028 financing-pause risk. In the past two pauses, test and especially assembly/packaging equipment fell far more than wafer-processing equipment.

**Cited findings: past pauses**
- **2019:**
  - Total equipment -7% to $59.8B (2018: $64.5B). Wafer processing -6%; **test -11%; assembly and packaging -27%** (SEMI, Apr 2020; [I-Connect007](https://iconnect007.com/article/122510/2019-global-semiconductor-equipment-sales-slip-7-to-598-billion-semi-reports/122513/ein); [The Edge](https://www.theedgemarkets.com/article/global-semicon-equipment-sales-fell-7-yoy-2019-us598-billion-says-semi)) [S].
  - SEMI's Jul 2019 mid-year forecast had been far worse: total -18.4% to $52.7B; wafer processing -19.1%; test -16.4% to $4.7B; assembly/packaging -22.6% to $3.1B ([I-Connect007](https://iconnect007.com/article/118372/global-sales-of-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment-to-drop-184-to-527b-in-2019/118375/ein)) [S].
- **2023:**
  - Total -1.3% to $106.3B (2022 record $107.6B). Wafer processing **+1%**; other front-end +10%; **test -17%; assembly and packaging -30%**, after already contracting in 2022 (SEMI, Apr 2024; [The Star, 12 Apr 2024](https://thestar.com.my/business/business-news/2024/04/12/global-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment-sales-dip-to-us1063bil-in-2023); [I-Connect007](https://iconnect007.com/article/140318/global-semiconductor-equipment-billings-slip-to-1063-billion-in-2023/140315/ein)) [S].

### 3.5 Export controls and trade restrictions

**Takeaway:** a managed truce. The rules that bite on inspection, metrology and test stay in force, and the scheduled changes cluster on **27 Nov 2026** (China's gallium/germanium suspension) and **10 Jan 2027** (the Affiliates Rule stay, rare earths and tariffs).

**Cited findings**
- **Oct 2022 interim final rule (7 Oct 2022):** licences required for US persons supporting IC development or production at certain China fabs, effective 12 Oct 2022. One summary says activities related to servicing specific items are carved out absent knowledge of a violation ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2022/10/sweeping-us-export-controls-on-semiconductor-supercomputer-manufacturing-in-china-raise-compliance-questions); [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2022/10/us-imposes-additional-export-controls-restrictions-on-advanced-computing-and-semiconductor-manufacturing-items)) [S].
- **Oct 2023 and Apr 2024:** "updated rules" (BIS) [P]; details not retrieved.
- **Dec 2024 package** (2 Dec 2024; compliance date for some controls 31 Dec 2024; [BIS press release](https://bis.gov/press-release/commerce-strengthens-export-controls-restrict-chinas-capability-produce-advanced-semiconductors-military)) [P]
  - Controls on **24 types of tools**, including "metrology and inspection", and 3 software tools.
  - HBM controls; software-key controls.
  - New foreign-direct-product (FDP) rules for chip equipment (where there is "knowledge" of a Macau or D:5 destination) and for entities designated under footnote 5. De minimis applies to items with "any amount" of US-origin ICs.
  - **140 Entity List additions** (fabs, tool makers, investors) and 14 modifications.

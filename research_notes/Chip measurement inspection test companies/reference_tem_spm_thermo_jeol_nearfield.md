## 2. Supporting detail: business quality and transitions (no share-price data here)

### 2.1 How reference data is produced, and the data-scarcity problem

#### Takeaway
Reference data comes from focused-ion-beam (FIB) lamella preparation followed by TEM/STEM imaging, plus slice-and-view on a FIB-SEM. All the vendors are automating this chain and moving it from the lab into the fab. The technical literature agrees that reference data is scarce and expensive because it is destructive and slow.

#### Cited findings
**Thermo Fisher's own description (14 Aug 2025) [S14]:**
- Four reasons TEM is the reference: 3D complexity, shrinking critical dimensions, EUV, and atomic interface analysis.
- Gate-all-around and 3D NAND need "atomic-scale imaging".
- Manufacturers generate "tens of thousands of TEM datasets monthly" (company claim, unconfirmed).

**Thermo Fisher automation products:**
- **Metrios 6 (S)TEM** (27 Jun 2023): fully automated. Claims up to 20% higher productivity and EDS data collection at least twice as fast (company claims) [S12].
- **Metrios 6S** adds SEMI-standard grid handling and a host connection, so grids move automatically from the Helios 6 HXS FIB-SEM [S13].
- **Vulcan Automated Lab** (18 Mar 2025): robotic handling, AI-enhanced imaging and "real-time lab-to-fab data exchange", described as "among the first commercially available" systems of its kind (company claim). The workflow runs Helios 6 HXS plus Metrios 6S, linked by mobile robots or overhead transport [S11][S13].
- **Helios MX1 PFIB-SEM** (28 Oct 2025): placed in the fab for "immediate in-line data", with automated 3D reconstruction and metrology [S10].
  - Argon plasma FIB "improved throughput by 20x compared to traditional gallium-based milling" (company claim; no test conditions given).
  - "Outsourcing metrology or defect analysis to the lab can add several days to the turnaround time" (Mohan Iyer, VP/GM Semiconductor, 6 Oct 2025) [S16].

**JEOL (29 May 2026) [S21]:**
- FIB/TEM structural measurement has become "indispensable for yield improvement" in advanced fabs, and "evaluation at major customers is progressing smoothly" (company claims).
- Products: JIB-PS500i FIB-SEM, JEM-ACE200F high-throughput TEM.

**Literature on data scarcity:**
- Onto Innovation (SPIE, posted 7 Sep 2022): ML-based OCD suffers from "expensive and limited reference data or labeled data set", which "often leads to under- or overlearning". The proposed remedy is to add process information and physical modelling [S49].
- Boehm & Dey (arXiv 2606.24817; v1 23 Jun 2026, v4 26 Oct 2026):
  - TEM's "destructive sample preparation, slow imaging and high costs severely limit the availability of diverse datasets".
  - They train a diffusion model from 15 samples to make synthetic TEM images as ML training data.
  - Their headline MS-SSIM of 0.985 applies only to the closest 1% of image pairs; the mean is 0.587 [S49].
  - A search summary gives the affiliations as Chemnitz University of Technology and imec; the arXiv page does not confirm this.

**Analyst view:** TrendForce (13 Oct 2026) says buried gate-all-around structures make post-fabrication measurement nearly impossible (paraphrased), which pushes inspection in-line. It also says HBM dies must be verified as known-good before stacking, and that "the cost of testing is lower than the cost of failure" [S50].

**Market size and shares:** only aggregator figures, which conflict. For example, the top three vendors are put at about 97% of TEM, with no methodology [S39].

#### Inferences
- Rising destructive-data volume, together with ML's appetite for labelled data, raises the value of whoever holds the reference data.
- The vendors' answer so far is automation and in-fab placement, which keeps the data inside the customer's walls.

#### Gaps
- Cost and throughput per TEM sample.
- How tool purchases split between chipmaker labs and independent labs.
- Third-party market size and shares.

### 2.7 Who ends up owning the reference data? Evidence on both sides

#### Takeaway
As of 12 Nov 2026 the evidence points to the **chipmakers**. They generate and keep the datasets. Labs hold data only temporarily, under confidentiality. Tool makers own how the data is produced and connected, but I found no evidence that they hold rights to the data.

#### Cited findings
**Toward chipmakers:**
- Manufacturers generate the "tens of thousands" of monthly TEM datasets (Thermo Fisher claim) [S14].
- Automation is sold into customer fabs with a host connection [S10][S11][S13].
- Intel and imec run in-house automated TEM [S11].
- Samsung's metrology VP decided Nearfield's qualification [S29].
- The NIST/NSF data-sharing workshop (Nov 2025) pooled data from "a coalition of manufacturers". It found that "training data from similar operations at different factories led to more robust modeling". Vendors are not mentioned [S48].

**Toward labs:**
- Lab revenue grew 16-32% in Q3 2026 on R&D and validation work [S37][S44].
- Labs sell segregated capacity (MSScorps' dedicated zones) and security (EAG) [S44][S47]. This suggests the data stays with the client, but I found no contract terms (inference).

**Toward tool makers:**
- Integration layers: Thermo Fisher's lab-to-fab data exchange [S11], and Shimadzu combining its own data with Tescan's [S41].
- Software platforms ingest TEM data alongside CD-SEM and optical data (Pollen Metrology), and analytics is offered with an on-premises option (PDF Solutions; snippets only) [S51].
- Synthetic TEM data trained from 15 samples could reduce dependence on chipmaker data (inference) [S49].
- **No vendor-owned reference dataset, data-licensing deal or vendor reference fab was found.**

#### Gaps
No chipmaker-vendor data-sharing contract, no lab data-rights terms, and no details of chipmakers' in-house AI metrology programmes were found.
